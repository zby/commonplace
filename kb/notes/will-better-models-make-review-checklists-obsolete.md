# Will better models make review checklists obsolete?

“New models stop making yesterday’s mistakes” is a good reason to retire yesterday’s checklist items. It isn’t a reason to retire critique. “Does the title state a claim?” names the mistake in advance. “What’s the strongest objection to this claim, and does the text answer it?” leaves the objection for the reviewer to discover. A better writer can make the first check unnecessary; a better critic can make the second question more useful without changing a word of the instruction. Keep both while they earn their cost.

## Scope

That distinction is a reason to test what a better critic can do, not evidence that a particular upgrade improved criticism. The [Commonplace critique instruction](../instructions/critique-note.md) also permits “no surviving attack.” Judge the critic by whether its objection holds up against the text, not by how many faults it reports.
