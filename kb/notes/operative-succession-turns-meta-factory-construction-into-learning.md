# Evidence-responsive operative succession turns meta-factory construction into learning

Greenfield already permits factory construction to be product activity of a
meta-factory. The extension here is not that a factory can produce another
factory. It is that evidence from production computationally determines a
change to reusable production machinery and that the retained result becomes
operative with authority over subsequent work.

The product/factory distinction is producer-relative. For a fixed reference
factory, [product development](./definitions/factory-development.md) changes one
of its members and factory development changes its reusable machinery. When
meta-factory \(A\) constructs factory \(B\), the same work is product development
relative to \(A\) and factory development relative to \(B\). \(B\) is both a
factory-valued member of \(A\)'s family and a producer for its own family. It is
not a [successor factory](./definitions/successor-factory.md) merely because it
was produced.

Four transitions must therefore remain separate:

| Transition | Immediate result | What remains unestablished |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary member production | A family member whose state is expressed through lifecycle work products | The producing factory's reusable machinery need not change. |
| Meta-factory construction | A factory-valued member that can produce members of its own family | The target specialization may be human supplied, and no predecessor or operative succession relation need exist. |
| Successor installation | Changed factory machinery becomes operative after a declared incumbent and governs later routed work | The change need not be evidence-responsive, computationally determined, or better. |
| Closed factory learning | Permitted production evidence computationally determines and makes operative a reusable factory change | Improvement, breadth, and indefinite repeatability still require separate evidence. |

The second row is established prior art. Greenfield and Short say verbatim that
“software factories can be used to produce other software factories” ([their
2003 account](../sources/greenfield-short-software-factories-oopsla-2003.ingest.md)).
Their factory-building factory still equips human product-line developers to
define and package the target machinery.

Cook and Kent make generation and recursion more concrete, stating verbatim:
“This allows the tool factory to bootstrap itself from one version to the next”
([their Tool Factory proposal](../sources/cook-kent-tool-factory-2003.ingest.md)).
The bootstrap consumes a language-family definition and, for generator
bootstrapping, supplied mappings to implementation languages and frameworks.

MDSoFa similarly states verbatim that “a model driven software factory can
produce a model-driven factory” ([its factory-factory
account](../sources/langlois-exertier-mdsofa-software-factory-factory-2004.ingest.md)),
while human metamodel designers and architects provide metamodels, mappings,
aspects, expertise, platforms, and strategy choices.

These systems automate increasing portions of factory construction. They do not
establish the stronger transition in which production experience
computationally determines changed family specialization and makes it operative
for later work. A generator can reproduce its own tool class while every
semantically decisive change to the language family still originates with a
person.

## Evidence-responsive operativity supplies the missing conjunct

Let \(F_t\) be the factory governing production at time \(t\) and \(e_t\) the
permitted evidence from that production process. The proposed learning
transition is:

`production under F_t -> evidence e_t -> computationally determined reusable change -> operative retention -> F_{t+1} governs later production`

The transition may update machinery directly. It may instead expose proposals,
reject-capable evaluation, selection, and promotion. Those are implementation
roles, not definitional requirements. A direct update qualifies when
computation determines the reusable change from permitted evidence and makes it
operative.

Factory development becomes an activity of the operative factory in this
extended sense when its declared computational organization performs that
factory-development work. Succession is a further relation: the resulting
factory must enter a live authority path and govern later covered work. Learning
adds the evidence-responsive causal relation. A stored factory candidate has
none of these operative consequences; an arbitrary human-installed factory may
be a successor without being a computational learning result.

When every in-scope development decision and transition on this path is
computational, conditional only on permitted external evidence and interaction,
the occurrence belongs to a [computationally closed software-factory learning
loop](./definitions/computationally-closed-software-factory-learning-loop.md).
A closed occurrence therefore produces a successor factory. The converse does
not hold: succession alone says neither who determined the change nor why.

## Generic harness optimization is only an analogy unless the family boundary exists

A harness that persistently changes later software-production behavior is
[behavior-determining organization](./definitions/behavior-determining-organization.md).
Updating it can be system learning, and making an update operative can produce a
system successor in an ordinary sense. It counts as Greenfield-style factory
machinery only when it belongs to a declared family-specific lifecycle
production arrangement. A generic coding harness, optimizer, or agent runtime
does not become a software factory merely because its behavior affects later
software work.

Within an actual software factory, searching or directly updating a persistent
harness component can be factory development. The result becomes a successor
factory only when the changed family-scoped arrangement enters its live
production authority path. Episode-local adaptation, a better answer, or an
optimized harness left in an experiment store does not cross that boundary.

## Operative recursion does not establish reach

Moving factory development into operation says which actor performs a
transition and where its result lands. It does not say which specializations the
pathway can construct. A closed updater for one fixed product family may be
genuinely recursive and narrow. A meta-factory that accepts arbitrary
human-authored schemas may be constructionally expressive while leaving every
novel family's semantic decomposition outside the computational loop.

[Domain extensibility](./definitions/domain-extensible-software-factory.md) adds
the missing breadth condition. Over a declared demand class and evidence
protocol, computation must acquire and install the family-specific machinery
needed for sufficiently novel covered demands without a human performing the
target factory-development decisions. The fixed general method may remain human
designed. The stronger claim concerns computational production of required
task-specific specialization, not self-modification of every runtime, language,
evaluator, or trusted kernel.

## Scope

- *Factory activity* is an explicit extension used for this theory. It does not
  assert that Greenfield classified factory development as an activity inside a
  software factory schema.
- Factory-valued production and factory-construction processes are prior art.
  The proposed addition is evidence-responsive computational determination plus
  operative retention relative to an incumbent.
- A schema, template, tool, or partial asset is candidate factory machinery, not
  by itself a complete configured software factory.
- One installed successor does not show that the successor preserves the same
  learning path or can produce another successor.
- Calling the transition learning requires evidence-responsive change under a
  declared objective. Installing an arbitrary generated factory establishes
  succession, not learning or improvement.
- Fixed general machinery is compatible with the claim. A component falls
  inside the required update surface only when the declared learning, closure,
  or domain-reach claim assigns its revision to the pathway.
