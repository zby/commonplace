# Productive deferral requires a preserved option, discriminating evidence, and a convergence rule

Productive deferral is a controlled way to postpone a consequential choice.
It requires a preserved option, discriminating evidence, and a convergence
rule. Without the option, the choice has already been made or lost. Without
the evidence, waiting or further work has no route to a different conditional
choice. Without convergence, preservation and learning do not say when or how
the choice returns to a decision process. These conditions are individually
necessary. Together they do not prove that waiting will be worth its cost.

## The option must remain materially live

A preserved option is an alternative that present commitment would destroy or
make costly to recover, and that remains exercisable through the later decision
point. An alternative retained only in a list is not preserved if expiry,
missing preparation, or exercise lead time will make it unavailable when the
decision returns.

[Real-options theory](../sources/pindyck-irreversibility-uncertainty-investment.ingest.md)
supplies the primary mechanism. When commitment is irreversible but can be
delayed, commitment surrenders the opportunity to observe before deciding
whether or when to exercise the future choice. The opportunity can expire,
and waiting can carry costs of its own. One live future exercise choice is
enough for this condition; productive deferral does not require several
parallel alternatives.

## Evidence must discriminate between possible actions

Evidence is discriminating only relative to the named decision. At least one
possible result of a later observation or bounded test must be capable of
changing selection, timing, modification, or abandonment. If every possible
result leaves the same action preferred, the delay or activity may produce
information, but it does not make this decision better informed. The evidence
need not remove all uncertainty.

Evidence may arrive without intervention, or work may produce it. In the
second case, focused tests can address a named knowledge gap and eliminate or
narrow alternatives before a latest convergence point, as in
[set-based practice](../sources/kennedy-sobek-kennedy-set-based-rework.ingest.md).
Such work is evidence-producing only when its possible outputs can change the
follow-on decision.

## Convergence couples a return condition to a response

A convergence rule names both what returns the choice to decision and what
happens next. The return condition may be one or a combination of a date, an
observed state, a test result, an evidence threshold, or approaching expiry.
The response may select, modify, abandon, authorize more bounded work, or
transfer the reopened choice to an authorized decision procedure. The rule
must fire early enough for the preserved option to remain feasible.

Different source methods realize this function differently. PMI defines
rolling-wave planning as detailed near-term planning with later work kept at a
higher level as information improves
([PMI](../sources/pmi-lexicon-project-management-terms-v4.ingest.md)). Githens
adds a fixed replanning date, assessment of learning, and replanning of the
next horizon
([Githens](../sources/githens-manage-innovation-programs-rolling-wave.ingest.md)).
Dynamic adaptive policy pathways use monitored signposts, triggers, mapped
responses, and lead-time-aware action
([Haasnoot et al.](../sources/haasnoot-dynamic-adaptive-policy-pathways-2013.ingest.md)).
A date without a response merely schedules discussion. A signal without a
mapped response still permits an ad hoc choice.

## Deferral still has a cost boundary

Even a controlled deferral can be the wrong choice. Foregone benefit, delay,
carrying several alternatives, preemption, lead time, or expiry can outweigh
the value of retaining a later choice. Commitment on current evidence or
deliberate abandonment can then be warranted. The three-part test rejects false
deferrals before this domain-specific comparison; it does not supply a
portable quantitative threshold for that comparison.

## The test distinguishes four postures

These postures apply the test; they are not an exhaustive taxonomy.

| Posture | Evidence route | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Commit on current evidence | No deferred evidence route is needed because no material option can be preserved, the current decision rule is met, or waiting costs dominate. | Select or abandon now. This is not a deferral posture. |
| Passive waiting | Await a named exogenous observation that can change the conditional action. | Preserve the option, specify a review or exercise rule, and check lead time and expiry. |
| Bounded probe | Pay for limited active work whose possible outputs can change a named decision. | Name the knowledge gap, cost, output, stop condition, and follow-on decision. |
| Maintain alternatives | Keep several feasible candidates live while a named observation or bounded test supplies comparison evidence against shared criteria. | Acknowledge carrying cost, eliminate or narrow candidates with evidence, and set a narrowing boundary. |

## Scope

The combined rule is a cross-source inference, not a formulation found in any
one source. Real-options timing, rolling-wave replanning, dynamic policy
triggers, and set-based narrowing differ in where evidence comes from, how many
alternatives they preserve, whether uncertainty declines, and how convergence
works. They support only the shared option–evidence–convergence mechanism.
This narrow transfer follows the rule that
[borrowed patterns transfer only over shared mechanism](./borrowed-patterns-transfer-only-over-shared-mechanism.md).

The supplied sources do not test whether the combined rule improves planning,
delegation, or structural-change outcomes in LLM-agent systems. They also do
not support a domain-independent numeric threshold that combines information
value with reversal, carrying, delay, and expiry costs. The rule can expose an
uncontrolled deferral and the estimates a domain-specific decision still
needs; it cannot establish target-side effectiveness or decide universally
between deferring and committing.
