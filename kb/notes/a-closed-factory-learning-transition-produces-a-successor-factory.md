# A closed factory-learning transition produces a successor factory

When learning occurs in a computationally closed software-factory pathway, the pathway computationally produces a [successor factory](./definitions/successor-factory.md). This follows from the meanings of *factory*, *learning*, *operative*, and *closed*; recursive self-production need not be assumed as a separate starting objective.

The derivation has five steps:

1. Relative to a fixed producer, a [software factory](./definitions/software-factory.md) is the family-scoped production machinery that governs software work, not the product work it produces.
2. A learning occurrence changes the system's own behavior-determining organization in causal response to evidence. Improving only the current product is therefore not factory learning.
3. Changing the reusable production machinery is [factory development](./definitions/factory-development.md), regardless of whether the transition updates it directly or first generates and evaluates candidates.
4. The change counts as learning only when it is operative: it enters a live authority path and later production depends on it. The operative result of such a retained factory-development transition is, by definition, a successor factory.
5. [Computational closure](./definitions/computationally-closed-software-factory-learning-loop.md) assigns every required decision and transition in that declared path to computation. It therefore makes the production of the successor computational, conditional on the permitted external task and environment evidence.

When the product is itself a factory, these roles do not collapse. The result is
a product relative to its meta-factory and production machinery relative to its
own family. It becomes a successor only if a separately declared transition
makes it operative relative to an incumbent.

The conclusion is narrower than “a closed loop always changes itself.” An iteration may reject a candidate, abstain, fail, or retain its incumbent through an entirely computational path. Such an iteration can exercise a closed pathway without being a learning occurrence. The claim applies when evidence actually produces an operative factory change.

Nor is *successor* a success claim. A badly evaluated or harmful change can become operative and therefore have a successor relation. Improvement, warrant, and rollback safety require separate evidence.

## What the derivation distinguishes

| Event | Producing factory's later machinery changes? | Successor factory? |
|---|---:|---:|
| A generator produces an ordinary new family member | No | No |
| A developer repairs one product work product | No | No |
| A candidate factory is generated or stored but never installed | Not operatively | No |
| A person interprets feedback and installs changed reusable machinery | Yes | Yes, but the transition is not computationally closed |
| A declared computational path turns permitted evidence into installed machinery used later | Yes | Yes, computationally produced |

External interaction does not defeat the last case. Users may state requirements, answer clarification questions, report bugs, supply tests, or judge product behavior through declared interfaces. The loop remains closed when computation, rather than a person, performs every factory-development decision assigned to the assessed pathway. Supplying the decisive schema, evaluator, candidate, promotion, or recovery is different: it exports a required transition and leaves that claimed path open.

## Scope

- The result is relative to a declared factory-learning path, task scope, evidence protocol, boundary, horizon, and coverage rule. It is not a whole-organization autonomy claim.
- One successor does not establish that the transition can repeat after installation, that improvement compounds, or that later successors preserve the path.
- Closure says nothing about how many product families the pathway covers. A narrow fixed-family loop and a broad domain-extensible loop can both satisfy the derivation.
- Fixed general machinery may remain outside the update surface. The derivation requires an operative change to the factory aspects the learning claim actually names, not universal self-modification.
