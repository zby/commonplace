# Abstractions

Make each abstraction in the artifact either earn its place everywhere or give
way to the ordinary word everywhere, so that one thing has one name.

Effort: judgment. Whether any sentence needs the distinction the abstraction
draws is a claim question, and the decision is per term, not per occurrence.

1. List each abstraction the artifact uses with the ordinary word it stands in
   for: retained adaptation → learning, system-definition artifact →
   instruction file. An abstraction is a term the artifact coins, the KB
   vocabulary registers, or a linked note defines. Do not apply anything yet.
2. For each term, read every occurrence and ask whether the ordinary word
   would change what that sentence claims: by also covering what the
   abstraction excludes (learning that is not retained), or by losing what it
   includes (retention no one would call learning). Note each occurrence where
   it would.
3. Propose one decision per term:
   - **No occurrence needs the distinction.** Replace with the ordinary word
     throughout. A definition the body then no longer uses is reported as a
     candidate cut, not removed; search inbound uses first, since a term
     other artifacts cite this one for stays.
   - **At least one occurrence needs it.** Keep the term throughout, including
     the sentences where the ordinary word would have done. Name the
     occurrence that decided it. If the term is not defined or linked at its
     first use, report that for the opening-and-title pass.
   - **The artifact names two things.** The abstraction is needed for one
     thing and the ordinary word names a different one (learning in the
     training sense next to retained adaptation of a process). Both stay;
     each occurrence gets the word for the thing it names. Report the pair
     and the sentence that separates them.
4. Return the proposals. The operator strikes items. Apply only what
   survives.

Report: per term, the decision, the deciding occurrence when the term is
kept, and the count of replacements.

Preserve: quoted text; a term inside a link title, a filename, or a
frontmatter field; the claim of every sentence. A replacement that would
widen or narrow a claim is a sign the term is needed, not a reason to
reword the claim.
