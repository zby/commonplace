# Report high-impact simplification opportunities

Read the complete artifact and report only opportunities likely to materially improve a reader's understanding of its central claim, conceptual structure, or argumentative flow. Do not edit the artifact.

An opportunity qualifies when the benefit would be large but the change requires broader judgment—for example, selecting among plausible readings of an ambiguous passage, surfacing a buried thesis, clarifying the role of a load-bearing paragraph, removing substantial duplication, or reorganizing an explanation whose current structure hides the argument. Exclude local wording improvements, cosmetic changes, minor compression, and alternatives that are merely different.

Treat **conceptual-role conflation** as a high-impact opportunity when a passage blurs a term, the concept it names, a source account, the current artifact's explication or application, or the system or case to which the concept applies. Use the surrounding text and provenance to identify the separate relations a revision must express—for example, what is established, what is inherited, and what the current artifact frames or extends. Report any relation the artifact does not settle as a choice for the author rather than silently resolving it.

Prefer one to three findings and return none when nothing clears this threshold. For each finding, give:

- The location and the smallest useful quotation;
- The important reader problem;
- The direction a revision could take; and
- The meanings, implications, or structural choices the author must resolve or preserve.

Do not present a rewrite as settled. A short candidate formulation may illustrate the opportunity, but label it as one possible resolution. Keep fact-checking, claim evaluation, and general completeness review outside this pass unless they are necessary to explain why the simplification is unsafe to perform automatically.
