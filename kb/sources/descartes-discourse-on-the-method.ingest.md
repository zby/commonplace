# Ingest: Discourse on the Method (Descartes, 1637)

## Classification

A first-person method treatise that argues a position rather than reporting a study. Its support is autobiography plus analogy — the single architect's town against the accreted one, the wise legislator's laws against laws forced on a people by experience — and its one appeal to results is Descartes' report that observing the precepts gave him "such ease" in algebra and geometry over two or three months. Nothing is measured, no alternative method is tried, and the argument's object is how reason should be conducted, which places it in the conceptual-essay lens rather than the practitioner-report one.

Author: René Descartes, whose own work created the analytic geometry the argument leans on, so the geometers' long chains are a first-hand rather than a borrowed example. The observation is John Veitch's English translation as published by Project Gutenberg; wording is a translator's mediation from 1637 French, so specific phrasings should be read as argument, not as terminology.

## Summary

Part II gives four precepts that Descartes offers as a sufficient replacement for the whole apparatus of scholastic logic, provided the operator holds "the firm and unwavering resolution" never once to break them: accept nothing not clearly and distinctly known; divide each difficulty into as many parts as possible and as necessary for its adequate solution; conduct thoughts in order from the simplest and easiest to know up to the more complex, assigning an order even to objects that have none in their own nature; and make enumerations so complete and reviews so general that nothing is omitted. He then states the motivation directly: the long chains of simple and easy reasonings by which geometers reach their hardest demonstrations led him to imagine that all knowable things are connected the same way, so nothing is beyond reach — on two conditions, that one never accepts the false for the true, and always preserves the order needed to deduce one truth from another. Read for decomposition work, this is the earliest crisp statement that a hard problem becomes tractable by division plus ordering plus per-step evidence, and it is unusually explicit about what it assumes: the two conditions are stipulated as acts of will, and the precepts supply no criterion for clarity, no rule for where to cut, no test for which object is simplest, and no check behind the demand for completeness. Skip Parts I and III–VI unless the provisional moral code, the cogito, or Descartes' physics is the question.

## Connections Found

The source's primary role here is as the missing antecedent for a cluster of decomposition claims the KB argues on its own terms. Precept 2's second clause — as many parts as possible *and* as necessary for adequate solution — is a pre-modern statement of the stopping condition in [short composable notes maximize combinatorial discovery](../notes/short-composable-notes-maximize-combinatorial-discovery.md), which holds that atomicity follows from a purpose rather than from fragmentation for its own sake. Precept 3's clause about assigning order to objects that do not stand in a relation of antecedence and sequence is the direct ancestor of [title as claim enables traversal as reasoning](../notes/title-as-claim-enables-traversal-as-reasoning.md): authored order, not intrinsic subject-matter order, is what makes a chain read as an argument. [Soft-bound traditions as sources for context engineering strategies](../notes/soft-bound-traditions-as-sources-for-context-engineering-strategies.md) surveys twelve traditions that derive working rules from a processor bound and does not include the Cartesian method, which is a thirteenth case and one whose bound is stated openly.

Its second role is as the rival position. The long-chains passage asserts that division plus preserved order plus evident steps reaches everything knowable, and stipulates step-correctness as a resolution. [Error correction works with above-chance oracles and decorrelated checks](../notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md) replaces that free per-step oracle with a purchasable probabilistic one, and [topology, isolation, and verification form a causal chain for reliable agent scaling](../notes/topology-isolation-and-verification-form-a-causal-chain-for-reliable.md) argues verification is a separate requirement rather than a consequence of good topology — the Discourse is the clean statement of what both are arguing against. [MAKER](./meyerson-maker-million-step-llm-zero-errors.ingest.md) is the sharpest source-level pair: it runs precepts 2 and 3 to their limit with single-step microagents and harness-imposed order, and shows what the program costs once the checker has to be bought. On acceptance criteria the opposition is cleaner still: precept 1 makes indubitability the test and builds upward from a foundation, where [Popper's problem–theory–criticism cycle](./popper-a-realist-view-of-logic-physics-and-history-1966.ingest.md) denies there is a foundation and keeps acceptance tentative. The KB's Popperian commitments are argued without the foundationalist rival ever being stated; this source states it.

Finally, the Discourse is a compact worked case for [a methodology governs its own extension only as far as it settles the meta-decisions its recommendations raise](../notes/a-methodology-governs-its-own-extension-only-as-far-as-it-settles.md) and for [actionable methodology](../notes/definitions/actionable-methodology.md). The four precepts settle none of their own meta-decisions and lean on the operator's resolution to fill every gap, and Descartes says so — he declines to prescribe ("not to teach the method which each ought to follow ... but solely to describe the way in which I have endeavored to conduct my own"), warns that for most readers the undertaking may be "more than they can safely venture to imitate", and sorts readers into classes for whom the method is and is not usable. That is a methodologist scoping his methodology by operator, which the definition currently illustrates only with a constructed example. Weaker leads were dropped: the resemblance to the KB's `complete:` and `covered_by:` marks is analogical rather than material, and the architect-and-city passage argues from aesthetics and politics, not from any cost that transfers.

## Extractable Value

1. **Precept 2 carries its own stopping condition** -- "as many parts as possible, and as might be necessary for its adequate solution" binds division twice, so the canonical division rule was never maximal fragmentation. This is a citable antecedent for the KB's position that atomicity is downstream of a purpose, and it corrects the common reading of Descartes as a maximal-decomposition source. [quick-win]
2. **Precept 3 licenses imposed order** -- order may be "assigned in thought" to objects that have no natural antecedence. That sentence is the strongest external ancestor the KB holds for authored link order doing the reasoning work, and it separates *ordering* as an operation from *discovering* an order that was already there. [quick-win]
3. **The long-chains passage separates two claims the KB keeps seeing conflated** -- reach follows from decomposition plus order *only given* that the false is never accepted for the true. Descartes makes the per-step condition explicit and then discharges it by resolve, which is exactly where a decomposition claim borrows its warrant from an unpriced checking claim. The general form — a decomposition's warrant and its steps' checkability are independent — is not held anywhere in the KB. [deep-dive]
4. **The foundationalist position the KB argues against, in its own words** -- precept 1's indubitability criterion and the sweep-it-all-away rebuild are the rival to the KB's Popperian error-elimination stance, which is currently argued with no statement of what it rejects. Holding the rival makes the KB's own commitment falsifiable rather than assumed. [deep-dive]
5. **A methodologist scoping his methodology by operator** -- the Prefatory material and the two-classes passage are a historical instance of the operator-relative restriction `actionable-methodology` makes technical, replacing a constructed example with a real one. [quick-win]
6. **The compounding claim** -- "each truth discovered was a rule available in the discovery of subsequent ones" is an early statement of why reusable intermediates are worth retaining rather than recomputing, useful as a named antecedent wherever the KB argues for saving intermediate results. [just-a-reference]

## Limitations (our opinion)

This is a conceptual essay, and its weaknesses are the ones that lens predicts. The central inference is an untested analogy: geometers chain simple steps to hard conclusions, therefore *all* knowable things are "mutually connected in the same way." Nothing in the text tests whether domains without geometry's truth-preserving step relation behave that way, and the KB's own material suggests they do not — [ConvexBench](./convexbench-can-llms-recognize-convex-functions.ingest.md) finds compositional performance collapsing with depth rather than with step difficulty. The supporting evidence is also selected: Descartes generalizes to all sciences from success in algebra and geometry over two or three months, the two fields where his conditions most nearly hold, which is the local-use warrant that [use tests a decomposition locally](../notes/use-tests-a-decomposition-locally-rationale-makes-transfer-testable.md) argues is insufficient for transfer. Precept 1's "clearly and distinctly" names a criterion without giving one, so it cannot be applied by anyone who does not already agree about a case; the same holds for precept 4, which asks for assurance that nothing was omitted and supplies only the resolve to be thorough where a completeness check would go. The deepest gap for our purposes is that precepts 2 and 3 treat the division and the ordering as settled once found: the method has no move for a bad division, which is what [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../notes/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md) identifies as the failure that working harder inside the parts cannot repair. Do not read the precepts as an operational procedure — read them as a statement of what a decomposition-and-ordering method assumes. Two boundaries on this analysis: the reading is concentrated on Part II, so the provisional moral code, the cogito, and the method applied to physics are unprospected rather than judged irrelevant; and every quotation is Veitch's English, so nothing here should turn on a single translated word.
