# Ingest: From Organizational Routines to Dynamic Capabilities (Zollo & Winter, 1999)

## Classification

This is a theoretical scientific paper in working-paper form. It proposes a definition, a model of three learning mechanisms, and three testable hypotheses (H1–H3), and it reviews cited empirical studies as "preliminary evidence"; it reports no new data or method of its own. The capture is INSEAD working paper 99/48 (PDF dated 1999-09-23), with draft artifacts such as reviewer markers `[SW1]` and `[SW2]` left in the text. It is the precursor of Zollo and Winter (2002), "Deliberate Learning and the Evolution of Dynamic Capabilities", *Organization Science* 13(3):339–351, doi:10.1287/orsc.13.3.339.2780. This ingest covers the 1999 working paper only. It does not establish what the 2002 article says, so no `doi` is recorded here. Author: Maurizio Zollo (INSEAD) and Sidney G. Winter (Wharton), co-author of Nelson and Winter's evolutionary theory of the firm, whose routine concept the paper extends. That is a strong signal of fluency in the field, not independent confirmation of the hypotheses. Zollo also authors two of the cited supporting studies.

## Summary

The paper asks how organizations acquire dynamic capabilities, which it defines as "a learned pattern of collective activity through which the organization systematically generates and modifies its operational routines in pursuit of improved effectiveness". It places this inside a knowledge evolution cycle adapted from variation–selection–retention: ideas are generated, screened by internal selection, replicated across contexts, and retained as routines, whose application produces new information that can start the next cycle. Three mechanisms drive it. Experience accumulation is semi-automatic, tacit learning from repeated execution. Knowledge articulation makes implicit knowledge explicit through collective discussion, debriefs and performance evaluations. Knowledge codification writes that understanding into manuals, memos, post-mortems, spreadsheets and decision-support tools. Articulation and codification "constitute the internal selection mechanism". The three are ordered by rising learning investment. The paper's distinctive claim is that codification's benefit lies largely in the act of writing, which forces a group to make action–outcome links explicit, and not only in later use of the document. Hence H1–H3: the explicit mechanisms gain relative effectiveness as a task becomes less frequent, more heterogeneous and more causally ambiguous. Costs include time, attention and organizational inertia. Cited studies of bank acquisitions, hospital re-engineering, joint ventures and quality projects are offered as consistent with the hypotheses, including an inverted-U effect for codification.

## Contribution assessment (our opinion)

The problem matters: dynamic-capability theory had named an ability to change routines without explaining where it comes from, and the paper's definition removes the "capability as ability" near-tautology while locating capability in learning routines that act on operational routines. The main addition is a falsifiable contingency model: which learning mechanism pays depends on task frequency, heterogeneity and causal ambiguity, and codification is credited with a cognitive, selection-side effect, not only diffusion. That is a sharper, testable conjecture than the general behavioral-versus-cognitive debate it draws on, and the inverted-U observation keeps it from being a blanket endorsement of formalization. The support is thin and indirect. It is argument plus a handful of cited correlational studies, partly by the first author, with no measurement design, no attempt to separate the effect of writing from selection effects (firms that codify may differ in other ways), and no test of the single learning-investment continuum. The figures did not survive the text capture. From background knowledge, the 2002 article became a widely cited statement of this position, but that is not evidence for its hypotheses, and originality relative to Boisot, Nonaka or Kogut and Zander cannot be judged from this capture beyond the paper's own positioning against them. Tentatively: an influential conjecture with a clear causal story, at an early evidential stage.

## Connections Found

For the occasion, the source serves as a background anchor from organizational learning: an established, citable account that separates semi-automatic experience accumulation from deliberate, effortful articulation and writing. Throughout this section, "codification" is the paper's sense: writing understanding into manuals, post-mortems and tools. It is not the KB's [Codification](../theory/definitions/codification.md), which moves a rule into symbolic form so that a consumer is held to a reference. Most of the paper's written tools stay in natural language and would count as natural-language constraining under that definition. The paper's claimed benefit comes from the act of writing, whoever later consumes the result.

Its closest theory link is as evidence for the premise conceded in [An adversarial human-agent loop can reconstruct the writing-is-thinking filter](../theory/adversarial-loop-can-reconstruct-the-writing-is-thinking-filter.md). Writing a manual, the paper argues, forces a group to expose logical steps, surface hidden assumptions and make causal links explicit, which turns writing into an internal selection mechanism. This is an organizational-scale statement of that premise with some outcome studies behind it. It bears only on the premise, not on the note's claim that a loop can relocate the filter. The paper is also evidence for [Raw accumulation does not create usable memory](../theory/raw-accumulation-does-not-create-usable-memory.md): it holds that information from repeated execution is "typically unusable" without evaluation and distillation, and its cited studies found codification effort, not accumulated experience, associated with performance on infrequent tasks. Its heterogeneity argument (H2) is evidence for [Abstract an experience into a lesson only when you can state where the lesson stops](../theory/abstract-an-experience-only-when-you-can-state-the-boundary.md): lessons transferred across dissimilar occurrences produce inappropriate generalizations unless an explicit effort identifies the dimensions of difference.

It compares with [Codify-versus-LLM decision heuristics](../theory/codify-versus-llm-decision-heuristics.md) and with [Minton's EBL utility analysis](./minton-utility-of-ebl-aaai88.ingest.md) on task frequency as a retention criterion. Minton's admitted rules earn their place through reuse frequency, and the heuristics note treats recurrence as a cost signal. Zollo and Winter predict that writing a procedure gains relative value as frequency falls, because they also count the understanding the writing produces. It compares with [Progressive constraining commits only after patterns stabilize](../theory/progressive-constraining-commits-only-after-patterns-stabilize.md), which waits for stable repetition before committing to code. The two recommendations look opposed, but they concern different senses and purposes of codification: the note's is execution fidelity in symbolic form, and the paper's is selection through written prose. It compares with [Explicit retention provides direct targets for selective revision](../theory/only-explicit-retention-is-durable-writable-and-addressable.md) through the HP-versus-Corning contrast: a written alliance manual against apprenticeship, with tacit retention more exposed to staff turnover. The paper reports that both firms succeeded and calls this possible equifinality. A dynamic capability also compares with [factory learning](../theory/factory-learning-is-experience-responsive-retention-that-improves.md): both are learning that changes the machinery producing later work, and both exclude one-off adaptation.

Among sources, it compares with [Argyris](./argyris-organizational-learning-and-mis-1977.ingest.md), whose constructive confrontation underlies the paper's articulation mechanism and whose double-loop distinction it uses against Teece et al.; Argyris treats formal control systems as self-sealing, while Zollo and Winter treat codification inertia as a cost to weigh. It compares with [Karlsson's writing loop](./how-to-think-in-writing.ingest.md) on writing as exposure of premises, adding the group setting and the cost side.

## Learning Claims (our opinion)

**Mechanism on its own terms.** Organizational knowledge evolves through variation, internal selection, replication and retention. Learning about a task proceeds through three co-evolving mechanisms with rising cost. Experience accumulation adjusts behavior "semi-automatic[ally]" in response to unsatisfactory performance and needs stable personnel, performance monitoring and incentives. Articulation improves members' grasp of action–performance links by sharing and challenging experiences. Codification forces "explicit conclusions about the action implications of experience" while producing a written tool. A dynamic capability is the learned, systematic pattern of these activities that generates and modifies operational routines. Organizations that adapt "in a creative but disjointed way to a succession of crises" do not have one.

**On the occasion's four questions.**

- *Deliberate learning against experience accumulation.* The working paper does not define "deliberate learning" as a term; the phrase is the 2002 title. What it offers is a graded contrast. Experience accumulation is "semi-automatic", tacit, and cheap. Articulation and codification are "increasingly deliberate investments" of time, attention and resources aimed at understanding action–performance links. Deliberateness here means directed cognitive effort, not intention to learn: codification yields its learning effect "even though the learning process might not be the deliberate goal". Whether the 2002 article tightens this into a definition cannot be checked from this capture.
- *Roles of articulation and codification.* They are the internal selection stage of the cycle. They screen proposed variations by reducing a problem's dimensions and clarifying causal links, at the price of reduced variety and added inertia. Codification also serves replication, which the paper calls the familiar role and does not analyze.
- *By what judgment a changed routine is kept.* The paper gives no explicit acceptance criterion. Internal selection evaluates a proposal's "potential for enhancing the effectiveness of existing routines" against articulated and codified understanding of prior experience; replication then generates performance evidence through which the selection-stage hypotheses "can be now potentially tested"; the external environment acts as the final selector through performance feedback. The yardstick throughout is "improved effectiveness", left undefined. Who decides, and by what procedure, is not specified.
- *Whether the criteria are revisable.* The paper states that in fast, unpredictable environments "learning routines themselves will need to be updated repeatedly", so the second-order machinery is revisable. It does not discuss revising the effectiveness or performance criteria by which changes are judged; that question is open in the source, not answered negatively. Its closing question, "To what extent is intentionality necessary to produce adaptive adjustments in existing routines?", is likewise left open.

**Theory-builder conditions.** Judged against the [theory builder](../theory/definitions/theory-builder.md), with the organization as the declared system:

- *Localized content:* met for codification, whose manuals, post-mortems and guidelines are stated texts. Articulation is stated but largely oral and unretained. Experience accumulation fails the condition: its content sits in individual dispositions.
- *Consumption:* plausibly met, since codified tools are "intended to simply provide guidelines as to the execution of future tasks". The paper itself doubts adherence ("if people actually follow the codified procedures!"), so consumption is asserted, not shown.
- *Criticism:* partly met. Articulation involves "constructive confrontations" that challenge viewpoints, and writing exposes hidden assumptions; both aim at stated content. The paper's model of selection, however, is outcome-driven (performance feedback, environmental selection), and it does not describe criticism that can blame a test or auxiliary assumption.
- *Iteration:* claimed at the model level. The cycle is recursive and replication evidence "can prime the initiation of a new knowledge cycle". No case shows a criticism's result carried into a next round.
- *Persistence:* organizational and across task occurrences, with codified knowledge more robust to turnover than tacit knowledge in the paper's account.

The paper's codification-plus-articulation system is thus close to a human-staffed theory builder in outline, with criticism and iteration the weakest-evidenced conditions. Our reading, not the paper's claim.

**Learning claim and evidence.** The capacity claim, that explicit mechanisms improve later performance more for rare, heterogeneous, ambiguous tasks, rests on cited correlational studies. These show associations between codification effort and outcomes, not that writing caused the improvement. The fixed decomposition matters: the three mechanisms, the three task features and the single investment ordering are chosen before the evidence is reviewed, and the cited studies vary codification or experience measures within that frame. They do not test alternative partitions of learning activity, for example separating stated criticism from the writing itself. In particular, the paper cannot distinguish whether the benefit comes from writing or from the confrontation and analysis that writing occasions.

**What it adds or puts in question.** It supports Commonplace's preference for explicit, written retention and its account of writing as a filter, from an independent field. It adds a contingency the KB lacks: the value of writing a procedure has a learning component whose relative weight rises as task frequency falls, which retention policies that price only reuse would miss. It puts in question any reading of [progressive constraining](../theory/progressive-constraining-commits-only-after-patterns-stabilize.md) as a general rule for when to write things down, though that note concerns symbolic commitment and is not directly contradicted. Its account of what decides whether a change is kept is thinner than the KB's: effectiveness is the standard, and the standard itself is not treated as revisable.

## Extractable Value

1. **A graded definition of deliberateness for the naming workshop** -- the paper separates learning modes by directed cognitive investment (semi-automatic accumulation, then articulation, then written codification), and decouples the learning effect from the intention to learn. A workshop that names a "deliberative" method can cite this as the established neighbor and state where it differs: the paper does not define "deliberate learning", has no explicit keep-criterion beyond "improved effectiveness", and does not treat that criterion as revisable. [quick-win]
2. **The term difference for "codification"** -- the paper's knowledge codification (prose manuals, post-mortems, tools, valued for the act of writing) is mostly natural-language constraining in KB terms, not [Codification](../theory/definitions/codification.md). Any KB text citing this literature must scope the word, especially beside progressive constraining or codify-versus-LLM. [quick-win]
3. **Opposite frequency dependence of two retention values** -- combined with Minton and the codify-versus-LLM repetition heuristic, the paper implies that reuse savings grow with frequency while the understanding gained from writing a procedure matters relatively more for rare, heterogeneous or causally ambiguous tasks. A retention rule that prices only reuse would under-retain for rare tasks. This is a candidate `kb/theory/` note; the evidence is a conjecture plus correlational studies. [deep-dive]
4. **Organizational evidence for the writing-is-thinking premise** -- the draft-paper and manual-writing argument, with outcome studies, gives a scholarly source for a premise the KB currently grounds in a polemic. Its support is correlational, so it can qualify the premise but not test the loop claim. [quick-win]
5. **Learning routines as the object of a capability** -- the operational-routine versus learning-routine distinction, with learning routines themselves needing updates under fast change, is a usable vocabulary for second-order method change in an organization. The paper does not extend the regress to the criteria. [just-a-reference]

## Limitations (our opinion)

The empirical support is cited, preliminary and correlational. Firms that codify integration processes may differ in competence, scale or management attention, and none of the cited designs reported here separate writing effort from those differences. Two of the supporting studies are by the first author. The hypotheses are stated as relative effectiveness, but "effectiveness in developing dynamic capabilities" is not operationalized, so H1–H3 are hard to falsify until a measure is fixed. The causal story that writing forces understanding is supported here by introspection about drafting papers and an illustrative firm contrast; a simpler account, that more attentive organizations both codify and perform better, is not excluded. The HP/Corning case cuts against strong conclusions, since both approaches succeeded. Figures 1–4 are garbled in the text capture, so their exact content (including the frequency-effectiveness curves in Fig. 4) is only partly recoverable. This is a 1999 working paper with draft markers. Claims about the 2002 *Organization Science* article, including its definition of "deliberate learning", cannot rest on this ingest.
