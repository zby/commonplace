# Ingest: Operationalism

## Classification

This is a reference essay in philosophy of science. It is secondary scholarship: it surveys P. W. Bridgman's operational analysis, the mid-century critiques of "operationalism", and the doctrine's later uses in psychology. It quotes Bridgman, Hempel, Margenau, Feest and others rather than presenting new data. Its final section is the author's own reconstruction, not a report of consensus. Author: Hasok Chang, historian and philosopher of science and author of *Inventing Temperature*; he wrote the 2009 Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry and its 2019 substantive revision. Section 3 draws on his own earlier work (the Wedgwood case is cited to Chang 2004), so its readings are partisan.

## Summary

Bridgman (1927) held that a concept means the set of operations that measure it. Different operations in different domains therefore yield different concepts, and a concept carried past the domain of its operations may lose meaning. Bridgman called these domain edges "joints". The entry gives four standard critiques. Operational definitions do not exhaust meaning: if the operation is the whole meaning, asking whether a measurement is valid becomes a tautology. Not every useful concept needs an operational definition. "Operation" was never defined without becoming either trivial or too restrictive (Margenau). Bridgman also treated operations as private. Psychology adopted a provisional version: Feest describes operational definitions as partial and temporary statements of which empirical indicators scientists take to indicate a concept. Chang then reconstructs four uses that survive the critiques. Operations serve as units for analysing scientific practice, including the agent's purposes, assumptions and tacit skills. Operationalism regulates extending a concept to new domains through credibly linked operations; Wedgwood's pyrometry is the example. It increases empirical content in Popper's sense by keeping parameters independently measurable. Finally, operational analysis exposes complexity, because it "can always be pushed to the point where sharpness disappears". Read the entry for the discipline's own meaning of operationalization and the known ways it fails. It does not discuss automation or agency.

## Connections Found

For the occasion, the source serves as the discipline-meaning anchor for "operationalization". It supplies the historical sense and its failure modes, against which a task- and executor-scoped use can be checked. In the current KB it plays three roles.

It is a counterpoint to [Constraining](../theory/definitions/constraining.md). That definition counts "definition notes that replace vague terms with operational meanings" as constraining. The critiques in the entry name what this narrowing costs: an operational definition constrains a concept's use without exhausting its meaning, and a definition fixed to one operation leaves no ground for asking whether the operation is valid.

It compares with [A proximate target is checked for achievement, not for warrant](../theory/a-proximate-target-is-checked-for-achievement-not-for-warrant.md) on whether a measurable criterion is the concept or a stand-in for it. Bridgman's reductive doctrine collapses the two. The construct-validity point (Gillies; Cronbach and Meehl) and Bunge's renaming of operational definitions as "indicator hypotheses" keep them apart. This is the same unevaluated linking claim the note places between objective and proximate target.

It compares with [Generality bought to avoid counterexamples is paid for in precision](../theory/generality-bought-to-avoid-counterexamples-is-paid-for-in.md) and with [Popper's *Logic of Scientific Discovery*](./popper-logic-of-scientific-discovery.ingest.md) on empirical content. Chang reads operationalism as a strategy that raises content by keeping parameters independently measurable. Bridgman's electron-diameter passage shows the reverse: once length is defined through the equations it should test, the concepts fuse, and the number of testable relations falls. Commonplace inherits Popper's demarcation by falsifiability, not Bridgman's criterion of meaning. Only Chang's content-increasing reading of operationalism is compatible with that stance.

Two weaker comparisons are kept. [Natural-language meaning is coordination among learned approximations](../theory/natural-language-meaning-is-coordination-among-learned-approximations.md) gives an independent, also untested, account in which several independent checks stabilize meaning. Bridgman's claim that a construct reachable by several distinct operations is "real" (§3.3) and convergent operationism in psychology are analogues from other disciplines; they are not corroboration. [Abstract an experience only when you can state the boundary](../theory/abstract-an-experience-only-when-you-can-state-the-boundary.md) shares Bridgman's worry about concepts extended "unthinkingly". Chang's §3.2 adds a positive mechanism the note lacks: extension through operations that overlap with the original domain.

## Extractable Value

1. **Operationalization is partial and provisional, not a definition of the knowledge.** In the discipline's mature sense (Feest, convergent operationism), operationalizing a concept specifies, for a purpose, which operations and indicators count as applying it. It is not the concept's whole meaning. Carried over to the workshop, operationalizing knowledge K for task T and executor E produces something narrower than K, and the residue is what makes the result assessable. This occasion-bearing distinction stops "operationalized" from being read as "K reduced to procedure". [quick-win]
2. **Validity requires meaning beyond the operation.** Gillies' argument (§2.1) is that if the operation exhausts the concept, the operation is valid by definition. An operationalization of K for (T, E) can therefore be checked only against parts of K it does not encode. This gives the workshop a test: an account of operationalization that leaves nothing to check the operationalized form against cannot say when operationalization went wrong. It bears directly on [proximate targets](../theory/a-proximate-target-is-checked-for-achievement-not-for-warrant.md). [quick-win]
3. **"Operation" relative to an executor runs into Margenau's dilemma.** Bridgman's operations covered instrumental, mental/verbal and "paper-and-pencil" kinds. Margenau showed that the doctrine is trivial if "operation" includes all of them and too restrictive if it means laboratory procedures only. The executor-scoped analogue: if "executable by E" includes open-ended judgment, operationalization rules nothing out; if it means codified steps only, it reduces to [codification](../theory/definitions/codification.md). The workshop needs a typed range of operations between the two, as Chang's §3.1 asks for in science. Context-bound: the source argues this about scientific concepts, not agents. [deep-dive]
4. **Extension to a new task needs overlapping operations, not a wider definition.** Chang's §3.2 holds that a concept is extended to an adjacent domain by building a new "secure net of uses" credibly linked to the old one. The Wedgwood case used causal operations (heating, cooling, melting) that span both domains, plus an intermediate standard where the two scales overlapped. This supports keeping "generalize to T′" separate from "operationalize for T", and it suggests what a transfer step must supply. [experiment]
5. **Treat an operational definition as an indicator hypothesis.** Bunge's renaming (§3.3) makes the link between concept and indicator a conjecture open to test. This fits Commonplace's [epistemic stance](../reference/epistemic-stance.md) better than reductive operationalism does, and it gives a Popperian reading of "operationalized for (T, E)": a fallible claim that these operations serve K for this task. [quick-win]
6. **Execution, function-level automation and decision autonomy are not addressed.** The source has no account of automation or delegated decision. The closest material is Bridgman's insistence that checking and judging "can be done for me by no one else" (§2.4) and Chang's point that a full account of an operation includes the agent's purposes, assumptions and tacit skills (§3.1). Both bear on whether an operation keeps its meaning when a different executor performs it. Neither is an argument about automation, and the privacy thesis is the part of Bridgman that almost everyone rejected. [just-a-reference]
7. **Disambiguate "operational".** The KB already uses "operational" for the `operationalized-from` lineage relation, for the workshop's provisional "operationalization", and for the constraining definition's "operational meanings". The source adds Bridgman's operationalism and psychology's construct-to-variable operationalization. If the workshop adopts the term, the [vocabulary collision ledger](../reference/vocabulary-collision-ledger.md) should record which sense governs new text. [quick-win]
8. **A construct earns its place by mediating between independently measurable concepts.** Bridgman (§3.3) held that a theoretical concept without direct operational meaning is worthwhile when it links two or more operationally meaningful concepts into a testable relation. This is a usable criterion for KB abstractions that connect checkable claims, but it is context-bound to empirical science. [just-a-reference]

## Limitations (our opinion)

This is secondary scholarship. Its quotations of Bridgman and the critics are selected to support a narrative that runs from first enthusiasm, through consensus rejection, to Chang's revival. Claims about what Bridgman held should be checked against *The Logic of Modern Physics* before they carry weight. Section 3 is one author's programme, in which his own temperature research is the main example. The survival of the extension and content-increasing readings is argued, not shown to be accepted in the field. The Wedgwood case is a single historical episode used as illustration. Mapping any of this onto agents is analogy. "Operation" in the source is a scientist's measurement or manipulation, and the entry gives no reason to think an LLM executor's "operations" have the same structure, the same domain joints or the same validity problem. Items 3 and 4 are therefore conjectures to test in the workshop, not established transfers. The source is also silent on the workshop's three target relations: execution, function-level automation and decision autonomy. A conclusion about them cannot rest on this entry alone.
