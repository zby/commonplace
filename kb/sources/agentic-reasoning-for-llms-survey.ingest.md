# Ingest: Agentic Reasoning for Large Language Models

## Classification

This arXiv preprint (2601.12538v2) is a survey. It offers a conceptual taxonomy, a light POMDP-style formalization, comparison tables, and application and benchmark reviews. It reports no new experiment, and the capture shows no stated literature-search or inclusion protocol; the authors say coverage runs "up to 2025".
Author: Tianxin Wei, Ting-Wei Li, Zhining Liu and 26 co-authors, mainly from UIUC with others from Meta, Amazon, Google DeepMind, UCSD and Yale; corresponding authors include Jiaxuan You, Heng Ji, Hanghang Tong and Jingrui He. Treat it as a field-map signal from an established ML/NLP group rather than as evidence for any one mechanism.

## Summary

The survey organizes LLM-agent work along two axes. The first axis has three layers: foundational single-agent reasoning (planning, tool use, search), self-evolving reasoning (feedback and memory), and collective multi-agent reasoning (roles, collaboration, multi-agent evolution and memory). The second axis contrasts in-context reasoning, which keeps weights frozen and spends inference-time interaction, with post-training reasoning, which internalizes behaviour into weights through RL or SFT. The parts most useful to this KB are in sections 2.2, 4 and 8. Section 2.2 gives a cross-episode meta-update `S_{k+1} ← U(S_k, τ_k, F_k)` over an "evolvable system state" of memories, tool libraries or code, with parameters only optional. It classifies that update as verbal (Reflexion-style reflections), procedural (Voyager-style skill libraries) or structural (AlphaEvolve-style agent code). Section 4.1 separates three feedback regimes: reflective self-critique within an episode; parametric adaptation into weights; and validator-driven retry, which resamples independently until a unit test or environment signal passes. Section 4.2 sorts memory into flat, structured, and RL-trained memory control (MemAgent, Mem1, Memory-R1, Mem-α, Memory-as-Action). Section 5.3.2 covers multi-agent memory architecture, topology, content and management. Section 8 lists open problems, including credit assignment across "tokens, tool calls, skills, and memory updates" and governance at model, agent and ecosystem level. Read it for a broad map and vocabulary. It does not test whether any listed mechanism works.

## Contribution assessment (our opinion)

The problem is important: agent research is split across planning, tool use, memory, feedback and multi-agent work, and a shared map helps place new methods and spot gaps. The survey's main addition is the pairing of its three layers with the in-context versus post-training split, plus a single update rule that puts reflection, skill libraries and code search into one frame. That framing is useful but thin. The update rule names state, trajectory and feedback, yet it does not constrain what `U` may do, so it classifies methods without predicting which will work. As far as we can tell, the in-context versus training split and the memory taxonomy closely follow existing surveys, including the [Adaptation of Agentic AI survey](./adaptation-of-agentic-ai-survey-post-training-memory-skills.ingest.md), which shares two authors. Our originality judgment rests on that comparison and our background knowledge, not on a coverage check. Several placements look loose (see Limitations), which weakens the tables as a classification authority without undermining the overall map. Its likely value is navigation and vocabulary, and its section 8 problem list names credit assignment and governance as open. It is unlikely to change how anyone designs a specific mechanism.

## Connections Found

The survey plays two roles in this KB. It is an outside ML-community reference frame for our learning-outside-weights notes. It is also a second data point for the claim that ML adaptation surveys classify updates by optimization locus and leave governance of individual updates out of their general frameworks.

Against [Treat continual learning as representational-form coevolution](../theory/treat-continual-learning-as-representational-form-coevolution.md) the survey **compares with** the note on how update targets are classified. Its verbal, procedural and structural classes, plus optional parameters, line up only partly with our natural-language, symbolic and distributed-parametric forms. Verbal evolution maps to natural language. Procedural and structural evolution are both symbolic, and the survey divides them by role (tool library versus the agent's own code), not by form. The survey's section 8.2 recognizes credit assignment across heterogeneous components as an open problem. Its list mixes trajectory units with retained updates rather than distinguishing representational forms, so it does not corroborate the note's cross-form framing.

The survey **is evidence for** [The deployed system, not the model alone, is the unit of learning](../theory/the-deployed-system-not-the-model-is-the-unit-of-learning.md): a mainstream ML survey defines self-evolution over memories, tool libraries and agent code, with weights optional. It likewise **is evidence for** [Error correction works with above-chance oracles and decorrelated checks](../theory/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md). Section 4.1.3 describes validator-driven feedback as independent resampling against unit tests or environment signals, and says it works best where validation is reliable and cheap. That is the note's amplification regime, reported across program synthesis and embodied agents, though as a survey description rather than a measurement. Section 4.2.3 offers research leads for [Feedback-trained memory management is oracle-dependent even when its operations are hand-designed](../theory/memory-management-policy-is-learnable-but-oracle-dependent.md). It lists further RL-trained memory controllers, such as Memory-R1 learning when to apply hand-designed add, update and delete operations. They are candidate cases only: the survey does not describe their reward designs well enough to count them for the note's oracle claim.

On governance, the survey **compares with** [The adaptation survey corroborates memory requirements but misses artifact governance](../theory/agent-memory-requirements/adaptation-survey-corroborates-memory-requirements.md). Its general update rule folds selection into feedback `F_k` and has no step for validating, authorizing or retiring a write to `S_k`, and section 8.6 places governance at model, agent and ecosystem level. The omission is not total, however. Section 5.3.2 reports write admission through reproducible replay (SEDM) and immutable per-entry provenance with private and shared access policies (Collaborative Memory) in multi-agent memory systems. So the governance gap is in the survey's general framework, not in the literature it catalogues.

Among sources, it **compares with** the [Adaptation of Agentic AI](./adaptation-of-agentic-ai-survey-post-training-memory-skills.ingest.md) ingest on where adaptation happens: that survey splits agent and tool adaptation into A1/A2/T1/T2, while this one treats memory as a self-evolution substrate rather than as a tool. It **compares with** [Externalization in LLM Agents](./externalization-in-llm-agents-unified-review.ingest.md) on weights versus external infrastructure. That review reads progress as moving from weights to context to harness; this survey keeps post-training as an equal mode and treats internalization into weights as consolidation. On memory taxonomy, it **compares with** the [From Human Memory to AI Memory survey](./from-human-memory-to-ai-memory-survey-llm-memory-mechanisms.ingest.md): flat, structured and policy-controlled memory here versus object, form and time there. It also **compares with** [the agent-memory landscape](../agentic-systems/comparisons/memory/agent-memory-landscape.md), which counts code-grounded memory mechanisms across 243 analyses. The landscape can therefore check which of this survey's paper-derived categories actually appear in inspected systems.

One term needs care. The survey's "flat memory" means unstructured retrieval storage (dialogue buffers, dense retrieval). It is a different concept from the role-collapse sense in [Flat memory predicts specific cross-contamination failures](../theory/flat-memory-predicts-specific-cross-contamination-failures-that-are.md), so the two should not be cited as mutual support. Reflective feedback also stands in tension with [Reasoning production is not reasoning evaluation](../theory/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md). The survey presents same-model self-critique as improving reliability without addressing whether the critic evaluates the reasoning or reconstructs an answer, so it should not be cited as support for self-critique.

## Learning Claims (our opinion)

**Mechanism on the source's terms.** Self-evolution is a loop across episodes: after episode `k`, an update operator `U` turns the trajectory and environmental feedback (rewards, execution errors) into a new evolvable state. That state is textual reflections, an executable skill or tool library, or the agent's own code, and gradient-based updates to parameters are optional. Section 4.1 adds three feedback regimes. Reflective feedback changes the trajectory within an episode. Parametric adaptation consolidates feedback into weights. Validator-driven feedback selects among independent resamples and is explicitly "non-diagnostic". Section 5.3.1 separates within-episode ("intra-test-time") from cross-task ("inter-test-time") evolution. The survey reports these as mechanisms in the literature; it presents no evidence of its own that any of them causes improvement.

**Mapping onto our concepts.** The update rule matches our framing of the [deployed system as the unit of learning](../theory/the-deployed-system-not-the-model-is-the-unit-of-learning.md): `S_k` is retained system-definition material, not only weights. The mapping to representational forms is partial, as noted above. The survey's classes mix form with the role of the changed artifact, and they have no slot for retained rules or tests acting as validators. Its validator-driven regime also cannot be mapped onto a learning loop in our sense, because nothing it learns is retained: it is selection on outputs. The survey treats the three feedback regimes as a "continuum". We read them as distinct operations that differ in what is retained and where, not as points on one scale.

**Theory-builder conditions**, judged for the self-evolving systems the survey describes (survey descriptions only, weak evidence):

- *Localized content.* Met by verbal and procedural evolution in the exemplars: Reflexion's reflections and Voyager's skills are stated units that can be pointed at. Not met by parametric adaptation. Structural evolution operates on code, which is localized.
- *Consumption.* Described for verbal evolution: reflections "condition future reasoning policies". Skills extend the action space and are used when invoked.
- *Criticism.* The survey describes criticism aimed at the current output or trajectory (generate–critique–revise), not at retained `S` content. It reports no process that asks whether a stored reflection or skill is itself wrong, apart from the multi-agent write-admission and verification systems in section 5.3.2. Validator-driven retry criticizes outputs without diagnosis. This is the weakest condition in the survey's account.
- *Iteration.* Met by construction in the cross-episode rule. Reflective feedback also iterates within an episode.
- *Persistence.* Graded: within an episode for reflective feedback, across episodes for `S_k`, and across problems for parametric adaptation.

Whether these systems learn, in the sense of improved capacity for future action, is not something the survey establishes. It relays primary-paper claims without effect sizes or comparisons we could check from the captured text.

**Fixed decomposition.** Most surveyed methods learn inside a fixed update space, as [Learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../theory/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md) describes. Memory-R1 learns when to invoke hand-designed add, update and delete operations. MemAgent learns what to keep in a constant-size memory. Each reflection method fixes what kind of `S` exists. The survey reports gains from these methods but does not separate improvement within the chosen operations from evidence that the operation set, memory format or `S` type was the right choice. Structural evolution is the one category that searches the agent's decomposition itself, and the survey gives it a single sentence. The survey therefore adds breadth to our account but does not challenge it. Its open problems overlap with ours: credit assignment across heterogeneous components (8.2), and governance framed at model, agent and ecosystem level (8.6). The latter is a limitation of its general framework, not an absence of per-write governance from the mechanisms it catalogues in section 5.3.2.

## Limitations (our opinion)

This is a survey, so what is missing is testing. It runs no experiments and states no literature-search or inclusion protocol. Its tables and claims of effectiveness therefore reflect the authors' selection and the cited papers' own reports, with no comparison under matched conditions. Several placements look inconsistent by our reading. Table 5 lists Constitutional AI and RLAIF under inference-stage reflective feedback, although both are usually described as training procedures. MemGPT and Voyager also appear there, though the survey treats them elsewhere as memory and procedural evolution. The tables are therefore weak authority for classifying any single method. The update rule `S_{k+1} ← U(S_k, τ_k, F_k)` is hard to fault because it constrains almost nothing: any retained change fits it. It organizes the field but makes no prediction a result could refute. The survey's improvement claims for self-evolution and memory control rest on primary papers whose baselines and fixed decompositions it does not examine, so it cannot show that a method's gain comes from its learning rule rather than from the operation set or memory format it fixed (see the fixed-decomposition note above). Its presentation of same-model self-critique as a reliability mechanism is in tension with [Reasoning production is not reasoning evaluation](../theory/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md). Finally, the capture is a pdftotext extraction. Tables and figures are flattened, and some rows (for example the Table 6 memory listing) cannot be read reliably as aligned records. Table-level details should be checked against the PDF before reuse.
