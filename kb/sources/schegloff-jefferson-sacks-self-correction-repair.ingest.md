# Ingest: The preference for self-correction in the organization of repair in conversation

## Classification

A theoretical-empirical journal article (Language 53(2), 1977) in conversation analysis (CA). It argues from a large corpus of transcribed natural conversation, analysed through cited fragments. It reports no counts, coding scheme or statistics; its quantitative claims ("vastly more common", "overwhelmingly") are qualitative judgments over the corpus.
Author: Emanuel A. Schegloff, Gail Jefferson and Harvey Sacks (UCLA and UC Irvine), the founders of conversation analysis. This paper is one of the field's canonical texts and the standard citation for the organization of repair.

## Summary

The paper replaces "correction" with "repair". Repair is not contingent on error: it occurs where nothing is hearably wrong, hearable errors often go unrepaired, and "nothing is, in principle, excludable" from the class of repairables (trouble sources). It separates repair *initiation* from repair *outcome*. Either can be done by "self" (the speaker of the trouble source) or by "other", giving six observed initiation-to-outcome routes, including failure. Self- and other-initiation have different positions, techniques and trajectories. Self-initiation occurs in the trouble-source turn (marked by cut-offs, sound stretches and "uh"), in its transition space, or in third turn. Other-initiation occupies one position, next turn, using a graded set of devices: "Huh?"/"What?", question words, partial repeats with or without a question word, and "Y'mean X?". The positions form a three-turn "repair-initiation opportunity space" ordered so that self-initiation opportunities come first. Others withhold initiation until the trouble-source turn completes, and often a little longer, which leaves an extra gap in which the speaker self-repairs. This withholding can override turn-taking's usual minimization of gaps, which the authors take as evidence that repair organization is independent of turn-taking. Self-initiated repair usually succeeds within the same turn. Other-initiation typically only *locates* the trouble, "even when 'other' clearly 'knows'" the correction, and so yields self-repair in the following turn. Two preferences result: for self-initiation and for self-correction. "Preference" is defined structurally, not as motivation. Other-correction is rare, modulated by uncertainty markers or "Y'mean X?" formats, placed after invitations, and treated as disagreement. Recipients whose understanding suffices to correct should instead produce the next turn the prior turn calls for. Other-correction is more common toward the not-yet-competent, as a device of socialization. The paper closes by calling repair "the self-righting mechanism" of language use in interaction and argues that an adequate theory of natural language needs an account of it.

## Contribution assessment (our opinion)

The problem matters: before this paper, linguistics treated correction as error or ignored it, and self- and other-correction were studied by different disciplines. The main contribution is a reconceptualization that has proved durable. Repair becomes a sequential organization with its own positions, devices and preferences, and the observed skew toward self-repair is explained by a case-by-case mechanism (ordered opportunities plus other-initiators that only locate) rather than left as an aggregate fact. That is a strong explanatory move: it predicts specific placements (no other-initiated interruptions, post-completion gaps) that the fragments then display. The evidence is illustrative rather than measured. The fragments show that each type exists and how it is shaped, but the distributional claims are unquantified, the corpus is not described in size or composition, and several mechanisms (the opportunity space, the preference for self-initiation, other-initiator strength) are deferred to promised later papers. The socialization claim rests explicitly on passing observation. My informed guess is that later quantitative CA work and cross-linguistic studies confirmed the core organization while revising details, but this capture cannot establish that.

## Extractable Value

1. **Repair is organized independently of error: direct support for the workshop's claim** -- The paper shows repair is not keyed to error (§2.12: repair occurs without hearable error; errors pass unrepaired; nothing is excludable from the repairables). It also shows that a repair-initiation opportunity space attends every turn whether or not anyone uses it (§4.3). Repair organization can supersede turn-taking (fn. 20), and the paper calls it the "self-righting mechanism" of language use (§7). Together these support "a structural feature of conversation with its own organization, not a failure mode" at the strength of a foundational, illustratively argued CA account. Quote retention should target §2.12-2.13, the §4.3 opportunity-space paragraph and the §7 sentence. [quick-win]
2. **The organization the occasion asked for** -- Initiation and outcome are separate, and self/other applies to each independently (§2.2). Positions: self-initiation in same turn, transition space or third turn; other-initiation in next turn, withheld past completion (§3.1, §4.2). Preferences: self-initiation over other-initiation, and self-correction over other-correction, defined structurally (fn. 4, §4.3, §5). Other-initiators locate but do not correct (§5.22), and other-correction is modulated and heard as disagreement (§6). [quick-win]
3. **The paper does not keep apart conventional meaning, speaker intention and what to do next** -- Its unit is the trouble source as participants treat it, across "problems in speaking, hearing, and understanding". It classifies trouble sources by kind (word replacement, person reference, next-speaker selection), not by meaning layer. Some fragments implicitly concern what the speaker meant to say versus said (selling/buying cigarettes, ex. 43), and "Y'mean X?" checks the recipient's understanding of the turn (§6.2). The paper never theorizes a distinction between expression meaning and speaker meaning. It does tie repair to the next action: other-initiations treat trouble as an "obstacle" to producing the next turn that the prior turn calls for, and adequate understanding is shown by producing that turn (§6.3). So it supports "repair serves continuing the sequence" more than any three-way separation of meanings. A workshop claim needing that separation must cite another source, such as the Purver-Ginzburg-Healey clarification ingest's readings. [quick-win]
4. **Producer-first repair as a design reference for agent workflows** -- Ordered opportunities, others who locate but leave the fix to the producer, and visible initiation form a coherent human baseline. Agent workflows depart from it in both directions: weak self-initiation (The Bug That Shipped) and silent repair of others' gaps (silent disambiguation). A theory note could use the baseline to argue where detection and repair belong, but it must first establish which of the paper's mechanisms (sequential position, disagreement, competence norms) operate for agents. [deep-dive]
5. **Other-correction as a socialization device** -- Other-correction appears more freely toward the not-yet-competent, as a transitional usage awaiting self-correction (§6.5). This is a possible frame for operator-to-agent correction, but it rests on passing observation. [just-a-reference]

## Limitations (our opinion)

The central claims are argued from selected fragments, not measured. The corpus is never characterized in size, setting or speaker population. "Vastly more common" and "overwhelmingly" are unquantified, and the fragments are chosen to show that each type exists and how it is shaped, not how often it occurs. The organization therefore rests on the analysts' judgment that the fragments are representative. All data are mid-1970s American English conversation, mostly among peers, so cross-linguistic and cross-setting reach (institutional talk, asymmetric expertise, text chat) is not established here. Several load-bearing pieces are deferred to later papers: the opportunity-space turn metric, the preference for self-initiation, other-initiator strength and the adjacency-pair structure of repair sequences. The socialization claim is explicitly based on "passing observation". "Preference" is a technical structural notion: reading it as speaker motivation, or transferring it to an agent setting as a normative rule, goes beyond what the paper claims. Finally, the paper's explanation of the skew is internal to conversation's sequential organization. Using it as evidence about agent detection and repair is analogy unless the operative mechanism is shown to exist there.
