### 1. Citation retention is a failed transition, not just unobserved success

The new publication initially asserted successful citation persistence, according to retained refusal evidence. Its corrected public synthesis now says that a nonempty generated-citation commit is refused because assignment belongs to the original focus resource, not the newly created resource.

Independent source checks support the distinction:

- SRC-1 `apps/dispatcher/handlers/src/handlers.rs:215-234` constructs assignment with `job.params.resource_id`.
- `apps/worker/src/semiont_worker/held_job.py:398-432` uploads, optionally links the original resource, commits citations on `new_resource_id`, and only then completes.
- `packages/sdk-python/src/semiont/claims.py:419-458` skips an empty annotation batch, attaches the original job ID to nonempty commits, and rethrows non-timeout refusal.
- `apps/archivist/src/commands.rs:56-110,346-390` checks the commit target's own assignment before recording annotations; upload instead checks the source resource.

This supports attempted citation construction/commit, not successful retention. The public synthesis's representative-workflow section explains empty-batch completion, nonempty refusal and surviving upload/source linkage. Its Limitations table explicitly disclaims the workflow's unqualified “commits any generated citation annotations and completes” sentence. The overview description also flags the assignment mismatch. The qualification is public, not an audit-only repair. I therefore do not elevate the remaining workflow wording to a hold on the whole qualified set.

The selected MCP wrapper is genuinely narrower: SRC-1 `packages/mcp-server/src/handlers.ts:304-338` gathers with `contextWindow: 2000`, passes no `cite` or format option, and returns success text/progress rather than new resource identity or bytes.
