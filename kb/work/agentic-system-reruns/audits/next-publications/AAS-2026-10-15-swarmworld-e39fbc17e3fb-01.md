## Consequential comparisons and qualifications

The historical account explicitly says BUILD does not enforce prior TEST despite its error text, and that materials can be consumed before program rejection. The new runtime preserves fabrication-before-program-parsing and lack of general rollback (`RT-RTE-world-stepping`); the synthesis explicitly warns that simulator rejection provides no general whole-tick rollback. It does not claim TEST is a BUILD prerequisite.

Checked `S/src/biofoundry/simulation.py:1526–1584` and `:1275–1319`: BUILD requires a supplied recipe/specification in research mode, calls `_execute_recipe_from_pool`, and only then parses the optional program. The pool helper validates/executes the recipe and subtracts inventory/depot inputs without consulting a prior-test record. The historical mechanism is supported at the frozen source. The new account compresses it but does not reverse the admission guarantee. Explicit repetition of the misleading error text would be useful detail, not an integration blocker in this publication.

### Inheritance is configured; verified is not competent

The historical review names a fifty-agent profile with economy disabled. The new synthesis instead describes **configured** inheritance and says measured/transferable verification does not certify competence. Checked `S/configs/openai-gpt-5.6-luna-technology-ecology-50.yaml`: economy is disabled while attention, skill library and reuse are enabled. Checked `simulation.py:963–1020` and `program_library.py:212–283`: replacement can preserve verified entries ranked by measurement count and recency; ordinary views prefer verified entries but do not filter exclusively to them. This supports the published measured-exposure qualification, not successful skill inheritance in the named profile. The new account does not assert that profile exercised the route. Loss of the profile-specific example is not a consequential false generalization.

### Recovery and replay qualifications survive

Checked `policies/llm.py:245–342` and `cli.py:415–453`: any scheduled transient failure can trigger configured batch noncommitment; the driver waits before stepping, while convenience `actions()` ignores the commitment boolean and consumes queues. The synthesis carries this distinction and disclaims eventual completion.

Checked `counterfactuals.py:58–135` and `simulation.py:2549–2556`: committed research state is restored and recorded actions are stepped, while authoritative memory serialization lists record deques rather than selector statistics. The synthesis's fixed-action versus adaptive-continuation distinction preserves the historical warning. No replay was executed here.

That is not evidence of a missing mechanism: the publication retains research-state continuation, history/progress input and adaptive access, while separating those positive routes from stronger causal-property claims. No historical wording was promoted to independent truth. No actual candidate/criticism/revision trace was supplied to settle the stronger claim.
