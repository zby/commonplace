## Consequential findings

### 1. Public qualifications are adequate for the supported contribution

`P/overview.md` identifies code-grounded evidence and unobserved compliance, repair and improved capacity. Its validation section explicitly denies semantic certification. The linked synthesis explains the actual producer-to-consumer mechanisms and keeps implementation distinct from observed operation. It disclaims execution, exhaustive variant coverage, repair causation, complete restoration, enforced approval and successful self-improvement. Its assessment and Limitations table carry the three settled local limits: session-fact capacity precision, destructive deferred delivery and missing default promotion distillation in partial profile trace inventories. Integration need not rely on this report to supply those qualifications.

### 2. Remedy candidate gates are consumer-specific, not a universal safety boundary

The current synthesis correctly rejects the source comment's broader never-served candidate claim. Independent inspection of `S/internal/index/fixpair.go:929-1008` shows confirmed-first selection and fallback to eligible held candidates only when no confirmed result remains, with policy and ignored-project filters on that fallback. Scoped source searches locate the unconfirmed renderer in `S/cmd/deja/fix.go:147-169` and the narrower candidate refusal in `S/cmd/deja/hook_plan.go:342-347`. `P/memory.md` and `P/epistemic.md` preserve this distinction; the synthesis carries it publicly. This matters because a reader must not infer that every delivered remedy passed the confirmation gate or caused a repair. No new contradiction was established.

### 3. Lifecycle admission and rule approval are not code-enforced semantic acceptance

Independent source inspection of `S/cmd/deja/promote.go:115-213` confirms that promotion appends first, attempts indexing, and only later emits accepted-note overlap warnings. The historical claim that overlap prevents storing another version is not supported at this revision. The publication already says overlap is advisory, not contradiction detection or admission rejection.

The complete frozen `S/skills/deja-search/SKILL.md` prescribes two-session support, at most 15 rules, user selection before writing and selected-line distribution. These are instructions to the foreground model, not proof of actual approval or compliance. `P/workflow.md` and the synthesis distinguish extraction/distribution from prescribed interpretation and stopping. This addresses a consequential change from the historical no-rules characterization without upgrading it to enforced consent.

### 4. The known capacity defect is real but publicly bounded

`S/internal/index/sessionfacts.go:25-33,112-155` sets the ordinary four-command budget but can retain one further verification command when none has yet been retained. `P/memory.md` locally states a four-command bound. The report verifier and synthesis explicitly qualify that precision defect and withhold exact four-entry capacity comparisons. The known-exit versus merely-recorded-command distinction remains supported by `SessionCommand.Passed`. This is an acceptable recorded limit, not an unqualified capacity claim.

### 5. Alternative delivery cannot inherit the ordinary emitter's guarantee

`S/cmd/deja/hook_deferred.go:81-178` queues text in files; `takeDeferred` renames the queue and defers removal before returning assembled text. This is a destructive take, not host acknowledgment. The synthesis explicitly separates it from the ordinary full-stdout-write-before-delivery-mark account and withholds end-to-end retry/receipt and all-host immediacy guarantees. Completing every wrapper variant would exceed this audit's useful scope; no broad guarantee depends on doing so.
