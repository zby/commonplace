# Step 1: checks for deliberative learning

Tested against the [workshop draft](./draft-definition.md), not an adopted library definition. Keep the numbered cases and their assumptions stable when testing new wording. A change that flips a placement deserves an explanation. Run the checks on the named system and store; do not credit an automated loop with judgments its designers make elsewhere.

## Stipulated cases

The three conditions are **S**: a readable store carries the change; **J**: the loop itself decides what to keep for future work, without being bound to a measure it cannot question; **C**: later rounds can find that retention judgment mistaken and change what the system does. Membership requires all three. No case assumes improved capacity unless it specifies the comparison.

| # | System, store and decision | Expected result and discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A team keeps a routing rule after weighing saved searches against misrouting. Later failures can lead it to revise the rule. | Inside: S, J, C. If later work is worse, the team made a bad retention judgment; it did not cease to use the process. Human judgment counts, but is not autonomous. |
| 2 | A service appends every interaction to a searchable log. A later tool can edit the log, but no judgment selects which entries to keep. | Outside: S alone does not give J. Editable storage is not evidence for C. |
| 3 | The team in case 1 still weighs the reasons, but no later round can challenge its decision or act differently in response to an error. | Outside on C, despite S and J at admission. Contrast an irreversible deletion whose lesson can still change the next deletion: C concerns correction of the judgment, not restoration of the file. |
| 4 | A link repairer keeps a patch exactly when its fixed validator passes. Separately, the maintaining team reviews where that test suffices, changes the validator after bad repairs, and weighs ambiguous repairs itself. | Repairer alone: outside on J. Team plus repairer, for its repair rules: inside on S, J, C. A deterministic check can settle whether a link resolves; it cannot establish by itself that it resolves to the intended page. |
| 5 | A team judges whether to deploy a better model release, but the training run retains only numeric weight updates under a fixed loss. | Training run and its weights: outside on S and J. The deployment verdict is not the step that retained a weight update. To classify the team, specify its own store and loop instead. |
| 6 | A team keeps an unexplained prompt variant after weighing mixed outcomes and revisits the choice on new evidence. By stipulation it never formulates or criticizes a claim about why the prompt works. | Inside on S, J, C; outside the builder definition on content-directed criticism. Neither an explanation of the prompt nor a measured downstream gain is required for process membership. |
| 7 | An automated builder criticizes what a retained theory says but must select the highest-scoring revision. | Builder inside; deliberative learner outside. See the joint check below. |
| 8 | As in case 7, but the same loop can reject the score winner for a stated reason about future work and can later correct that judgment. | Both definitions are met. The additional capability changes J; automation by itself changes neither placement. |

**Joint check for case 7.** Hold the boundary fixed: the running program, not the team that chose its score. It stores a written theory (**localized content**); uses a prediction to choose an action (**consumption**); names the faulty premise and proposes a correction when the prediction fails (**criticism**); and carries a selected revision informed by that criticism into the next round (**iteration**). These are the four [builder checks](../../theory/definitions/theory-builder-checks.md), not four ways of reading a score. For the learning draft, the theory passes S. The program must keep the score winner even when it diagnoses why that choice will hurt later work: J fails. It can correct a prediction or replace yesterday's winner, but cannot correct the judgment that winning the test suffices for retention. That limitation is enough to exclude it regardless of whether particular retention decisions count as revisable under C. Together cases 6–8 witness overlap without containment **under this reading of the current definitions**. They neither redefine the builder nor decide which definition should lead the program.

## Applying the checks to surveyed systems

These are contrasting cases from the [historical eighteen-system survey](../../articles/which-existing-self-improving-systems-are-theory-builders.md), not a new survey or a count of today's systems. The selection deliberately distinguishes an admission gate, a benchmark used for evaluation, and human approval. Use the survey's accounts and local source records, not the mere presence of an LLM, a benchmark, or a human. These are judgments about the described protocol, not reproduced results.

| System and store | S: what persists? | J: what actually admits it? | C: can the same loop revisit it? | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Recuris](../../sources/recursive-experiential-working-memory-evolution.ingest.md), fixed Meta-Agent and its memory package | Yes: inspectable skills and rule patches. | No: component patches must pass the paired held-out gate; the gate is outside the permitted patch surface. Several tests in a fixed rule are still a fixed rule. | Skills can be revised again, but the loop cannot revise the basis on which the gate admitted them. | Outside on J. This is stronger evidence than merely seeing a benchmark result. |
| [Knowledge-Centric Self-Improvement](../../sources/knowledge-centric-self-improvement-2607.19592.ingest.md), agents, forums and distiller during curation; store under test: distilled guidance, not the automatic attempt log | Yes: scoped claims, rejected hypotheses and checks. | Supported: the distiller selects claims for later work on evidence, relevance and scope. The benchmark scores attempts; it does not dictate which claims survive. | Supported at protocol level: later agents use the bundle, challenge claims with new attempts, and distill again. The frozen transfer recipient does not run this loop. | Inside for guidance during curation. Reported transfer supports bundle-level utility on tested tasks, not that any particular claim was learned on balance. |
| [Warp skill improver](../../sources/how-warp-builds-self-improving-agents-on-claude.ingest.md), scheduled improver **and human reviewers**, store: base skill | Yes: a reviewed skill edit. | Human PR review decides admission in response to task feedback, not the scheduled proposer alone. The account supports discretionary selection but says little about how reviewers resolve competing reasons. | Later feedback can prompt another edit to the same skill. A traced reversal of an earlier retention judgment is not reported. | Consistent with membership at the human-inclusive boundary; stronger confirmation would show a reviewer weighing a reason to reject an otherwise passing edit. Not an autonomous admission loop. No downstream gain established. |

The expectation that most admissions would be bound to benchmark scores is **not borne out by these selected examples**. Only Recuris establishes that exclusion. This selection cannot estimate the proportion among eighteen. The useful test for J is to ask whether the score decides what is retained, and who, inside the named boundary, may decide otherwise.

## What the checks expose

- **Which writes count as changes?** The draft says “each change.” The same KSI protocol logs every attempt and selects distilled claims. If the named store is the entire KB and every write must qualify, KSI's placement changes. A claim about the distilled guidance should not silently become one about every write in the KB. Specify whether the quantifier covers candidate learning changes or all writes to the named store.
- **Which comparison supports “learned”?** Suppose a routing rule saves search time on later matched tasks, with no change on an independently reasonable measure of answer accuracy. This is better on one measure and tied on another, not learned under the strict test. A success after a concurrent solver upgrade without a matched baseline does not even establish that limited gain. Neither case invalidates the decision to try the rule.
- **What could change these readings?** For Recuris, show a permitted in-loop change to the admission rule. For KSI, show that a fixed score rather than distillation ultimately selects every claim, or that earlier guidance cannot be reconsidered. For Warp, trace the authority and reasons at acceptance and subsequent correction. Those observations test different conditions; a higher aggregate score would not substitute for any of them.
