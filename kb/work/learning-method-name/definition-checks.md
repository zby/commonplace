# Step 1: checks for deliberative learning

Tested against the [workshop draft](./draft-definition.md), not an adopted library definition. Revised on 2026-11-21 by Fable (claude-fable-5-2) after the operator ruled that a fixed measure is a special case of the second condition and named the two kinds single-loop and double-loop, and updated on 2026-11-21 by Neon (gemini-4-neon) for the reliance wording, graded attribution, and Gödel-machine boundary cases (cases 13–16). The stipulations of cases 1–12 are unchanged; the version before the fixed-measure ruling is in commit `3f4e48736`. [What the ruling changed](#what-the-ruling-changed) lists the placements that moved. Keep the numbered cases and their assumptions stable when testing new wording. A change that flips a placement deserves an explanation. Run the checks on the named system and store; do not credit an automated loop with judgments its designers make elsewhere.

## Stipulated cases

The two conditions are **S**: what is learned is held in the contents of a store that can be read and pointed at, the system learns by changing those contents, and its later work consumes them; **J**: a change of the named class is proposed, judged in the loop on the evidence the system has, and can be rejected; a judgment that applies one fixed criterion counts; a rule that updates the store directly from an input does not, and neither does a trigger that says when to write without judging what is written. Membership requires both. A claim names the system, the store, the class of changes and the horizon. Until 2026-11-21 there was a third condition, **C**, open to criticism. The operator ruled that it is not a condition ([proposal and replies](./positions/proposal-two-conditions.md)). Two properties are now reported in its place. **Correction from later evidence**: can evidence from later use change what the system does about an earlier change, and if so can it identify the responsible change (*targeted*) or only reverse everything since a baseline (*bundle*)? Otherwise *none*. **Displacement**: can a newer alternative replace an earlier change by scoring higher on the same test? The two are independent. A change is an edit to the store's contents, including an edit to what later work loads or selects ([note](./positions/changes-to-the-store-not-reliance.md)). For the [kind of learner](./rulings.md#settled-double-loop-ruling), ask separately: can the named system revise the criterion that governs future decisions of this class? A different verdict under the same criterion is not a different criterion. If the case does not specify that capacity, report the kind as undetermined. No case assumes improved capacity unless it specifies the comparison.

| 18 | A nightly job keeps a readable table that ranks help articles for a search box. It raises an article's rank when users click it and lowers it when clicks fall off. Nothing in the job can change the rule. | Outside on J. The rule updates the table directly from its input. No change is proposed and none can be rejected. The table is legible, so S passes. | 19 | A code-review bot keeps a readable list of rules. It adds a rule when the same class of bug has been reported five times. Inside, single-loop and with no correction, only if a further step judges the drafted rule and can reject it. An outside reader could not decide this case until the draft separated a trigger from a judgment. |
| 20 | A wiki receives every page from an external feed automatically. Editors read it while working and delete pages that prove wrong. | Depends on the named class. The imports are not made by a decision and are outside. The deletions are, and are inside for that class. |

**Joint check for case 7.** Hold the boundary fixed: the running program, not the team that chose its score. It stores a written theory (**localized content**); uses a prediction to choose an action (**consumption**); names the faulty premise and proposes a correction when the prediction fails (**criticism**); and carries a selected revision informed by that criticism into the next round (**iteration**). These are the four [builder checks](../../theory/definitions/theory-builder-checks.md), not four ways of reading a score. For the learning draft, the theory passes S, and selection by the score passes J as a fixed measure. The property of correction turns on a detail the case leaves open:

- **7a. The score is a fixed test that takes in nothing new.** The failed prediction produces a candidate, but it is not evidence in the retention step. Yesterday's winner is displaced only when a candidate scores higher, as in case 9. Inside, single-loop. Correction: none. Displacement: yes.
- **7b. The failed prediction counts in retention**, because it enters the score or removes the criticized theory from the candidates. The kept theory can then lose because of evidence against it, as in case 10. Inside, single-loop. Correction: targeted. Neon reads case 7 this way: the failed prediction triggers replacement.

Both readings are now inside, so case 7 no longer shows a theory builder that is not a deliberative learner. Case 6 still shows a deliberative learner that is not a theory builder. Neon's position is that every continuing theory builder with a text store is a deliberative learner, with two edge cases from [the builder's checks](../../theory/definitions/theory-builder-checks.md): a localized theory that is not held in text, and a theory formed and discarded within one episode. The cases neither redefine the builder nor decide which definition should lead the program.

## Case 12 — keeping a current decision: the OASIS arrival sequencer

[Rao and Georgeff (1995)](../../sources/bdi-agents-from-theory-to-practice.ingest.md) describe a sequencer that chooses a landing schedule using a cost function. A change in wind need not reopen that choice: the sequencer can keep the schedule while the next aircraft can still meet its assigned time. The feasibility check can overturn a particular commitment. The question for the definition is **what changes as a result**.

- **Name the current schedule as the store.** There is an identifiable decision to inspect (S), a test for keeping it (J), and later evidence that can defeat that decision (targeted correction). Read without a distinction between finishing the current job and improving capacity for future work, the conditions could count ordinary replanning as single-loop learning. Rejecting a schedule after the wind changes need not even mean the original choice was mistaken.
- **Name the scheduling procedures as the store.** The plans are legible, but the reported feasibility check decides whether to keep *this schedule*, not whether to retain or correct a procedure for subsequent scheduling. The paper does not show J or correction for that store in the sequencer's own loop. Developers adding plans during debugging would change the system boundary.

**Ruling of 2026-11-21: the placement depends on the declared horizon.** The draft now requires a claim to name its horizon, following the KB's definitions of a self-improving system and an operative change. The placement below is for a horizon that spans tasks, which is the one claims about Commonplace use. The workshop does not pursue the reading at the horizon of a single wave of arrivals.

**Expected placement:** the described episode demonstrates commitment management, not learning a scheduling procedure. This is not a claim that no BDI architecture learns. Evidence that would change the placement: the sequencer retains a revised feasibility rule because of what happened here, applies it to a later scheduling decision, and can correct that retention judgment in light of later outcomes. The distinction is what carries into the later decision, not whether a process restarted or how long a schedule persisted.

## Cases 13–16 — later evidence, graded attribution and the Gödel-machine line

These cases test what later evidence must be able to do, graded attribution, and the double-loop boundary developed in [what later evidence can overturn](./what-later-evidence-can-overturn.md).

| # | System, store and decision | Expected result and discriminator |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | **[Darwin Gödel Machine](../../sources/darwin-godel-machine-open-ended-evolution-self-improving-agents.ingest.md)** (archive of self-modifying agents). Every compiling agent stays in the archive; benchmark score measured once at admission weights parent selection. | Inside on S (inspectable agent code) and J (admission requires that the agent compiles and can still edit code, and the benchmark score sets how often it is selected). Correction: none. Displacement: yes. No post-admission evidence updates an agent's own score. Its sampling weight falls in two ways, and neither is evidence about the agent: higher-scoring agents enter the archive, and its own count of functioning children grows, even if none of them scores higher. The count allocates exploration: "this parent has been explored enough" is not "its descendants turned out poorly". Case 14 differs on exactly that point, so the pair separates displacement from correction. Correction from Astra, checked against the ingest. |
| 14 | **[Huxley-Gödel Machine](../../sources/huxley-godel-machine-human-level-coding-agent-development.ingest.md)** (archive of agents weighted by descendant performance). An admitted agent's selection weight drops when its descendants perform poorly. | Inside, single-loop. Correction: targeted, because descendant outcomes supply new evidence from after admission that directly lowers how often the ancestor is selected, even if no other branch improves. Shows that the evidence need not come from running the admission test again. Single-loop for archive parent selection: new descendant scores change the weights assigned to parents, not the rule for assigning weights. Edits inside an agent do not revise that archive rule. |
| 15 | **Classical [Gödel machine](../../theory/goedel-machines-are-a-proof-governed-case-of-self-modification.md)** (proof-governed self-modification). Stores readable code `p`, injects live observations via `state2theorem` against an unknown environment prior `µ ∈ M`, and rewrites any sub-policy, heuristic evaluator, or proof searcher upon proving higher expected terminal utility `u`. | Inside, **double-loop capable**. Readable code passes S; proof decides the switch (J); later observations can end a trial (C, §6.1 item 2). Crucially, §6.1 item 4 permits revision of the utility function and target theorem under the old standard. A successor that changes the ranking of future alternatives changes the criterion. No cheap policy gate or observed successful rewrite is required to establish the architectural capacity. |
| 16 | **Schmidhuber's [incremental self-improvement](../../sources/on-learning-how-to-learn-learning-strategies.ingest.md)** (probability distribution over actions). Keeps modifications only while payoff per time since the modification exceeds the prior baseline; otherwise pops the stack and restores the earlier state, with no challenger needed. | Outside on S (opaque probability distribution). Its correction is of the bundle kind. Demonstrates that evidence from after a change can reverse it without a challenger and without condemning the original trial, and shows what an addressable store adds: targeted correction in place of blind stack rollback. |

## Case 17 — EURISKO changes how it judges heuristic generalizations

**Inside, double-loop for deciding which generalizations of heuristics to keep.**
In [Lenat's H12′ episode](../../sources/eurisko-learns-new-heuristics-and-domain-concepts.ingest.md#quotes)
(*Heuretics*, pp. 89–90), replacing AND with OR made a heuristic fire its
preliminary relevance check more often without finding more occasions when
it was actually useful. Dropping H12′ on that evidence reversed the addition of one readable
heuristic (S, J, targeted correction). That alone would be single-loop behavior.

EURISKO also generated restrictions that would have kept H12′ from
surviving. It tried several and retained one against replacing AND with OR
in a heuristic's If slot. The distinction is what happens to the *next*
generalization: its form now counts against it before its own performance
record is available. The restriction was tried probabilistically, not imposed
as an unconditional veto. Lenat attributes this step to EURISKO, not to a
programmer. The episode shows a change in this working criterion, not a
rewrite of EURISKO's overall worth function or proof of improved performance.

## Applying the checks to surveyed systems

These are contrasting cases from the [historical eighteen-system survey](../../articles/which-existing-self-improving-systems-are-theory-builders.md), not a new survey or a count of today's systems. The selection deliberately distinguishes an admission gate, a benchmark used for evaluation, and human approval. Use the survey's accounts and local source records, not the mere presence of an LLM, a benchmark, or a human. These are judgments about the described protocol, not reproduced results.

The account supports discretionary selection but says little about how reviewers resolve competing reasons. | Later feedback can prompt another edit to the same skill. Kind undetermined: trace a change to the reviewers’ standard for subsequent edits, not just a rejection under the existing standard. Not an autonomous admission loop. No downstream gain established. |

The expectation that most admissions would be bound to benchmark scores is **not borne out by these selected examples**. Only Recuris is bound to a fixed gate, and after the ruling that makes it single-loop at most, not excluded. This selection cannot estimate the proportion among eighteen. Two questions now do the sorting. For membership, ask whether a readable, addressable store is changed by a decision. For the properties, ask whether later evidence can count against a change that was made, and whether a newer alternative can displace it. For the kind, ask whether the named system can change the criterion by which subsequent changes are judged.

## What the checks expose

- **Little is excluded, and the properties do the sorting.** After the ruling for two conditions, what stays outside is an opaque store, a change that nothing decided, and state that does not outlast the declared horizon. The line between displacing an earlier edit and finding evidence against it still matters. It now separates two properties. [An outside reader](./positions/step-2-outside-reading.md) agreed with it when it was still a condition.

- **Which writes count as changes?** The draft says “each change.” The same KSI protocol logs every attempt and selects distilled claims. If the named store is the entire KB and every write must qualify, KSI's placement changes. A claim about the distilled guidance should not silently become one about every write in the KB. Specify whether the quantifier covers candidate learning changes or all writes to the named store.
- **Which comparison supports “learned”?** Suppose a routing rule saves search time on later matched tasks, with no change on an independently reasonable measure of answer accuracy. This is better on one measure and tied on another, not learned under the strict test. A success after a concurrent solver upgrade without a matched baseline does not even establish that limited gain. Neither case invalidates the decision to try the rule.
- **What could change these readings?** For Recuris, show a permitted in-loop change to the admission rule. For KSI, show that a fixed score rather than distillation ultimately selects every claim, or that earlier guidance cannot be reconsidered. For Warp, trace the authority and reasons at acceptance and subsequent correction. Those observations test different conditions; a higher aggregate score would not substitute for any of them.
