# Assessment

**Purpose:** Explain how on-balance judgments guide decisions without a global measure, and where analysis helps or stops. The reader is someone deciding whether to adopt or keep a change despite mixed gains and losses.

**Decision:** Proceed with scoped passes. The pairwise argument stands. The release verdict, the partial-order condition, the investigation boundary and the closing claim about checkability need operator decisions, but each can be addressed locally without replacing the argument. Keep the title and the Commonplace heading and anchor.

**First hostile-review target:** “Would we take Python 3.11 as the default for continuing application development, given the choice between those two releases? **On balance, yes.**” In particular, the verdict invites the question the note itself raises: how common and costly are the blockers in the projects for which this would be the default?

**What reads well:** The progression from a better diagnostic to a mixed release choice to the routing threshold shows why settling one comparison does not settle the larger decision.

## Ranked changes

The second paragraph can tell the reader directly that reasons about what changed, for whom and why it matters can support a choice without ranking every version. Keep the concrete opening and the diagnostic contrast. Moving the entire checklist ahead of the cases would help a reader looking only for a decision aid, but would give a first-time reader categories before showing why the choice of comparison matters. The local recast buys orientation without that cost. Do not preview the contested investigation boundary as if it were settled.
2. **Put the library-hours example where the limits of analysis are explained** — `readability-and-flow`; cost: one paragraph move and a transition. Use it beside the value-trade-off discussion, not inside the three-question checklist. This lets the release section end on the release decision and puts the example next to the distinction it demonstrates: perfect counts still cannot choose whose access matters. Keeping it in place gives the reader an immediate contrast after Python; if uninterrupted example-to-example reading matters more than finding the point under its heading, trim it in place instead. Trimming the repeated “another month of counts” sentence pays off independently (`fix-strategy-taxonomy`, finding 3; cost: one deletion). Keep the explicit hypothetical status and both branches. A short transfer cue is useful for a KB reader; deleting it along with the repeated sentence would save little and make the application less obvious. No need to rewrite the checklist glosses to make this move work.
3. **Explain what a marked tag page lets a reader assume** — `fix-strategy-taxonomy`, finding 1; cost: one inline gloss and definition link. In the tag-coverage paragraph, make clear what the search missed and why a page marked as listing every member can let someone stop searching. The reader can then distinguish the observed detection gain from the separate cost of maintaining the mark.
4. **Resolve the two misleading referents** — `fix-strategy-taxonomy`, finding 8; cost: two independent local clarifications. The Python opening should identify the ASCII test input, not invite a reading about given names. Leave the keep/investigate/leave-open sentences untouched. Both fixes preserve the examples and either pays off alone.
5. **Add a discoverable summary of settled scope limits** — `fix-strategy-taxonomy`, finding 6; cost: a short section with some deliberate repetition. Keep the qualifications beside the claims they limit; pulling them all into a separate section would make the examples easier to misread in place. A compact `Scope` section can surface the already-settled limits (binding requirements, what contrast was tested, and no automatic component credit) without deciding the unresolved cases or duplicating every caveat.

The order above ranks reader value, not execution. The plan runs the type repair at depth 2, the terminology repair at depth 3, the flow work at depth 4, and the two referent fixes at depth 5.

## Claim questions for the operator

- **Python default and discoverability of losses — local defects on the stronger readings; support, not divergence.** [Composition friction] and [premise decomposition] give two distinct problems. “On balance, yes” can read as a provisional example judgment under stated assumptions or as an evidenced default for continuing application development generally. The four checks give reasons, not their prevalence or coverage. A costly blocker can be common even though all four checks come out exactly as written; the two readings give different advice about adopting the default. Decide whether to state the assumption at the verdict or supply evidence for the broader reading. Separately, “The compatibility cost falls on identifiable callers” can include an untested path through a transitive dependency, whose callers the pre-move checks do not identify. Qualify what the tests and dependency inventory can reveal, rather than treating testability as comprehensive. Preserve the worked release comparison either way.
- **Partial order — local defect; formal representation is support.** [Premise decomposition], [composition friction] and [critique] point to the difference between a consistent set of priorities and transitive results. If a fixed rule favors the largest salient difference, speed can favor A over B, compatibility B over C, and library support C over A; there is then no partial order representing all three preferences. “Can represent” permits the defensible conditional reading, but the given condition can also be read as sufficient. State transitivity as a condition or describe the defended pairwise comparisons without promising that they compose. A stable ranking is not needed for the pairwise thesis. [Synthesis]'s proposal to move the paragraph is a **choice**, not a fix for that defect; leave the paragraph in place pending the decision.
- **Investigation stopping point — local defect in the imperative reading; the reversal test is divergence.** Finding 7 (`semantic/completeness-boundary-cases`), [composition friction], [critique] and [premise decomposition] bear on this. “Only if” gives a necessary condition, but “If a plausible frequency would reverse the preference, investigate those tasks” reads as a sufficient reason to investigate. Suppose a plausible level of future use would reverse the choice, but no available check can narrow the range before action is due. One reading calls for more investigation; the other does not. Ask whether to qualify the instruction to distinguish a reversing answer from evidence that can usefully narrow the uncertainty. “Leave the overall judgment open” can remain an epistemic outcome; it does not pick a fallback action. Apply the test relative to the decision's priorities: if Saturday wins solely by changing whose access counts, that is a value disagreement, not a fact to measure. No pass should set new fallback policy or revise the test.
- **Commonplace's objective — scalar reading merits a local qualification; the objective and its local status are divergence.** [Critique] identifies a reading of “for the human judgment spent” as a computed ratio, versus the case-by-case weighing described in [composition friction] and the rest of the section. A reader on the first reading could look for a score instead of supplying reasons; the second is better supported by the text. Consider making that distinction explicit without changing what counts as progress or substituting “effort” for “judgment.” Setting new protected losses, measurable components or a procedure for recording antecedent priorities is a **choice** if it redefines the objective; the reports do not show that the current declared objective entails post-hoc fitting. The [role report] shows the anchor and objective serve `kb/reference/commonplace-as-an-instrument.md`, `kb/reference/commonplace-declared-frame.md`, `kb/reference/proposals/ablation-baselines-for-the-declared-objective.md`, and `kb/reference/proposals/reusable-commitment-support-across-kinds-of-knowledge-work.md`. Changing their meaning would require coordinated operator action. Leave the entire anchored section untouched by these passes.
- **Checkability of reasons — local defect; criticizability without proof of agent competence is divergence.** [Critique] and [premise decomposition] distinguish checking what changed from judging whether it was worth the cost. “Those reasons can be checked independently of who offered them” permits both public scrutiny and verification of the weighing itself. Two reviewers can agree on the library facts and disagree on whose loss takes priority without either falsifying the facts. Make the distinction locally; keep the conclusion about criticizability that [composition friction] finds sound.
- **Separate the program objective into reference — choice, no defect in the argument shown.** Finding 5 (`kb/theory/COLLECTION`) identifies a real placement cost for readers who want to cite only the transferable claim. Moving the declaration would, however, disrupt the four anchor users named above unless the operative declaration were retained at the anchor. A local application framing might preserve their use, but has little benefit for this purpose compared with keeping the explicit declaration and the worked application. Do not split or change the section in this run. Keep the complementary requirement boundary used by `kb/theory/a-better-factory-claim-compares-operative-states.md`; passing a requirement does not settle the broader choice, and the broader choice does not override a binding requirement.

**Stale qualifications:** None identified. The release caveats scope the evidence actually presented; the possible-loss qualification in the routing case and the local-objective qualification still do work.

## Findings and proposals not acted on

- **2 — Unidentified references:** Identifying Commonplace would help an unfamiliar reader, but the warning targets the very sentence under the scalar-reading question. Defer the gloss to the same local operator decision. The role of `AGENTS.md` is inferable here; no separate glossary pass is warranted.
- **4 — Marginal-value redundancy / synthesis consolidation:** Keep the explicit local-versus-universal sentence; the operator specifically requires that distinction, and the [role report] confirms that readers arrive at the anchor for the program objective. The “does not do the weighing” trim could independently shorten the formal paragraph, but that paragraph is reserved for the partial-order question. Keep the checklist glosses: the questions already have different openings, and a reader applying them benefits from short concrete reminders. No repeated-opening fix is warranted.
- **5 and 7:** Reserved as claim questions above; not pass work.
- **9 — Stock phrase:** Deleting “This distinction guides a decision” is harmless in isolation if the following sentences stay, but it is not worth touching the decision paragraph while its boundary is open. The preceding possible-versus-observed distinction is substantive and stays.
- **10 — General before specific:** Do not invert the release and analysis sections or restate the full checklist before the table. The local opening recast addresses orientation without trading away the example-led explanation. If readers primarily retrieve the note as a checklist, a checklist-first structure could be preferable; no such priority is specified here.
- **Remaining report extensions:** [Composition friction]'s empirical KB case about aggregate gain hiding a particular group's loss and investigation for later decisions, [critique]'s procedure for checking successive decisions against antecedent priorities, and [premise decomposition]'s cheap plausibility-bounding procedure and guard against oscillating decisions all ask for more coverage. The text does not promise that local choices compose into lasting progress, that analysis sets plausibility or value priorities unaided, or that this test answers a future decision. No extension is needed for the current purpose. The table's timing protocol is useful context for the local result; don't cut it merely to shorten the example.
