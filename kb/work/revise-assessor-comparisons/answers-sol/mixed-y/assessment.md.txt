The bold answer supports the second reading, but a costly broken dependency in an untested path could reverse the decision and the checks do not establish prevalence. “The compatibility cost falls on identifiable callers” likewise reads either as a description of the observed API removal or as assurance that teams can find the relevant losses before upgrading. - **Reversal test — local application defect; the rule itself is divergence and stays.** Finding 7 (`kb/reports/state/review/results/kb/theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure.md/semantic/completeness-boundary-cases.md`) and Critique identify two different actions a reader could take. “Only if” makes reversal potential necessary for investigation; “If a plausible frequency would reverse the preference, investigate those tasks” reads as making it sufficient. If future use could reverse the choice and no available investigation can usefully narrow its range, the first reading permits leaving the judgment open while the second sends the reader to investigate. Similarly, if the same known attendance facts reverse the choice only under a different priority, the library case directs readers to resolve the priority, not gather more counts. Ask for a local clarification of when inquiry can help and when to surface a value disagreement. Do not infer that an estimate must settle future use exactly; useful bounds may suffice. - **Partial order — local defect on the stronger reading; supporting representation that may be narrowed.** Friction, Critique, and Premises distinguish “a partial order can represent a suitable set of preferences” from “consistent priorities suffice to make defended pairwise preferences a partial order.” A rule favoring the largest salient difference could put A over B for speed, B over C for compatibility, and C over A for library support. On the first reading those preferences need not compose; on the second the reader could infer a transitive ranking. Ask whether to make transitivity explicit as a condition or to speak simply of pairwise preferences. Keep the limit that no stable ranking is guaranteed. Do not change or move the paragraph in a pass.

Two readers may agree on both attendance counts and who loses access, yet disagree about which outcome is worth choosing. The unrestricted “Those reasons can be checked” invites readers to treat both kinds of reason as independently verifiable; the closing sentence supports the narrower reading that the judgment is open to criticism. Ask to separate those senses. Keep the closing paragraph out of the passes.
