**Proposed purpose:** Help designers of deployed adaptive systems distinguish stored content and externally selected maintenance from durable, evidence-responsive change, and identify what their update path needs to govern.

**Decision: Stop for a claim rewrite.** The note makes a useful distinction between persistence and governing what affects behaviour. But its test gives different answers for the same deployed learner. No claim-preserving pass can decide that test. No write brief or earlier decisions were supplied.

**First hostile-review target:** “Continual learning, as the field uses the term and as this note uses it, asks for more: the system does the selecting itself.” The field attribution is unsupported, and the critique's online-gradient example tests the boundary the sentence purports to settle.

**What reads well:** The opening's example of the same artifact serving as advice in one path and instruction in another makes it clear why stored bytes do not determine behavioral authority.

## Ranked changes

1. **Settle what governance is required for.** **Defect** ([critique][critique], [premises][premises]). The title and opening say a deployed system needs candidate selection and validation to learn continually. The final paragraph's “for retained candidates” supports a different reading: search and reject-capable evaluation are required for proposal selection, not for direct updates. A deployed online gradient learner can durably adapt without a candidate-adoption gate. On the first reading it does not qualify; on the second it can qualify, and the practical question is how to protect the deployed system against regression. The broad criterion for *learning* is not itself inconsistent with defining a narrower category; the problem is that the opening presents the narrower mechanism as necessary across the very system scope that includes parametric continual learning. Removing “as the field uses the term” is worthwhile independently but leaves the title, opening and discriminator at odds. Prefer distinguishing the requirements for reliable deployment from the requirements of proposal selection, unless the operator intends a smaller note specifically about proposal-selection systems. The latter would yield a sharper discriminator but no longer give a general deployed-system account. **Cost:** a new commission and a coordinated rewrite. **Pass:** none; use `commonplace-revise rewrite NOTE --commission FILE` with the commission prepared using `cp-skill-write-multistage`.
2. **Ground the regression premise independently of the discriminator.** **Defect** ([composition friction][friction], [premises][premises]; finding 9). The scheduler citation addresses code-enforced workflow state, not whether successive deployed adaptations accumulate undetected harm. Removing or relabelling that link alone prevents a misleading citation but leaves the asserted causal premise unsupported. The learner with a bounded update rule and monitoring or rollback also tests the difference between protection against regression and a mandatory pre-install check on every update. The reader needs a supported account of the risk and the unit at which validation applies; replacing the universal premise with a bounded one would lose breadth, not the reason to protect the deployed system. This repair pays off even if the operator chooses the narrower thesis. **Cost:** source or mechanism reconstruction and a narrower claim if warranted. **Pass:** none for the missing support; address in the rewrite commission.
3. **Keep the structural compression proposals subordinate to the claim decision.** [Compression synthesis][synthesis]: frontloading self-selection is a **choice**, not a defect established by its position alone. It would save readers looking for the learning/maintenance discriminator from reading to the end, but putting it in the opening now would entrench one disputed reading. If the rewrite is about reliable deployment generally, foregrounding self-selection there would mislead; if it is about proposal selection, the placement is useful. **Cost:** recasting the opening. **Later pass candidate:** `opening-and-title` for presentation only after the claim is settled; title changes outside its bounds require an operator decision.
   - **Choice, independent:** Moving codification and relaxing beside form placement would give the final paragraph a clearer job without losing the linked example of movement between forms. Keeping it where it is makes governance's revisability visible at the point governance is introduced. Merging the human-selection passages would reduce repetition but could lose either release engineering as a general practice or the incident edit as a concrete comparison. The smallest useful repair would keep one comparison that still says both that the system learned and that people selected the change. Keep the cadence sentence if it states an operational deadline rather than echoing installation cost. **Cost:** paragraph move and preservation check. **Later pass candidate:** `readability-and-flow`, only on settled text.
   - **Choice, independent:** In the opening, removing only the last restatement would preserve the evidence/advice versus instruction contrast and both links. Compressing the definitions as well would foreground the consumption-path premise, at the cost of making the distinction harder to recover without following links. Keep the fuller contrast if first-time designers are the priority. **Cost:** one local edit, or a more careful recast. **Later pass candidate:** `readability-and-flow` after the opening is settled. There is no comparison list with repeated openings that obscure a reader's choice.

## Claim questions for the operator

- **Governance at write time or at use (local defect, [critique][critique], [premises][premises]).** The opening makes authority a property of consumption, but “governing writes” can be read as requiring controls at admission. A store-everything design that selects relevant records and controls their force only when reading makes the difference practical. Persistence alone still does not suffice. Clarify whether governance can act on the consumption path and when a retained advisory record amounts to durable adaptation. A qualification that preserves the consumption-path claim need not disturb the SkillOpt or METR ingest.
- **A human approval gate (local defect, [premises][premises], finding 6).** A system proposes and evaluates a change from deployment evidence; a person has the final veto. Does that count as selection inside the loop, or as external maintenance? The hand-authored incident example does not settle it. Preserve the distinction between external authorship and evidence-responsive selection without silently settling where an approval gate belongs.
- **Form choice and deployment evidence (local defects, [premises][premises], [composition friction][friction]).** A system with one writable form fixed at design time can still adapt without deciding form placement on each update. Keep coordination for updates that touch multiple forms. Sparse evidence does not establish cheap installation: it can favour caution or batching, and dense online feedback can support incremental parametric updates. Tie the cost requirement to the deployment deadline and state when the evidence is sparse rather than presenting one as the reason for the other. The authority-path duty also needs to follow from the need to control which use gives a change force, not from a definition alone.
- **Stale or misleading qualifications.** The description's “across the representational forms a system can change” can imply broader coordination than the body's “whichever forms that update touches.” The addressability footer gloss assigns the form-level deferral to explicit retention instead of the coevolution note; the other axis glosses should claim only relationships the body actually uses. The scheduler footer overstates the comparison with parametric writes. Two footer link titles overstate their targets (finding 12). No stale scope bullet, table row or ordinal appears in the supplied text. Resolve these in the commission or as separate approved repairs; do not rename the file as an editorial fix.

**Support versus divergence.** Field usage is supporting attribution, not a claim to preserve at all costs. The universal compounding premise, the sparsity-to-cost inference and the necessity of runtime form choice may be narrowed or replaced with support. Preserve the durable-capacity criterion, human-selected changes as learning, persistence versus consumption-path authority, the evidence-responsive discriminator *for proposal-selection loops*, the non-weight update paths and coordination of touched forms, and the need to protect reliable adaptation against regression without denying parametric forgetting. These are the divergence commitments in `must-keep`; they do not settle the disputed universal thesis, the hybrid case or the location of a check.

**Dependent impact.** The [role report][role] shows that `kb/sources/hot-take-llm-can-jump.ingest.md` and `kb/sources/on-learning-how-to-learn-learning-strategies.ingest.md` rely on the system-selected discriminator; distinguish preserving their cases from preserving the unrestricted definition they cite. `kb/sources/hyra-2-next-step-for-auto-research.ingest.md` already treats human approval as governance, so the operator should resolve that boundary when changing the discriminator. `kb/sources/dfa-moe-dual-forgetting-vision-language-continual-learning.ingest.md` and `kb/sources/dual-nature-generalization-on-policy-distillation.ingest.md` rely on regression validation; replacing the unsupported rationale does not require dropping that requirement. Preserve the governance functions used by the agentic-reasoning and Intern-S2 ingests and the codification move used by the long-horizon-agents ingest. The role report shows 30 of 48 linking files, so lack of a visible use is not grounds to cut text.
