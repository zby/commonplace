   When a recommendation combines independent edits, distinguish those that still pay off without the others. For a contested edit, compare it with keeping the passage or making the smallest repair that addresses the same reader problem. Say what the reader gains and what they might lose—not just how much text changes. If the brief settles that trade-off, name the commitment that settles it. If a plausible priority consistent with the brief reverses the choice, state the condition rather than presenting the edit as an unqualified improvement. Do not invent weights or turn an ordinary editorial uncertainty into a plan stop.
