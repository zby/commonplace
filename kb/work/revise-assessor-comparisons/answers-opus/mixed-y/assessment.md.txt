# Assessment: On-balance judgments can guide improvement without a global measure

**Purpose (operator's):** Explain how on-balance judgments guide decisions without a global measure, and where analysis helps or stops.

**What reads well:** The worked cases are concrete, checked and individually scoped: the Python 2/3 text case, the `random.py` diagnostic, the 3.10/3.11 table and the `AGENTS.md` routing line each show a reason for a preference and state its own limit. The reversal rule comes with a concrete case that shows when investigation pays off.

**Sentence a hostile reviewer would attack first:** "A partial order can represent the preferences we can defend under a consistent set of priorities." Three depth 1 reports attack it independently. On-balance pairwise judgments need not be transitive, and the note does not say where the "consistent set of priorities" is fixed before a change is judged.

**Verdict on stopping:** No stop. No depth 1 report or synthesis proposal shows a defect that would require replacing or restructuring the text the passes work on. The defects that were shown can each be corrected by adding a boundary sentence or sharpening one sentence. They are listed under claim questions, and the passes are scoped away from those passages. The highest-value changes are local: state the thesis before the examples accumulate, place the library case where analysis stops, and remove a few reader obstacles. The role report shows that four dependents link to `#what-commonplace-chooses-to-count` and rely on the objective sentence, its rationale, the automation sentence and the binding-requirement boundary. The plan leaves all of these unchanged.

## Ranked changes

1. **State the two-part thesis near the top, and introduce the release section as an instance.** Cost: one added general sentence after the opening Python example, plus at most one lead-in clause in the release section. Pass: `readability-and-flow` (finding `structural/general-before-specific`, depth 4; synthesis opportunity 2). Value: the purpose asks how on-balance judgments guide decisions *and* where analysis helps or stops. At present the reader meets three cases before any general sentence, and the reversal rule appears only in the third section. One sentence after the Python 2/3 example can state both halves: reasons that name what changed, for whom and why it matters can justify preferring a successor without a global score; and an unknown is worth investigating only if a plausible answer could reverse the choice. The reader then reads the later sections as development of that claim. This edit pays off without the others. Constraint: the preview must keep the rule scoped to "the decision at hand". It must not restate the rule as universal, because binding requirements bound it. It previews the rule and does not rewrite the bold rule sentence. The finding's other option, moving "Three choices fix the comparison" above the release table, is a larger reordering. The smaller repair, a thesis sentence plus a lead-in, addresses the same reader problem and keeps the cases first, which the reviewer judged a deliberate order. Recommend the smaller repair.

2. **Move the library-hours case into "What analysis can settle" as the illustration for "Who gains, and who bears the loss?", and cut the repeated sentence.** Cost: one paragraph moved, one sentence cut and a bullet gloss shortened to a back-reference. Pass: `readability-and-flow` (synthesis opportunity 1; covers finding `compression-bundle/detail-overhang`, depth 4). The two parts can be judged separately:

The bold lead, "less on uncertainty than on whose work the decision serves", carries that contrast and travels with the paragraph. The smallest repair is to leave the paragraph in place and cut only the repeated sentence. That fixes the redundancy but not the interruption or the double telling with the bullet. The purpose favours relocation. If the operator wants the release section to show both kinds of hard comparison together, keep the paragraph in place and make only the cut.

The detail-overhang finding would cut the transfer sentence. Report-1 calls it thin but does not show it wrong. Folding it into one clause answers the finding and keeps the claim.

## Claim questions for the operator

Each item below is a defect or a choice that a sentence or two would settle. The passes are scoped away from each passage.

1. **Partial-order sentence: transitivity (defect, one-sentence correction).** Passage: "A partial order can represent the preferences we can defend under a consistent set of priorities." Case: pairwise on-balance judgments, each turning on the dimension that differs most for that pair, can cycle (A over B on speed, B over C on compatibility, C over A on library support). A partial order requires transitivity, and "does not guarantee that a single stable ranking exists" concedes instability but not intransitivity. Repair: either state transitivity as a condition for the representation, or describe the judgments as defended pairwise preferences that need not compose. Reports: `kb/reports/state/review/results/kb/theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure.md/kb/instructions/premise-decomposition-gate.md`, `.../kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`, `.../kb/instructions/critique-note.md`. Related choice, with no defect shown: the critique asks where the "consistent set of priorities" is declared before a change is judged, connecting to the antecedence condition in the linked declared-objective note.
2. **"Those reasons can be checked independently of who offered them" (defect, one-sentence correction).** Case: one of the listed reasons is "whose costs count". That is a weighting, and a checker can only judge it again, not check it. Two readings follow: all three reasons are verifiable, or only the factual ones are. The reader would draw different conclusions about how far an on-balance judgment can be audited. Repair: separate checkable facts (which work changed, for whom) from weightings, which can be criticized for inconsistency with a declared objective or earlier judgments. Reports: `.../kb/instructions/critique-note.md`, `.../kb/instructions/premise-decomposition-gate.md`.
3. **The reversal rule and weights, and unknowns that investigation cannot settle (defect, boundary sentence).** Passage: the bold rule and the `AGENTS.md` decision paragraph. Case: an unknown that could reverse the choice but that investigation cannot settle. Future use is one; the note itself says an estimate "cannot settle" it. For that case the rule sends the reader to a useless investigation, and "leave the overall judgment open" gives no default when a decision is due. The description promises guidance on "which uncertainties justify further investigation before acting", so this case is inside the claimed coverage. Related point: whether an answer "reverses" the choice depends on weights. One sentence saying that the robustness check runs over plausible weightings as well as plausible facts, and that a flip between acceptable weightings is a value disagreement to escalate, would close it. Reports: `kb/reports/state/review/results/kb/theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure.md/semantic/completeness-boundary-cases.md`, `.../kb/instructions/critique-note.md`, `.../kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`, `.../kb/instructions/premise-decomposition-gate.md`. Any repair must keep the must-keep rule's wording.
4. **The 3.11 verdict's premise of rare blockers, and "identifiable callers" (one-sentence correction).** Passage: "The compatibility cost falls on identifiable callers" and the paragraph around "On balance, yes". Case: silent behaviour changes in untested paths, and downstream users of a library that drops 3.10, bear costs that pre-move testing does not reveal. The note concedes that widespread blockers would challenge the default but makes no survey. Repair: qualify the claim to losses that a team's tests and dependency inventory can find, or present the default as conditional on blockers being rare. Reports: `.../kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`, `.../kb/instructions/premise-decomposition-gate.md`.
5. **The Commonplace objective read as a scalar ratio (choice, no defect shown).** The critique proposes one sentence saying that "for the human judgment spent" names a direction of preference and is not operationalized as a score. Adding the sentence would leave the dependents' relied-on objective intact. Changing the objective's wording would not. Report: `.../kb/instructions/critique-note.md`.
6. **Move the Commonplace objective to `kb/reference/` (choice, no defect shown, not ranked first).** The COLLECTION review proposes splitting the declaration into a reference artifact, or recasting the section as an application. Four dependents link to the section anchor, and the operator's must-keep list preserves it, so a split would require changing the dependents too. The operator may take this up separately. Result: `kb/reports/state/review/results/kb/theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure.md/kb/theory/COLLECTION.md`.
7. **Placement of the partial-order paragraph (choice, no defect shown).** Synthesis opportunity 3 asks whether the paragraph belongs beside "What analysis can settle" or stays as the bridge from the objective. Its first sentence carries claim question 1, and the section holds the dependents' anchor. Decide the claim question first. Report: `kb/reports/state/review/runs/v2/revise/kb/theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure/20261120T185155Z/synthesis`.
