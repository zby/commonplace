# A theory's prototype standing is its revision cost: external binding plus lost investment

Take a theory in the sense of [Theory warrant should be tracked at the finest granularity evidence licenses](./theory-warrant-tracked-at-the-finest-granularity-evidence-licenses.md): an account whose premises, mechanism, consequences, and scope can be inspected and revised as named parts. Such a theory has *prototype standing* while it is still cheap to revise or reject. That standing is the theory's expected revision cost, which has two components. External binding is what a revision propagates to: the consumers coupled to the current version. Intrinsic reconstruction cost is what a revision discards: the investment sunk into the current version. Standing is the total of the two in kind, assessed in advance for the scope a revision would touch. There is no formula and no weighting; "expected" means anticipated at assessment time, not probability-weighted. The claim is universal over theories in this sense: each such theory's standing is set by these two components and by nothing that reduces to neither. The closing paragraph states what would refute it.

Prototype standing is a lifecycle standing, not an epistemic status. Whether a theory is [accepted](./definitions/discovery-lifecycle.md) is a recorded decision that its evidence meets a named criterion for an intended use. That decision adds no consumer and discards no investment, so by itself it neither raises nor lowers revision cost. An accepted theory can therefore keep prototype standing, and a conjecture can become entrenched or heavily invested before it earns acceptance. "Prototype" here is an engineering gloss for a build kept cheap to revise while its commitments are being tested. It is not the [collection-prototype](../reference/collection-prototypes.md) sense, which names clone-once creation-time text for a new contract, and it does not name an exemplar. The gloss carries this visible scope because [Load-bearing vocabulary collisions should be prevented or visibly scoped at write time](./vocabulary-collisions-prevented-at-write-time-not-read-time.md) requires one wherever a familiar word introduces a technical category.

External binding is the set of consumers coupled to a theory's current version, so that a change propagates to them. Procedures, validators, and executables are consumers of this kind; so, for instance, are a certified procedure or a training regime built on the theory. The coupling need not be machine-readable: a procedure, an audit, or a contract binds prose as firmly as a validator binds a schema. The mechanism is accumulation. Each coupled consumer adds a dependant a revision must migrate, so replacement grows expensive as consumers accumulate, because [Current-task fit alone does not warrant costly structural entrenchment](./current-task-fit-alone-does-not-warrant-costly-entrenchment.md). That note states it for a knowledge base's structural layer; applying it to theories is a transfer. Form does not cap the component. A harness-loaded instruction is natural language whose consumption is guaranteed on each run, so an accepted change becomes operative at once, as in [Gödel machines are a proof-governed case of reflective self-modification](./goedel-machines-are-a-proof-governed-case-of-self-modification.md); that note discusses prompt self-editing, so reading it as a theory case is likewise a transfer.

Intrinsic reconstruction cost is the investment a revision discards: for instance a large proof development, an approved safety case, or a trained model. It does not depend on consumer count; a proof development with no consumer still loses its proofs when a premise changes. This is why internal proof or model dependencies and lost evidential investment count before any external adoption: they are the second component, not an exception to the first. The component rests on the pre-formal stage account, since [Improvements outside the admitted formal language need a pre-formal stage somewhere](./unformalized-improvements-need-a-pre-formal-stage-in-the-loop.md) denies that the medium makes rejection cheap and names sunk work beside downstream coupling as what does. A faithful rationale lowers the component: a theory retained with the rationale that produced it can be repaired part by part, while one without it must be deleted and re-derived, since [Selective revision needs a faithful rationale, not just a legible one](./selective-revision-needs-a-faithful-rationale-not-just-a-legible-one.md). Reading that difference as a cost effect is this note's inference. The two components are paid to different parties, propagation to the consumers and discarding to whoever holds the theory, so one can be zero while the other is large, as with an unbound proof development or a one-line policy that many procedures cite. A maximum would hide one bill, which is why standing is their total. With no consumers, standing is set by reconstruction cost alone, and an artifact of either form with neither consumers nor investment is cheap to discard.

Representational form, taken here as natural-language versus symbolic, determines neither component. Prose bound through a procedure, an audit, or a contract is expensive to revise. A low-authority formal sketch stays disposable. [Codification](./definitions/codification.md) is a change of form and consumer kind, from natural language into a symbolic artifact with assigned consequences; "committed to a symbolic consumer" there means a formal consumer assigns the consequences, not that consumers are coupled, so the definition says nothing about deployment or acceptance. Codification makes formal consumption available. Standing changes when a validator or executable is actually coupled, and the added consumer, not the form, is the cause; form gets mistaken for standing when availability is read as coupling. Form does determine which checks are available and what each use costs in interpretation: a natural-language premise is revisable without a build step but is reinterpreted at each use. It does not determine what a revision costs, since [Theory warrant should be tracked at the finest granularity evidence licenses](./theory-warrant-tracked-at-the-finest-granularity-evidence-licenses.md) treats form as changing a part's available checks without changing its warrant. At most, form correlates with the second component: symbolic artifacts may tend to carry construction investment that prose does not. That is a tendency, and prevalence evidence that, at equal binding, form fails to predict discarded investment would refute it.

Since binding is a cost component, binding a consumer to a theory before the theory is accepted for that consumer's scope spends standing that evidence has not yet licensed. Acceptance for the bound scope licenses binding. Current fit does not, and current form does not. Executable success does not either, because [Exact implementation does not validate a requirement against its objective](./exact-implementation-does-not-validate-a-requirement.md): a passing executable shows the artifact matches its requirement, and adoption supplies authority rather than prudence. So binding is the act to withhold before acceptance for the bound scope, whatever form the theory has. The scope clause is an inference from per-part warrant and the stated-scope condition on entrenchment; no source states it directly. Binding before acceptance may still be chosen for coordination value or forced by an enduring constraint. It is then knowingly spent standing, priced where the current-task-fit note prices entrenchment.

Standing is assessed per part, at the grain consumers bind to. Warrant is already tracked per part, and binding and investment attach to parts as well, so their total is per part too. Parts differ in standing where either component differs. In partial codification, binding is the component that usually differs, because codified and uncodified parts share the same discarded investment unless the codified part carried its own construction, such as a proof development. That "usually" is an observation about where the difference tends to sit, not part of the claim. A premise that becomes a checked operative invariant loses standing while the rest of the theory keeps it.

A symbolic artifact can exist unbound, so an unbound, lightly invested formal model has prototype standing. Where the cost of construction, proof generation, or checking was the bottleneck, cheaper formalization lets formal models enter the prototype loop as experiments, and competing formal models of one theory preserve alternatives by exposing different consequences before anything is bound. The conditional limits the effect. Formalization cost is a bundle whose parts fall independently: translation into a model, construction, proof generation, checking, and evidence of world fit, per the pre-formal stage note linked above. Cheaper checking leaves translation and world-fit costs where they were, and it leaves the need for cheap rejection in place. It also leaves what a proof warrants where it was: entailment inside the model, not correspondence to the world, as [Causal and proof obligations are two formal routes to assessing explanatory-reach](./formal-systems-assess-explanatory-reach-through-causal-and-proof.md) shows.

The claim is universal, so one counterexample refutes it. Two theories of the same form and the same epistemic status should differ in prototype standing when their binding or reconstruction cost differs. Two theories of different form should not differ when both components are equal. A pair equal in both components that differs in standing refutes the claim. So does one theory whose revision cost is materially set by something that reduces to neither component. The pre-formal stage note names authority and rollback cost beside coupling and sunk work; on this note's reading, rollback cost is propagation to consumers and authority is consumers obliged to follow, so both reduce to binding. If either resists that reduction, it is the counterexample. No paired case has been observed, so the comparison stands as a prediction.

## Scope

- Rejection retracts the claim and ends its standing. Revision and suspension preserve a surviving claim's standing only while both components stay low. Procedures and state records are superseded rather than refuted, so they fall outside the theory sense used here.
- Cheap and local are relative to named consumers and dependencies.
- Standing can be mixed across parts; the grain consumers bind to is the grain assessed.
- The "determines neither" bound covers natural-language versus symbolic form and no other.
- [Distributed-parametric](./definitions/representational-form.md) form is outside that bound. There a revision is a retraining rather than a per-item edit, so the reconstruction component is set largely by the form.

## Open Questions

- Which observable costs measure external binding and reconstruction cost, so that the paired comparison above can be run rather than stated?
- How should a system inventory prose authorities whose binding is real but not machine-readable?
- When consumers bind to partially codified theories at different grains, which grain should a review or acceptance decision target?
