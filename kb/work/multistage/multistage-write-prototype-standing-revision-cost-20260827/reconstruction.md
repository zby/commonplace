## Governing question and commission

Governing question (brief, verbatim): "What determines whether a theory has *prototype standing* — the lifecycle standing in which it is still cheap to revise or reject — and how does that standing relate to representational form (natural-language vs symbolic) and to epistemic status (accepted vs conjectural)?"

Target claim (user-supplied, verbatim): "A theory's prototype standing is its expected revision cost, which has two components: 1. External binding — consumers coupled to the current version, so a change propagates: procedures, training, certification, validators, executables. 2. Intrinsic reconstruction cost — the investment discarded on revision: a large proof development, an approved safety case, a trained model. Representational form (natural-language vs symbolic) correlates weakly with the second component and determines neither. Epistemic status (accepted vs conjectural) is a separate axis. This makes the prior review residual — that internal proof/model dependencies and lost evidential investment count even before external adoption — part of the mechanism rather than an exception."

Working title: "A theory's prototype standing is its revision cost: external binding plus lost investment".

## Material items supported by the inputs

3. **Codification.** "The far end of the constraining spectrum — the point where constraining … crosses from natural language into a symbolic artifact with formal semantics or assigned consequences"; "Use the term codification when an operation or rule has been committed to a symbolic consumer"; same-form natural-language formalization (legalese, formal policy) is constraining, not codification. — `kb/notes/definitions/codification.md`, opening, "Scope", "Exclusions". 4. **Representational form.** Three forms: natural-language (consequences from interpretation), symbolic (consequences assigned by a defined consumer, localized), distributed-parametric ("numerical state distributed across parameters … weights, adapters, embedding spaces … learned controllers"). Derived from two axes, assigned consequences and localization. "Addressability rides on the localization axis alone, which is why a prompt and a validator are both revisable one item at a time while weights are not." — `kb/notes/definitions/representational-form.md`, "Scope", "Derivation". Definition plus a stated claim about per-item revisability.

5. **Discovery-lifecycle acceptance.** Acceptance is "gated commitment on the successful branch": "record that the evidence meets a named criterion for the claim's intended use; declare its boundary and maintenance regime." Testing, acceptance, integration are different acts; integration "update[s] affected artifacts"; revision returns to conjecture; later counterevidence opens a new instance and "the prior acceptance remains part of the record rather than shielding the claim from reassessment." — `kb/notes/definitions/discovery-lifecycle.md`, table and following paragraph. Definition. The lifecycle names no binding act and no cost; it does not say what makes revision cheap or expensive.

6. **Adoption vs entrenchment; reversibility cost.** "Adoption uses a choice for current work. Entrenchment embeds that choice in dependencies or migration costs that make replacement expensive. A binding choice that remains cheap to extend or replace is outside this claim." Rule: "use current-task fit to adopt; entrench only what enduring constraints force, discriminating use or proof earns for a stated scope, or coordination value justifies." Also: "Adoption by more tools or notes raises migration cost; it does not demonstrate transfer"; "A nominally local structure with cross-subtree dependants may already be expensive to replace." — `kb/notes/current-task-fit-alone-does-not-warrant-costly-entrenchment.md`, third paragraph, "What warrants entrenchment", "Scope". Stated claims, about the structural layer of a KB, not theories.

7. **Exact implementation vs requirement validation.** "Exactness belongs to the artifact–requirement link; proxyhood belongs to the requirement–objective link above it." Posture: provisional codification — "Harden local conformance when the gain … exceeds the expected costs of change and failure; adoption supplies authority, not prudence. Harden a conjectured link only to the degree its evidence warrants, keeping the artifact inspectable, tested, and easy to relax." ## Support for the two-component mechanism

(i) **External binding as a determinant.** Supported. Pre-formal note item 10(c)-(d): "no operational machinery bound to its commitments"; "downstream coupling … rollback cost" make rejection expensive; binding prose "through a procedure, audit, or contract" is outside the prototype stage. Current-task-fit item 6: entrenchment = dependencies that make replacement expensive; more consumers raise migration cost. Gödel note item 14: a harness-loaded prompt has guaranteed consumption ("the wire is guaranteed"). The specific consumer list in the target claim (procedures, training, certification, validators, executables) is user direction; the inputs give procedures, audits, contracts, validators, tools, notes, and harness-loaded prompts as examples; "training" and "certification" appear in no input.

(ii) **Intrinsic reconstruction cost.** Supported in outline, thin in detail. Pre-formal note item 10(d) names "sunk work" as a determinant distinct from coupling, and "Nonbinding prose can motivate months of building." Selective-revision item 14: without a faithful rationale a counterexample forces "wholesale replacement — expensive", which is a reconstruction cost that a rationale lowers. Coevolution item 14: parametric updates need heavy infrastructure and cycle on days to weeks. The three examples in the target claim (large proof development, approved safety case, trained model): the trained model is supported by the coevolution note; the proof development is supported only by the general "sunk work" sentence; the safety case appears in no input. User direction for the examples; the packet's "lost evidential investment" residual is cited in the brief but the packet is not available to reconstruction.

(iii) **Form correlates weakly with reconstruction cost and determines neither component.** "Determines neither" is directly supported for the NL/symbolic pair: item 10(d) ("Nor does the medium make rejection cheap"; formal sketch can stay disposable, prose can bind), item 14 Gödel (natural-language prompt with maximal binding), item 6 (entrenchment is about dependencies, not form). The "weak correlation" with reconstruction cost is inference: no input measures it. Caution: for distributed-parametric form the inputs suggest a stronger link — item 4 (weights are not revisable one item at a time) and item 14 coevolution (retraining cost, slow cycle). The target claim restricts "form" to natural-language vs symbolic in its parenthetical; keeping that restriction avoids contradiction with the parametric material. DECISION NEEDED below.

(iv) **Epistemic status as a separate axis.** Inference from items 5, 6, 7. Discovery lifecycle defines acceptance as a recorded decision and says nothing about cost; current-task-fit distinguishes adoption from entrenchment; exact-implementation says adoption "does not establish that the choice is … cheap to reverse." No input states "epistemic status and revision cost are independent axes" in those words. User direction plus inference.

(v) **Warrant rule: binding is the act not to perform before acceptance for the bound scope.** Partly supported, partly inference. Current-task-fit item 6 gives the closest rule: adopt on current fit; entrench only what enduring constraints force, discriminating use or proof earns for a stated scope, or coordination value justifies. Exact-implementation item 7: harden a conjectured link "only to the degree its evidence warrants … easy to relax"; "adoption supplies authority, not prudence." Pre-formal item 10(e): codifying before concepts settle raises replacement cost toward entrenchment. The reframing "for the bound scope" is inference from item 2 (warrant per part and per scope) plus item 6 ("for a stated scope"). Tension: current-task-fit's third warrant lets coordination value justify entrenchment before discriminating evidence; the rule as stated in the brief has no coordination exception. See Conflicts.

(vi) **Grain point.** Inference. Item 2 (warrant at the finest granularity evidence identifies; mixed status below the document level) supports assessing per part. Item 4 (localized forms are revisable one item at a time) supports that a part can be bound while others are not. Pre-formal item 10(c): "the part a formal consumer needs is codified … while the rest stays reopenable" — direct support that partial codification leaves mixed standing. "Only where binding differs" is user direction extending this.

(vii) **Cheap formalization; formal artifacts as experiments in the prototype loop; cost-bundle caveat.** Supported by pre-formal item 10(f): separable costs listed; "formal prototypes enter earlier" when construction, proof, checking fall; conditional on the language already containing the alternatives — matching the brief's "only where formalization cost was the bottleneck." Also item 10 second paragraph: "Partial specifications and competing formal models also preserve alternatives and support revision." The phrase "a symbolic artifact can exist unbound" is supported by "a low-authority formal sketch can stay disposable." The Eigenius fact that proof checking runs without a correspondence check (item 15) is an example of a formal artifact checked without binding, but it belongs to the correspondence branch, not to this paragraph.

(viii) **Testable consequence.** User direction. No input states a prediction of this shape. It follows deductively from the target claim (if standing = f(binding, reconstruction cost) then equal inputs give equal standing across forms). The inputs offer no cases that instantiate the comparison; the pre-formal note's contrast (prose motivating months of building vs disposable formal sketch) is the only paired example and it is illustrative, not observed. ## Conflicts and evidential strength

1. **Pre-formal note's one-line definition vs its own cost sentence.** Item 10(c) defines prototype by binding alone ("no operational machinery bound"); item 10(d) lists sunk work and rollback cost as well. The target claim adopts the wider list. After the rebuild the pre-formal note's definition will be narrower than the target's. Not a contradiction, but a wording inconsistency the run should record (handoff; the pre-formal note is not in scope for editing).

2. **Codification definition "committed to a symbolic consumer" vs "a symbolic artifact can exist unbound."** Item 3 uses "committed" in the sense of "has consequences assigned by a formal consumer", not "deployed to consumers." The brief says the definition "already separates form from adoption"; strictly it does so by silence, not by an explicit statement. Reduction to one sentence plus a link is safe if the sentence says the definition is about form, not deployment.

3. **Coordination-value exception vs the warrant rule.** Item 6 allows entrenchment before discriminating evidence when coordination value justifies it. The brief's rule (v) has no such clause. Either scope the rule (binding before acceptance is unwarranted by fit or status alone; other warrants are the current-task-fit note's business) or state the exception.

4. **Distributed-parametric and form-cost independence.** Items 4 and 14 (coevolution) tie parametric form to per-item non-revisability and slow, heavy updates. "Determines neither" holds cleanly only for the NL/symbolic pair. The brief's terminology section already says the form–cost correlation "is a tendency and must be stated as such with what would refute it, or omitted."

5. **Duplication of the correspondence branch.** The scheduler example, Eigenius, and DiscoverPhysics are already in the pre-formal note (item 10(g)), and the general claim is already in the formal-systems note (item 8). Moving the branch out of the target creates a third home unless it is folded into one of these.

Strength ranking: strongest — (i) external binding (three independent notes state it); (vii) cheap formalization (one note, explicit and detailed); the theory/codification/form/lifecycle definitions. Medium — (ii) reconstruction cost (one sentence plus two indirect notes); (v) warrant rule (assembled from three notes, one with an exception). Weakest — (iii) weak-correlation tendency, (iv) separate axis, (vi) "only where binding differs", (viii) consequence: user direction and inference, no instantiating cases.
