# Claim skeleton: costly entrenchment and option value

## One central claim

> Current-task fit alone warrants at most reversible adoption: costly
> structural entrenchment still requires an enduring constraint,
> discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope, or
> coordination value, while real-options reasoning governs only whether and
> when to preserve, investigate, or exercise a meaningful structural
> alternative under decision-changing information and the costs of delay.

This is a universal conditional claim about task-fitted structures whose
adoption would destroy a meaningful alternative or create dependencies costly
to reverse. It is high-confidence as a bounded methodological claim selected
by the brief and supported by the accepted local premises and reconstructed
option mechanism. It is not an empirical claim that the rule improves
Commonplace or agent outcomes. Real-options reasoning must remain a timing and
replaceability test around the three warrants, never a fourth warrant.

## Ordered paragraph and section plan

1. **Opening thesis.** State that present fit can establish present usefulness
   and support reversible adoption, but cannot by itself establish the scope or
   shared value needed for costly entrenchment. Name the three warrants in the
   opening, then state that option reasoning can change *when* a warranted
   commitment is made or whether a live alternative should be preserved while
   evidence arrives. **Scope/confidence/basis:** universal within the
   costly-reversal branch; high confidence from `brief.md`, C1, and I1, with the
   three accepted warrant premises. **Inference:** present usefulness is weaker
   than an entrenchment warrant; the option test follows rather than enlarges
   the warrant test.

2. **Define the applicability boundary.** Define **adoption** as using a
   structure for current work and **entrenchment** as creating dependencies or
   migration costs that make replacement expensive. Preserve one sentence from
   the accumulation material: adoption by more tools or notes can raise
   migration cost without demonstrating transfer. A cheap additive edit or a
   binding but cheaply replaceable choice is outside this claim. **Scope/
   confidence/basis:** structural choices such as types, schemas, link
   vocabularies, routing conventions, and validators; high confidence from the
   brief, the reconstructed destroyed-choice mechanism, C2, and I3/I9/I10.
   **Inference:** if no meaningful alternative is destroyed or burdened, there
   is neither costly entrenchment nor an option-value problem.

3. **Explain why current fit does not travel.** Current routing, validation, or
   writing benefit is evidence of usefulness for the current question set, not
   discriminating evidence for a changed one. Retaining the content does not
   neutralize a mismatched access structure; question-set drift can therefore
   expose the transfer problem inside one long-lived KB before export does.
   **Scope/confidence/basis:** conceptual transfer claim, not a prevalence
   claim; moderate-to-high confidence from the reconciled I2 and I5 and their
   existing citation boundaries. Omit the unsupported claim that internal
   mismatch is harder to notice. **Inference:** because current fit leaves
   cross-question behavior untested, another premise must supply entrenchment
   warrant.

4. **State the three substantive warrants without dilution.** Present them as
   alternatives that can justify costly commitment:

   - an **enduring constraint**, positively derived from independently stated
     boundary commitments; a boundary-preserving rival defeats inheritance,
     while failure to find one proves nothing;
   - **discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope**, where
     evidence earns only the empirical domains and failure modes it covers and
     proof earns only its formal domain; inherited source warrant also needs a
     target bridge; or
   - **coordination value** created when actual needed adopters gain from using
     the same structure, not from imagined future adopters or the convention's
     intrinsic superiority.

   **Scope/confidence/basis:** high confidence as accepted Commonplace premises
   from the enduring-constraint, derivation/inheritance, and coordination-value
   notes (C12-C14; I6-I8). **Inference:** only these premises answer *why* costly
   entrenchment is warranted; later option analysis can answer *whether now*.

5. **Separate source methodology from the transferred mechanism.** Attribute
   to the retained Pindyck extracts only the source-side relations: irreversible
   commitment gives up a later information-conditioned choice; delay can be
   infeasible or costly; a limited early action can produce information before
   the larger commitment; and an opportunity can expire. Then label as an
   inference the shared mechanism that a costly structural commitment can
   likewise remove the ability to condition a later structural choice on later
   information. **Scope/confidence/basis:** high confidence for the
   investment-domain relations from the ingest's retained Quotes; moderate
   confidence for the KB transfer because it is a mechanism analogy rather
   than target-side evidence (C3-C6 and the reconstruction's evidence
   boundary). If cited directly, plan `has-external-sources`; do not imply a
   monetary threshold.

6. **Define when replaceability has decision value.** A **meaningful option**
   is a later structural choice that present commitment would destroy or make
   costly to recover, that remains exercisable at the named return point, and
   that could change in selection, timing, modification, or abandonment after
   a named observation or bounded probe. Delay must be feasible. If no possible
   result changes the choice, or the alternative will be unavailable after its
   lead time, nominal replaceability has no option value for this decision.
   **Scope/confidence/basis:** qualitative necessary conditions, not sufficient
   proof that waiting is worthwhile; high confidence from the accepted
   productive-deferral note and moderate-to-high confidence for the ordered
   shared-mechanism conjunction in C7-C8. **Inference:** uncertainty alone does
   not support deferral; a live alternative and discriminating information are
   both required.

7. **Compare passive observation with a bounded probe.** Passive waiting needs
   a named exogenous observation whose possible outcomes change the later
   action and a return condition before the option expires. A **bounded probe**
   is limited evidence-producing work aimed at a named knowledge gap, with
   decision-changing possible outputs, cost, output, stop condition, and
   follow-on decision. A probe that creates the full costly dependency
   structure exercises rather than preserves the option. **Scope/confidence/
   basis:** high confidence for the distinction and probe conditions from the
   productive-deferral note; moderate confidence for the negative dependency
   test as the explicit cross-source inference in C9-C11. **Inference:** active
   evidence production can dominate passive waiting, but neither has value
   merely because it postpones commitment.

8. **Apply the cost and timing test without creating a warrant.** Compare the
   later conditional choice with foregone routing or validation benefit,
   fragmentation, foregone actual coordination, migration accumulated while
   waiting, probe and carrying cost, lead time, and expiry. These are
   Commonplace mappings of the general delay-cost relation, not Pindyck's KB
   claims, and the inputs do not rank them. Existing or urgently needed
   coordination can make present commitment timely, while existing sharedness
   can make later replacement costly; keep both effects distinct from
   hypothetical coordination. Most importantly, delay cost can defeat
   deferral but cannot warrant entrenchment where all three substantive
   warrants fail. **Scope/confidence/basis:** high confidence in the
   qualitative comparison and anti-fourth-warrant inference from the brief,
   C17-C19, and the coordination definition; low/unknown confidence in any
   decision-specific ranking. **Inference:** with a substantive warrant,
   choose timing by the live-option/cost comparison; without one, choose
   reversible adoption, abandonment, or another justified response rather
   than costly entrenchment.

9. **Scope and residual open question.** State that the note governs only
   costly-to-reverse choices. It welcomes task-derived architecture and does
   not require option analysis for cheap reversible changes. A stable question
   set or short useful life can reduce expected reversal exposure, and actual
   coordination can favor present commitment, but none turns task fit into
   transfer evidence. Publish that the rule is qualitative: no portable
   threshold compares the named costs, and no supplied evidence shows that the
   test improves KB or agent outcomes. Retain at most one open question about
   how a concrete decision should measure its coverage, coordination, and
   timing costs. **Basis:** I14-I18 and C20. This section bounds rather than
   weakens the central claim.

## Required inference chain

`current-task usefulness` -> `reversible adoption only` unless a choice creates
costly dependencies -> if it does, require `enduring constraint OR scoped
transfer evidence/proof OR actual coordination value` -> only then ask whether
a live, later, decision-changing alternative exists -> compare information and
replaceability value with delay/probe/expiry costs -> select timing. At no point
may `waiting is costly` or `replacement is inconvenient` bypass the
substantive-warrant gate.

## Definitions and comparisons the draft must preserve

- Adoption versus entrenchment: present use versus dependency-bound commitment
  with costly reversal.
- Meaningful option versus nominal rival: later exercisable and
  decision-change-capable versus merely imaginable or expiring.
- Passive observation versus bounded probe: exogenous evidence arrival versus
  limited active evidence production.
- Substantive warrant versus timing consideration: why commitment is justified
  versus whether it should occur now.
- Pindyck source claim versus shared mechanism versus Commonplace consequence:
  investment-domain support, cross-domain inference, and KB-specific mapping.

## Unresolved-marker disposition

- **Blocking:** none, provided the draft remains qualitative and keeps the
  three warrants intact.
- **Omittable:** claims about any concrete structure's dependencies, exact
  observation or probe results, future availability, or cost ranking when no
  decision-specific evidence is supplied; the unsupported claim that internal
  mismatch is harder to notice.
- **Publish as limitations/open question:** no calibrated threshold for delay,
  migration, coordination, or probe costs; no target-side effectiveness
  evidence; concrete coverage, bridge, and cost comparisons remain
  decision-specific.
