# Reconstruction: costly entrenchment and option value

## Evidence boundary

This reconstruction uses the complete `## Quotes` section of
`kb/sources/pindyck-irreversibility-uncertainty-investment.ingest.md` as the
only primary evidence from Pindyck. The ingest's summary, connections,
extractable value, limitations, and recommendation are compact analysis, not
source support. The four assigned Commonplace notes are accepted local
premises or syntheses. The brief selects the intended target-side refinement;
it is direction, not factual evidence.

No assigned source supplies empirical evidence about Commonplace outcomes or
a calibrated threshold for a KB decision.

## Fixed target claim

The brief preserves the incumbent's central proposition: fit to the current
task can warrant reversible adoption, but it cannot by itself warrant costly
structural entrenchment. Entrenchment still needs one of three substantive
warrants:

1. an enduring constraint;
2. discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope; or
3. coordination value that justifies shared commitment.

Real-options theory is to refine whether and when a meaningful alternative
should be kept available. It is not a fourth entrenchment warrant. The branch
is relevant only when the contemplated action would destroy an alternative or
create dependencies that are costly to reverse. A cheap, reversible edit does
not need option analysis merely because its future is uncertain.

Support: maintainer direction in `brief.md`, with the three warrant premises
developed by the assigned Commonplace notes.

## Source theory: what the retained Pindyck extracts establish

The retained extracts directly support four source-side propositions.

1. **Irreversible commitment surrenders a later choice.** Pindyck says an
   irreversible investment exercises or kills the option to invest: the firm
   gives up waiting for new information that could affect desirability or
   timing and cannot disinvest after an adverse change. This supports the
   mechanism of commitment destroying the ability to condition a later action
   on later information.
2. **Delay must be feasible and has costs.** Pindyck says an opportunity to
   delay is not always available. Strategic preemption can require early
   investment; where delay is feasible, competitor entry and foregone cash
   flows are examples of costs to weigh against information gained by waiting.
   The source therefore does not present waiting as an unconditional good.
3. **Early action can produce information without exercising the whole
   commitment.** In Pindyck's sequential-investment example, market research
   reveals which future state obtains, after which the factory is built only
   in the favorable state. This directly establishes that a limited earlier
   expenditure can have value because its result conditions the later, larger
   commitment. It does not establish that every pilot or research activity is
   useful.
4. **The opportunity can expire.** Pindyck uses an expiring patent or lease to
   show that less available delay and a greater cost of delay reduce the
   decision effect of irreversibility. Preserving a nominal alternative is
   therefore insufficient if it will not remain exercisable at the decision
   point.

These are theoretical decision relations illustrated in an investment
setting. The quotes do not test the relations in knowledge-base design, do not
price a Commonplace option, and do not establish a monetary or other numeric
commitment threshold.

## Accepted Commonplace premises

### Productive deferral

`productive-deferral-requires-option-evidence-and-convergence.md` supplies an
accepted Commonplace synthesis. Deferral requires all three of the following:

- a materially live later option that current commitment would destroy or
  make costly to recover;
- evidence whose possible results can change selection, timing, modification,
  or abandonment for the named decision; and
- a convergence rule that returns the choice to decision while the option is
  still feasible and couples that return condition to a response.

The note expressly says those conditions do not prove that waiting is worth
its cost. It distinguishes passive waiting for a named exogenous observation
from a bounded probe whose possible outputs change a named follow-on decision.
It also requires a probe to name its knowledge gap, cost, output, stop
condition, and follow-on decision. This is accepted local synthesis, not a
verbatim Pindyck formulation.

### Coordination value

`definitions/coordination-value.md` defines coordination value as value
created when artifacts, tools, or agents adopt the same structure. The value
resides in shared commitment rather than in the intrinsic superiority of the
selected convention. Defection destroys it, and re-coordination carries a
switching cost. The definition also rejects merely anticipated coordination:
no coordination warrant exists where no adopter yet needs the shared
structure.

### Enduring constraint

`a-framework-rule-with-a-boundary-preserving-rival-is-not-inherited.md`
supplies a one-way test. A rival that removes a rule while preserving
independently stated boundary invariants demotes that rule from inherited
constraint to design choice. Failure to find a rival does not certify the
rule; positive derivation from the invariants is still required. Thus a claim
of enduring constraint cannot be established by current implementation or by
an unsuccessful rival search alone.

### Transfer evidence and proof

`derivation-and-inheritance-give-starting-warrant-earns-scope.md` distinguishes
starting warrant from earned scope. Derivation from independently supported
constraints gives conditional starting warrant. Inheritance from a tested
source ontology gives transferred starting warrant only with a target bridge.
Discriminating evidence earns only the empirical domains and failure modes it
covers; proof earns only the formal domain fixed by its axioms and
formalization. Mere use, longevity, surface variety, or provenance does not
earn broader scope.

Inheritance as provenance in this premise should not be conflated with an
inherited boundary constraint in the preceding premise. The former transfers
source warrant subject to a bridge; the latter is a rule forced by an
independently stated framework boundary.

## Shared mechanism reconstructed across the inputs

Option value is conditional on a conjunction, not on uncertainty alone:

1. Present commitment would destroy or make costly to recover a meaningful
   alternative.
2. Delay is feasible.
3. A later observation or the possible results of bounded work can change the
   preferred structural action, its timing, its modification, or its
   abandonment.
4. The alternative will remain exercisable through that later decision point,
   including any preparation or lead time it needs.
5. The value of retaining the conditional later choice is not outweighed by
   the cost of delay, the cost of the probe, carrying costs, or loss of the
   opportunity.

If condition 1 fails, the action is reversible adoption rather than the
costly-entrenchment case. If conditions 2 or 4 fail, there is no continued
opportunity to preserve. If condition 3 fails, waiting or probing may produce
information but not discriminating information for this choice. If condition
5 fails, option value does not select deferral.

The inputs support three distinct information postures:

- **Passive observation:** wait for a named exogenous observation whose
  possible outcomes can alter the decision. A return condition must fire
  before the alternative expires.
- **Bounded probe:** pay for limited work designed around a named knowledge
  gap. At least one possible output must alter the later decision. The work
  needs a cost, output, stop condition, and follow-on decision, and must avoid
  silently creating the full dependencies whose preservation it was meant to
  test.
- **No productive information route:** if no plausible later observation or
  probe result changes the decision, delay has no option-evidence rationale.

The last requirement on a probe not to create the full dependency structure
is a cross-source inference: a probe that itself exercises the costly choice
does not preserve the option defined by the source mechanism.

## Commonplace consequence

For a task-fitted schema, type, link vocabulary, routing convention, validator,
or similar structure, the shared mechanism yields a qualitative sequence:

1. First ask whether current adoption is cheap to reverse. If so, adopt
   provisionally when useful; the real-options branch adds little.
2. If adoption would create costly dependencies or eliminate a meaningful
   rival, identify which of the three entrenchment warrants, if any, supports
   the structure. Current-task fit remains insufficient.
3. Identify a later observation or bounded probe and state how its possible
   results would change the structural choice. Confirm that the rival will
   remain usable at the return point.
4. Compare that preserved choice with the costs of waiting or probing. On the
   target side, the brief identifies foregone routing or validation benefit,
   fragmentation, missed coordination value, migration accumulated during
   delay, and expiry or loss of an alternative. These are Commonplace mappings
   of the general delay-cost relation, not claims made or tested by Pindyck.
5. Choose among reversible adoption, bounded evidence production, timed
   waiting, abandonment, or warranted commitment. State the condition that
   reopens a deferred decision before the option ceases to be live.

Delay cost can defeat deferral, but it cannot silently supply a fourth reason
to entrench an otherwise unwarranted structure. It can make present exercise
preferable once an enduring constraint or earned scope already warrants the
choice. Actual coordination value is itself the third warrant and its foregone
benefit can favor commitment now. Without one of those warrants, the end of a
deferral should lead to reversible adoption, abandonment, or another justified
response rather than to costly entrenchment merely because waiting is costly.

Coordination value has two timing effects that must remain separate. Before
shared adoption, a demonstrated need to converge can warrant commitment and
the benefit forgone while fragmented counts against delay. After shared
adoption, existing coordination makes replacement costly even when a rival is
better in principle. Neither effect licenses hardening vocabulary for adopters
that do not yet need to coordinate.

## How option value refines the three warrants

| Entrenchment warrant | What establishes the warrant | Option-value refinement | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enduring constraint | A positive derivation from independently stated boundary commitments; a boundary-preserving rival defeats the claim. | Ask whether later evidence could alter the alleged derivation. If no possible result changes it, waiting has no discriminating route. | Failure to find a rival is not proof, and option value does not turn a design choice into a constraint. |
| Discriminating transfer evidence or proof for stated scope | Evidence that could expose a relevant boundary failure, or proof inside a specified formal domain; source inheritance also needs a target bridge. | Preserve replaceability or run a bounded probe when named future evidence could change the claimed scope and the rival stays available. | Derivation, inheritance, use, and accumulated examples alone do not earn untested scope. Waiting itself earns nothing. |
| Coordination value | Needed adopters gain value from converging on the same structure. | Foregone convergence, fragmentation, and later re-coordination cost count against delay; an expiring convergence opportunity may make current commitment preferable. | Hypothetical future adopters do not create coordination value. A marginally better rival does not erase value already created by sharedness. |

The source theory therefore changes the timing and replaceability analysis
around each warrant. It does not replace the warrants, aggregate them into a
financial threshold, or show that costly commitment is justified whenever
delay has a cost.

## Tensions and evidential differences

- There is no direct factual conflict among the assigned inputs. Pindyck's
  extracts and the productive-deferral synthesis agree that waiting has value
  only through a live later choice informed by relevant information, and that
  waiting can lose to its costs.
- The productive-deferral note adds convergence and a four-posture operational
  synthesis that is not stated in the Pindyck quotes. The entrenchment
  refinement needs only the minimum convergence implication: a deferred choice
  must return while the rival remains available. Its full planning taxonomy is
  not a new entrenchment warrant.
- Coordination creates a real tension rather than a contradiction. Preserving
  alternatives can retain learning value, while delayed convergence can
  destroy present sharedness benefits and make later migration harder. Which
  dominates is decision-specific.
- Pindyck's information-producing research example establishes the existence
  of a valuable sequential step in its own domain. Treating a bounded KB probe
  as analogous is a shared-mechanism inference. It requires target-specific
  identification of the knowledge gap, possible outputs, dependency effects,
  and later decision.
- The inherited-constraint heuristic is negative, whereas a costly
  entrenchment warrant is affirmative. Demoting evidence is not certifying
  evidence, so an inconclusive rival search cannot be presented as a reason to
  harden.
- The derivation/inheritance note grants conditional or transferred starting
  warrant but reserves earned scope for discriminating evidence or proof. The
  candidate must not weaken the second entrenchment warrant into provenance or
  repeated local success.

## Unresolved evidence and definition markers

- **EVIDENCE NEEDED (decision-specific, non-blocking for a qualitative
  note):** For any concrete structural choice, identify the dependencies that
  make reversal costly, the observation or probe results that could change the
  choice, and evidence that the alternative will remain available through the
  return point.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED (decision-specific, non-blocking):** Estimate or at least
  compare current routing or validation benefit, fragmentation, coordination,
  migration, carrying, probe, lead-time, and expiry costs. The assigned inputs
  supply no Commonplace measurements.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED (published limitation):** Any claim that this qualitative
  test improves Commonplace decisions or LLM-agent outcomes needs target-side
  evaluation. None is supplied.
- **DEFINE in candidate:** A meaningful option is a later structural choice
  that present commitment would destroy or make costly to recover and that
  remains exercisable at the named decision point. A listed or theoretically
  imaginable rival that will be unavailable then is not a preserved option.
- **DEFINE in candidate:** A bounded probe is limited evidence-producing work
  whose possible outputs can change the later entrenchment decision, with a
  named cost, output, stop condition, and follow-on decision. It is not a
  euphemism for incremental full commitment.
- **DECISION NEEDED:** none. The brief fixes the central claim, the role of
  Pindyck, and the required refinement.
