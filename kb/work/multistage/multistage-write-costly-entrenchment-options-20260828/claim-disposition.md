# Claim disposition: costly entrenchment and option value

## Source-first disposition

This disposition uses only the brief, reconstruction, assigned evidence, and
the `kb/notes/` collection and `note` type contracts. It does not assess the
incumbent, the live target, or any draft. An existing-artifact judgment about
the current target therefore remains deliberately open until incumbent
reconciliation.

### Exact atomic contribution

> Current-task fit alone warrants at most reversible adoption: costly
> structural entrenchment still requires an enduring constraint,
> discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope, or
> coordination value, while real-options reasoning governs only whether and
> when to preserve, investigate, or exercise a meaningful structural
> alternative under decision-changing information and the costs of delay.

This is one importable proposition, not a synthesis exemption. Its first
clause states the warrant boundary; its second explains the timing and
replaceability boundary around the same entrenchment decision. The second
clause cannot justify entrenchment when none of the three warrants applies.
The intended target remains
`kb/notes/current-task-fit-alone-does-not-warrant-costly-entrenchment.md`.

The timing clause is true only in the costly-to-reverse branch. A meaningful
alternative is one that present commitment would destroy or make costly to
recover, that will remain exercisable at the later decision point, and whose
selection, timing, modification, or abandonment could change after a named
observation or bounded probe. If adoption is cheap to reverse, provisional use
does not need an option-value argument. If no possible information changes the
choice, or the alternative will expire before reconsideration, replaceability
has no decision value for this choice. If foregone benefit, fragmentation,
coordination loss, accumulated migration, probe cost, lead time, or expiry
outweighs the later conditional choice, commitment may be timely only to the
extent that a substantive warrant already supports it; otherwise the response
is reversible adoption, abandonment, or another justified action rather than
unwarranted entrenchment.

### Necessary supporting claims

The central contribution needs five kinds of support, but none warrants a
second central claim in this artifact.

1. **Warrant premises.** An enduring constraint needs positive derivation from
   independently stated boundary commitments; a boundary-preserving rival
   defeats inheritance, while an unsuccessful rival search proves nothing.
   Discriminating evidence earns only the empirical scope it covers, proof
   earns only its formal domain, and inherited source warrant needs a target
   bridge. Coordination value arises from actual needed shared commitment,
   not from a convention's intrinsic superiority or hypothetical future
   adopters. These premises already have independent citation boundaries.
2. **Destroyed-choice mechanism.** Costly commitment can remove the ability to
   condition a later action on later information. Delay is not always feasible
   and has opportunity costs; a limited earlier action can sometimes produce
   decision-changing information without making the full commitment; and the
   opportunity to decide later can expire.
3. **Productive-deferral test.** Deferral requires a materially live later
   option, evidence whose possible results discriminate among later actions,
   and a return rule that fires while exercise remains feasible. These
   conditions reject inert waiting, but do not prove that deferral beats its
   costs.
4. **Information posture.** Passive waiting is warranted only for a named
   exogenous observation that can change the later action. A bounded probe is
   limited work aimed at a named knowledge gap, with possible outputs that can
   change the later entrenchment decision and with a stated cost, output, stop
   condition, and follow-on decision. A probe that itself creates the full
   dependency structure fails to preserve the option it purports to test.
5. **Commonplace application.** For a type, schema, link vocabulary, routing
   convention, validator, or similar structure, identify the dependencies or
   lost rival that make reversal costly; name the later observation or probe
   result that would change the choice; check availability and lead time; then
   compare the preserved choice with foregone routing or validation benefit,
   fragmentation, coordination loss, migration accumulated while waiting,
   probe cost, and expiry. End the deferral with an explicit return condition.

### Evidence and transfer boundaries

**Pindyck source methodology.** Only the retained verbatim extracts in
`kb/sources/pindyck-irreversibility-uncertainty-investment.ingest.md` support
source-side claims. They support the loss of a later information-conditioned
choice through irreversible commitment, the feasibility and cost boundary on
delay, an information-producing early step followed by conditional larger
investment, and expiry of the opportunity. They do not support a KB-specific
outcome, a monetary or other numeric threshold for Commonplace, or a general
preference for delay.

**Shared mechanism.** Across the source extracts and the accepted productive-
deferral note, option value requires more than uncertainty: present commitment
must destroy or burden a meaningful later choice; delay must be feasible; a
later observation or bounded activity must have a possible result that changes
the decision; the alternative must remain exercisable; and the later choice's
value must survive delay, carrying, probe, lead-time, and expiry costs. The
bounded-probe transfer to KB work, including the requirement that the probe
not silently perform the full commitment, is an inference from that mechanism,
not a Pindyck formulation.

**Commonplace consequence.** The three entrenchment warrants, the examples of
structural choices, and the mappings from general delay cost to routing,
validation, fragmentation, coordination, and migration are Commonplace claims
selected or supported by the brief and local notes. The Pindyck source does not
establish them. Real-options reasoning refines the timing of an entrenchment
decision and the value of keeping a rival replaceable; it neither adds a
fourth warrant nor converts costly waiting into positive evidence for the
structure.

### Omissions

Omit financial mathematics, net-present-value or monetary thresholds,
complete-market or stochastic-process assumptions, the numerical research
example, and investment-domain detail beyond the minimal destroyed-choice,
delay-cost, information-production, and expiry mechanisms. Omit hysteresis and
any theory of installed-structure persistence because the assigned extracts do
not support that transfer. Omit rolling-wave planning, adaptive-policy
machinery, set-based taxonomies, the productive-deferral note's full posture
table, and general project-management process. Omit any recommendation to keep
several alternatives, wait passively, seek more information, or preserve
replaceability without a named decision-changing evidence route and a live
later choice. Omit hypothetical coordination value and any implication that
an inconclusive rival search, repeated use, surface variety, longevity, or
source provenance earns entrenchment scope.

### Uncertainties retained for drafting and scope

- A concrete use must identify which dependencies make reversal costly, what
  result could change the structural choice, and whether the alternative will
  remain exercisable; the assigned evidence cannot fill those values in.
- The record does not measure routing or validation benefit, fragmentation,
  coordination loss, migration, carrying, probe, lead-time, or expiry costs.
  The target can name comparison terms but cannot rank them universally.
- The source-side sequential-investment example supports the possibility of an
  information-producing early step. Calling a KB activity a bounded probe
  remains a mechanism transfer that must name its knowledge gap, possible
  outputs, dependency effects, and later decision.
- No evidence establishes that the resulting qualitative rule improves KB or
  agent outcomes. This is a published limitation, not a blocker for the
  bounded theoretical claim.
- No source-first `DECISION NEEDED` or blocking `EVIDENCE NEEDED` marker

### Conflicts and unsupported commitments

- **Replaceability conflict:** “keep it replaceable until another warrant
  appears” is too broad. Revision must require a live later choice,
  decision-changing information, later availability, and acceptable delay or
  probe cost.
- **Enduring-constraint conflict:** failure to find a boundary-preserving rival
  leaves a rule undemoted; it does not make entrenchment defensible without a
  positive derivation.
- **Coordination conflict:** expected benefit must not mean imagined future
  adopters. A coordination warrant begins with an actual need to converge.
