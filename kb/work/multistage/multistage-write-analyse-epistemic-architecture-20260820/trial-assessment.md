# Trial assessment: analyse an external system's epistemic architecture

## Overall result

Both cold agents completed the instruction without asking for clarification. They produced the six required output blocks, kept conclusions inside their inspected source boundaries, and used the same procedure to expose materially different architectures. The trial therefore supports the instruction's practical purpose and central distinctions.

The trial also exposed four specification gaps that the agents could bridge only by inventing local conventions. Those gaps concern execution, not the fixed evaluative commitments in `claim-disposition.md`: the draft lacks a disposition for indeterminate truth-apt transformations, conflates implemented lifecycle capability with an observed candidate's state, treats content transformations and non-content authority transitions as if they shared one ledger field, and gives no material-route cutoff for a whole-system analysis. The no-candidate sentence also has inconsistent global and per-object readings. These are blocking repairs before audit.

## 1. Cold executability

**Answer: yes, with schema improvisation.**

The ARC agent established a revision and source boundary, inventoried objects, built a route ledger, disposed candidates, compared system claims with routes, and reached a bounded conclusion. It explicitly reported that no clarification was needed. The GBrain agent did the same despite a missing Bun runtime, correctly treating that environment failure as missing observed-run evidence rather than as evidence about GBrain. It also explicitly reported that no clarification was needed.

This is enough to establish first-reading executability in the practical sense: neither agent stalled, silently converted the task into product ranking, or supplied a system-wide epistemic grade. It is not enough to call the schema fully determinate. Both agents added conventions that the draft does not state:

- ARC separated implemented lifecycle routes from absent run instances in prose.
- GBrain invented lifecycle records for `indeterminate` transformations.
- Both used the transformation field for admissions, status changes, selection, retention, and operational consumption that do not themselves transform content.
- GBrain chose its own material-route boundary for a large system; ARC followed a much more expansive boundary.
- ARC and GBrain handled individual objects with no truth-apt candidate differently.

## 2. Evidence layers and scoped negatives

**Answer: yes. Both preserved the required evidence layers and scope negatives.**

ARC kept implementation probes, doctrine, reported campaign operation, observed runs, and causal experiments separate. It treated its synthetic probes as implementation evidence only, stated that no ARC-game run was observed, and did not convert reported campaign counts into observed facts. Its negative findings were consistently bounded to the checkout, inspected dependencies, and named routes. It also withheld claims about the external agent's belief or policy state.

GBrain likewise separated inspected implementation, doctrine, attributed production reports, observed-run evidence, and causal evidence. It stated that `bun: command not found` supplied no evidence about system behavior. Its implementation absences were scoped to inspected source, including the missing proposal-decision consumer and unwired normal-cycle auto-resolution. It did not infer that no informal or external procedure could exist.

Both reports preserved the distinctions most important to the commission:

- ARC separated event-local prediction acceptance from notes retention, model-history fit, plan continuation, and replay admission. It also distinguished implemented capability from the absence of an observed ARC candidate, although the output schema forced that distinction into prose.
- GBrain separated direct page retention from evidence-consuming acceptance, cache retention from canonical resolution, and canonical resolution from the warrant of its inputs. It treated SkillOpt as benchmark-bounded behavior/policy adaptation rather than as acceptance of a truth-apt memory claim.
- Neither report equated later use with acceptance. GBrain made especially clear that unpublished calibration candidates can receive operational force without thereby receiving epistemic authority.

The remaining concern is representational: the reports preserved these boundaries through explanatory qualifications that the schema should have required directly.

## 3. Discrimination across architectures

**Answer: yes. The same instruction discriminated materially different epistemic architectures.**

ARC exposed several implementation routes with narrow evidence-consuming checks and consequential consumers. Immediate action predictions can be compared with returned settled observations; batch or plan execution can continue or stop on those results; executable rules can be checked against recorded history; and local replay can gate continuation. The report bounded each license to the checked event, projection, history, or replay surface. It found no observed run instance and no warrant for a general game explanation, but it did identify implemented routes capable of producing narrowly checked event and fit facts.

GBrain exposed a different dominant pattern. Dream pages, recurring-pattern pages, concept narratives, and enriched dossiers are generated and integrated without a post-generation truth-acceptance transition. Fact and proposal extraction preserve only coarse lineage where semantic preservation is untested. Proposal acceptance was absent in inspected source, normal-cycle grading did not retrieve outcome evidence or apply a resolution, and calibration candidates could affect later prompts without truth acceptance. SkillOpt supplied a separate benchmark-gated policy-selection route whose accepted output is an operative instruction change, not retained descriptive knowledge.

These are not cosmetic differences. The instruction distinguished checked event predictions and model-relative derivations from unaccepted synthesis, suspended or absent acceptance routes, and direct policy adaptation. It did so without requiring natural-language claims, proposal comparison, or one system-wide oracle. The trial therefore validates the route-level output shape.

## 4. Ambiguities and missing branches that caused improvisation

### Indeterminate truth-apt transformations

Step 4 permits `indeterminate` when semantic preservation or entailment cannot be established. Output 4 then offers only an ampliative lifecycle schema and a non-ampliative lineage/derivation/update schema. GBrain therefore invented a third form for O1, O4, O7, and O12: `transformation: indeterminate`, `discovery lifecycle: not determinable`, followed by lineage and missing evidence. That was a sensible result, but it is not specified by the instruction.

Forcing these objects into the ampliative schema would falsely assert candidate generation; forcing them into the non-ampliative schema would falsely assert preservation or entailment. A defined indeterminate disposition is required.

### Implemented capability versus observed candidate state

The allowed phase states combine several different dimensions. `Evidenced` can mean that code implements a transition or that an inspected candidate traversed it. `Absent` can mean that no route exists or that no run artifact shows the phase. `Accepted`, `rejected`, `revised`, and `failed` are candidate outcomes, while `suspended` can describe a configured implementation path.

ARC called conjecture, test, and integration routes `evidenced` from code while calling the state of any actual ARC candidate `not determinable`; its cold-execution note explicitly identified the problem. GBrain similarly described implemented generation/integration routes despite having no observed output instances, and used `suspended` for a default-disabled transition. The qualifications are sound, but the single state slot invites a later executor to overstate operation from capability.

### Non-content transitions in the ledger

The ledger covers each consequential “check or transition” but calls its main edge field `input-to-output transformation`. Step 4 also directs the executor to classify every input-to-output edge using content-transformation classes. Many consequential routes do not transform truth-apt content.

GBrain improvised labels including `operational selection`, `admission/retention`, `claimed transition absent`, `transition suspended by default`, `direct operational use`, and policy integration. ARC used the same field for syntax/action admission, aggregation and search admission, provenance/freshness admission, and run continuation. These rows are necessary because they carry force, but calling all of them content transformations obscures whether a route changes content, changes status, retains an object, or consumes a result operationally.

### Global versus per-object no-candidate handling

Output 4 says, “If no truth-apt candidate exists,” followed by a global no-lifecycle sentence. It does not say whether that condition applies to the entire source boundary or to one inventory object. It also instructs the executor elsewhere to record a non-lifecycle update route for direct adaptation, even though direct adaptation need not have a truth-apt candidate.

ARC wrote a bespoke explanation that no separate lifecycle record was possible for O12 because the harness fixed no candidate output. GBrain placed the global sentence under O15 as an individual object disposition. The reports reached compatible substantive conclusions, but the schema did not determine their form.

### Whole-system and material-route cutoff

“One row per operative part” has no cutoff when scope is a whole agentic system. GBrain explicitly chose “material automatic transformation and authority routes” and omitted routine import, indexing, retrieval, UI, helpers, and diagnostics. ARC inventoried more support and continuity plumbing, yielding a much longer route decomposition. Both choices are defensible, but the instruction provides no rule for choosing between them or for stating what an omitted support route prevents the reviewer from concluding.

Without a cutoff, completeness is not testable and analysis cost depends heavily on the executor's appetite for implementation detail.

## 5. Cost and report length

**Answer: the analytical depth is proportionate to these difficult cases, but the delivered report length is not proportionate for routine use.**

The ARC report is 428 lines and about 9,300 words. The GBrain report is 179 unusually dense lines and about 9,000 words. Their similar word counts despite very different line counts show that compact tables alone did not control cost.

Some cost is justified. Both systems are heterogeneous, and the reports needed enough detail to prevent warrant leakage. The excess comes from two sources:

- the missing material-route cutoff encouraged extensive treatment of support, admission, freshness, and continuity routes; and
- the same facts recur in the inventory, ledger, lifecycle dispositions, claim comparison, and bounded conclusion.

The cutoff is a blocking executability repair because it determines coverage and the truth conditions of “complete.” The other reductions below are optional compression: the current reports are cumbersome, but repetition did not corrupt their conclusions.

## 6. Exact repairs required before audit

### Blocking repairs

#### B1. Split route capability from candidate-instance state and add an indeterminate disposition

Replace the single undifferentiated phase-state rule with two explicitly sourced axes for every ampliative phase:

`phase | route availability: implemented / doctrine only / no route found within boundary / not determinable + evidence | observed candidate state: no instance observed / not reached / phase evidenced / accepted / rejected / revised / failed / suspended / integrated / not determinable + evidence`

Add these decision rules:

- Use `no route found within boundary` only for an evidenced implementation absence.
- Use `no instance observed` when a route is implemented or declared but no run artifact shows a candidate traversing it.
- Never use implementation evidence alone to mark an observed candidate accepted, rejected, revised, failed, or integrated.
- Preserve the evidence layer on both axes.

Add a third output-4 schema for a truth-apt object whose transformation is indeterminate:

`candidate object ID | relevant route IDs | transformation: indeterminate | classifications still possible | preserved lineage | implemented checks, retention, or use | current warrant limit | evidence needed to decide preservation, entailment, or ampliation`

Do not apply the discovery lifecycle until ampliation is established. This repair preserves the existing commitment to report uncertainty rather than adding a new evaluative category.

#### B2. Distinguish content transformations from non-content authority transitions

Change the ledger schema to:

`route id | route kind | object/candidate id | content transformation if any | transition or check target | evaluator/condition and domain | timing | possible or observed result | implemented force | epistemic authority and scope | operational authority: consumer, channel, force, horizon | evidence layer and source | claim IDs or route mismatch | gap/limit`

Require `route kind` to distinguish at least:

- content transformation;
- check or disposition;
- retention or integration;
- operational admission, selection, or consumption;
- direct behavior/policy adaptation; and
- claimed but absent or suspended transition.

Revise Step 4 to classify every **content-changing edge**, not every consequential route. For a route that only changes status, retention, admission, continuation, or later use, write `content transformation: not applicable` and still record its target, condition, force, authorities, consumer, and horizon. Sequential content transformation and later operational consumption remain separate rows when their timing or force differs.

#### B3. Make no-candidate handling explicitly per object and globally

Replace the current no-candidate sentence with both of these rules:

- For an individual inventory object with no candidate truth-apt output, write: `No lifecycle record for <object ID>: no candidate truth-apt output for this object; relevant direct-adaptation or update routes: <route IDs or none>.`
- Only when the entire inventory contains no candidate truth-apt output, additionally write: `No candidate lifecycle records: no candidate truth-apt output found within the source boundary.`

For direct behavior/policy adaptation, require the per-object line to name the update evaluator or signal, implemented force, consumer, scope, and evidence in either the route ledger or the same disposition. This resolves the current conflict between “record the update route” and “create records only for truth-apt candidates.”

#### B4. Define the material-route boundary for whole-system work

Add this boundary rule before the inventory:

> For a whole-system question, inventory every material route within the declared boundary. A route is material when it produces or changes truth-apt content; checks or disposes a candidate; grants, withholds, or changes epistemic or operational authority; retains or integrates a candidate for later reliance; directly adapts behavior or policy from evaluation; or is necessary to assess a consequential system knowledge/warrant claim. Include pure transport, storage, retrieval, formatting, freshness, and recovery plumbing only when it changes lineage or warrant, carries consequential force, or is part of such a claim. Name omitted route classes and the conclusions their omission prevents.

Change “one row per operative part” to “one row per operative part within the declared material-route boundary.” If exhaustive whole-system coverage remains infeasible, require named route families, explicit unassessed families, and a statement that no system-complete conclusion is licensed.

This rule would retain ARC's consequential grading, replay, planning, and continuation routes and GBrain's synthesis, grading, calibration, and SkillOpt routes while giving both agents the same basis for including or excluding support plumbing.

### Optional compression

- In the ledger, replace the free-text `system claim versus route` field with claim IDs and a short mismatch marker; keep the full comparison only in output 5.
- Let output 1 assign evidence-source IDs. Later blocks may cite those IDs plus a local anchor instead of restating the evidence layer and full path repeatedly.
- Require the bounded conclusion to synthesize only findings that change the answer to the analysis question. It may group homogeneous object or route IDs with identical warrant and force; it need not paraphrase every ledger row.
- Permit a lifecycle table with one row per candidate and compact phase cells after B1's two-axis distinction is explicit. Do not regain brevity by merging candidates or routes that have different targets, scopes, consumers, or force.
- State once that an unobserved implementation route is capability evidence, then use a stable source-boundary reference in later records. Preserve candidate-specific exceptions rather than repeating the global caveat in every field.

## Trial verdict

Trial verdict: PASS WITH REPAIRS

Blockers:

1. Output 4 needs a defined indeterminate-transformation disposition and separate implemented-route versus observed-candidate state.
2. The ledger needs a non-content route branch so admissions, status changes, retention, consumption, and policy adaptation are not forced into content-transformation classes.
3. No-candidate handling must distinguish individual objects from the whole source boundary and must cover direct adaptation consistently.
4. Whole-system execution needs a material-route cutoff and an explicit completeness boundary.
