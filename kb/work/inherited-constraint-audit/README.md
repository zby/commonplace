# Inherited-constraint claims audit

**Trigger (2026-08-12).** [A framework rule with a boundary-preserving rival is not an inherited constraint](../../notes/a-framework-rule-with-a-boundary-preserving-rival-is-not-inherited.md) was trimmed to the demotion direction it actually supports: the rival test demotes rules, it certifies nothing, and its former "worked candidates that resist demotion" canon was removed. The citers that used that note *as* the certifier were reworked in the same pass to carry their own warrant. This audit is the follow-up: the graph still contains first-person assertions that specific rules **are** inherited from boundary commitments. With the central note no longer even appearing to certify, each of those assertions stands on whatever warrant it carries itself. This workshop records which ones hold, which overclaim, and where the one genuinely missing derivation is.

**What closes this workshop.** Each item under "Overclaims" is either fixed in its home artifact or explicitly accepted with a reason; a decision is made on the "deepest gap" item (write the derivation, soften the claims, or accept them as near-definitional); the vocabulary observations are either acted on or dismissed. Conclusions worth keeping get extracted; then this directory is deleted.

**Method.** Swept `kb/notes/`, `kb/reference/`, and `kb/index.md` for `constraint packet`, `inherited constraint`, `consumer-inherited`, `is/are inherited`, `inherited from`, `first principle`, and `forced`, then read each claim site and asked: which boundary commitment is named, what warrant is offered in place, and does the claim respect the one-way discipline (a rival demotes; absence of a rival certifies nothing)?

## Overclaims — fixes recommended

1. **`kb/notes/programming-patterns-get-a-fast-pass-but-other-borrowed-ideas-must.md` (asymmetry paragraph).** "First principles are scarce — we have only a handful (finite context, no import mechanism, text-in text-out) — but each one is strong because it's derived from real constraints that **won't change**." Two problems. "Won't change" is exactly the certification the framework-rule note refuses: the framework's own doctrine (in [task-fitted structure costs cross-task reuse](../../notes/task-fitted-structure-costs-cross-task-reuse.md)) is that a constraint's guarantee ends when the commitment that forces it changes — a consumer with enforced scoping or an import mechanism is a live research direction, not an impossibility. And "we have only a handful" quietly re-installs a canon of certified constraints, the structure the trim just removed. Fix: "constraints that hold as long as the consumer commitment holds" (or similar), and drop the closed-list framing.

2. **`kb/reference/design-rationale-management.md` (Design constraint row).** Defines: "A first principle is a constraint inherited from a consumer, substrate, domain, or machinery commitment." This keeps the retired "first principle" vocabulary and defines it in the certifying direction, in the reference layer, one hop from the note that now refuses that reading. Fix: reword the row to "inherited constraint" and note that classification as inherited is a claim the rationale must warrant, not a label it may assume.

3. **`kb/notes/a-citation-cannot-assert-more-fidelity-than-its-capture-preserved.md` (capture-is-layered section).** "Any framework whose consumer is an LLM and whose sources are real documents faces all three." Legs 1–2 (finite context, copyright on real documents) are solid mechanism-level warrants. Leg 3 (provider output filters block mass verbatim reproduction) is a fact about current hosted providers, not derivable from "LLM consumer": a self-hosted open-weights consumer has no output filter, so "any … faces all three" is false as stated. The conclusion (capture stratifies) survives on legs 1–2 alone. Fix: scope leg 3 to hosted providers, or demote it to an aggravating factor.

4. **`kb/notes/technical-constraints-make-kb-objective-choice-engineering.md` (constraint packet).** The four-item "constraint packet" bundles unlike things. Items 1 and 4 (bounded context; the verification boundary) carry real derivation sketches — the second is near-analytic. Item 3 (fixed lexicon) is plausible but its independence is already contested by the note's own open question, which is honest. Item 2 (the codification option) is not a constraint at all — it is an affordance of the substrate and machinery — yet packet membership presents it as one, and "packet" language ("pick an LLM reader and its lexicon comes along whole") implies membership is settled rather than claimed. Fix: present item 2 as a lever the commitments make available rather than a constraint they impose, and let "packet" read as the note's own bundling, not an inherited unit.

## The deepest gap: the two claims that still assert universality

[A universal knowledge framework demotes content taxonomies to defaults and keeps answerability](../../notes/a-universal-knowledge-framework-demotes-content-taxonomies-to-defaults.md) keeps two positive universals: every collection must **declare its contract**, and every knowledge artifact must be **answerable**. These are now the strongest certification-shaped claims in the graph — the README label in [document-system-README](../../notes/document-system-README.md) states them flatly ("only the declaration obligation and answerability stay universal") — and nothing derives them from independently stated invariants. They are also the claims *closest* to being derivable: answerability looks like definitional unpacking of the domain commitment (an artifact that answers to nothing is not knowledge in any sense the framework could operate on), and the declaration obligation looks like unpacking machinery coherence (routing and validation cannot act on a collection whose contract resolves to nothing), with [ADR 017](../../reference/adr/017-collection-md-is-the-register-convention-boundary.md) recording the design freedom that remains (*where* the contract lives). Options, one to be chosen at closure:

1. Write the short positive-derivation note the framework-rule note says survivors owe — state the domain and machinery invariants independently, derive the two obligations, and let the universal note and the README label rest on it.
2. Soften both statements to "undemoted so far" and accept that the framework currently certifies nothing.
3. Accept them as definitional truths of the commitments (not empirical claims), and say so where they are asserted — cheaper than option 1, but the definitional status should then be explicit rather than implied.

Option 1 or 3 is recommended; option 2 undersells claims that are genuinely near-analytic.

## Vocabulary observations

- **Two senses of "inheritance" now coexist.** Constraint inherited from a boundary commitment (this audit's subject) vs. scope warrant inherited from a source theory ([only derivation and inheritance warrant a scope claim](../../notes/only-derivation-and-inheritance-warrant-a-scope-claim-use-earns-it.md), e.g. the reflective-system definition inheriting from Maes/Smith). The senses are related but not the same relation; a reader following "inheritance" links across the two clusters can conflate them. Worth a disambiguating sentence in one of the two homes if confusion is ever observed; no action otherwise.
- **"First principle" residue.** The term survives in note titles and prose about a *reasoning style* ([first-principles analysis](../../notes/first-principles-analysis-maps-design-space-before-selection.md), [first-principles reasoning](../../notes/first-principles-reasoning-selects-for-explanatory-reach-over.md), the borrowing filter) — those uses are fine. The uses that tie the term to certified-constraint status are items 1 and 2 under Overclaims; once those are fixed, "first principle" means a way of reasoning everywhere it appears, and "inherited constraint" means the (demotable, never certified) status.

## Disposition

Overclaims 1–4 fixed (2026-08-12, same branch), with maintainer framings: item 1 reworded as a working heuristic whose confidence rests on — and expires with — the commitments; item 2 resolved by shifting constraint-status vocabulary from "first principle" to "inherited constraint" in `design-rationale-management` and `first-principles-analysis` ("first principles" survives only as the name of a reasoning style); item 3 demoted the provider-filter leg to a provider-contingent barrier; item 4 reorganized `technical-constraints` into three constraints plus the codification lever and dropped "constraint packet" as a settled unit. The frozen replication packets under `kb/work/linking-contract-consistency/` retain the old text as experiment inputs — deliberately untouched.

Still open: the deepest-gap decision (derive, soften, or declare-definitional the declaration obligation and answerability universals) and the two vocabulary observations. Closure waits on those.
