answer secrecy. At the unchanged source revision, S's
`OpenMLE-Gym/openmle-sandbox/README.md:80–109` describes candidate execution,
`DATA_DIR` pointing to public data, and scoring against private answers on the

convention, not a security boundary. The shipped launcher
`OpenMLE-Gym/openmle-sandbox/node_workers/sandbox_builder/start_sandboxes.sh:150–154,177–181`

Read-only access prevents writes; it does not conceal `data/private/test_answer.csv`.

P's `runtime.md:32–39,148–170` discusses score authority, sandbox grants and
external split integrity. `synthesis.md:26,34,56,69` carries configuration,
isolation and causal-attribution caveats. Neither names candidate access to
private answers. A search of all published Markdown found no equivalent
qualification. The synthesis's attributed held-out contrasts remain attributed,
but readers still need this concrete source-level evaluation limit to assess
what the default executable scoring route can establish.
