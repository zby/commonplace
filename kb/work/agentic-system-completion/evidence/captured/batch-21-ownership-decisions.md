# Batch 21 ownership decisions: operator recommendations

## Decision and authority

**Keep all eight original runs held. Prefer bounded ownership reconciliation for
ACE and Agent Skills; propose separately authorized fresh replacement lineages
for the six filesystem incidents.** These are recommendations, not releases,
execution plans or integration approvals. A replacement lineage means a new,
separately identified production history and independently audited artifact. It
does not mean resetting the old run or declaring its actions harmless.

The purpose is to decide whether more retained evidence can answer the actual
ownership question, or whether new work should be considered without pretending
to settle history. The parent owns scope, scheduling and integration decisions.
Only this file is authored. No delegation, acquisition, installation, target
execution, worker-prompt/log inspection, owner/mailbox contact, reservation
release, cleanup, source/method/config/state mutation, reset, advance, judge,
integration or commit is authorized by this document or performed for it.

Basis: the complete [batch plan](./batch-21.md) and
[machine bindings](./batch-21.json), plus the retained findings linked below.
The machine record fixes exact source identities, old run/worktree paths,
attempt snapshots and publication maps. This is not a fresh semantic audit or
an expanded historical compliance audit. The one native attempt inspected was
ACE's open reconciliation metadata; no pinned prompt or worker trace was opened.

## What evidence can and cannot decide

A bounded read-only reconciliation can compare retained assignment/release
records, native attempt metadata and existing audit decisions. It can establish
what is recorded and identify contradictions. It cannot grant ownership,
terminate a worker, release a reservation or prove that no writer can return.
Process absence is not evidence of release. No current owner identity or release
is invented where the records do not establish one.

For the six filesystem incidents, retained audits already establish the actions
and their connection to the old publication. They do **not** retain a sufficient
pre-write image of the affected paths. Reading those reports again, checking
current absence, or producing correct new content cannot recover overwritten
bytes or prove prior ownership. Those facts are irrecoverable **from this retained
evidence**, not necessarily from every possible external backup. An already
retained, independently attributable pre-incident snapshot could change the
answer under a separately authorized, bounded evidence review. No such snapshot
is established here. Do not expand into worker prompts/traces to search for one.

**Two different decisions must stay separate:**

- **Judging the old publication:** preserve the established unauthorized acts,
  unresolved collateral effects and any historical validation/grounding limits.
  Semantic quality and exact-byte binding do not establish historical compliance.
  Unknown loss is not an acceptable premise for clearing the old hold. Nothing
  here recommends accepting it.
- **Creating a new artifact:** a separate commission could authorize a new owned,
  isolated workspace and independently audited production history. It would need
  an explicit source identity/boundary, supported method, authorized write scope,
  normal verification, exact-byte binding and a fresh independent audit covering
  content and operational scope. A future claim of historical compliance must
  remain limited to evidence actually audited. New content does not certify old
  behavior, release old owners or resolve the original incident.

For the six proposed replacements, prefer a fresh source-first lineage rather
than copying accepted old members and calling the result independently produced.
The retained reports remain diagnostic evidence for the parent, not analytical
answers to pass to fresh source-first authors. This costs more than narrow
re-verification, but gives a genuinely separate artifact. It is a request for a
future authority decision, not permission to prepare or execute that route now.
Any future workspace ownership must be established independently; the old
worktree and foreign paths remain untouched and held.

## Parent handoff and stop conditions

The actionable choice is between retaining these holds without new production
and separately commissioning the recommended reconciliation or replacement
route. Neither option grants mutation/execution authority here. Partial progress
is sufficient: an unresolved owner or unavailable pre-state record means the
old hold stays, not that investigation must expand until it manufactures closure.

Any future authorization would need to distinguish new artifact acceptance from
old-incident resolution, establish current ownership without process-absence
inference, and bind the intended evidence and write scope. Conflicting ownership,
new evidence of contamination, or a request to accept unknown collateral loss
returns to the parent for decision; none is silently settled here. The original
run, publication, audit and incident evidence remain preserved regardless of
whether a new artifact is eventually accepted.
