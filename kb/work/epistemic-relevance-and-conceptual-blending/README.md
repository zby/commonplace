# Epistemic relevance and conceptual blending

## Commission and current status

Commissioned by the Commonplace operator on 2026-10-31. The initial investigation asked why an LLM might turn two productive but conceptually distinct directions into one unsupported explanation. The operator then chose a practical next step: keep the possible failure on a checklist, correct the next few instances, and use those observations to develop a more substantial hypothesis.

**Status: observational watch, not an established systematic bias.** The targeted literature review found neighboring effects but did not establish the proposed utility-driven blending mechanism. That is a search result, not a claim that no confirming research exists. The immediate work is observation and correction during ordinary knowledge work, not another broad survey, a benchmark, or a training intervention.

This framing governs the current agenda. The retained research and experimental designs are background, not instructions to begin experiments or adopt their conclusions. No model trials or new source ingests have been performed.

## What to watch for

An agent retains two useful ideas but assigns them an unsupported relationship: for example, usefulness of both A and B to a broader goal becomes a reason to treat B as part of A's definition or explanation. Also watch for a correction that acknowledges their distinction while preserving the old organization in subsequent claims or decisions.

Mere topical overlap, a productive tangent, or synthesis is not the error. B can help answer a question about A as a contrast without being part of A. Shared benefits may justify a project-level grouping; stronger conceptual relationships need their own warrant. The hypothesis may need narrowing, splitting, or rejection as cases accumulate.

## Working loop

Use the [watch checklist](./watch-checklist.md) when a suspected instance arises in an ordinary investigation, synthesis, or review. Record it in the [observation log](./observations.md), with the pre-correction evidence when available. Correct an established local error within the current task's authority, preserve a valuable tangent without forcing it into the main explanation, and check whether consequential uses actually change. Do not wait for a general mechanism to be proved before correcting a demonstrably unsupported claim.

The checklist is local to this workshop. The Active Workshops entry makes it discoverable; it is not automatically injected into every agent call. This change installs no scheduler, recurring alert, global review gate, or new machinery. The operator or an agent already working with this workshop supplies the check during ordinary work.

A brief record is enough. Preserve checked false alarms and justified syntheses as well as errors. Revisions in one conversation are one episode, not independent recurrences. Record unknowns rather than reconstructing an unavailable trace.

## Review point and closure

Review after the next **three independently arising suspected episodes**, or earlier when a counterexample or well-captured case materially changes the explanation. Three is a practical interpretation of "the next few," not an evidential threshold. Count checked non-errors too; do not wait only for confirmations.

At review, compare the actual relations, why each secondary idea was useful, what made the original relation unwarranted, and whether correction changed later behavior. Distinguish task ambiguity, association, candidate selection, presentation-only misplacement, and persistence of an earlier frame. Reformulate the hypothesis around the common observations, or split/reject it when they do not share a mechanism. Counts from this selectively noticed case series cannot establish prevalence or training causation.

The review should produce a more specific conjecture with scope, counterexamples, competing explanations, and a discriminating next observation or test. It may instead conclude that existing concepts suffice. Further experiments require separate authorization; a return date is not needed for this event-driven checklist.

Close when the watch has produced a justified disposition: a supported extraction into the appropriate collection, a documented rejection, or an explicit operator decision to defer or stop it. Use the [workshop lifecycle](../COLLECTION.md) when closing: extract anything worth retaining, remove the active entry, and retire the temporary workshop. Do not promote a general LLM-bias theory merely because a few similar cases were noticed.
