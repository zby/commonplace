# Experiment design for theory-mediated deployment-time learning

> **Status:** This is a proposed comparison, not a completed experiment or implementation plan. It does not establish that explicit theories improve search or selection, that retained theories outperform reconstruction, that selective evaluation or generated procedures reduce total cost, or that Exo's available self-revision surfaces produce beneficial or compounding improvements.

The [theory-mediated improvement model](./theory-mediated-improvement-loop.md) assigns an explicit system theory several possible roles: diagnosing a problem, searching for candidate changes, selecting among candidates, deriving their consequences, choosing evidence, and interpreting results. A credible test should isolate these roles before combining them. It should also separate a theory constructed on the spot from one retained and revised across episodes, and keep SPADE-inspired procedure generation as another factor. HCL supplies the governance pattern for proposal, evaluation, and commitment; SPADE motivates the procedure-generation factor; Exo supplies a concrete end-to-end target with a mutable substrate and a later-improvement test. Every arm needs an independent audit surface and full cost accounting so that producing fewer candidates or running fewer evaluations is not mistaken for performing better.

## Make the working theory observable

The first practical treatment asks an LLM to construct a working theory `tau_n` from the current system, objective, and evidence inside the run. The artifact should state its proposed mechanism or invariant, premises, scope, intended-effect predictions, collateral-effect predictions, uncertainty, and a possible falsifier. It must be recorded before the decision it is claimed to guide.

For a candidate-search claim, freeze `tau_n` before candidate generation. For a post-evidence candidate-decision claim, freeze it before the candidates and their matched evidence are ranked. For an evidence-selection claim, freeze its candidate-specific projection before outcomes are revealed. A rationale produced after the candidate or verdict is diagnostic text, not evidence that the theory mediated that decision.

The theory-construction call, tokens, latency, and review cost belong in the treatment cost. Withheld, shuffled, premise-modified, and rival working theories can test whether changing a load-bearing part changes search or selection in the predicted direction. Without such interventions, an explicit theory may be unused narration beside the actual computation.

## Candidate-search and candidate-selection comparisons

The search comparison should hold the task evidence, base model, inference budget, candidate budget, and full independent evaluator fixed. Initial arms should include direct candidate proposal with no explicit theory, proposal after constructing a fresh `tau_n`, and proposal after instantiating `tau_n` from a frozen retained `T_n`. A supplied or oracle theory can serve as an upper-bound diagnostic where the task family makes one available. Theory revision belongs in the later lifecycle comparison rather than this search-isolation phase.

Evaluator-only anchors and their outcomes should remain hidden until candidate generation and pre-evidence prioritization are frozen; exposing them is a separate treatment. Every candidate should then receive the same full evaluation so that theory-guided evidence selection cannot explain a search result. Measure viable-candidate hit rate, best candidate within budget, candidates and model calls to the first admissible improvement, intervention-locus accuracy, proposal diversity, current gain, full-suite regressions, and total search cost. Evaluation cannot rescue a good intervention that the search policy never proposes.

Candidate choice is a separate comparison. Give every arm the same fixed candidate pool and the same fixed evidence, then compare ranking, rejection, and commitment with and without the working theory. The theory may organize how observed results and unresolved consequences bear on each candidate, but its projections do not count as additional observations. Hidden full outcomes can score candidate-selection regret and harmful adoption. This isolates using a theory to choose among known changes from using it to generate those changes or choose their tests.

## Evidence-selection comparison

The evidence-selection phase tests the [provisional selective-evaluation model](./selective-evaluation-model.md) on a fixed recorded candidate set with a fixed commitment rule. Every arm should see the same candidate changes and have matched access to the obligation registry, procedure metadata, and available cost estimates. Baselines should include full registered-set evaluation, budget-matched uniform sampling, and diff-similarity selection. Theory-guided conditions should include deterministic dependency-and-authority selection, explicit causal-model selection, and a mixed-form explanatory-theory selector allowed to use mechanisms, invariants, program structure, semantic contracts, and learned predictions. The explicit causal condition is best treated as an ablation of the mixed theory when possible. If selectors receive different artifacts or structural information, that difference should be recorded explicitly as part of the treatment rather than hidden inside the method name.

Every theory-guided arm should record its candidate-specific `I_tau(S, Omega, Delta)` before evaluation: the affected and unaffected obligations it predicts, unresolved effects, confidence or proof status, the scope of the claim, and the load-bearing premises used to derive it. A shared evidence-acquisition policy should consume those projections where the comparison aims to isolate projection quality. If a route requires its own policy, the result is an end-to-end selector comparison and should not be attributed to theory quality alone.

The full registered set is a baseline, not complete ground truth. Before selector outputs are revealed, the study should build an independent reference surface from seeded known effects and held-out human-audited obligations. During scoring, it should also continue random audits of obligations that each selector would omit. A **harmful miss** is a material effect identified by this reference process that an arm fails to surface before its commitment decision. Discoveries outside the reference surface reveal limits in that surface and should inform later audits, but they do not retroactively turn the initial labels into a perfect oracle.

Coverage needs two separate measures. **Procedure availability coverage** asks whether the registry or generator can supply a procedure capable of observing a reference obligation. **End-to-end detection coverage** asks whether the selected or generated procedure actually detects the seeded or audited effect. Probabilistic projections should be calibrated against independently audited effects; deductive exclusions should be scored for soundness and coverage. Together, these measures distinguish theory error, impact-derivation error, obligation-mapping failure, missing procedures, invalid observations, and evidence-selection failure.

Calibration and familiar-change accuracy do not establish explanatory-reach. The offline set should therefore hold out entire change families, include paired candidates that vary one stated load-bearing premise, and contain rival theories that fit the observed candidates but disagree on held-out changes. A theory shows the relevant explanatory-reach only if the impact projection transfers when its named mechanism or invariant is preserved and changes in the predicted direction when a load-bearing premise breaks. Success confined to familiar diffs remains useful prediction, but it is not evidence for the workshop's explanatory claim.

Cost accounting should include model calls, tokens, latency, dollars, environment actions, retries, construction, technical and observational validation, sandboxing, execution, human adjudication, maintenance, and reuse. The protocol should predeclare its primary endpoints and advance rule. An arm supports an efficiency claim only if it reduces total cost while staying within prespecified harmful-miss and route-appropriate calibration or soundness bounds. Otherwise, the result is a measured tradeoff, not a success.

This comparison should keep the procedure registry fixed so that it isolates projection and selector quality. A later comparison can cross qualifying selectors with procedure-source conditions: selection from the fixed registry, non-adaptive synthesis, and adaptive retrieval, adaptation, or generation. This ordering prevents failures in generated procedures from being attributed automatically to the theory or selector.

## Theory lifecycle and joint online comparison

The isolated phases establish only local contrasts. The stronger cumulative theory-mediated-learning claim concerns what later episodes gain from earlier theory work. A repeated stream should therefore compare raw episodic history consumed directly with no explicit theory, a fresh on-the-spot `tau_n` reconstructed from that history each episode, `tau_n` instantiated from a frozen retained `T_n`, `tau_n` instantiated from an append-only retained theory, and `tau_n` instantiated from a retained theory whose premises and scope can be revised through a separate gate. A current-episode-only direct arm can provide an additional lower-information baseline, but should not be conflated with the matched-history comparison.

Give the matched-history arms the same underlying source observations and authorized evaluator outcomes. Hold the base model and inference budget fixed where possible, and charge each arm for raw-history context, theory construction, retrieval, and revision. Otherwise the comparison would confound theory form and persistence with access to more history or a cheaper compression of it.

The task stream should contain structured shifts: surface changes preserving the named mechanism, changes invalidating one applicability condition, changes invalidating the theory, and deceptive correlations that fit the source episodes. Measure theory construction, storage, retrieval, applicability checking, revision, and correction costs alongside later search quality, candidate quality, harmful misses, evaluation cost, rescoping after a premise break, stale-theory negative transfer, and held-out retention. The retained arm supports a compounding claim only if later episodes actually retrieve and consume the earlier theory and the benefit survives those costs.

Harness and theory decisions need separate gates. A harness candidate that passes empirical checks can still rest on a false explanation, and a failed candidate can still supply a useful counterexample to the current theory. Where feasible, lifecycle conditions should receive the same audit outcomes through replay or common checkpoints so that theory revision is not credited for evidence only one trajectory happened to observe.

After the isolated phases qualify, a role ablation can give the same theory to candidate search only, candidate choice only, evidence selection only, or all three. The all-three object-level treatment is especially exposed to confirmation: one theory can propose what it expects to work, choose evidence inside the same expected region, and then judge itself on that evidence. Independent full audits, sentinels, rival-theory probes, and separation between generator and evaluator roles remain load-bearing.

Different commitment decisions will still change later harness states, operator responses, and candidate opportunities. A final online comparison should start repeated runs from common checkpoints, standardize inputs where possible, and record every uncontrolled branch. It cannot substitute for the isolated comparisons, and the isolated results alone cannot establish deployment value.

## Exo-shaped end-to-end and compounding comparison

[Exo](./exo-case.md) provides a concrete target after the isolated search, choice, evidence, and lifecycle phases qualify. Its protected substrate, mutable executor, event history, build and test path, restart and rollback path, managed tools, memory, skills, prompts, and code create places where accepted changes can become operative and remain available. These are [source-pinned implementation facts](./exo-evidence.md), not evidence that a live Exo instance has made a beneficial self-change, that explicit theory caused one, or that one improvement has helped produce another.

The relevant baseline is therefore Exo with the same model, task episodes, free-form memory, skills, tools, prompts, tests, and freedom to modify symbolic artifacts. Initial treatments can add an explicit on-the-spot object theory, a retained object theory, or an explicit promotion and revision theory. The last treatment is meta-level: it governs which observations become episodes, claims, skills, tests, tools, prompts, or code; what authority they receive; how they are activated; and when they are revised or retired. It should not be credited with the object theory's impact predictions, and the object theory should not be credited with the promotion policy's retention decisions. Cross the treatments only after their separate effects are measurable.

The experimental unit for compounding is at least a pair of improvement episodes. The first must produce a retained benefit. The second must pose a new improvement problem for which that benefit could alter diagnosis, candidate search, evaluation, installation, retention, or resource allocation. Measure the later episode's total cost, candidate quality, harmful adoption, breadth, time to an admissible improvement, human decisions, and theory correction. Record whether the earlier artifact was retrieved and used, or whether saved resources were deliberately reinvested in later improvement work. The accepting metric for the first change cannot establish this dependency.

This yields three nested conclusions rather than one:

1. **Deployment-time learning:** task evidence produced an operative retained change that later behavior used.
2. **Accumulation:** useful retained changes built a stock used by later operation.
3. **Compounding:** an earlier retained benefit measurably improved the production of a later improvement through a traced dependency.

A successful first episode can establish the first claim and contribute to the second while leaving the third false or untested. Likewise, a retained theory can improve the current episode without supporting compounding. The stronger result requires repeated later-episode dependencies, not merely the existence of an addressable theory or mutable substrate.

Within each episode, the objective and comparison rule must remain fixed independently of the candidate. A proposed evaluator, promotion-policy, or theory-gate change belongs in a separately authorized episode; otherwise the candidate can move the standard that judges it. Use common checkpoints or replay where possible, preserve failed attempts outside rewound mutable state, and compare absent, frozen, simpler-memory, and revisable-theory conditions after counting formation, retrieval, context, review, maintenance, staleness, and repair costs.

## Adaptive procedure generation is a separate factor

[SPADE](../../sources/spade-self-play-in-adaptive-synthetic-executable-environments.ingest.md) generates executable Python environments and trains the same model in Environment Designer and Reasoning Agent roles. Its released code supports environment generation, smoke-test validation and, for tool use, feasibility screening, together with a learning-value signal. Its reported training and benchmark gains, however, remain paper-only and unreproduced in this KB.

SPADE matters here as an architectural example, not as evidence for an explanatory theory, its impact projection, or a selector. Its current shared model generates environments on demand from corpus and environment-memory context; learner outcomes affect later generations through updates to the shared weights and memory. The paper does not expose a theory of the learner that guides those proposals. SPADE motivates our proposal that an evidence-acquisition policy might retrieve, adapt, or synthesize a procedure instead of mapping every obligation to a fixed registered evaluation. It does not show that a working theory has explanatory-reach, that its projection correctly identifies affected functions, that a generated procedure observes the intended obligation, or that generation protects a separately versioned harness against regression.

The generation condition therefore needs its own checks. **Technical validity** asks whether the procedure executes, remains contained, and satisfies its structural constraints. **Observational validity** asks whether its success criterion detects the intended obligation against the independent reference surface. **Decision value** asks whether constructing and running the procedure is worth its cost for the current commitment. A separate omitted-region audit should check effects that neither the selector nor the generated procedure chose to expose.

Generation may improve detection, increase cost, or both. The inspected SPADE loader executes generated Python, while feasibility and solvability checks do not by themselves establish containment or observational validity. Construction, validation, sandboxing, execution, and reuse should therefore be measured parts of the generation factor, not presumed savings.
