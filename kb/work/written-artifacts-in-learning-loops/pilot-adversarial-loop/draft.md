# Recorded composition checks make attempted epistemic work auditable but do not establish its effects

For substantive KB claims whose commitment or support is still unsettled, a human-agent workflow can make three candidate operations explicit and auditable: **commit-and-expose**, **challenge-and-locate**, and **respond-and-decide**. Giving each operation a defined input and a task-specific trace creates a testable allocation of work. It does not show that any stage performed its operation well, that the allocation reproduces an epistemic effect of composition, or that it improves on solo writing. Naive prose delegation instead treats fluent rendering and ordinary approval as if the claim and its support had undergone these checks. This boundary does not apply to settled rendering, transcription, or stylistic editing, where the relevant commitments and checks have already occurred elsewhere.

These three operations are a compact synthesis of practitioner accounts, not a canonical or complete taxonomy of composition. They remain candidates because neither the taxonomy nor its relocation has been validated. **Commit-and-expose** turns a fluid view into a definite central claim whose constraints, premises, and inferential route can fail. **Challenge-and-locate** tests those actual commitments and that route for counterexamples, incompatibility, and missing support, then identifies the load-bearing issue. **Respond-and-decide** directs inquiry at that issue and records a supported rebuttal, revision, qualification, narrowing, switch, rejection, or explicit uncertainty before acceptance. [Putting ideas into words](../sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md), [thinking through conjecture and counterexample](../sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md), and [learning by iterated writing](../sources/learning-by-writing.md) motivate these bundles through reported practice, not controlled comparisons. Reader simulation and fresh criticism are possible methods; idea generation, stabilization, and changed understanding are adjacent effects or outcomes.

A minimum auditable loop records the pre-acceptance claim, constraints, premises, and route. A separately run checker receives that exact object and returns premise-linked objections, counterexamples, missing support, and explicit uncertainty rather than a reassuring global grade. An acceptor then maps every load-bearing finding to a substantive disposition: rebut with support, investigate, revise, narrow, switch, reject, or retain an unresolved marker. Preserving the input, report, and disposition makes both attempted work and handoffs inspectable. It does not establish that the checker found a real fault or that the disposition was apt. Nor does the solo-writer analogy assign the actors: checker and acceptor are distinct roles, and either may be a human, agent, or policy when that allocation has its own warrant.

Performance evidence must validate these stages separately. Critics can be tested on known supported and unsupported prose arguments, including true conclusions reached through invalid routes, because [producing a sound route is not the same as evaluating the route presented](./reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md). For a targeted error class, the check must discriminate rather than merely agree: its true-positive rate must exceed its false-positive rate, and errors across nominally separate checks must be measured for correlation. Accurate findings must also cause apt dispositions, not only edits or acknowledgements. A fresh runner, different prompt, or report shape is a decorrelation intervention, not proof of independence. The supplied evidence provides neither calibrated prose critics nor a before-and-after case showing such performance.

Authorship and approval are poor proxies for those tests. Fluent generation may conceal an unmet constraint, but the proposed mechanism by which [LLM generation relaxes goals where human writing stalls](./llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md) remains conjectural. Reviewing finished prose may also anchor the reviewer and invite passive assent, as a [practitioner account of substantive AI writing](../sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md) argues. Yet [human writing can likewise stabilize credible nonsense or false precision](../sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md), so a categorical human-machine contrast is unsupported. Human approval can establish authority to admit an artifact; it shows neither that the approver checked the artifact's route nor that the human re-derived its load-bearing commitments. What matters is tested checking and substantive disposition, not who typed or nodded at the prose.

The claimed outcome must therefore be named before saying that a loop preserves, reconstructs, or recovers composition's effects. An **artifact outcome** requires independently adjudicated corrections, better-supported boundaries, or appropriate rejection. A **human-understanding outcome** requires the human to restate or update the implicated claim, constraints, route, or uncertainty; assent to improved wording is insufficient. A **later-system outcome** requires relevant retrieval and changed use or decision, not merely persistence. **Acceptance** records admission authority and proves none of the other three. No supplied source compares a disciplined human-agent loop with solo composition or naive delegation on any of these outcomes. Until a comparator and one measured outcome are specified, the defensible claim is only that the workflow makes attempted operations auditable and is designed to test their allocation.

## Open Questions

- Can blinded prose critics distinguish invalid routes from valid arguments with useful discrimination and sufficiently uncorrelated errors?
- Does recording commitments before showing generated prose reduce anchoring in the acceptor?
- Which measured outcome, if any, reaches a solo-composition baseline under a distributed allocation?
