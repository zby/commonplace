# Reconstruction: epistemic functions in a human-agent writing loop

## Governing question and evidence posture

**Question:** Can a human-agent writing loop preserve the epistemic functions of composition that naive prose delegation bypasses? If so, which functions and conditions are supported, and where does the claim remain conjectural?

The supplied material does not contain a controlled comparison of solo composition, naive delegation, and a disciplined human-agent loop. It therefore cannot establish functional equivalence or comparative superiority. It does contain:

- practitioner accounts of what writing does for a human author;
- local theories of generation and verification, some explicitly conjectural;
- operational procedures that instantiate candidate checking operations but do not validate their efficacy; and
- collection/type contracts that govern the eventual note but are not evidence for its factual claims.

The experiment's purpose and retained commission come from `kb/work/written-artifacts-in-learning-loops/pilot-adversarial-loop/brief.md`. That brief is authoritative about scope and workflow, not about whether the proposed loop works.

## Working definitions and distinctions

| Item | Reconstruction | Support and evidential status |
|---|---|---|
| **Naive prose delegation** | Supplying an agent with an idea, outline, or notes and treating its fluent rendering plus ordinary review as if the underlying commitments had already been worked through. The relevant omission is not keystroking by a human; it is failure to perform or independently reconstruct the epistemic operations below. | The contrast between rendering and concretizing is explicit in `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md`; passive assent after generated prose is described in `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md`. The first is a mechanistic hypothesis; the second is practitioner/conceptual evidence. |
| **Composition's epistemic functions** | Operations by which an initially vague or premature view becomes a definite, criticizable artifact; its assumptions and inferential route are exposed; weaknesses route further inquiry; and the view is revised, narrowed, switched, or rejected. Stabilizing a result for later reuse is an additional function, distinct from showing that it is true. | The operation sequence is distributed across `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`, `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`, `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`, and `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md`. These are practitioner/conceptual accounts, not demonstrations that every act of writing performs every operation. |
| **Writing-is-thinking filter** | A proposed operational rather than slogan-level definition: **(1)** commit a view to one sufficiently definite claim; **(2)** make its constraints, premises, and inferential route explicit; **(3)** test that route from outside the originating intention, including counterexamples and missing support; **(4)** localize failures; and **(5)** revise, narrow, switch, or reject the claim before acceptance. Inquiry routing and stabilization can result from this cycle, but are separable from the filter's accept/reject function. | Commitment, neutral-reader testing, correction, and discarding: `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`. Definite conjectures, premise expansion, and local/global counterexamples: `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`. Iterated hypothesis/weakness/research/revision: `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`. The local Filter/Signal decomposition: `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`; this is a procedure, not efficacy evidence. **INFERENCE:** the five-operation definition synthesizes these sources; no source gives this exact definition. |
| **Rendering versus epistemic work** | Rendering chooses words for an already settled structure. Epistemic composition searches for a tenable commitment and may discover that no current formulation satisfies the constraints. Delegating rendering need not lose the filter if the commitment and checks occurred elsewhere; delegating the search and then accepting fluency can lose it. | `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md` explicitly limits its mechanism to composition as discovery and exempts settled restatement. This boundary is part of that note's theoretical hypothesis, not comparative evidence. |
| **Inspectability versus effective criticism** | A readable draft can be inspected, diffed, challenged, and revised. That makes criticism possible. It does not establish that a reviewer is independent, notices the relevant error, traces the actual reasoning, or causes correction. | Inspectability as a property of readable artifacts: `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md`. Verification as a separate axis: `kb/notes/vibe-noting.md` and `kb/notes/the-boundary-of-automation-is-the-boundary-of-verification.md`. |
| **Epistemic checking versus acceptance authority** | A checker tries to discriminate supported from unsupported content. Acceptance authority decides whether a KB mutation is admitted. The roles may be held by different humans, agents, or policies. Human analogy alone does not require a human to perform every epistemic check. | Allocation must be justified separately from the function in `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md`. Agent inspection is possible in `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md`. Human attestation records authority over the current contents but says nothing by itself about truth or review history in `kb/types/note.md`. |
| **Human judgment present versus human re-derivation** | A human seeing or approving a draft establishes neither that the human reconstructed its constraints nor that the human checked its reasoning. Re-derivation means the human can restate the commitments, trace the load-bearing route, and decide what follows from identified weaknesses. Passive agreement with fluent text is a distinct, weaker event. | Passive assent and sensitivity to approximate wording: `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md`. The need to re-derive hidden constraints: `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md`. Route inspection rather than answer agreement: `kb/notes/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md`. |

## Material functions attributed to composition

These functions are separable. The sources do not show that one prose pass or one critic reconstructs all of them.

| Function | Mechanism described in the inputs | Specific support and limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Commitment / concretization** | Turning a diffuse view into definite words removes the ability to preserve several incompatible meanings or goals at once. A single formulation makes omissions and trade-offs available for inspection. | `kb/sources/borretti-human-routers-of-machine-words.md`, `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`, and `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`. All are practitioner/conceptual arguments. `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md` formalizes this as a search for a witness satisfying a conjunction of constraints, but labels the proposed human/LLM mechanism conjectural. |
| **Error exposure and localization** | A failed transition, unsupported “because,” contradictory premise, or missing qualification can halt or disturb composition at the point where understanding runs out. The artifact turns an unlocalized sense of understanding into an inspectable fault. | Stalls and defective connectives: `kb/sources/borretti-human-routers-of-machine-words.md`. Neutral-reader detection of incorrectness and incompleteness: `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`. Cracks, immediate corrections, and felt-but-not-yet-articulated problems: `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`. These are reported experiences, not prevalence estimates. |
| **Premise and route expansion** | Explaining why a conjecture might hold exposes additional premises and inferential joints. More surface area gives criticism specific targets and can reveal that an explanation fails even if the conclusion survives. | `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md` distinguishes expanding an explanation, local counterexamples that damage a premise/explanation, and global counterexamples that undermine the conclusion. `kb/notes/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md` requires reviewers to trace the artifact's actual premises and route. The latter transfers a documented math-evaluation failure pattern to prose review as a local theoretical implication. |
| **Reader simulation** | The writer attempts to read only what is on the page, without importing unexpressed intention, and iterates until the artifact seems correct and complete to that simulated stranger. | `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`. This is one author's described method; it does not show that self-review is reliably independent. |
| **Adversarial criticism** | A critic seeks the strongest countercase, probes assumptions, and distinguishes flaws in the explanation from counterexamples to the whole claim. | `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md` and `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md` give practitioner loops. `kb/instructions/critique-note.md` and `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md` instantiate fresh-runner, report-only procedures. The instructions show implementability, not effectiveness. |
| **Inquiry routing** | Writing a current hypothesis makes weaknesses explicit, allowing reading and discussion to focus on subquestions most likely to change the bottom-line view instead of accumulating undirected information. | `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md` gives a detailed first-person process and worked case. It is strong process description but still one practitioner's testimony, without a comparison group. |
| **Revision and rejection** | The loop changes wording only when that repairs the underlying commitment; otherwise it narrows the claim, changes premises, switches sides, or discards the idea. | Discarded broken ideas: `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`. Revised or reversed hypotheses: `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`. Local/global counterexample responses: `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`. |
| **Idea generation and changed human understanding** | New claims can arise during formulation, and repeated criticism/revision can change what the writer believes rather than merely improve expression. | `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`, `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`, and `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`. These sources report the authors' experiences; they do not establish that agent-produced drafts followed by review produce the same change in a human. |
| **Stabilization / relay results** | A written result can offload a settled step from working memory and serve as a building block for later reasoning or a later session. This is continuity, not certification. | `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md` describes written “relay results.” `kb/notes/vibe-noting.md` argues that maintained, routed artifacts can preserve explicit reasoning state across stateless sessions, while expressly separating inspectability from verification and contextual activation. |
| **Preserving unresolved uncertainty** | Composition can also fail by forcing premature precision: plausible details fill gaps and then look settled because they appear beside harder-won content. A sound loop must permit an accurately vague or explicitly unresolved result. | `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md`. The claim is a practitioner synthesis with historical reports; its neuroscience discussion is explicitly speculative and is not needed here. |

## Failure modes of delegated prose and review

1. **Silent constraint relaxation.** The local hypothesis is that an LLM produces the most plausible artifact available even when one requested constraint is not met, yielding a fluent witness to a weaker problem without marking the dropped constraint. Source: `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md`. **Evidential limit:** the note explicitly labels the argmax/relaxation mechanism conjectural. The supplied sources do not measure how often this happens or compare it with human papering-over.

2. **Vague or subtly wrong wording can look finished.** One practitioner reports generated prose dense with locally plausible but underspecified or misleading choices, including when substantial context is supplied. Source: `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md`. **Evidential limit:** the detailed example and meeting-summary report are informative cases, not controlled estimates of current-model error rates.

3. **Anchoring and passive assent.** Once a fluent wording exists, a reviewer may allow an approximate phrase to stand rather than regenerate the wording from domain sensitivities and commitments. Mere endorsement therefore does not show that the reviewer performed the omitted work. Source: `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md`. This is conceptual/practitioner evidence, including a quoted expert argument, not a direct experiment in the supplied snapshot.

4. **Insufficient evaluator knowledge.** A reviewer without foundational knowledge may not know what to ask, understand the question, or judge the answer. Source: `kb/sources/fernando-borretti-human-bottlenecks.md`. **Evidential limit:** this is a summarized conceptual essay, not a calibrated threshold for reviewer competence.

5. **Conclusion-agreement substitution.** A critic can reconstruct a good route to a plausible conclusion and overlook whether the artifact's own route is valid. Source: `kb/notes/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md`. Its concrete grounding is a mathematical-solution evaluation result; applying the mechanism to prose-note review is a reasoned transfer, not supplied prose-review evidence.

6. **Correlated criticism.** The same model, framing, evidence ordering, or content bias can cause generator and critics to share an error. Repeating the same check may converge on that bias. Source: `kb/notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md`. The abstract amplification condition is stronger than the empirical case for any particular prose critic.

7. **Inspectable but unchecked output.** Readability permits review but cannot make review happen or make its verdict sound. Sources: `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md`, `kb/notes/vibe-noting.md`, and `kb/notes/the-boundary-of-automation-is-the-boundary-of-verification.md`.

8. **False precision by the human writer too.** Human composition is not an automatic honest-stall oracle. A human can autocomplete credible nonsense or stabilize an uncertainty too soon. Source: `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md`. This directly limits any contrast that treats human writing as reliably self-verifying.

## Conflicts and differences in evidential strength

### Conflicts or productive tensions

1. **“Writing is thinking” versus productive wordless thought.** `kb/sources/borretti-human-routers-of-machine-words.md` makes the strongest identity claim: there is no ideating before writing. `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md` argues that words are necessary for fully formed nontrivial ideas, while admitting formal-domain and internal-verbal cases. `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md` presents expert mathematical wordless search as productive, fast, and broad, while retaining writing as a validity check and stabilizer. The common residue is narrower than identity: writing performs precision, validation, and stabilization functions that wordless discovery does not reliably perform by itself.

2. **Human stalls versus human false fluency.** `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md` treats the human stall as an honest default and LLM fluency as camouflage, but labels its mechanism conjectural. `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md` reports that human prose can also autocomplete credible nonsense and force false precision. `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md` claims AI errors are more convincing and harder to notice than human errors, but provides cases rather than a comparison. A categorical human/machine contrast is not supported by the supplied evidence.

3. **Close human review as a possible exception versus passive assent.** `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md` allows that extremely close vetting and heavy editing might avoid the problem, while arguing that review is vulnerable to anchoring and may cost as much as writing. This supports a conditional possibility, not a demonstrated recovery method.

4. **No human required for inspectability versus human authority in the proposed procedure.** `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md` says a human is not required merely to make a readable artifact inspectable. `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md` keeps role allocation open. `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md` deliberately routes attention to a human judge, and `kb/types/note.md` defines a human-attestation field. These are not logically inconsistent: inspection capability, epistemic evaluation, and authority are different roles. They do prevent an inference that the human analogy fixes all component boundaries.

5. **Weak oracles can be amplified versus prose verification remains hard.** `kb/notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md` gives conditions under which weak checks can be amplified. `kb/notes/the-boundary-of-automation-is-the-boundary-of-verification.md` argues that judgment-heavy KB work resists automation because such oracles are expensive and may be gamed. The former is a conditional theorem/design principle; the latter says the condition is not known to be cheaply satisfied here.

## Explicit inferences and current claim envelope

1. **INFERENCE — functional relocation is architecturally possible in a weak sense.** Because commitment, route expansion, criticism, revision, authority, and stabilization are distinguishable operations, they need not all occur inside one person's act of typing. The allocation principle in `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md` supports reopening the boundaries, while `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md` and `kb/instructions/critique-note.md` witness concrete allocations. This establishes a design possibility, not successful preservation.

2. **INFERENCE — the weakest presently defensible recovery claim is partial and conditional.** A distributed loop can instantiate counterparts of some composition functions and may recover some detectable errors if its checks are discriminatory, sufficiently decorrelated, route-focused, and allowed to change or reject the claim. The inputs do not warrant calling this functional equivalence, full reconstruction, improved human understanding, or better performance than solo writing.

3. **INFERENCE — mere human presence is not load-bearing; substantive adjudication may be.** Nothing supplied shows that a human must conduct every check. If the claimed outcome is human understanding, however, the human must do more than assent: the human must reconstruct and update at least the load-bearing commitments implicated by the criticism. If the claimed outcome is only artifact quality, a different allocation might suffice, subject to verifier evidence.

4. **INFERENCE — a single generic critique is unlikely to cover the whole filter.** The functions include commitment, counterexample search, route validation, inquiry routing, revision, and stabilization. The sources give no reason to treat one check as complete coverage, and the decorrelation account warns against correlated repetitions.

5. **INFERENCE — preservation is a process property, not an authorship property.** Human-authored prose can preserve false precision, and agent-authored prose can remain inspectable. The relevant question is whether the process exposes commitments to effective, independent checks and closes the loop through correction or rejection. This inference reconciles `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md` with `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md` without asserting that the candidate process succeeds.

## Unresolved questions

- **DEFINE — “reconstruct” or “preserve.”** Does success require functional equivalence to solo composition, recovery of a defined subset of operations, or detection of at least some errors naive delegation misses? The supplied evidence supports only the latter two as live hypotheses.
- **DEFINE — filter boundary.** Are inquiry routing, reader simulation, idea generation, and stabilization parts of the “writing-is-thinking filter,” or adjacent benefits of the wider composition loop? The operational core above treats accept/reject-relevant operations as the filter and the rest as separable functions, but this is a synthesis choice.
- **DECISION NEEDED — final claim strength.** The eventual note must choose between an existential design claim (“a distributed process can instantiate these operations”) and an empirical preservation claim. The latter currently lacks comparative evidence.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — comparative outcome.** Run blind or otherwise controlled comparisons among solo composition, naive agent drafting plus ordinary review, and the proposed adversarial loop on matched substantive tasks. Measure error discovery, claim narrowing/rejection, retained uncertainties, and time/cost. No supplied source does this.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — prose-oracle calibration.** Estimate TPR and FPR for each critic on known supported/unsupported argument pairs, including true conclusions with invalid reasoning. Measure error correlations across fresh contexts, prompts, models, evidence orderings, and adversarial framings.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — anchoring.** Compare review of generated prose with review from claims/evidence reconstructed before seeing the prose. Determine whether fresh-agent criticism and routed issues reduce passive assent in the human acceptor.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — coverage.** Determine which operations each stage actually performs. In particular, test whether a filter/joint check also generates counterexamples, routes inquiry, or changes the claim, rather than assuming transfer among functions.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — human understanding.** Test whether the human can independently state the revised claim, its key constraints, weakest inference, and rejected alternatives after acceptance. Artifact edits are not a proxy for this outcome.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — later system behaviour.** Test whether a later agent retrieves the revised artifact and changes a relevant decision. Persistence alone is insufficient.
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED — human/agent contrast.** Measure the frequency and detectability of silent constraint relaxation, stalls, false precision, and papering-over in both human and agent composition. The categorical contrast is currently based on practitioner observation and a conjectural model.
- **DECISION NEEDED — acceptance allocation.** Specify who has authority to admit the note and what evidence they must inspect. Do not infer this allocation from the human solo-writer analogy.
- **DECISION NEEDED — stopping rule.** Specify when repeated criticism is enough, given weak and potentially correlated prose oracles. The supplied 1/(gap squared) principle explains cost but does not choose an acceptable confidence or budget.
