# Claim revision log: adversarial challenge of operation relocation

This log tests the high-impact joints in `claim-skeleton-v1.md`. A procedure is treated as evidence of what can be specified and recorded, not as evidence that the named epistemic work succeeded.

## 1. Does a named stage or report instantiate an epistemic counterpart?

**Targeted source check.** I reread `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`, `kb/instructions/critique-note.md`, `kb/notes/inspectable-artifact-not-supervision-defeats-the-blackbox-problem.md`, `kb/notes/the-boundary-of-automation-is-the-boundary-of-verification.md`, `kb/notes/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md`, `kb/notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md`, and `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** The two instructions show that a workflow can assign a critic a definite input and require a task-specific trace: a central claim, inferential joints, an opposing case, or routed findings. They deliberately withhold acceptance authority. The inspectability note supports the weaker point that such traces can be inspected by either humans or agents. These sources establish specifiability and observability.

**What it does not resolve.** Neither instruction shows that a reported joint is genuinely weak, that an omitted weakness was found, that the critic checked the artifact's actual route, or that a later decision was improved. The friction instruction's claim that separation “gives the check teeth” is a procedural rationale, not measured independence. The production/evaluation note supplies a concrete reason a plausible report may still miss invalid reasoning. The verification and error-correction notes require discriminative checks and sufficiently decorrelated errors; no prose calibration is supplied. The allocation note explicitly calls each component allocation a hypothesis whose capacity must be tested.

**Disposition: governing claim narrows.** A named stage plus a report counts as an **auditable attempt**, not as performance of a functional counterpart. A task-specific trace can show that an actor enumerated premises or proposed a counterexample, but “localized a failure” additionally claims that the localization was epistemically apt. That requires stage-specific validation or adjudicated outcome evidence. V1's definition—an identifiable input, performance of the named operation, and an output—was circular at “performance.”

**Discarded formulations.**

- “The loop instantiates counterparts of composition's functions.”
- “Inspectable outputs establish operation coverage.”
- “The procedures are an architectural witness for the filter.”
- “A report-only critic performs the missing check.”

## 2. Can “preserve” or “reconstruct” state the result?

**Targeted source check.** I reread `kb/notes/llm-generation-relaxes-goals-where-human-writing-stalls.md`, `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md`, `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md`, and `kb/notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md`, against the evidence posture in `reconstruction.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** The generation note labels recovery by a separate operation an open question. The allocation note says a candidate allocation's ability to preserve a capacity must be tested. The error-correction note gives conditional requirements for successful checking, not evidence that prose critics satisfy them. The friction instruction calls its method a reconstruction, but as an instruction it stipulates a procedure and supplies no comparator or outcome validation.

**What it does not resolve.** The evidence gives no baseline rate for the solo-composition function, no matched comparison with naive delegation, no calibrated prose oracle, and no demonstrated correction, rejection, human update, or later behavioural transfer. Redefining “preserve” to mean that a box with the operation's name exists would change an empirical comparison into a bookkeeping claim.

**Disposition: positive use reverses.** Remove both verbs from the supported result. They may appear only as the research question, as an explicitly unverified hypothesis (“attempt to reconstruct”), or as a future empirical outcome with a named baseline and metric. “Recover” has the same restriction.

**Discarded formulations.**

- “A designed loop preserves a defined subset of composition functions.”
- “Preserve means explicit operation coverage.”
- “The loop reconstructs the filter in a weak architectural sense.”
- “Fresh criticism recovers errors hidden by generation.”

## 3. Is the five-operation core supported and compact?

**Targeted source check.** I reread `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`, `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`, and `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** All three practitioner accounts support a common sequence: make a view definite and expose why it might hold; challenge the stated view or reasons; then change, qualify, reverse, discard, or further investigate in response. Graham combines commitment with neutral-reader testing and rewriting or discarding. Karlsson combines definite conjecture with premise expansion, then uses local or global counterexamples to repair an explanation or replace a view. Karnofsky cycles through a premature defended hypothesis, listed weaknesses, targeted inquiry, and revision or side-switching.

**What it does not resolve.** No source canonizes five operations. Failure localization is normally the product of challenging a premise or route, not a separately established operation. “Outside the originating intention” is one possible checking tactic: Graham and Karlsson describe self-criticism, while Karnofsky adds other readers only after repeated self-criticism. The sources also do not show that every composition episode includes all benefits such as reader simulation, idea generation, inquiry routing, stabilization, or changed human understanding.

**Disposition: core narrows from five items to three bundles.**

1. **Commit-and-expose:** state a definite central claim together with its relevant constraints, premises, and inferential route.
2. **Challenge-and-locate:** test the actual claim and route for counterexamples, missing support, or incompatible commitments, and identify the load-bearing issue.
3. **Respond-and-decide:** direct inquiry at that issue and then revise, qualify, narrow, switch, reject, or mark the claim unresolved before acceptance.

This is still an inferential synthesis, not a universal taxonomy. Freshness, reader simulation, and decorrelation are candidate methods for the second bundle. Stabilization and changed understanding are possible outcomes, not assumed parts of the core.

**Discarded formulations.**

- “The writing-is-thinking filter has five core operations.”
- “Outside-route testing is intrinsically a separate operation.”
- “Failure localization is independent of adversarial testing.”
- “Inquiry routing, reader simulation, idea generation, and stabilization follow from core coverage.”

## 4. Does a downstream disposition close the loop?

**Targeted source check.** I reread the report-only constraints in `kb/instructions/composition-friction-gate.md` and `kb/instructions/critique-note.md`, then the revision and rejection episodes in `kb/sources/putting-ideas-into-words.md`, `kb/sources/how-to-think-in-writing.md`, and `kb/sources/learning-by-writing.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** The practitioner accounts make response load-bearing: claims are qualified, explanations repaired, hypotheses reversed, and broken ideas discarded. A workflow that cannot change the commitment omits this part of the proposed core. The two instructions intentionally stop before mutation or acceptance, so they can feed a response stage but cannot witness its execution.

**What it does not resolve.** The presence of an acceptor or a revision step does not show that a finding was understood, correctly adjudicated, or allowed to alter a favored conclusion. Even an observed edit may be cosmetic or wrong.

**Disposition: design requirement survives; performance claim narrows.** A candidate workflow should record a disposition for each load-bearing finding—rebut with support, investigate, revise, narrow, switch, reject, or retain as explicit uncertainty. That trace closes the procedural handoff. It does not establish that the epistemic loop succeeded.

**Discarded formulations.**

- “An explicit acceptor closes the filter.”
- “A revision stage demonstrates correction.”
- “A critique that causes an edit has recovered an error.”

## 5. Are fresh critics independent and discriminative?

**Targeted source check.** I reread the fresh-runner claims in both instruction files, the discrimination and correlation analysis in `kb/notes/error-correction-works-above-chance-oracles-with-decorrelated-checks.md`, and the route-substitution failure in `kb/notes/reasoning-production-is-not-reasoning-evaluation.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** Separate context and route-focused prompts are defensible interventions aimed at changing failure modes. A critic must discriminate supported from unsupported cases, and a route checker must distinguish a true conclusion with invalid reasoning from a valid argument.

**What it does not resolve.** A fresh context is not evidence of independence; shared models, evidence, framing, and content biases can correlate errors. No source reports TPR, FPR, error correlation, or a prose regression set for these critics. The route-checking evidence is transferred from mathematical evaluation to prose.

**Disposition: remains uncertain and conditions every recovery claim.** Describe freshness and prompt separation as decorrelation attempts. Treat route-focused, discriminative criticism as a testable requirement, not a present property of the workflow.

**Discarded formulations.**

- “A different runner supplies an independent check.”
- “Fresh criticism gives the loop teeth.”
- “Joint enumeration ensures actual-route evaluation.”

## 6. Do human approval or authorship supply the missing evidence?

**Targeted source check.** I reread `kb/sources/why-almost-never-use-ai-to-write-anything-substantive.md`, `kb/sources/when-is-it-better-to-think-without-words.md`, `kb/notes/human-analogies-suggest-functions-not-component-boundaries.md`, and `kb/notes/vibe-noting.md`.

**What the evidence resolves.** The first practitioner source gives a specific passive-assent mechanism: fluent wording can anchor a reviewer into accepting an approximate phrase. The wordless-thought source reports that human prose can also impose credible false precision. The allocation note leaves checking roles open, and the persistence note separates inspectability from verification and later activation. These support a process-based diagnostic rather than an authorship rule.

**What it does not resolve.** The sources do not measure anchoring, show that fresh criticism prevents it, or demonstrate that approval changed the human's understanding. Human acceptance records authority, not artifact truth, human learning, or later system behaviour.

**Disposition: distinction survives.** Keep epistemic checking separate from acceptance authority. Require independent evidence for artifact improvement, changed human understanding, and later behavioural transfer. Human re-derivation is relevant only when changed human understanding is the claimed outcome; it is not a universal requirement for every allocation.

**Discarded formulations.**

- “Human adjudication preserves the omitted work.”
- “Human presence makes the loop epistemically closed.”
- “An accepted artifact demonstrates human or system learning.”

## Revised claim decision

The v1 architectural-existence claim does not survive. The evidence supports a narrower design proposition: the three candidate operation bundles can be made explicit, routed, and auditable across a human-agent workflow. Whether any allocation performs the epistemic functions, improves an artifact, changes a human view, or affects later behaviour remains an empirical hypothesis.
