## Purpose

Test whether a source-directed claim–criticism–reading loop produces a materially better note than the incumbent and its prior post-draft improvement history. The experiment may preserve, narrow, or reject the incumbent's governing claim, but it may not replace it with an unrelated contribution.

## Inputs

The source and evidence paths are fixed in [brief.md](./brief.md). The incumbent is frozen separately as [original.md](./original.md) and must remain hidden from reconstruction, skeleton, and drafting agents. It becomes available only to the audit and comparison stages.

## Comparison rule

The candidate wins only if blind review finds a clearer warranted contribution, stronger evidence-to-claim alignment, fewer unsupported inferential jumps, and at least comparable context efficiency. Fluency or additional coverage alone cannot win. A tie or disagreement leaves the incumbent in place.

## Experiment outcome

The source-directed multistage candidate beat the incumbent 3–0. A full improvement pass then beat that candidate 3–0 while cutting its length from 1,054 to 716 words. Closing review found that the compressed version did not explicitly require adjudication of the final response and left the practice-to-handoff analogy implicit. The current `candidate.md` repairs those findings and narrows critic reliability to the claim its evidence supports.

Fresh review over the current bytes finds no blocker: source-grounded acceptance passes, sentence and source-bridge gates pass, and seven of eight semantic gates pass. The remaining semantic WARN is only the known workshop-location link artifact; all targets resolve from the intended `kb/notes/` destination. Composition friction reports `SURVIVES` and retains the exact three-stage decomposition as a provisional design question.

This selects `candidate.md` for human consideration; it does not promote, replace, or rename the library note.
