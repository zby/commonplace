# Blind comparison commission

## Question

Can a distributed human-agent writing loop preserve the epistemic functions of composition that naive prose delegation bypasses? If so, which functions and conditions are supported, and where does the claim remain conjectural?

## Audience and intended update

Commonplace maintainers and agents designing or evaluating KB-writing workflows should be able to distinguish:

- rendering prose from exposing and correcting a weak idea;
- an inspectable draft from criticism that is independent and effective;
- human judgment being present from a human re-deriving claims and constraints; and
- improved artifact text from changed human understanding or demonstrated later-system learning.

The note should give the weakest warranted account of whether commitment, criticism, revision, and acceptance can be allocated across humans and agents. It may preserve, narrow, or reject the positive preservation claim, but may not switch to a general essay about AI authorship, writing advice, continual learning, or Commonplace architecture.

## Constraints

- Produce one composable main claim with its mechanism near the top.
- Define the relevant composition filter by operations rather than slogan.
- Treat practitioner essays as practitioner or conceptual evidence, not controlled demonstrations.
- Do not claim superiority over solo writing without comparative evidence.
- Do not infer actor allocation from a human analogy; separate epistemic checking from acceptance authority.
- Address anchoring or passive assent if human review carries argumentative weight.
- Separate artifact change, human understanding, later system behaviour, and acceptance.
- Preserve uncertainty plainly and keep current-model or cognitive claims within their cited boundaries.
