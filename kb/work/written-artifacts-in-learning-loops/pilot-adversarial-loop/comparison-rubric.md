# Pre-registered comparison rubric

This rubric is fixed before reconstruction produces a candidate. Judges receive anonymized versions in balanced order and must not be told which is the incumbent or source-directed rewrite.

## Hard constraints

A version is ineligible to win if it:

- replaces the commissioned contribution with an unrelated thesis;
- makes a material claim that its cited source or linked premise does not support;
- conflates changed human understanding, changed artifact content, and demonstrated system learning;
- claims superiority over solo writing without comparative evidence;
- has broken local links or violates the note and collection contracts.

## Scored dimensions

Judge each dimension from 1 (materially weak) to 5 (strong). Scores route attention; the final choice must still be argued from passages rather than computed mechanically.

1. **Warranted contribution** — The note gives its intended reader a specific, non-generic update and keeps one composable center.
2. **Claim precision** — The central claim resolves what “filter,” “reconstruct,” “adversarial,” and any human role mean where those choices affect truth.
3. **Mechanism and inference** — The note shows how commitment, criticism, revision, and acceptance produce the claimed result; conclusions do not outrun premises.
4. **Grounding and calibration** — Sources carry only the scope they establish, practitioner testimony remains testimony, and conjectures remain recognizable.
5. **Boundary handling** — The note addresses passive assent, correlated reviewers, competence limits, and the lack of a direct solo-versus-loop comparison without padding itself with generic hedges.
6. **Reader usability** — The argument is easy to follow, places definitions and qualifications where needed, and can be cited without importing unrelated branches.
7. **Context efficiency** — Each paragraph changes what the reader understands, infers, or can do. Additional length must buy argument, evidence, or a necessary boundary.

## Decision rule

Each judge returns `VERSION 1`, `VERSION 2`, or `TIE`, with:

- one paragraph explaining the governing difference;
- a score table for all seven dimensions;
- the strongest passage in each version;
- the most important remaining weakness in each version;
- any hard-constraint failure.

The candidate wins only when:

- at least two of three blind judges prefer it;
- no judge identifies an unresolved material grounding or hard-constraint failure in it;
- the separate semantic audit and acceptance review contain no blocker; and
- any extra context cost is justified by a named contribution.

Otherwise the incumbent remains the promotion default. A candidate can still yield process lessons even when it does not win.
