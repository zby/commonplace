# Friction check: Recorded composition checks make attempted operations auditable but do not establish their effects

**Note:** kb/work/written-artifacts-in-learning-loops/pilot-adversarial-loop/candidate.md
**Central claim (one sentence):** For substantive claims whose commitments or support remain unsettled, recording defined inputs and task-specific traces for commit-and-expose, challenge-and-locate, and respond-and-decide makes the staged artifacts and handoffs inspectable and available for testing, but does not itself establish the stages' quality, reproduce composition's epistemic effects, or outperform solo writing.

## Filter

**Verdict:** SURVIVES
The claimed properties can coexist: a workflow can expose stage artifacts and handoffs while withholding any claim that the recorded operations worked, changed understanding, or beat a comparator. The operation taxonomy, actor allocation, and outcome tests remain candidate design choices rather than being smuggled into the auditability claim as established effects.

## Signal — thinnest joints

1. **“Giving each operation a defined input and a task-specific trace makes the allocation observable”** — THIN — Operation-labelled records expose a functional decomposition, but they do not expose the actor allocation the note later treats as an independent design choice unless the trace contract records which human, agent, or policy performed each role and under what conditions. The note names possible actors without requiring that provenance in the trace.
2. **“An acceptor then records a disposition for every reported challenge … investigate, revise, narrow, switch, reject, or retain an unresolved marker”** — THIN — The list mixes interim routing (`investigate`) with claim-level decisions. Recording an investigation as the disposition shows that a challenge was handed onward, but not that respond-and-decide performed a substantive adjudication; the later requirement for a “substantively adjudicated disposition” leaves this completion condition unresolved.
3. **“Performance evidence must validate these stages separately”** — UNSUPPORTED — Separate validation is needed to claim that each stage works or to attribute an end-to-end effect to a stage, but a blinded comparison on a named artifact or human-understanding outcome could establish loop-level performance without validating every stage separately. The paragraph also supplies tests for critics and acceptor dispositions but no performance criterion for commit-and-expose, so it does not complete its own stage-by-stage requirement.
4. **“An artifact outcome requires evidence that independently upheld findings led to corrections, better-supported boundaries, or justified rejection”** — UNSUPPORTED — This makes an upheld critic finding necessary for an artifact outcome without showing why. Commit-and-expose could improve or reject an artifact before a critic finding, and blinded before-and-after artifact assessment could evidence improvement directly; the proposed criterion therefore measures one challenge-response path rather than the whole stated operation taxonomy.
5. **“Naive prose delegation instead treats fluent rendering and ordinary approval as if the claim and its support had undergone these checks”** — THIN — The cited practitioner accounts support the risks of hidden error, anchoring, and passive assent, but the note neither defines the comparator's protocol nor establishes that fluent rendering plus approval is treated as evidence for all three operations. “Naive” currently carries the missing comparator specification.

## For the human

Look first at the trace contract: decide whether the claim is only that stage artifacts are inspectable or also that actor allocation and completed attempts are auditable, then distinguish an interim `investigate` route from a substantively adjudicated disposition.
