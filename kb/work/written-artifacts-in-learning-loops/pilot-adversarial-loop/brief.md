# Brief: source-directed rewrite of the adversarial writing loop

## Governing question

Can a human-agent writing loop preserve the epistemic functions of composition that naive prose delegation bypasses? If so, which functions and conditions are supported, and where does the claim remain conjectural?

## Audience and intended update

The audience is Commonplace maintainers and agents designing or evaluating KB-writing workflows.

The reader should be able to distinguish:

- rendering prose from exposing and correcting a weak idea;
- a draft being inspectable from criticism actually being independent and effective;
- human judgment being present from the human re-deriving the draft's claims and constraints;
- improved artifact text from changed human understanding or demonstrated system learning.

The artifact should give this reader a defensible rule for when delegation preserves the relevant writing functions and when it merely produces fluent prose around unresolved commitments.

## Intended contribution

Investigate the incumbent contribution rather than presuppose it: the error-exposure function commonly attributed to a solo writer's act of composition may belong to a larger loop of commitment, criticism, revision, and acceptance. Determine the weakest claim the evidence supports about relocating those functions across humans and agents.

The run may preserve, narrow, or reject that claim. It may not switch to a general essay about AI authorship, writing advice, continual learning, or Commonplace's architecture.

## Scope and constraints

- Target collection: `kb/notes/`, theoretical profile.
- Target type: `kb/types/note.md`.
- Produce one composable main claim with its mechanism near the top.
- Define the “writing-is-thinking filter” by the operations it performs; do not leave it as a slogan.
- Treat the human essays as practitioner or conceptual evidence, not controlled demonstrations.
- Do not claim that the loop outperforms solo writing without comparative evidence.
- Do not infer that a human must occupy every judging role merely from a human analogy. Separate epistemic checking from authority to accept a KB change.
- Name anchoring and passive assent as failure modes if the argument depends on human review.
- Separate changed human understanding, improved artifact content, and later system behaviour.
- Keep current-model or cognitive claims within their cited boundaries.
- Preserve uncertainty plainly instead of protecting the claim with generic hedges.
- Do not use `original.md`, any prior case-03 artifact, or any draft during reconstruction, skeleton construction, or drafting.

## Known uncertainties

- No supplied source directly compares a disciplined human-agent loop with solo composition.
- The sources identify several possible functions—commitment, error exposure, inquiry routing, reader simulation, stabilization, and rejection—but do not show that one check reconstructs them all.
- The error-correction argument supplies conditions for useful checks but does not establish that present prose reviewers meet them.
- Human review can be anchored by fluent generated text and become passive assent.
- A human may retain acceptance authority even if agents perform some epistemic checks; those are different roles.
- It is unclear whether “reconstruct” should mean functional equivalence, partial substitution, or merely recovering some detectable errors. The final claim must resolve or narrow this choice.

These uncertainties are non-blocking because the artifact may state a narrower result and retain empirical questions explicitly.
