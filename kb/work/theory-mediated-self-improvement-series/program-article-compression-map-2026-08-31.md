# Compression map for the learning-software-factories program article

## Goal

Make the program article a compact entry point. Preserve the central argument and the evidence needed to make it testable; move derivations and taxonomies to their existing supporting homes.

Target: roughly halve the body without weakening the research claim.

## Spine to preserve

1. A software factory learns when production experience changes reusable production machinery used later.
2. Minimal factory learning does not require theory, but open-ended coherent modification requires a project-specific theory-bearing capacity in Naur's functional sense.
3. Retained theory is not yet held theory: a holder recognizes when the theory is relevant and brings it to bear. The theory may be weak and fallible; it guides search, backtracking, recovery, and revision rather than deducing a correct patch.
4. Natural-language theory is the tested realization, not the necessary carrier. LLMs make it potentially operative across heterogeneous machinery; generate-and-verify and other learning mechanisms remain inside the system.
5. The claim is causal and longitudinal: theory must affect consequential decisions, consequences must bear on it, revisions must persist, and later operation must change.
6. Test this in a controlled programming-agent testbed and in the human-inclusive Commonplace loop. The bootstrap asks whether recurring human judgments can move into reusable computational machinery.

## Keep / compress / move / delete

### From agentic systems to learning software factories

**Compress heavily.** Keep only enough ontology to distinguish substrate, factory, factory development, and factory learning. The bounded-context and scheduler material is useful support but should not open the article. Remove the task-family/product-family excursus from the body; link the definition if needed. Compress prior art on recursive factory construction to one sentence.

### Naur's constraint + weak theory

**Merge into one section and keep central.** This is the article's conceptual hinge. Preserve:

- minimal learning versus coherent modification;
- Naur's functional necessity claim, without claiming a necessary carrier;
- retained theory versus held theory;
- weak/fallible theory guiding search, backtracking, recovery, and revision under delayed feedback.

Delete repeated explanations that theory need not be one document, need not be natural language, need not produce a correct first answer, and need not replace generic search. State each once and link to the bearer/coherent-search notes.

### Natural-language theory

**Compress to 2–3 paragraphs.** Keep the contingent implementation bet: natural language is addressable and LLM-interpretable, so one theory can coordinate heterogeneous machinery. Keep one sentence that theory shapes generate-and-verify rather than replacing it. Drop the six-item inventory of what theory can represent and the decomposition/self-knowledge excursus; those are supporting-note material.

### Two testbeds + evidence

**Merge into one section.** Introduce Commonplace and programming agents, then give the causal path and four-level evidence ladder. Keep the retained -> held transition in the path. Compress mediation-trace caveats to one sentence. The four-functional-roles table is architecture detail and should move out of the main flow unless a later edit shows it is indispensable to understanding the experiment.

### Warrant and fit

**Move out.** The article needs only one sentence that system use can test causal usefulness/fit but does not replace independent factual or formal evaluation. The derivation belongs in existing notes and the decisions companion.

### Current evidence and next tests

**Keep, but compress.** Current Commonplace evidence gets one short paragraph with its limitation. The controlled test gets the minimum contrasts: retained-to-held relevance recognition plus usable/withheld/wrong or information-matched theory. Do not include an unrun measurement catalogue. The longitudinal Commonplace bullet list can become one sentence about whether human judgments become reusable machinery and marginal human contribution falls.

### Bootstrap and system boundary

**Compress to one bridge section.** State that current Commonplace evidence is human-inclusive and that remaining operator judgments are explicit bootstrap targets. Keep a single progress criterion: how much of the learning path can the computational subsystem carry without task-specific human decisions. Link to *The decisions that stay human* for fixed-boundary, closure, warrant, and transfer machinery.

### Stronger claims remain separate

**Delete the table from the program article.** One sentence is enough: factory learning does not by itself establish reflection, closure, self-improvement, compounding, or broad reach. Definitions live elsewhere.

### What would change the strategy

**Compress.** Keep only the strongest failure conditions: theory is causally inert; another representation/direct or mixed learner performs better at comparable total cost; human-supplied specialization does not fall as scope widens. The long list is a workshop/checklist, not article prose.

### Bitter Lesson / Sutton

**Move almost entirely to the companion article.** The program statement needs only the narrow point that the Bitter Lesson constrains how specialization is produced, not that learned state must live in weights, plus the requirement that the bootstrap outgrow recurring human-supplied specialization. Remove the detailed Sutton interview comparison and quotation from the program article; link the companion.

## Proposed body structure

1. **Learning software factories** — minimal ontology and learning path.
2. **Coherent modification requires held project theory** — Naur + retained/held + weak theory/search.
3. **Natural-language theory is the implementation bet** — LLM interpretation, heterogeneous machinery, generate-and-verify.
4. **How the program is tested** — two testbeds, causal path/evidence ladder, current evidence, minimum controlled contrasts.
5. **Bootstrap and failure conditions** — human-inclusive boundary, declining human specialization, Bitter Lesson bridge, alternatives/falsifiers.

## Guardrails

- Do not imply every factory improvement must be theory-mediated.
- Do not weaken Naur to merely one useful mechanism: for open-ended coherent modification, retain the theory-bearing functional constraint.
- Do not equate held theory with first-shot success; fallible search and recovery are essential.
- Do not make natural language the necessary carrier.
- Do not let the article become an autonomy/closure/warrant treatise.
- Do not expand experimental protocols before they are run.
- Use supporting notes as the canonical homes for derivations rather than repeating them in the article.
