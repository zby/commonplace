## What the program claims about a partly automated path

On a declared path, the portion whose decisions are proposed by search or a
model and accepted by an oracle the candidate did not author has moved to
scalable production; that portion is governed by the bitter lesson and
compatible with it, whatever form its retained outputs take. The remainder is
predicted rather than embarrassing: transfer prefers the decisions it can
warrant, so what stays hand-supplied is what no available oracle can check —
and its difficulty does not reclassify the moved portion, because a method's
ceiling bounds the method, not the transfer already made. The program declines
to claim that the moved portion scales (that burden is per portion), that
portions stack toward closure, that the remainder will move, that the
remainder's artifacts survive absorption, or any proportion of a path.

A portion is worth having on the workshop goal's own criterion: for a
nontrivial task class it increases accepted outcomes obtainable from a fixed
amount of total human programming effort, counting configuration, review,
recovery, and repair.

## Why three rows and not one

The operator asked whether the four pieces are one stance applied three
times. They are, and the portfolio now says so once. But the portfolio's
entry rule is per defense, and a defense is individuated by what it declines
to establish. The per-portion rule declines convergence and proportion;
production freedom declines the forecast that the loop will take over
authorship; the regime declines universality. Merging them would let the hub
cite one row for three different non-claims, which is the overreach the
second column exists to prevent. So: one subsection stating the shared move,
three rows, and the regime as scope rather than as a row, because it bounds
every member and defends nothing.

## The interaction check, and what FunSearch shows

Search over a decision does two separable things. It replaces the person who
would have drafted the candidate, and it may replace the person who accepts
it. Only the second moves the decision out of the human cut, and only when a
check the candidate did not author can reject it — P2's binding condition. So
the portion of a path the lesson governs is the portion warrant has reached,
and the two questions (P5: which form; P2: who decides) stay orthogonal while
their answers meet at one point.

FunSearch is the pattern with every part labelled: the frozen model proposes
function bodies, the executable evaluator accepts or rejects, and the
specification, evaluator, skeleton, and function boundary stay supplied. The
supplied items are the objective and unsettled-criterion rows of the residue
table. AlphaDev is the same shape with a learned proposer and an exhaustive
correctness check for its small-sort domain. Neither moved the decision of
what to optimize; both moved the decision of which candidate wins, and did so
exactly where an oracle discriminates.

For the remainder this means search changes who proposes and nothing else.
Run over a decision with no independent oracle, it produces the
captured-evaluator or boundary-export pattern from the
[closure–capability map](./closure-capability-map.md#degenerate-closure-patterns),
not a transfer.

## The disanalogy premise

### Earned reach cannot carry it

The brief named
[selection removes unearned reach, not structure](../../notes/bitter-lesson-selects-against-unearned-reach-not-against-structure.md)
as the sharpest candidate. That note was narrowed on 2026-08-24 (commit
92314f92, "Ground claim cleanup cohort 02") from *scale replaces
generalizations whose scope was asserted; structure whose reach was earned is
what search converges on* to *unsupported proxy scope may explain a
structured method's loss under scaling* — a case-level conjecture whose two
listed cases (IRM, DomainBed) do not complete its mechanism, and whose scope
section disclaims the inverse: "the evidence does not show that adequately
assessed structure necessarily survives scaling." The filename kept the old
title, so inbound links still read as the strong claim. The portfolio row was
never updated; it is now.

Even in its strong form the criterion would not have carried the disanalogy.
Earned reach filters *content* — which structure passes selection when the
function it serves is demanded. The vision features did not lose because
their reach was unearned in that sense; edge detection is exact relative to
its own requirement. They lost because demand for the *function* moved to a
learned substrate that supplied it better. A criterion that speaks only to
content cannot say why demand for the function survives.

### Substitute versus complement is the candidate reason, and where it stops

The reason must speak to function: hand-crafted features were a substitute
for a capability the model lacked, so demand for the structure evaporated
when the capability arrived while demand for the function grew; artifacts
carrying current state, commitments, authority, and project-specific facts are
complements, supplying what no model holds from its weights. This explains the
vision case instead of denying it, and it is checkable per artifact class.

Its status is **candidate**. No note carries it; the two existing carriers of
the complement half are the commitment and reproduction notes, and the
portfolio row is written so the hub cites the criterion as classified, not
established. The historical test is the absorption-survivors search
(report-only, in flight). If that report tells against the split, the
candidate row and the register's third divergence are withdrawn; the
per-portion and production-freedom rows do not depend on it.

Where it stops, in three places. Methodology, heuristics, and conventions are
substitutes — a capable model supplies the function they stand in for — so the
standing concession survives intact and covers most of what Commonplace
writes. Complement is not permanence of form: a governed parametric artifact
can become the authoritative record when its release, appeal, and revision
guarantees are supplied, [as the reproduction note already allows](../../notes/parametric-reproduction-cannot-replace-an-authoritative-record.md).
And a committed theory note is both at once: its content may be regenerable
(substitute), while the record of which theory this project adopted is a
commitment (complement) — what the complement defense protects is the record,
not the text.

### The two-class tension: two criteria, not one residue

The brief flagged that commitments and structured theories seem to survive
absorption for opposite reasons, and warned against the tidy synthesis that
the bitter-lesson residue and the adverse-selection residue are one residue.
They are not, and the reason can be stated.

There are two criteria, asked of different things. The function criterion
(substitute or complement) asks what an artifact class supplies that weights
do not. The content criterion (a reject-capable test) asks which candidate
passes selection once the function is demanded. Commitments survive on the
function criterion: nothing entails a decision before it is made, so there is
no oracle, and the decision is both non-transferable (it stays in the human
cut) and retained as a record (a complement). Structured theories with a
reject-capable test survive on the content criterion — and the same oracle
that lets their reach be earned makes their production transferable to
search. So oracle-bearing structure is *in the moved portion*, not in the
residue. The adverse-selection residue and the set of absorption survivors
overlap at the commitment class and diverge over oracle-bearing structure.
The tidy synthesis is wrong because it treats the oracle as protecting
structure from search when it is what hands structure to search.

## Production freedom: what would have to be undone, per artifact class

The claim is that Commonplace fixes representational form and leaves the
production axis open, so present human authorship is a stage. The checkable
form asks, per class, what would have to be undone or added for a search loop
to author it. Answers other than "nothing" are the point.

Add read-back of outcomes against the note (the co-indexed path) so exposure can select, and decorrelated judges; the adoption "no" stays supplied | Proposal moved; selection partly moved; adoption not |
| Source ingests and grounding quotes | `cp-skill-ingest`, `cp-skill-ground` | Validator checks verbatim quotes against the pinned snapshot (mechanical); the ingest's reading is gate-checked | Nothing for the mechanical part. Which source to ingest is demand choice — the objective row, supplied by category | Moved for the mechanical part; source selection stays supplied |
| Validators, schemas, commands (symbolic) | Agents and people | Tests | Nothing for authorship. Add an oracle for whether the requirement serves the objective — [exact implementation does not validate a requirement](../../notes/exact-implementation-does-not-validate-a-requirement.md); in the traced tag-readme episode a person noticed the strain, the loop produced the validator | Authorship movable; the requirement stays supplied |
| Skills and instructions (system-definition) | People, agent-drafted | Delivery probes (does the runtime deliver it) and structural validation; no outcome oracle for whether an instruction change improves work | Add an outcome measure at fixed task class and acceptance threshold; keep the verified consumption path, since a loop-authored instruction binds only through it | Not moved; the missing piece is the oracle |
| Type specs, `COLLECTION.md` contracts, tag READMEs (decomposition and routing) | People | Validator enforces marks and schemas; the decomposition itself has only composition failure — missed retrievals, recurring corrections — as a signal | The decomposition would have to enter some loop's update space, and composition failure would have to become a signal that loop acts on; every current loop treats the decomposition as fixed | Not moved; this is the fixed-decomposition layer |
| Review criteria (gate texts) | People | None for criterion quality except the outcomes it gates | A decorrelated check on criterion changes; without it a loop-authored criterion is the captured-evaluator pattern | Not moved; the second-order oracle is missing |
| ADRs and other commitments | People (agents may draft) | None, by construction | Not a target: external by category with the objective and the adoption "no" | Stays supplied |
| Connect reports, freshness baselines, review state (derived) | `cp-skill-connect`, the review pipeline | Connect reports are proposal-only (report kind; a person decides); baselines are mechanical | Nothing; already loop-produced | Moved — and a correction to the brief: connect is a proposal loop, not a proposal-selection loop |

The seam is the one piece 1 names: the classes whose answer is "nothing" are
the moved portion; the classes whose answer names a missing oracle or a fixed
decomposition are the remainder, and each names the capacity that must grow
before it moves — the residue table read on the production axis.
