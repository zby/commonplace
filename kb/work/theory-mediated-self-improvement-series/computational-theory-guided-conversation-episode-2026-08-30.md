# Computational theory-guided conversation episode, 2026-08-30

This record captures a live human-inclusive Commonplace episode. It is evidence
that the current bootstrap already uses computation and retained project
knowledge. It is not evidence of autonomous theory learning or of scaling with
additional computation.

## Declared boundary and task

- **Boundary:** the operator, GPT-5.6 Pro, the Commonplace knowledge base and
  published site, GitHub, and the repository tools used to read and revise it.
- **Task:** review the theory-mediated self-improvement workshop, reconcile its
  central claims, and update the active workshop and researcher-facing material.
- **Objective:** make the research program coherent and expose claims and tests
  that can change its status.
- **Starting state:** the workshop and related Commonplace artifacts before and
  during pull request
  [#122](https://github.com/zby/commonplace/pull/122).

The boundary matters. With the operator included, this is a human-agent learning
process. With the operator excluded, the decisive global-fit judgments and final
acceptance remain exogenous.

## Causal sequence

1. **Retained knowledge was retrieved.** The model read the workshop, linked
   theory notes, articles, evidence records, and repository state rather than
   answering from a generic prompt alone.
2. **Computation searched and synthesized.** The model compared formulations,
   detected tensions, generated a consolidated research question, proposed
   distinctions and experiments, and produced repository edits.
3. **The operator supplied sparse high-level selection.** The operator accepted
   much of the cleanup but rejected increasingly strong formulations of the
   Bitter Lesson response: first the portfolio of defenses, then the claim that
   bootstrapping itself was a defense, and finally wording that made computation
   sound like a later addition.
4. **The theory state changed.** The workshop moved from a defense portfolio, to
   a conditional bootstrap thesis, to a first-strategy account under incomplete
   global evaluation. It then gained the sharper claim that the current loop is
   already computational and that the unresolved bottleneck is high-level
   selection and credit assignment.
5. **The change was retained.** The revised distinctions, notes, articles, and
   workshop structure were written to the pull-request branch.
6. **The retained state affected later work.** Subsequent turns read and revised
   the updated branch rather than reconstructing the argument from its earlier
   form.

## What the episode supports

### Computation is already part of the learning loop

The LLM did not merely execute a completed human theory. It performed a large
part of the search over interpretations, candidate claims, distinctions,
article structures, experiments, and repository changes. Repository tools also
performed retrieval, comparison, validation, and retention. The bootstrap is
therefore not a pre-computational design phase.

### Commonplace knowledge was operative input

The model's work used project-specific concepts and constraints retrieved from
Commonplace, including coherent modification, theory mediation, warrant,
computational closure, representational form, and the Bitter Lesson distinction
between production method and representation. The artifacts constrained the
space of plausible revisions and supplied premises and rejected alternatives.

This is good evidence of **mediation**, but not a quantified causal estimate. A
matched run with the artifacts withheld, replaced, or reduced to an
information-matched record would be stronger evidence of how load-bearing the
retained theory was.

### The composite performed theory learning

Operator feedback did not merely choose between two finished patches. It changed
the theory's content and scope: bootstrapping was demoted from a defense to a
first approach, truth was separated from global fit, and the location of the
current bottleneck was revised from computation in general to selection and
credit assignment.

### The episode reached recurrence at the human-inclusive boundary

The revised theory state was retained in the repository and then used in later
reasoning and editing. This supports recurrence for the composite of operator,
model, knowledge base, and tools. It does not establish recurrence by the model
alone or computational closure over acceptance.

## What the episode does not support

- It does not establish that every retrieved artifact made a causal difference;
  there was no theory-withholding or replacement control.
- It does not establish independent truth of the revised claims. The strongest
  feedback concerned fit in the research program and the operator's intended
  meaning.
- It does not establish autonomous selection. The operator supplied decisive
  corrections about the big picture and authorized repository changes.
- It does not show that more inference-time computation improves results, that
  human effort falls with scale, or that the method beats direct search,
  end-to-end learning, stronger-model, or weight-update alternatives.
- Repository tests establish software integrity, not correctness of the theory.

## Bitter Lesson relevance

The immediate question is no longer whether Commonplace uses computation. It
does. The sharper questions are:

1. Does retained project theory make the computational search more effective
   than an information-matched record or generic context?
2. Does additional computation improve proposal, criticism, comparison, and
   recovery rather than merely producing more prose?
3. Can recurring operator judgments be captured so that later episodes require
   less bespoke global correction?
4. Does the resulting process transfer beyond the domains and artifact
   decompositions anticipated by its designers?
5. Does the full human-agent process remain competitive at comparable total
   cost?

The current arrangement is best described as **computational theory-guided
search with human-assisted global selection**. The bootstrap task is to improve
and extend the selection machinery, not to introduce computation after the
system has been hand-built.

## Selection machinery exposed by this episode

Three recurring judgments are candidates for operationalization:

- distinguish a narrow rebuttal from a defense of the whole research strategy;
- distinguish evidence that a claim is true from evidence that it fits the
  larger working theory; and
- distinguish the presence of computation from computational control over
  global selection and credit assignment.

Possible reusable outputs include a Bitter Lesson claim-role checklist, an
episode schema that records computational and human contributions separately,
and an evaluator prompt that challenges claims which describe present
computation as merely future machinery.

## Evidence status

- **Mediation:** supported by retrieval traces, project-specific use, and retained
  artifact references; not ablated.
- **Empirical contact:** supported only in the limited sense of operator-facing
  use and correction, not independent world evidence.
- **Theory learning:** supported by explicit changes in content and scope.
- **Recurrence:** supported at the human-inclusive boundary by later use of the
  revised repository state.
- **Computational autonomy and closure:** not established.
