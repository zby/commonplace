# Workshop: atomic steps

**Posed by:** the operator, 2026-08-27, while reading [review-attention-price](../review-attention-price/README.md). The idea, in the operator's words:

> Maybe the limit to 5 sources is OK. We don't want to make steps too big to check — maybe this should be another idea here, beside atomic notes: atomic steps. But there would also be a way out — if an article wants to ground in more than 5 sources, it could quote them verbatim; then the quotes would be validated automatically. Maybe we should even decrease the number, but that would be for later optimisation.

**The idea, as understood in the same session.** Atomic notes bound a note to one claim so notes can be co-loaded. Atomic steps bound a note to one *checkable inference*: the evidence a reviewer must read to check the note's grounding fits in one pass. Where atomic notes are justified by combinatorial co-loading, atomic steps are justified by the reviewer — the note is sized to the check, rather than the check being calibrated to the note. That reverses the direction of the review-attention-price workshop, which prices what the reviewer can read; here the cap is a contract on the artifact and the reviewer's budget never binds on a conforming note. The escape hatch is a change of representational form: a source that is quoted verbatim turns a semantic check (does the source support this?) into a mechanical one (does this string occur in the source?), so quoted sources do not count against the step. The two ideas are not the same: the corpus's over-five notes are mostly one-claim evidence notes over six to nine sources, so a note can be atomic and still not be an atomic step.

**Why an article first.** The operator is not sure yet whether this is a theory note, a fragment of one, or only an operational rule. The article is the place to find out: write the argument for an outside reader, see what it has to rest on, and then decide what the KB keeps as a note. The review-attention-price mechanism decision waits on this.

**What closes it.** An article draft under the articles contract circulating behind its banner, grounded in KB notes and in retained source quotes for any externally attributed claim; a decision recorded here on what the KB extracts (a note, a fragment folded into an existing note, or nothing beyond the article); the review-attention-price workshop told what changed for it. Then this workshop is deleted.

**Evaluation boundary.** The article's claims are about checkability and the sizing of artifacts to a bounded check. It is not the place to decide the number, the validator rule, or ADR 079's fate — those are decisions for the review-attention-price workshop or an ADR, and the article should cite the current state rather than pre-empt it. The one argument the article cannot avoid and the KB does not yet hold is why links to other notes are exempt from the step count while links to sources are not; see the open questions in [grounding.md](./grounding.md).

**State, 2026-08-27 (session 2).** The draft exists: [Atomic steps: size the note to its check](./draft-article.md), circulating behind its banner and listed in the articles index. It rests on KB notes and on two new ingests with retained quotes — [Descartes, *Discourse on the Method*](../../sources/descartes-discourse-on-the-method.ingest.md) (second and third precepts, the long-chains passage) and [Lamport, *How to Write a 21st Century Proof*](../../sources/lamport-how-to-write-a-21st-century-proof.ingest.md) (step detail is reader-relative; structuring re-found a published error; "possible, not inevitable"). The article quotes both inline with the `verbatim` marker, so it practises the hatch it argues for: `commonplace-validate` resolves its six quotes against the ingests. Drafting exposed a checker false positive — a link to ADR 046 carried the marker word in its *path* and registered as an unpaired citation — fixed in `quote_verification.py` (link targets are masked before the marker search) with a regression test. The other literature candidates (De Millo et al., code-review change-size evidence, Lightman et al., Luhmann on atomicity) were not ingested; the draft attributes nothing to them. The review-attention-price README now records what changed for it.

**What the article settled, and the proposed extraction (for the operator to confirm).** Writing it showed the idea is one theory claim plus one operational rule, not a single note. The claim the KB does not yet hold is the second open question's answer: *a note is an atomic step relative to a check, and for the grounding check its unit is the unquoted source — a source quoted verbatim is discharged by the validator, and a linked note is charged to the representation check because it carries its own certificate (claim title, own grounding review).* That deserves one note in `kb/notes/` (candidate title: "A note is an atomic step relative to the check that reads it"), with the note-link exemption argument as its mechanism section and the four-miss reinterpretation as evidence; the atomic-note note gets a `contrasts` link to it. The number, the validator rule, and ADR 079's fate stay with review-attention-price. Recommended next steps once the operator confirms: review the draft under the articles contract; write the note via `cp-skill-write`; then delete this workshop.
