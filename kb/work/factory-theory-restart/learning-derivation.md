# Learning follows from holding a theory of a changing system

The first document derived that a software factory system which keeps
changing coherently must hold a theory in Naur's meaning. This one derives
the next step: such a system must also keep learning. The step is not
assumed; it follows from two things Naur establishes and one fact about the
setting.

**Naur's negative result: the theory cannot be handed over.** The later
compiler group had the program and extensive documentation, and still
proposed changes that cut across the design. Documentation records what the
theory's holders wrote down; it does not transmit the theory. In Naur's
account there is one way a theory gets built: by working on the program —
production experience, for new holders in interaction with people who
already hold it.

**The object does not hold still.** Products change; in any long-lived
setting the machinery changes; and — the squeeze premise from the
history document — a scope that pays for itself keeps bringing novelty. A theory is a theory of its object.
When the object changes and the theory does not, the holder drifts into the
position of the later group: equipped with an account of how things used to
be organized, and why they no longer are.

**Therefore holding is rebuilding.** A theory adequate over a horizon must
be built from production experience and rebuilt as experience accumulates —
because experience is the only thing that builds it, and because what it
must fit keeps moving. Holding a theory of a changing system just is
continually rebuilding it from the system's own production. That is
learning: production experience causing retained changes to what the system
holds, keeping the theory — and with it the system's work — fit.

So learning was left out of the definition and returns as a conclusion: a
software factory system can exist without learning, but it cannot keep
changing coherently without it. The definition stays lean; the necessity is
earned.

## What this means per carrier

For people, the conclusion is familiar: theory maintenance is on-the-job
learning, which is exactly why the theory leaves when they do. For codified
assets, rebuilding means re-codification — the cost that bound the first
wave. For retained readable artifacts, rebuilding means revising the
artifacts from production experience and having the reader hold the revised
theory — cheap in principle, and exactly the holding-and-revision machinery
the previous document conjectures is not yet there. For weights, rebuilding
means retraining.

## What would refute it

A system holding a fitting theory across a long, novelty-bringing horizon
without revising it from experience. That requires the demand stream to
stop bringing novelty — the same degenerate scope that disarms the first
derivation. The two derivations are armed by the same parameter.

## What this does not yet give

Whether a particular revision made the theory or the system better, how
that would be measured, and who or what gets credit for the learning —
quality, measurement, and attribution — belong to the testing layer, not to
the derivation.
