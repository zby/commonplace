# To Bartosz Naskręcki: what to give the writer, and what to withhold from the referee

## TL;DR

In a Commonplace editing trial, a one-sentence reminder preserved little that writers would otherwise forget. What helped was spelling out **what the writer must not change**. In a separate literature check, withholding the claim a reader was supposed to verify led to narrower verdicts on three of four claims. For Labyrinth, I’d try opposite changes at two handoffs: give the writer the referee’s exclusions; withhold the hoped-for conclusion from the first reader of a disputed source.

**Quick fix: send the limit of the verdict to the writer.** Your [Codex test](https://github.com/nasqret/labyrinth-exploration/pull/1) already got a fresh referee to enumerate all 64 four-vertex graphs. Suppose the next job is to shorten that result for a table. The writer is unlikely to forget the result. What might disappear is the difference between “replayed at four vertices” and “the same code was used at a larger size.”

In [Commonplace’s ten-document pilot](https://github.com/zby/commonplace/blob/main/kb/theory/evidence/full-write-briefs-cut-edit-drift-one-line-briefs-did-not.md), editors lost 14 of 78 requirements they could not infer from the document without a brief, 13 of 80 with a one-line brief, and 5 of 80 with the original detailed brief. These were document edits, not proofs. Commonplace adopted [optional writer-only briefs](https://github.com/zby/commonplace/blob/main/kb/reference/adr/092-write-briefs-are-optional-sidecars-named-by-a-validated-pointer.md), not longer summaries for every reader. Try the smallest version: when the coordinator hands a verdict to the writer, include the **exact claim licensed** and **one tempting stronger claim not licensed**. Ask for a compressed table row and check whether the distinction survives. No new project file is needed.

**Structural improvement: separate reading a theorem from matching it to your claim.** A fresh referee can still know the answer the project wants. In [one Commonplace case](https://github.com/zby/commonplace/blob/main/kb/theory/evidence/independent-pass-tightened-three-of-four-pirolli-verdicts.md), a first reader found source support for three claims. A reader who had not seen those claims reconstructed the source; a separate judge compared the two accounts and narrowed all three. One comparison had joined two observations into a relationship the source did not establish. The procedure changed in several ways, so this is a reason to try the split, not proof that blinding works.

Reserve it for a literature premise whose scope would decide whether a proof goes through. Freeze the needed proposition. Give a reader the cited theorem and ask for its hypotheses, quantifiers, and conclusion **without showing the needed proposition**. Then compare. Pilot it on one genuine match and one near miss: a stricter procedure that rejects both is no improvement.
