# Behavioral-authority addressability synthesis

## 1. Strongest claim the evidence supports

Commonplace provides **broad, representative addressability over the repository artifacts that directly carry behavioral authority**. In the six hard paths examined, the canonical goals, contracts, criteria, procedures, validator rules, and model-selection declarations can be identified, inspected, criticized, and selectively revised. The repository records operative revisions of natural-language authority (collection and lifecycle contracts) and symbolic authority (schemas and validator code), including later reuse and another revision of installed revision machinery.

The current review store also establishes routine use of one examined path: 269 completed explanatory-reach assay pairs from 2026-04-06 through 2026-08-04. This is evidence that the gate is used, not that it implements the anchor theory's full revised four-part test; the live gate still omits observed fit.

The evidence does **not** establish complete addressability of every repository-defined authority relation. It is an examined-path result, not proof that the unrepresented remainder is empty.

The strongest defensible formulation is:

> Commonplace makes the canonical artifacts and most intended consumption relations in the hard authority paths examined inspectable and selectively revisable, and it has demonstrated operative reuse of both natural-language and symbolic revision machinery.

## 2. Strongest counterexample or unresolved path

The strongest unresolved path is **generic maintainer admission authority**.

The declared frame puts designated maintainers inside the system, and retained episodes rely on maintainer or user approval to make changes incumbent. Yet the repository has no canonical representation of who is designated, the scope of the grant, the generic condition that admits a system change, or the binding from an approval event to the exact installed content version. Git records can help reconstruct a transition, but Commonplace explicitly gives commits and merges no framework-wide semantic meaning. Path-specific rules such as article publication approval do not supply a generic admission protocol.

This is stronger than a missing specialized procedure. An agent cannot selectively criticize or revise the grant relation as a repository object because no such object exists. It must rely on live user/harness roles or first invent a representation. Identical final files receive identical force whether or not the intended approval path was followed.

Requested model bindings are the next strongest unresolved path. The request and partition are addressable, but retained evidence shows requested/recorded provenance diverging from actual execution. This blocks a strong operative-installation claim, though it does not make the requested binding artifact itself unaddressable.

## 3. Proposed revision to the behavioral-authority decomposition

The audit supports one candidate addition inside the decomposition:

> **Applicability scope** — which target cohort, target aspect, or operation receives the force, under which activation trigger.

The explanatory-reach gate supplies the discriminating case: reviewer, review channel, and audit-trigger force are unchanged whether the gate applies to every note or only notes carrying `title-as-claim`; `requires_trait` changes the authority path's reach. Tag-README validation similarly depends on canonical type, affected cohort, and invocation trigger. Complete coverage cannot be computed over artifact names or consumer–channel–force triples alone.

Treat this as provisional wording—`applicability` may prove a clearer field name than generic `scope`—but retain the distinction in the audit model now.

Three other observed needs should remain neighbouring records rather than fields of behavioral authority:

- **Authorization/admission:** designation, grant scope, source-state decision rule, and content/version binding. This asks whether force was legitimately conferred, not whether the live path has force.
- **Realization/operativity:** whether a requested binding became the actual consumer and affected later behavior.
- **Dependency/revision closure:** which derived criteria, interfaces, and checks must change or be revalidated with a revised authority artifact.

Consumer, channel, and force still describe a live local link well. Applicability says where that link acts. Authorization, realization, and dependency records answer different questions that the stronger revision-affordance claim also needs.

## 4. Stable and provisional distinctions

### Stable enough to use

- Complete addressability is coverage over scoped repository-defined authority paths; it is not global writability.
- Addressability, operativity, warrant, continuity, leverage, and compounding are separate claims.
- A review gate's audit-trigger force is not admission or blocking force.
- Requested configuration and realized execution are different facts.
- An installed artifact can exercise force without retaining the authorization that admitted it.
- A revision method can be reused and remain open to another challenge without covering every authority path.
- Consumer, channel, and force are a useful local description, but artifact identity alone is not the coverage unit.

### Provisional

- Whether `applicability scope` becomes a literal fourth field in the durable behavioral-authority definition or a mandatory adjacent qualifier.
- The representation and enforcement form for maintainer designation, grant scope, and content-bound approval.
- How much model-execution evidence is sufficient to treat a requested binding as realized across different harnesses.
- How dependency and freshness records should cover semantic derivatives such as theory → collection contract → gate.
- Any claim that the examined paths exhaust Commonplace's repository-defined behavioral authority.

## 5. Article-ready wording

The articles can responsibly say:

> In six hard Commonplace paths, the repository makes the operative goals, contracts, criteria, procedures, validators, and requested model bindings inspectable and selectively revisable; its review store shows routine use of the explanatory-reach gate, and its history records operative reuse of both natural-language and symbolic machinery. This supports broad, path-relative addressability, not complete coverage. The repository does not yet represent the designation and scoped admission grant by which a maintainer's approval makes a generic change incumbent, and requested model bindings are not reliably bound to observed execution.

For the companion article's sharper audit conclusion:

> The audit did not establish complete addressability of repository-defined behavioral authority. Its strongest counterexample is revision admission: Commonplace names designated maintainers and several path-specific approval requirements, but no canonical artifact records who is designated, what that grant covers, or which approval event authorizes the installed content version. Consumer, channel, and force remain useful for a live use; applicability scope is also needed to enumerate that use, while authorization, realization, and dependency closure require separate records.

Avoid saying:

- that Commonplace has complete addressability;
- that the missing admission representation makes the installed artifacts unreadable;
- that uncalibrated evaluators are unaddressable rather than weakly warranted;
- that provider weights or inference infrastructure are counterexamples inside the declared frame; or
- that the demonstrated lifecycle reuse establishes leverage or compounding.
