## Required distinctions and terminology

- Treat an **operative oracle** as a check whose output changes survival, use, rollback, or continued execution; do not infer acceptance force merely from a recorded grade.
- Separate the target checked, the oracle that checks it, and the force the result has.
- Separate **epistemic authority** (what may be relied on or called warranted) from **operational authority** (how much behavior a retained theory can authorize before another check).
- Distinguish prediction pre-registration, post-action consequence grading, prose-note status, executable-model replay, live-plan checking, and benchmark outcome.
- Use **explanatory-reach**, **warrant**, **discovery lifecycle**, and **freshness baseline** in their Commonplace senses.
- Treat code behavior as stronger evidence than README or skill-doctrine claims about what the harness enforces.

## Scope

Include:

- claim grammar and grading semantics;
- event evidence, notes, level transfer, executable rules, plan freshness, and halt-on-surprise behavior;
- the relation between immediate prediction fit, task success, model fit, and explanatory warrant;
- comparison with Commonplace and the workshop's five existing cases;
- implications for the response taxonomy, route-level asymmetry, and selection locus;
- empirically bounded observations about the campaign as claims reported by the repository.

Exclude:

- re-running ARC games;
- inspecting private game state or source;
- treating benchmark success as a component ablation;
- redesigning ARC in detail;
- modifying Commonplace library notes in this run;
- claiming that the campaign demonstrates causal benefits of prediction gating without a matched comparison.
