## Analytical definitions fixed by the inputs

- **Operative oracle:** the check whose result actually changes survival. For this commission, survival includes use, rollback, or continued execution, so an after-action check that discards the rest of a queue is operative even though it cannot prevent or roll back the action just observed. A recorded grade with no such force is not operative merely because it exists. **[B, CO, CL]**
- **Epistemic warrant:** support licensing reliance on specific truth-apt content over a specific domain. Support stays at the finest claim, conjunction, model, and scope the evidence identifies; comparative support for a bundle does not identify any component's contribution. **[CW]**
- **Explanatory-reach:** a claim's ability to keep working outside the cases that produced it because it captures why the pattern works. A criticizable explanation should expose load-bearing premises, rule out or constrain rivals, admit a specific failure, and remain accountable to observed fit. **[CE]**
- **Discovery lifecycle:** observation/anomaly → conjecture → consequence derivation → test/accumulation → acceptance under a named criterion → integration. Testing, acceptance, and integration are separate acts; a stated conjecture does not inherit acceptance. **[CD]**
- **Proposal-selection loop:** search produces a candidate, reject-capable evaluation can prevent adoption, and operative retention gives an accepted result a consumer, channel, and force. Repeated production without a reject-capable selection route is outside this subtype. **[CL, CO]**
- **Freshness baseline:** in Commonplace, a database record pins completed review evidence to exact note and criterion snapshots for one review pair and model partition. Freshness says the evidence still applies to current inputs; it is not endorsement or proof that report findings were handled. **[CV]**
- **Epistemic authority versus operational authority:** the brief requires separating what content may be relied on or called warranted from how much behavior retained content may authorize before another check. ARC can bound the second without granting the first. **[B, CA]**

## ARC objects and authority routes

The unit called “the theory” is not singular in ARC. At least six objects must be kept separate: a pre-action prediction string, its individually parsed claims, an optional ungraded reason, the editable prose notes, an optional executable `rules.py` model, and a generated action plan. Environment level/game state and the campaign scorecard are still other objects. **[AS, AP, AL, AU, AC, AR]**

| Route / target | Check or oracle | Timing | Implemented force | What the check can establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Admission of one manual action | `--predict` is required by the CLI; the prediction must be nonempty and parseable before `_paid_step` | before action | malformed or absent prediction prevents the action command from reaching the environment | pre-registration occurred in the accepted grammar, not that the prediction or explanation is good **[ACL, AP, AL]** |
| Structured prediction claims | deterministic comparison with the settled after-frame, level count, or game state | after the action | stores per-claim grades and aggregate `predict_ok`; returns `PREDICTED` or `SURPRISE`, but does not create a persistent block on the next standalone `act` | only the stated observable consequence on that transition **[AP, AL]** |
| Manual batch (`commit --step`) | every step has a parseable prediction; each executed step is graded | predictions parsed before the batch; grade after each action | first miss, level advance, game over, or win discards the remaining steps | a miss bounds continued execution of that queue; the surprising action has already happened **[AL, ACL]** |
| Prose `NOTES.md` | agent instruction says cite event IDs, separate Verified/Assumed/Plan, revise after misses, and demote across levels | during play | file is created and printed; misses and long levels produce nudges; no selected action path validates the headings, citations, claimed verification, or a post-miss update | self-declared status and recovery context, not harness-granted warrant **[AS, ACL, AI, AL]** |
| Executable `rules.py` replay | model `step` is compared with recorded transitions; exact `render` is pixel-checked when present, otherwise the model's own `observe` projection is compared after re-grounding | offline, over recorded history | first contradiction produces `MISMATCH`; declared `Unknown` gaps produce `INCOMPLETE`; no gaps/mismatch produces `HISTORY_FIT` | consistency with recorded transitions under the model's grounding and observation surface; exact board fit only when `render` is supplied **[AU, ACL]** |
| Rule-model search | A* searches `actions`/`step`, skips `Unknown` edges and optional dead states, and accepts goal states defined by the model | after replay inside `rules solve` | `MISMATCH` blocks search; `INCOMPLETE` does not, provided the current board can be grounded | reachability inside the supplied model, not truth outside modeled edges or observed history **[AU, ACL]** |
| Generated solve plan | source event ID, current observation hash, and `rules.py` hash must still match; each executed step is compared to `render` rows, `observe`, or expected level completion | freshness before execution; consequence check after every action | stale plan is refused; first live divergence discards remaining actions | current applicability plus sequential fit to model predictions; no rollback of the first divergent action **[AL, AU, AI]** |
| Environment progress | returned state and `levels_completed` | after action | controls level/game completion and reset state; completion can be recorded even when prediction grades miss | task progress, distinct from prediction or model fit **[AL, AC]** |
| Campaign scorecard | ARC benchmark replay/scorecard as reported by the README | after whole runs | external whole-system result; no in-harness theory status shown in supplied code | whole-system task outcome under the reported setup, not component contribution **[AR, B, CX]** |

## Prediction grammar and grading mechanics

- The grammar has `noop`, `change`, `cell X,Y=V`, `move X,Y DX,DY`, `vanish X,Y`, `region X0:X1,Y0:Y1`, `level+1`, and `win`; semicolon-separated clauses are parsed and graded separately. Aggregate prediction success requires every gradable clause to pass. **[AP, AS, AR]**
- `noop` checks same board shape and zero changed cells. `change` passes on a shape change, any changed cell, or a level advance. `win` checks environment state; `level+1` checks an increased completed-level count. **[AP]**
- `cell` checks only the after-frame value at one coordinate; it does not require that the cell changed or that the action caused the value. A claim naming the already-present value can therefore pass after a no-op. **[AP]**
- `move` flood-fills the before-frame same-colour object at the named coordinate, then checks that shifted cells have that colour and non-overlapping old cells were vacated. `vanish` checks that the before-frame component's cells no longer retain its colour. These checks do not forbid unrelated simultaneous changes. **[AP]**
- `region` requires at least one visible change and requires all changed cells to lie inside the half-open box; it does not predict which cells or values change. **[AP]**
- Unrecognized prose is stored as an ungraded `note`. If a prediction contains no structured claim, the parser adds an implied generic `change` claim. Thus the hard gate is a nonempty prediction with at least generic visible-change content, not a requirement that the prose mechanism itself be evaluated. **[AP]**
- `--because` is optional, recorded, and not graded. Explanatory reasons in `because` or prose claim fragments have no direct code oracle in the selected files. **[AL, ACL, AP]**
- Claims are graded against the settled result after the paid action. Prediction checking therefore pre-registers a consequence but cannot protect the current action from a bad theory; its direct operational protection is forward-looking when a queue exists. **[AL]**

## Event evidence, notes, and level transfer

- A run stores normalized settled frames, action/data, environment state, level counts, available actions, and action-count status in a contiguous JSONL event timeline. Live execution adds prediction text, per-claim grades, aggregate success, optional reason, and mutation ID before appending the event; receipts and rendered evidence are also written. **[AC, AL]**
- Inspection can expose the exact grid, crops, settled diffs, animation deltas, transition stories, and past-event views. Salience-ranked click candidates are explicitly labeled hypotheses, so derived perception suggestions are not presented as observations. **[AI]**
- The initial note template contains `Verified (cite event ids)`, `Assumed / open questions`, and `Plan`. This creates a proposed link from distilled prose back to episode evidence, but the selected implementation does not check the citations or derive the prose claims from events. **[ACL, AS]**
- On a level advance, the live notes are copied once into a level archive and the live file remains. Doctrine says prior Verified claims become Assumed until one test on the new board. Code shows a warning only while the live note's modification time is no newer than the archive; any later edit removes the warning, regardless of whether a cited re-test occurred. **[AS, AL, AI]**
- After at least three misses among the last ten graded predictions, status emits a nudge saying the notes' mechanics story is wrong and should be fixed. At 25 and 40 paid actions on one level it emits escalating prose/rules nudges. These are attention-routing signals, not action gates. **[AI]**
- The episode/rule pairing is structurally present but unevenly enforced. Event history plus event-cited notes could keep a prose rule re-derivable, while executable rules are actually replayed against the retained event history. The prose lineage is doctrinal; executable replay is operative. **[CR, AS, AC, AU]**

## Executable-model mechanics and epistemic limits

- The required model interface is `initial`, `step`, `actions`, `goal`, and `observe`; optional functions add deduplication keys, heuristics, exact rendering, and dead-state pruning. `Unknown(reason)` is an explicit modeled gap, while `None` means impossible in the current state. **[AU, AS]**
- Replay re-grounds at the opener, reset boundaries, level changes, and after some gaps. A modeled accepted action that returns `None`, a false goal on an observed level advance, a false dead result on an observed game over, a render mismatch, or an `observe` mismatch can produce the first `MISMATCH`. Explicit unknowns and ungroundable states instead accumulate gaps. **[AU]**
- `HISTORY_FIT` means no detected mismatch or gap on the walk that was performed. Without `render`, comparison is only equality under the author-chosen `observe` projection, so relevant board differences omitted by that projection are outside the check. The CLI itself warns that recorded-history fit does not establish unseen mechanics. **[AU, ACL]**
- `rules solve` recomputes replay, refuses a mismatch, but permits an incomplete replay. Unknown successor edges are skipped during search. A plan may therefore be operationally produced from a locally grounded modeled region even while other history remains explicitly unexplained. **[AU, ACL]**
- A solve plan records provenance for the current event, observation, and rule file. Live execution refuses changed provenance and compares each after-state to the saved prediction, stopping the queue at divergence. This is a hard applicability boundary for a plan, but it is not a claim that the model is explanatory or globally warranted. **[AU, AL, AI, CW]**
- Task completion and model/prediction fit are deliberately separable in the code. A level or game completion is returned as completion even when the same action's claim or model expectation misses; level-completion detail explicitly says to treat mechanics as unproven when the prediction also missed. **[AL]**

## Conflicts, overstatements, and doctrine/implementation gaps

1. **“Falsifiable claim” versus implemented admission.** The README describes a hard refusal without a falsifiable claim. Code hard-refuses an empty/malformed prediction, but arbitrary prose becomes generic `change`, and a `cell` claim can pass when the named value was already present. The enforced property is pre-registered, automatically gradable observable content at a potentially coarse grain, not a maximally discriminating or causal prediction. **[AR, AP, ACL]**
2. **Prediction fit versus model standing.** The README says a held prediction means the model is still standing and a miss locates where belief and reality split. Code supports exact localization of a failed observable clause. It does not link that clause to a unique explanatory model. A hit is compatible with rival models; a miss refutes the clause on that transition but need not refute every theory that motivated it. This limit follows from warrant granularity and explanatory-reach, not from a code defect. **[AR, AP, CW, CE]**
3. **“Every doctrine rule is enforced or graded.”** The README makes this broad co-design claim. Selected code enforces prediction presence, public-action availability, plan provenance, and some replay/queue checks. It only nudges or initializes note structure, post-miss revision, level re-verification, batching of “proven” mechanics, and escalation timing. The action routes do not inspect whether notes were repaired or whether a batch mechanic was genuinely verified. **[AR, AS, AL, AI, ACL]**
4. **One-page/full notes versus display code.** Doctrine requires one short page and says status prints it in full. Code imposes no page-size limit and bounds display to 120 lines with each line truncated to 240 characters, inserting an omission marker when longer. **[AR, AS, AI]**
5. **Level demotion is semantic doctrine but syntactic signaling.** Doctrine requires prior Verified claims to survive a new-level test. Code's warning is controlled by file modification time, so an edit is sufficient to remove it. No event citation or successful grade is required. **[AS, AI, AL]**
6. **Replay completeness versus solve authority.** Doctrine says model only verified mechanics and expose gaps honestly. Code does expose `INCOMPLETE`, but only `MISMATCH` blocks solve; a model with replay gaps may still authorize a plan from the current grounded region. The CLI warns about this rather than forbidding it. **[AS, AU, ACL]**
7. **Plan halt is containment, not prevention.** The plan does halt on its first surprise as claimed, but grading occurs after the action. The first theory-breaking action is spent and retained; only later queued actions are prevented. There is no equivalent persistent stop after a standalone surprise. **[AR, AS, AL]**
8. **Campaign claims exceed supplied primary artifacts.** The README gives exact totals and a scorecard reference, while the brief states no run directories are present. The numbers are usable only as repository-reported observations in this run, not independently reconstructed event evidence. **[AR, B]**

## Explicit inferences from the evidence

- **INFERENCE — ARC's operative oracle is route-level and temporal, not one system-wide gate.** Before an action, the hard oracle checks that a prediction exists in the grammar. After the action, observation grades the prediction. Only queued routes give that grade immediate survival force over future actions. Replay and freshness add separate force over model search and plan use. Treating “the ARC oracle” as one check would collapse distinct targets, timings, and consequences. **Basis: AP, AL, AU, AI, ACL; operative-oracle definition from B, CO, CL.**
- **INFERENCE — ARC contains operational authority more strongly than epistemic authority.** A theory can remain only self-declared in notes, or an executable model can remain `INCOMPLETE`, while a plan derived from it is still bounded by provenance and live step checks. Conversely, successful step checks permit continued behavior without granting general explanatory warrant. **Basis: AS, AL, AU, AI, CW, CA.**
- **INFERENCE — queued ARC execution has a one-surprise forward exposure boundary.** Within one manual batch or solve plan, no action after the first missed prediction executes. The first mismatching action already executed, and separate later standalone commands remain possible. This is a horizon on action authority, not rollback and not theory rejection. **Basis: AL.**
- **INFERENCE — the selection locus is often plans/actions rather than theories.** Prediction misses are retained as evidence; prose theories are rewritten by the agent without a harness acceptance event; `rules.py` is overwritten outside the selected code's evaluator. What the implementation decisively rejects is an absent prediction, a contradictory model's access to search, a stale plan, or the remaining suffix of a diverging queue. ARC therefore should not be described as selecting an accepted population of theories without further evidence. **Basis: AP, AL, AU, ACL; selection definition from CL, CO.**
- **INFERENCE — ARC realizes consequence-mediated participation.** Explanations in notes or executable state models guide predictions and plans, while the hard live oracle mostly checks observable consequences rather than the explanation itself. This differs from both explanation exclusion and direct epistemic grading. **Basis: AS, AP, AL, AU; comparison from EB, EA.**
- **INFERENCE — the event/model route is a stronger episode/rule pair than the event/note route.** Both retain event evidence alongside a distilled representation. Only executable rules have an automatic replay relation; note-to-event lineage depends on agent-authored event citations and compliance. **Basis: AC, ACL, AS, AU, CR.**
- **INFERENCE — ARC does not by itself test the weak-oracle underselection conjecture over explanatory quality.** The supplied campaign has no independently characterized candidate theory population, no accept/reject boundary for prose explanations, and no calibrated explanation-quality outcome. ARC can still exemplify unequal checks and route-level authority, but calling the campaign a selection result about explanations would exceed the conjecture's conditions. **Basis: CO, CL, AR, AP, AL, AU.**
- **INFERENCE — the reported plan/test miss-rate gap is expected to mix selection with execution mode.** Doctrine routes exploration to single actions and verified mechanics to plans, so the two classes differ before the plan mechanism acts. The reported contrast cannot identify planning, prediction gating, or halt-on-surprise as the cause of the lower planned-step miss rate. **Basis: AR, AS, CX.**
- **INFERENCE — the participation × containment 2×2 is not falsified, but its system-level use is underspecified.** ARC can be placed in the participates/contained cell at the executable-plan route, yet its notes, standalone-action, batch, replay, and benchmark routes contain different objects with different force. The case pressures the taxonomy to classify authority routes and to say whether containment is epistemic, operational, or both. **Basis: EA, EB, AP, AL, AU, AI.**

## Unresolved questions and markers

- **DEFINE: containment in the participation × containment taxonomy.** Does it mean bounding epistemic status, bounding downstream operational authority, or either? The answer changes whether ARC is merely another contained/participating case or evidence for two containment axes. **[EA, EB, AL, AU, B]**
- **DEFINE: explanation participation.** Distinguish direct evaluation/retention of explanatory content from consequence-mediated use, where a model generates predictions or actions and only their observable results are checked. ARC primarily supplies the second. **[AS, AP, AL, AU]**
- **DEFINE: ARC “model.”** In source prose, “model” can refer to the agent's current belief, a prose mechanics story, a narrow simulator, or the formal `rules.py` world model. Claims about fit or warrant must name which object. **[AR, AS, AU]**
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED (only for independent campaign verification):** run directories or equivalent exported events, receipts, notes archives, verification files, and plans; independently inspectable scorecard evidence for the reported totals. Their absence does not block the code-grounded architecture description. **[B, AR]**
- **EVIDENCE NEEDED (only for agent-compliance claims):** campaign artifacts showing notes were actually updated after misses, event citations were accurate, prior-level rules were genuinely retested, and only verified mechanics entered batches. Code and doctrine alone cannot establish compliance. **[B, AS, AL, AI]**

The current whole-system score and selected action-class rates do not isolate any component. **[B, AR, CX, CW]**

## Reconstruction bottom line

The strongest code-grounded account is not “ARC verifies theories before acting.” It is: ARC requires a pre-registered observable prediction before a manual action, grades that prediction after the action, and gives the grade route-dependent force. It records standalone surprises, truncates queued execution at the first surprise, blocks contradictory executable models from search, rejects stale plans, and checks generated plans step by step. Prose explanations and note status remain largely agent-governed; executable-model fit is history- and projection-bounded; task success is a separate environment outcome. **[AP, AL, AU, AI, ACL, CW, CE]**
