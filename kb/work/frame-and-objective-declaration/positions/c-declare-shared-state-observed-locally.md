# Position C: B's single-owner rule, with local statement of what was observed

Author: Astra, in a comment the operator relayed on 2026-11-20. Recorded by the
author of position B from that comment, not by Astra, who may replace this
file. Astra's earlier suggestion of a writing or review rule is withdrawn in
the comment and is not recorded as a separate position.

## Diagnosis

Accepts B's diagnosis. Better wording of an attribution cannot fix the main
gap: a reader can follow every link and still not learn which human judgment
Commonplace intends to count. That is an open decision about the objective, not
a missing explanation of the boundary.

Adds one distinction B's rule lacks. A shared declaration can say that
designated maintainers are inside the boundary. It cannot say who made the
decision in a particular episode. That fact is local to the claim.

## Remedy

Rule, replacing B's:

> Declare shared assumptions once and cite them at the first claim that depends
> on them. State locally what was observed and what the evidence supports.

Two example claims show where the line falls:

- "The validator caught a member the search missed" is an observation. It needs
  neither a boundary recital nor a control for model strength.
- "The framework reduced the human judgment needed to maintain the index" is an
  effect claim. Naming the validator does not establish the saving.

Ownership: the frame note owns boundary and partition. The instrument note owns
the objective and its accounting scope. The proposals cite that declaration and
say what their comparison measures. Each assessment owns its horizon.

## Answers

- D1: no view stated.
- D2: cite at the first dependent claim; state locally what was observed.
- D3: no view stated.
- D4: recommendation to the operator, in two parts.
  - *Scope.* If the question is whether Commonplace is worth building and
    sustaining, count construction, operation, repair and maintenance over a
    stated period. A study may report the narrower cost of one more task, but
    not as the program's overall return.
  - *Whose judgment.* Do not narrow "human judgment" to "designated-maintainer
    judgment". If a workflow saves maintainer time by moving diagnosis to a
    contributor, that is not a saving. Count the judgment the work requires and
    report who supplied it. Who is inside the system and whose effort counts
    need not be the same decision.
- D5: the instrument note owns the objective and its accounting scope; the
  proposals cite it.
- D6: narrower than B. Require the author of an effect claim to address the
  rival explanations that could change that conclusion. Do not import the
  closure note's declaration list into every effect claim.
- D7: no view stated beyond the ownership split above.
- D8: no writing or review rule now. First test the rule on the workshop's
  candidate set. The test is whether a cold reader draws the right inference,
  not whether every note contains a frame link.

## Against other positions

- **B, rule.** Citation alone leaves the episode-level fact unstated.
- **B, D4.** "Designated-maintainer judgment" ties the denominator to the
  boundary and lets a workflow show a saving by moving judgment outside it.
- **B, D6.** Importing the closure protocol is heavier than an effect claim
  needs.
