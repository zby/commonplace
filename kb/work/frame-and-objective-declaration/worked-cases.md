# Three changes tested against the marginal improvement claim

Only one of the three records shows a task the new mechanism handled better than the old one: the tag validator found a real member that the documented search missed. The routing record shows use, not a better operator decision. The review gate caught a real ambiguity, but so did its predecessor. These are different verdicts even before weighing costs.

## Routing an operator explanation to a skill

**Change and comparison.** Commit `80a5f3a08` added `operator-brief`, exposed it through two skill directories, and directed agents to it from the root `AGENTS.md` when a consequential explanation depends on several concepts or a long causal chain. Its parent had general prose guidance but neither this skill nor this route. The added communication paragraph alone was 897 UTF-8 bytes, excluding its newline. That is a source-size difference, not a token count or an estimate of task failure.

**What happened.** The [agent-operability audit](../../reports/retained/agent-operability-audit-2026-08-31.md) and the [ingest-session volume report](../../reports/retained/codex-ingest-session-tool-result-volume-20260831/README.md) both identify the instruction as an input to their operator accounts. This is evidence of use on the intended work. Neither compares the operator's understanding or decision with and without the route. Neither says whether the agent found the skill through the paragraph rather than the skill listing. The [communication-failures report](../../reports/retained/provider-treatment-of-operator-communication-failures-2026-08-29.md) proposes comparisons; it does not report their results.

**What could reverse the decision?** On unrelated tasks the paragraph consumes context even when nobody opens the skill. A task near its effective limit could fail and need rework. No such failure is attributed to this paragraph in these records. Later commit `e8e7ad8b4` shortened the root file, including this paragraph, while keeping the route. That gives a practical alternative to the original wording, not evidence that the original caused a failure. For the historical change the overall judgment stays open even if context cost is negligible: the benefit of the route itself is unknown. To decide whether to keep a root pointer, compare matched explanations with the skill available in both conditions and only the pointer varied. Ask what the operator understood or could decide. Reuse evidence about near-limit tasks across root-file changes rather than charging each pointer for a separate study.

## Checking a tag head against the actual members

**Change and comparison.** Before [ADR 026](../../reference/adr/026-tag-readme-type-with-completeness-and-coverage-marks.md), the documented search matched a tag on the `tags:` line. Commit `947698052` added checks against parsed membership. In the `learning-theory` pilot, the workshop entry in `ba1a7d9f` names the missed note. The parent version of `kb/notes/continual-learning-open-problem-is-behaviour-not-knowledge.md` puts `learning-theory` on the line *after* `tags:`. The pilot diff both gives that member a child tag and adds a caveat about the blind spot to the search recipe. This is a before/after comparison on a named input, not an inference from the validator's existence. The [trace](../../theory/evidence/tag-readme-trace-observed-causal-connection.md) separates the coverage catch from the earlier human decision to split the oversized index.

**Gain and cost.** For checking coverage on that pilot, the additional catch is established. It was not an observed failure of an already enforced completeness promise. The same mechanism also creates continuing work: when `dab163c65` extended single-note validation to related marked heads, two entries were added to the `artifact-analysis` head. That is a concrete example of an edit requiring attention to another file. It can be worthwhile maintenance, not necessarily wasted work. The 15-head migration in `9976a0811` establishes rollout effort, but charging its entire cost to this one check would also charge it for the type split.

**Judgment.** Better detection on the pilot stands regardless of the unmeasured effort. Whether maintaining marked heads was better on balance remains open: if heads are edited often and rarely relied on to stop a search, continuing upkeep can outweigh occasional avoided misses. The useful next evidence is how often readers rely on the mark and what omission or search it saves, alongside repairs induced by the check. A task that neither uses nor maintains a marked head is not evidence of that burden.

## Adding a review gate for ambiguous sentences

**Change and comparison.** Commit `71612e965` added `sentence/parsing-ambiguity` to the production catalogue and retained its experimental counterpart. The criterion asks for two *plausible* readings, not merely two possible parses. On the workshop [baseline](../review-revise-gated/baseline.md), “The mistake is not storing a trace” admits opposite advice: failing to store is wrong, or storing is fine. The [run-08 reviewer](../review-revise-gated/run-08/sentence-review.md) identified both, and the [revision](../review-revise-gated/run-08/revised.md) says storing is fine. The [audit](../review-revise-gated/run-08/gate-noise-audit.md) reports the same finding in runs 05–08. These are repeated reviews of one sentence, not four independent defects.

**Check against the predecessor.** At `70745b91b`, the earlier monolithic sentence reviews (`kb/work/review-revise/run-01` through `run-03`, each `sentence-review.md`) already flagged that very ambiguity. The new gate even uses the sentence as its fail example. The gated review produced a useful correction, but on this item it preserved detection rather than improving it. Later [review statistics](../error-catching/gate-stats.md) report a 43.9% lifetime catch rate over 246 verdicts for this gate. That establishes use on more than the example, not how many findings were right or would have escaped the earlier review.

**Judgment.** No incremental detection gain shown. A separately selectable gate could save effort when only this check is needed; if routinely bundled, warnings about harmless alternative readings could instead consume reviewer and author time. Neither saving nor unnecessary rework was measured for this gate in the cited runs. Compare the two review methods on previously unseen sentences, including ones with a harmless second reading, and count useful corrections and unnecessary revisions. Repeating the demonstration sentence would not settle the comparison.

## What to carry into step 2

The questions fit all three cases. What fails twice is the presumption in “the gain and its evidence”: a recorded use or a successful output need not be a gain over the predecessor. Ask **“What changed for the task compared with the prior state, if anything?”** before weighing losses. Distinguish an observed gain with an open overall judgment (tag coverage), an untested benefit (routing), and a result the predecessor already achieved (sentence review). Missing comparative evidence is not a defect in the form.

Keep the reversal question. No estimate of context-limit failures would establish that the routing paragraph helped an operator; no estimate of review cost would turn a repeated catch into a new one. Where the local gain *is* shown, as in tag coverage, ask which unmeasured losses could plausibly outweigh it. The cases support proceeding to the theory draft with that distinction. They do not establish that any of the three changes improved Commonplace overall.
