# Obligations when external outcomes do not assess a claim

> **Status:** Workshop boundary account, 2026-09-17. Implements the general
> case in the [main-path plan](./main-path-plan.md). It identifies missing
> support; it does not supply a general theory of warrant or a closure theorem.

An [externally tested theory builder](./externally-tested-theory-builder.md)
can compare outcomes without first proving every explanation it used to
produce them. The comparison supports performance on its assessed tasks and
conditions. It does not automatically establish each internal theory or
every broader scope the builder proposes.

The boundary applies to a claim and proposed use. State which consequences
the declared interface actually assesses. Where it does not assess the
claim, identify the additional obligation below before relying on it. A
builder can work on externally assessed tasks while also retaining broader
inquiry candidates; the entire system does not change category whenever it
considers an untested idea.

## 1. Support for the proposed use

State what would count against the claim, which unit the evidence supports,
and what kind of reliance that support licenses. The supported unit may be
a claim, conjunction, or model under a scope, as the
[warrant-granularity note](../../notes/theory-warrant-tracked-at-the-finest-granularity-evidence-licenses.md)
already requires. A system-level outcome does not uniquely locate a faulty
component or validate every component of a successful system.

The [retention-policy draft](./theory-retention-policy.md) owns candidate
retention, experimental use, routine reliance, and codification thresholds.
Those policies remain to be specified for particular uses. Calling a theory
[tentative](./tentative-theory.md) does not license all of them. A claim with
insufficient support may remain a clearly marked inquiry candidate, be
narrowed to tested scope, or be declined for the proposed use.

## 2. A comparison level for changing an objective

Identify what makes a claimed improvement better when a revision changes
the acceptance rule itself. External users can authorize a changed demand;
the comparison must then report the new objective and distinguish that
change from improvement under the old one. A new request may instead
instantiate the same objective, without changing it.

When no external acceptance applies, the builder must state what comparison
level licenses the objective change or refrain from claiming improvement.
This is the [objective clause](./theory-retention-policy.md#the-objective-clause),
not an inference that computation prohibits objective change. Neither the
scope of externally tested tasks nor autonomous role allocation proves the
contested seed-closure claim in the
[Gödel-machine comparison](./goedel-machine-comparison.md).

## 3. Attribution beyond the observed outcome

An external judge may establish that a task failed while leaving open
whether the cause was a theory, its interpretation, retrieval, execution, or
the environment. To assert a particular cause, specify a discriminating
trace, intervention, or test that could distinguish the alternatives. A
plausible explanation is a candidate for that test.

The [ideal-interpreter account](./resource-bounded-ideal-interpreter.md)
is a proposed comparison device when the claim specifically concerns
interpretive fidelity. Its scope, realizability, and completion conditions
remain open. Other failures may be localized using ordinary probes without
settling those questions. Failed attribution limits the causal claim; it
does not erase an observed performance difference under a sound comparison.

## What stays inside the main path

Diagnosing a failure, choosing what to observe, revising a self-theory, or
running an active experiment stays inside the main path when the resulting
performance claim faces the declared external assessment. A diagnosis may
remain provisional while its proposed repair is tested. The
[reflective episode](./main-path-episodes.md#2-reflective-machinery-revision)
illustrates that distinction.

Likewise, internal proxy tests can guide development. They do not become the
external acceptance criterion merely because the builder passed them. An
external outcome comparison still needs its declared sampling, exposure,
objective, and budget conditions; being external is not a claim of
infallibility.

## Disposition of claims beyond the interface

For an unsupported extension of scope, record the unassessed consequence,
the intended use, the evidence missing for that use, and the next bounded
test or reason to defer it. The
[third episode](./main-path-episodes.md#3-a-broader-claim-without-external-assessment)
shows this for a retrieval rule generalized beyond the task population.

This account identifies obligations. It does not resolve the general
retention policy, interpreter fidelity, or the internal operations of
[theory refinement](./theory-refinement-interface.md). Those remain separate
questions even when the main outcome comparison can proceed.
