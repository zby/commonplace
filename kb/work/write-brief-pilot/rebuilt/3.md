# Brief: Prototype standing as revision cost

## Governing question

What makes a theory still cheap to revise or reject — its "prototype" standing — and is that standing set by whether the theory is written in natural language or in symbolic form, or by whether it has been accepted?

## Audience and reader update

Readers are agents and maintainers reasoning about when a Commonplace theory, or any inspectable theory, should be codified, bound to consumers, or kept revisable. The pre-formal-stage note uses this one as the definition of the high-standing case, so the reader must come away with a usable definition: standing is expected revision cost, and that cost has exactly two components. The update the note should force is a decoupling: prose is not automatically cheap to change, code is not automatically entrenched, and acceptance is a separate axis from standing.

## Target claim or purpose

Title-as-claim, universal over theories in the addressable sense already defined in the warrant-granularity note. A theory's prototype standing is its expected revision cost, which is the total of (1) external binding — consumers coupled to the current version, to which a revision propagates — and (2) intrinsic reconstruction cost — investment a revision discards. Natural-language versus symbolic form determines neither component. Acceptance adds no consumer and discards nothing, so it is a separate axis. Anything that appears to set standing must reduce to one of the two components.

## Must keep

- The definition, with "expected" meaning anticipated at assessment time, not probability-weighted, and no formula or weighting.
- The separation from the discovery lifecycle and from acceptance: an accepted theory can keep prototype standing; a conjecture can be entrenched before acceptance.
- The vocabulary-collision disclaimer separating this "prototype" from the collection-prototype sense.
- Both components with concrete examples, including that binding need not be machine-readable (procedures, audits, contracts) and that a harness-loaded instruction is natural language with maximal binding.
- The argument for a total rather than a maximum: the two costs fall on different parties, so one can be zero while the other is large.
- The codification point: codification makes formal consumption available; the coupled consumer, not the form, changes standing. Allow a weak statistical correlation between symbolic form and investment, with its stated refuter.
- The binding-before-acceptance consequence: binding before acceptance for the bound scope spends unlicensed standing, unless chosen for coordination value or forced by an enduring constraint, weighed as the current-task-fit note weighs entrenchment.
- Per-part assessment at the grain consumers bind to.
- Unbound formal models as prototypes, and cheaper formalization lowering only some bundle components; proofs warrant entailment, not world fit. The bitter-lesson portfolio note relies on this passage.
- The explicit refutation conditions, including the reduction of authority and rollback cost to binding, and the admission that no paired case has been observed.

## Exclusions

- No measurement scheme or threshold for either component; leave that as an open question.
- No claim about distributed-parametric form, where retraining makes form set reconstruction cost; exclude it in Scope.
- Procedures and state records are out of the "theory" sense, since they are superseded rather than refuted.
- Do not resolve whether a bounded operative trial counts as binding before acceptance.

## Scope, modality, and terminology

Universal, with refuters stated in the body. Two transfers are flagged as transfers: the current-task-fit mechanism applied to theories, and the Gödel-machine prompt-editing case read as a theory case. Keep "external binding", "intrinsic reconstruction cost", "prototype standing", "acceptance", and "representational form" fixed; do not use "entrenched" as a synonym for standing loss without tying it to binding. The form bound covers natural language versus symbolic form only.

## Reserved decisions

- Adding a third component, or weighting the two, would change the claim; the operator decides.
- Whether to settle the trial-binding question or the grain question for partially codified theories.
- Any change to the "prototype" term, given the collision with collection prototypes.
