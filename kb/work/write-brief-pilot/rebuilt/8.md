# Brief: Lead article — can a fixed-weight theory builder learn?

## Governing question

Can a system built from today's LLMs, with fixed weights, run Popper's conjecture-and-criticism cycle over explicit theories with computation performing every step, and learn from it? What would show that it does?

## Audience and reader update

Technical outside readers with no KB context. The reader should leave understanding three things. First, what a theory builder is: the four conditions, with no success condition. Second, the bet: a fully automated one that learns is buildable now. Third, why it is plausible: a precise definition applied by an LLM interpreter occupies the middle between formalizing everything (Schmidhuber's Gödel machine) and leaving it to people (Naur). The reader should also learn the bet's costs, its compatibility with the Bitter Lesson, and how learning would be tested. This is the lead for four supplements: testing, bootstrap, software house, and survey. They cite it for the bet and for the definition, so both must stay explicit.

## Target claim or purpose

State and motivate a falsifiable bet, not a result. Frame theory building as a distinct learning paradigm whose payoffs follow from knowledge held in explicit text. The payoffs are learning without retraining, sample efficiency after a structure-preserving shift, and readable learned knowledge. Keep them apart from three conjectural advantages: criticism carries content, theories can be revised in parts, and persistence saves work.
