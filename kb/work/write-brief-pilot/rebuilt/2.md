# Brief: Current-task fit does not warrant costly entrenchment

## Governing question

When a KB structure (type, schema, link vocabulary, routing convention, validator) fits the current work well, what additionally justifies making it costly to replace — and what role, if any, does option value play in that decision?

## Audience and reader update

Agents and maintainers deciding whether to harden Commonplace structure. They should leave able to separate adopting a structure from entrenching it, to name which of three warrants a costly commitment rests on, and to see that option reasoning governs timing only. Several notes cite this one for the adoption-versus-entrenchment distinction and for "more adopters raise migration cost without earning transfer"; keep both easy to cite.

## Target claim or purpose

Title-as-claim: current-task fit licenses adoption at the narrowest useful scope, not costly entrenchment. Entrenchment needs an enduring constraint, discriminating transfer evidence or proof for a stated scope, or actual coordination value. Real-options reasoning, drawn from the Pindyck ingest, decides when to commit or whether to preserve an alternative; it is not a fourth warrant.

## Must keep

- Definitions of adoption and entrenchment, and the point that cheap reversible or additive choices fall outside the claim.
- The three warrants, each with its link (boundary-preserving rival, derivation versus earned scope, coordination-value definition).
- The three conditions for a meaningful option, and the passive-waiting versus bounded-probe distinction, including that a probe building the full dependency is exercising the choice.
- The Commonplace timing consequence: delay cost can defeat deferral but cannot create a warrant.
- The explicit caution that the Commonplace cost terms are mappings, not Pindyck's claims.

## Exclusions

No numeric thresholds or ranking of costs; no claim that applying the test improves outcomes; no opposition to scenario-derived architecture.

## Scope, modality, and terminology

Universal within its stated condition (choices that destroy an alternative or create costly dependencies); qualitative. Keep "adoption", "entrenchment", "warrant", and "option" fixed. Transfer from investment theory is marked as a cross-domain inference.

## Reserved decisions

Adding a fourth warrant, or letting option value count as one, is an operator decision. So is resolving the contested single-purpose-KB caveat raised by the universal-framework note.
