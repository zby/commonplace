# Brief: Epistemic architecture of an external system

## Governing question

Does an external memory subsystem or agentic system produce knowledge, and if so by which routes? How should an analyst establish this from inspectable sources without mistaking storage, retrieval, fluent generation, or later use for knowledge production, and without collapsing the answer into a single grade?

## Audience and reader update

The executor is an agent applying the procedure in one of two ways: standalone, or as the epistemic lens that `analyse-agentic-system` invokes with a frozen boundary and canonical records. The executor should come away able to trace, for each material route, what truth-apt content is acquired, reshaped, derived, or conjectured; what checks it; what acceptance and authority the check confers; and what reaches later behavior. It then returns bounded findings in a fixed six-block output. The main skill relies on two things here: this procedure's orchestrated overlay mode, and its keeping `architectural status` in its own vocabulary.

## Target claim or purpose

A procedure whose goal is route-by-route, evidence-bounded findings about a system's epistemic architecture. The findings inform a review; they do not accept the system's claims. Define "epistemic architecture" and "truth-apt content" up front. The discovery lifecycle and the acceptance definition come from the KB's theory. The procedure turns them into executable classification rules.

## Must keep

- The prerequisites list and the material-route test. Plumbing is included only when it affects lineage, warrant, or force, or belongs to a claim. Omitted route families must be named, along with the conclusions their omission prevents.
- Five separate evidence layers (implementation, doctrine/design, reported operation, observed run, causal experiment) that must never be upgraded into one another. Form-appropriate inspection of natural-language, symbolic, and parametric content, with `not determinable` where probes cannot individuate content.
- The scope branches: stop for ranking or adoption questions; produce output 1 only when no source boundary exists; treat operational or lab-tracking purpose as a boundary, not a failure.
- Orchestrated overlay mode: cite canonical IDs rather than copying records; use local proposal tags for new records; never mint canonical IDs; send targeted-read requests and anchored corrections; write IDs in full with no ranges. Keep quote-anchoring rules consistent with the main skill's publication matching.
- The six output blocks and their field schemas. These are the ledger contract.
- One functional kind per ledger row, with checking never merged with disposition and retention never merged with lifecycle integration.
- The two separate status vocabularies: the five architectural statuses, and the observed candidate states. Keep the worked example showing that an artifact plus implemented routes, with no linking trace, yields `not determinable`.
- Content-edge classification in order (acquisition, non-ampliative reshaping, entailed derivation, ampliative conjecture, indeterminate), with non-truth-apt adaptations kept outside that taxonomy.
- Definitions of check target, evaluator, operative result, epistemic authority, operational authority, and behavioral-authority path, with the target named before the evaluator.
- The early-branch logic for storage-only systems and for claims with no supporting route. The claim branch must never discard remaining implemented routes.
- The license separations in step 7, and component attribution only at the grain of the actual contrast.
- The conclusion rules: acquired is not produced; acceptance needs a named criterion, intended use, and scope; integration comes only after acceptance.
- The misuse guards and the Verify checklist.

## Exclusions

- No system-wide epistemic score, oracle, or verdict.
- No product ranking, adoption advice, or ontology design.
- No prescription that the system should hold natural-language claims, run a proposal loop, use Commonplace storage, or adopt a universal ontology.
- No theory-builder or learning assessment; the main skill owns that.
- No rationale for why each distinction matters beyond what edge cases need.

## Scope, modality, and terminology

Prescriptive and imperative, with explicit branches. Terms must match KB definitions: discovery lifecycle, acceptance, behavioral authority, representational form, epistemic versus operational authority. `implemented` is an architectural status, not a conclusion status, so do not align it with the main skill's vocabulary. Negative findings are always scoped to a recorded search boundary.

## Reserved decisions

- Changing the six-block schema, the status vocabularies, or the overlay contract, since the main skill and its result type depend on them.
- Merging this procedure's statuses with the main skill's conclusion statuses.
- Adding a system-level epistemic verdict.
