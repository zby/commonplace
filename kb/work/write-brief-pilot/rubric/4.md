1. [governing claim] The bitter lesson can apply gradually: whatever portion of a path has moved to scalable production is governed by the lesson and compatible with it.
2. [governing claim] The remainder's difficulty does not retroactively convert the moved portion back into hand-built knowledge that will lose.
3. [governing claim] A moved portion large enough to be practical is worth having, and is not merely scaffolding awaiting absorption.
4. [scope] Does not claim that the methods can reach complete automation.
5. [constraint] Presents the new defense's scope as assessing compatibility per portion of a path, in contrast to existing rows, which assess it for a methodology or an artifact class.
6. [constraint] The portfolio's role table has a new row for this defense stating what it contributes.
7. [constraint] The new row's second column states what the defense does not establish, so the row bounds itself.
8. [reader update] The reader can see why the new defense is not covered by an existing row.
9. [reader update] The reader can say what the program claims about a partly automated path.
10. [reader update] The reader can say what the program declines to claim about a partly automated path.
11. [reader update] The reader can say why the remainder's difficulty is predicted rather than embarrassing.
12. [constraint] States a reason why Commonplace's localized artifacts are not the hand-crafted features that lost (the disanalogy premise); the KB's sharpest candidate is that selection removes unearned reach, not structure, so structure survives when its reach is earned by a reject-capable acceptance test.
13. [constraint] States where the disanalogy reason stops.
14. [constraint] Addresses the two-class tension: commitments survive absorption because there is nothing to converge on (no ground truth, no oracle), while structured theories survive because their reach is earned by a reject-capable test.
15. [restraint] Does not assume the tidy synthesis that the bitter-lesson residue and the adverse-selection residue are one residue.
16. [constraint] Resolves, dissolves, or records as an open problem the two-class tension.
17. [restraint] States no fraction, percentage, or proportion (no "99% / 1%").
18. [constraint] States the claim over decisions classified by the reason each resisted transfer (the residue table in warranted transfer), not over a proportion.
19. [constraint] Keeps the standing concession: no current artifact, vocabulary, or decomposition is promised permanence, and stable guidance may migrate into weights; it is not withdrawn or weakened.
20. [restraint] Does not let the Bitter Lesson silently become an autonomy theorem; it concerns scalable production methods and possible power.
21. [constraint] Reconciles the defense with "scaffolding recurs at the moving frontier" by stating the condition under which that note's frontier clause supports it.
22. [restraint] Asserts no claim that steps stack toward closure.
23. [constraint] Carries the bounded gradual claim folded into the note it qualifies rather than deferring it to a new note, unless that new note has its own argument no existing note can carry.
24. [scope] The row is formulated so the hub article can cite it rather than argue it.
25. [reserved decision] Withdrawing or weakening the standing concession is reserved to the operator.
26. [reserved decision] If the two-class tension defeats the gradual claim rather than bounding it, that outcome is reserved to the operator.
27. [reserved decision] If the position is not a new defense but a restatement of an existing row, that result is reported rather than written as a new row.
