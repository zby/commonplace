# A context-operation interface bounds the projections its policy can realize

Retained state and active context are different runtime layers. Here, the retained substrate is projectable state available outside a bounded model input; retained controller state is tracked separately when it selects projections but is not itself projected into that input. Active context is what one bounded invocation actually receives. This follows the broader separation between [state retention and context assembly](./agent-runtime-analysis-should-separate-scheduling-context-state.md). A **context-operation interface** is the operations and permitted compositions through which a controller locates, materializes, transforms, and exposes retained state as active context. Its addressable units and exposure boundary are part of the interface. A controller or context policy chooses whether, when, and how to use that interface; it may be the receiving model, a learned controller, a host or proxy, or a mixed arrangement. Holding the retained substrate, model, and resource budget fixed, the interface bounds the projections that policy can realize.

Let `I` include the operation semantics, addressable units, composition rules, and the boundary where a view becomes model input. For retained state `S`, task and run signal `x`, fixed model or models `M`, and fixed budget `B`, define `Reach(I, S, x; M, B)` as the active-context views obtainable through legal interface traces. `B` includes the applicable active-context, model-call, time, and tool-use limits. A trace may locate, expand, transform, summarize, filter, delete, or expose retained material. It may also update retained state before exposure. “Projection” therefore means the task- and state-conditioned view delivered to the model, not a lossless or non-mutating mathematical projector. A required view lies outside `Reach` if producing it needs an unavailable operation, an unaddressable distinction, or a forbidden composition. In particular, [access and transformation impose distinct burdens](./access-burden-and-transformation-burden-are-distinct-query-dimensions.md): finding retained input does not imply that the interface can turn it into the view the call needs.

A policy selects legal traces, so it induces achieved coverage or a distribution within structural reach. A better policy may choose useful traces more often, sequence them more reliably, or reduce their cost. Those gains do not add a view outside `Reach`, nor do they show that excluded projections are unnecessary. This is the context-projection instance of the more general result that [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its omissions](./learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). If an intervention adds observations, external computation, tools, model capability, budget, or permission to change operation definitions, then a premise has changed. It is no longer only a better policy over the same interface.

## The interface as the controller's action space

Treat the controller that chooses context operations as a reinforcement-learning agent. Its policy maps what it observes (the task and run signal `x`, plus any retained controller state) to context operations. The context-operation interface is then its action space. The operations are the actions, and the addressable units are their arguments. The composition rules act as a legality constraint on action sequences, like an action mask that depends on history. The exposure boundary is the part of the transition that decides what the receiving model gets. `Reach` is the set of delivered views that some legal action sequence produces within budget `B`, where `B` plays the role of horizon and resource limits.

The claim is then the familiar point that optimization over a fixed action space cannot leave it. Training changes which trajectories the policy takes and how often. It cannot produce a trajectory the action space does not contain, so the best achievable return is bounded by the best view inside `Reach`. A rising learning curve is evidence about the policy. A plateau does not by itself say whether the policy or the action space is the limit. Reward quality is a third, separate limit: [feedback-trained memory management is oracle-dependent even when its operations are hand-designed](./memory-management-policy-is-learnable-but-oracle-dependent.md), and a good reward signal cannot compensate for missing actions.

The action-space design tradeoff is also familiar. A few coarse actions shorten the decision horizon and ease exploration and credit assignment, but they exclude trajectories a finer vocabulary would allow. An open action space such as code enlarges `Reach`, but the policy must then search a far larger space. Neither side of this tradeoff is ranked here.

One case needs care. When the trained policy is the receiving model itself, and some operations are model-computed (a summary, a sub-call), training the policy also changes `M`. The outputs of those operations change with it, so `Reach` can change through that channel. Such training is a premise change under the definition above, and an evaluation has to separate the two effects.

## Architectural contrasts

Different composition languages make the interface variable concrete. A [practitioner account of recursive language models](../sources/recursive-language-models-what-finally-gave-me-the-aha-moment.ingest.md) (RLM) describes model-authored programmatic exploration and transformation of a prompt held in a REPL, with results exposed through printed output, which the scaffold truncates, or a returned answer. The paper-described [lambda-RLM](../sources/the-y-combinator-for-llms-solving-long-context-rot.ingest.md) replaces arbitrary programs with a small typed combinator library. The model picks a task type from a fixed menu, and a deterministic planner builds and runs the combinator chain. Paper-described [agentic context management](../sources/acm-agentic-context-management-for-long-horizon-tasks.ingest.md) (ACM) fixes two memory operations, `manage_context` and `query_memory`, and learns when to invoke them or abstain because a search, retrieval, or commit-to-answer action fits better. These interfaces permit different legal traces even though each constructs bounded model context.

| | RLM (practitioner account) | lambda-RLM (paper) | ACM (paper) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who chooses | the receiving model | the model picks a task type; a deterministic planner fixes the rest | a learned controller (the trained agent) |
| Action space | arbitrary code in a REPL, plus recursive sub-calls | one choice from a fixed menu of task types | two memory operations, alongside search, retrieval, and answer actions |
| Composition | any program the sandbox runs | combinator chain built by the planner; model calls only at bounded leaves and specified synthesis steps | sequence of invocations the controller chooses |
| Exposure boundary | printed output, truncated by the scaffold, or a returned variable | result of the executed chain | summary in context; raw messages retrievable by query |
| What the source reports about how well it works | a mechanism explanation from building the system; no measured result is retained | design properties, not measurements: symbolic, deterministic, auditable control flow with the model as the only source of uncertainty; no benchmark result is retained | benchmark gains for ACM training on Qwen3.5-9B |

What each source reports, by row:

- **RLM.** The retained excerpts of the practitioner account explain the mechanism only; they carry no measured result.
- **lambda-RLM.** The retained excerpts claim reliability and auditability from the design: every combinator except the model call is deterministic and pre-verified. The KB's ingest retains no benchmark numbers, so this note reports none.
- **ACM.** In an ablation table, ACM training raises Pass@1 on Qwen3.5-9B from 0.635 to 0.727 on BrowseComp-Plus, from 0.405 to 0.425 on DeepSearchQA, and from 0.508 to 0.530 on SWE-Bench Verified. Average tool calls and peak tokens change in both directions across the three benchmarks. The retained excerpts do not say whether the untrained baseline row exposes the two memory operations. So the comparison cannot, on this record, separate a policy gain over a fixed action space from a change of action space.

These are source-described architectures and source-reported results, not verified executions. The three reports use different evidence kinds and different tasks, so they do not rank the interfaces. None of them tests interface adequacy in the sense below, and none estimates the effect of changing the interface alone.

Who selects a projection is a separate coordinate. In the RLM account the model selects its view; in ACM a learned controller chooses fixed operations. A host or proxy can instead assemble a view before the receiving model sees it, while a mixed push/pull arrangement can combine preloaded material with model-requested expansion. These placements can coexist with different operation vocabularies, so controller placement does not by itself determine structural reach. Within-run, cross-restart, and cross-task retained state instead describe how long prior state can condition selection. Changing a retained invocation policy is not by itself a change to operation semantics or composition rules.

## Evaluation consequence

An evaluation of controller improvement should report the retained substrate and addressable units; operation semantics and composition rules; controller placement and projection boundary; mutable policies or artifacts and their persistence horizon; model; and resource budget. It should identify which coordinates stayed fixed and which changed, including whether the baseline had the same action space as the trained controller. Gains with one fixed operation set show that a policy was useful in the tested regime. They do not establish that excluded operations are unnecessary. Testing interface adequacy requires a rival operation/composition interface, another constraint-changing intervention, or an argument that projections excluded by the interface cannot improve the objective. In RL terms, a learning curve over one action space tests the policy; an action-space ablation or extension tests the interface.

## Scope

Structural reach is not achieved use. Exact retained material can coexist with a lossy or missed active projection. A legal trace may never be discovered or produced reliably, and a delivered view may still fail to affect behavior. The claim here ends at exposure into active context; [storage, read-back, and contextual activation remain distinct](./knowledge-storage-does-not-imply-contextual-activation.md).

The claim does not rank interfaces. A restricted interface may trade admitted transformations for reliability, safety, inspectability, trainability, latency, or cost. An open-ended programming interface remains bounded by its primitives, sandbox, permitted compositions, model competence, and budget. The system contrasts show that these architecture coordinates vary across described systems. They do not estimate the causal performance effect of changing any one coordinate, and they leave open which interface is preferable under a given set of operational constraints.

The reinforcement-learning reading is a mapping of structure, not a requirement that the controller be trained by RL. The bound holds for a hand-written rule, a prompted model, or a trained policy alike, because it depends only on which traces are legal.
