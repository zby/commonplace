# A context-operation interface bounds the projections its policy can realize

Retained state and active context are different runtime layers. Here, the retained substrate is projectable state available outside a bounded model input; retained controller state is tracked separately when it selects projections but is not itself projected into that input. Active context is what one bounded invocation actually receives. This follows the broader separation between [state retention and context assembly](./agent-runtime-analysis-should-separate-scheduling-context-state.md). A **context-operation interface** is the operations and permitted compositions through which a controller locates, materializes, transforms, and exposes retained state as active context. Its addressable units and exposure boundary are part of the interface. A controller or context policy chooses whether, when, and how to use that interface; it may be the receiving model, a learned controller, a host or proxy, or a mixed arrangement. Holding the retained substrate, model, and resource budget fixed, the interface bounds the projections that policy can realize.

Let `I` include the operation semantics, addressable units, composition rules, and the boundary where a view becomes model input. For retained state `S`, task and run signal `x`, fixed model or models `M`, and fixed budget `B`, define `Reach(I, S, x; M, B)` as the active-context views obtainable through legal interface traces. `B` includes the applicable active-context, model-call, time, and tool-use limits. A trace may locate, expand, transform, summarize, filter, delete, or expose retained material. It may also update retained state before exposure. “Projection” therefore means the task- and state-conditioned view delivered to the model, not a lossless or non-mutating mathematical projector. A required view lies outside `Reach` if producing it needs an unavailable operation, an unaddressable distinction, or a forbidden composition. In particular, [access and transformation impose distinct burdens](./access-burden-and-transformation-burden-are-distinct-query-dimensions.md): finding retained input does not imply that the interface can turn it into the view the call needs.

A policy selects legal traces, so it induces achieved coverage or a distribution within structural reach. A better policy may choose useful traces more often, sequence them more reliably, or reduce their cost. Those gains do not add a view outside `Reach`, nor do they show that excluded projections are unnecessary. This is the context-projection instance of the more general result that [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its omissions](./learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). If an intervention adds observations, external computation, tools, model capability, budget, or permission to change operation definitions, then a premise has changed. It is no longer only a better policy over the same interface.

## The interface is the context controller's action space

In reinforcement-learning terms, the context-operation interface fixes the action space of the controller that chooses context operations. An action is one legal operation invocation over addressable units. Composition rules make the available actions depend on the current state, as action masking does. The budget `B` bounds episode length and cost. `Reach` is the set of active-context views that some legal action sequence can deliver within `B`.

The central claim then takes a familiar form. Let `A` be the action space that `I` defines, and hold the environment, model, observations, and budget fixed. No policy over `A` can deliver a view outside `Reach`. Its expected return is therefore bounded by the best return any policy over `A` can achieve. A learning curve on `A` measures how close a policy comes to that bound. It carries no information about how much higher the bound would be under a different action space `A'`, one that adds an operation, exposes a finer addressable unit, or permits a new composition. Policy improvement is within-action-space evidence. The adequacy of the action space is a separate question, and it is answered only by comparing action spaces or by arguing that the excluded views cannot raise return.

Two further points carry over from RL practice. First, a smaller action space is often easier to explore and assign credit in, so restricting `I` can improve what a policy actually achieves while shrinking `Reach`. Second, the controller's action space is not the observation space of the receiving model. The interface determines which views can reach that model. What the controller itself can observe when it chooses is a different boundary, covered by the information-omission case of the fixed-decomposition note. A trained controller also depends on its reward signal: [feedback-trained memory management is oracle-dependent even when its operations are hand-designed](./memory-management-policy-is-learnable-but-oracle-dependent.md). A weak reward limits how close training gets to the bound, while the action space sets the bound.

The RL analogy has limits. In some systems the receiving model is also the controller, so its own actions determine its next observation. A programmable interface makes each action a whole program, which gives a very large structured action space, not a small discrete menu. Changing a retained invocation policy, such as updated weights, is a policy change. It does not change `A`.

## Three systems with different action spaces

Different composition languages make the interface variable concrete. A [practitioner account of recursive language models](../sources/recursive-language-models-what-finally-gave-me-the-aha-moment.ingest.md) (RLM) describes model-authored programmatic exploration and transformation, with results exposed through printed output or a returned answer. The paper-described [lambda-RLM](../sources/the-y-combinator-for-llms-solving-long-context-rot.ingest.md) replaces arbitrary programs with a small typed combinator language. Paper-described [agentic context management](../sources/acm-agentic-context-management-for-long-horizon-tasks.ingest.md) (ACM) fixes two memory operations and learns when to invoke or abstain from them. These interfaces permit different legal traces even though each constructs bounded model context.

| | RLM (practitioner account) | lambda-RLM (preprint) | ACM (preprint) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence basis | One practitioner's thread from building the system; no code inspected here | Paper read from PDF; implementation not inspected | Paper read from web; implementation not inspected |
| Retained substrate | Prompt held as a `context` variable in a REPL | Prompt stored in a REPL | Raw earlier turns saved to disk under summary identifiers |
| Controller action space | Arbitrary code over the prompt: slice, search, transform, call sub-models | Model picks a task type from a fixed menu; a deterministic planner builds a chain of typed combinators (split, map, filter, reduce, and others) | The agent's usual actions plus two tools: summarize-and-archive earlier turns, and query the archive |
| Who selects the projection; what learns | The receiving model, per run; nothing is trained | Planner and combinators fixed; the model classifies and answers bounded leaf calls | A learned policy in the agent model chooses when to invoke or abstain; post-training updates its weights |
| Projection boundary | Truncated printed output, or a constructed variable returned as the answer | Leaf inputs within the context limit; composed result returned | Current summary plus whatever a query returns |
| Persistence horizon | REPL variables persist within a run | Per run | Archive within an episode; trained invocation policy across tasks |
| What the source reports about how well it works | A walkthrough on one counting problem where earlier architectures fail; no controlled measurement | More accurate than standard RLM in 29 of 36 model-task comparisons, with 3-6x lower latency among recursive methods; gains concentrate on weaker models, while standard RLM wins half the strong-model comparisons | Post-training raises pass@1 over the untrained ACM setup on all three benchmarks (0.635 to 0.727, 0.405 to 0.425, 0.508 to 0.530); peak context generally falls while tool calls often rise |

The reported results sit in different evidential tiers, and none is a code-grounded review. They also measure different contrasts, so they do not rank the three designs.

ACM's result is policy improvement inside a fixed action space. Training moved the policy closer to the best return over two operations. It does not test whether other operations, such as selective paging of tool output or retention by dependency, would raise that bound.

Lambda-RLM's comparison with standard RLM is the only one here that changes the action space. It changes the controller at the same time: a planner over combinators replaces a model writing code. Its reported pattern fits the trade-off above. The smaller action space helps most where the model's control skill is weakest. The larger reach of free-form code pays off when a strong model can use it, and in the paper's own failure cases the needed decomposition was outside the combinator library.

The RLM account shows what an open-ended action space admits. It does not measure what that reach achieves. Its interface is still bounded by the REPL's primitives, output truncation, model competence, and budget.

Who selects a projection is a separate coordinate. In RLM the model selects its view, in lambda-RLM a planner fixes the program shape, and in ACM a learned controller chooses fixed operations. A host or proxy can instead assemble a view before the receiving model sees it, while a mixed push/pull arrangement can combine preloaded material with model-requested expansion. These placements can coexist with different action spaces, so controller placement does not by itself determine structural reach. Within-run, cross-restart, and cross-task retained state instead describe how long prior state can condition selection.

## Evaluation consequence

An evaluation of controller improvement should report the retained substrate and addressable units; operation semantics and composition rules; controller placement and projection boundary; mutable policies or artifacts and their persistence horizon; model; and resource budget. It should identify which coordinates stayed fixed and which changed. Gains with one fixed action space show that a policy was useful in the tested regime. They do not establish that excluded operations are unnecessary. Testing interface adequacy requires a rival action space with the controller otherwise held fixed, another constraint-changing intervention, or an argument that projections excluded by the interface cannot improve the objective. A comparison that changes the action space and the controller together, as lambda-RLM's does, shows that the compound change mattered but does not isolate the interface.

## Scope

Structural reach is not achieved use. Exact retained material can coexist with a lossy or missed active projection. A legal trace may never be discovered or produced reliably, and a delivered view may still fail to affect behavior. The claim here ends at exposure into active context; [storage, read-back, and contextual activation remain distinct](./knowledge-storage-does-not-imply-contextual-activation.md).

The claim does not rank interfaces. A restricted interface may trade admitted transformations for reliability, safety, inspectability, trainability, latency, or cost. An open-ended programming interface remains bounded by its primitives, sandbox, permitted compositions, model competence, and budget. The system contrasts show that these architecture coordinates vary across described systems, and the reported results are the sources' own. They do not estimate the causal performance effect of changing any one coordinate, and they leave open which interface is preferable under a given set of operational constraints.
