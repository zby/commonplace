# A context-operation interface bounds the projections its policy can realize

Retained state and active context are different runtime layers. Here, the retained substrate is projectable state available outside a bounded model input; retained controller state is tracked separately when it selects projections but is not itself projected into that input. Active context is what one bounded invocation actually receives. This follows the broader separation between [state retention and context assembly](./agent-runtime-analysis-should-separate-scheduling-context-state.md). A **context-operation interface** is the operations and permitted compositions through which a controller locates, materializes, transforms, and exposes retained state as active context. Its addressable units and exposure boundary are part of the interface. A controller or context policy chooses whether, when, and how to use that interface; it may be the receiving model, a learned controller, a host or proxy, or a mixed arrangement. Holding the retained substrate, model, and resource budget fixed, the interface bounds the projections that policy can realize.

Let `I` include the operation semantics, addressable units, composition rules, and the boundary where a view becomes model input. For retained state `S`, task and run signal `x`, fixed model or models `M`, and fixed budget `B`, define `Reach(I, S, x; M, B)` as the active-context views obtainable through legal interface traces. `B` includes the applicable active-context, model-call, time, and tool-use limits. A trace may locate, expand, transform, summarize, filter, delete, or expose retained material. It may also update retained state before exposure. “Projection” therefore means the task- and state-conditioned view delivered to the model, not a lossless or non-mutating mathematical projector. A required view lies outside `Reach` if producing it needs an unavailable operation, an unaddressable distinction, or a forbidden composition. In particular, [access and transformation impose distinct burdens](./access-burden-and-transformation-burden-are-distinct-query-dimensions.md): finding retained input does not imply that the interface can turn it into the view the call needs.

A policy selects legal traces, so it induces achieved coverage or a distribution within structural reach. A better policy may choose useful traces more often, sequence them more reliably, or reduce their cost. Those gains do not add a view outside `Reach`, nor do they show that excluded projections are unnecessary. This is the context-projection instance of the more general result that [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its omissions](./learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). If an intervention adds observations, external computation, tools, model capability, budget, or permission to change operation definitions, then a premise has changed. It is no longer only a better policy over the same interface.

## The interface as the controller's action space

In reinforcement-learning terms, the context-operation interface is the context-facing part of the controller's action space, together with the transition rules those actions follow. Operation semantics say how each action changes retained state and the pending view. Composition rules say which action sequences are legal: a fixed menu of tools, parameterized macro-actions, or programs in a language. `Reach` is then the set of model inputs that legal action sequences can produce within budget `B`. When the controller is the receiving model, its context operations are information-gathering actions: they decide what the model will observe on its next step.

Policy learning, whether by RL, distillation, or prompting, searches over policies for one fixed action space. A reported gain therefore shows that a new policy scores higher than an old one in the same environment. It does not show how much value a different action space would have allowed. Changing the operations or composition rules changes the environment, so it is a different experiment from improving the policy.

The bound applies to the best achievable policy, not to what training actually reaches. If every legal trace under interface `I` is also legal under `I'`, at the same cost, then `Reach` under `I'` contains `Reach` under `I`, and the best policy under `I'` does at least as well. A learned policy under `I'` can still do worse. A larger or compositional action space makes exploration and credit assignment harder, and the controller may lack the competence to use it. This is why the claim ranks no interface: a restriction lowers the ceiling and can still raise the achieved result. The action space is also only one bound. The training signal is another, since [feedback-trained memory management is oracle-dependent even when its operations are hand-designed](./memory-management-policy-is-learnable-but-oracle-dependent.md).

The KB uses *action alphabet* in a different sense: the authorized set of operations that affect the world, which bounds authority ([compiling coordination preserves primitive, not aggregate, authority](./compiling-coordination-preserves-primitive-not-aggregate-authority.md)). The context-operation interface bounds which views reach the model. One controller can have both: ACM's policy chooses between context operations and task actions such as searching or answering in a single action space.

## Architectural contrasts

The three source-described systems below differ mainly in the controller's action space. None of the three descriptions was checked against code. The practitioner thread is a walkthrough without controlled comparison, and the other two are preprints. Each reported result was measured against its own baselines on its own tasks, so the rows are not comparable and do not rank the systems.

| | [RLM](../sources/recursive-language-models-what-finally-gave-me-the-aha-moment.ingest.md) (practitioner account) | [λ-RLM](../sources/the-y-combinator-for-llms-solving-long-context-rot.ingest.md) (preprint) | [ACM](../sources/acm-agentic-context-management-for-long-horizon-tasks.ingest.md) (preprint) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retained substrate | Prompt held as a REPL variable; variables persist across execution steps within a run | Prompt held in a REPL; per run | Raw earlier turns archived under an identifier; within one trajectory |
| Action space over context | Open-ended: any program the model writes, e.g. slicing, regex, sub-model calls | Restricted composition: a fixed library of typed combinators (split, map, filter, reduce, and others) | Fixed operations: `manage_context` (summarize and archive a prefix) and `query_memory` (retrieve from the archive) |
| Who chooses | The receiving model writes code each step | The model picks a task type from a fixed menu; a deterministic planner builds and runs the program | A trained policy chooses when to invoke each operation or abstain, alongside task actions |
| What reaches the model | Output printed and truncated by the scaffold, or a returned variable | Results of bounded leaf calls, composed by combinators | The running summary, plus whatever a query returns |
| What the source reports | A worked example on a constructed counting task and a claim of handling very long inputs; no controlled evaluation | Beats standard RLM in 29 of 36 model-task accuracy comparisons across four tasks and nine open-weight models, with 3–6x lower latency among recursive methods; standard RLM wins mostly with strong models and on code QA | Adding the two operations to a 9B model raises pass@1 over ReAct on three benchmarks (0.570→0.635 on BrowseComp-Plus); post-training the policy raises it further (to 0.727); peak context falls, tool calls often rise; a 4B model stops after about two turns and never reaches the regime where the operations help |

Read through the action-space lens, the reported results test different things. ACM's post-training gain is policy improvement inside a fixed action space. It shows that the invocation policy mattered. It does not test the two-operation split against rival operation sets, because every condition keeps that split or omits context operations entirely. The 4B case shows that a policy which barely explores cannot learn the value of operations it rarely reaches. λ-RLM against standard RLM is an interface comparison, but it also moves most control from the model to a planner, so it changes two coordinates at once. Its pattern of wins fits the gap between ceiling and achieved result: the restricted interface helped most where the model was weakest at writing control code, and open-ended programs won where task-specific control mattered. The RLM account reports the widest action space and no measurement of how often the model uses it well.

Who selects a projection is a separate coordinate from what the interface admits. A host or proxy can assemble a view before the receiving model sees it, and a mixed push/pull arrangement can combine preloaded material with model-requested expansion. These placements can coexist with different operation sets, so controller placement does not by itself determine structural reach. Within-run, cross-restart, and cross-task retained state describe how long prior state can condition selection. ACM's trained weights are a cross-task retained policy. Changing a retained invocation policy is not by itself a change to operation semantics or composition rules.

## Evaluation consequence

An evaluation of controller improvement should report the retained substrate and addressable units; operation semantics and composition rules; controller placement and projection boundary; mutable policies or artifacts and their persistence horizon; model; and resource budget. In RL terms, the action space and its transition rules belong in the environment specification, not in the method. The evaluation should identify which coordinates stayed fixed and which changed. Gains with one fixed operation set show that a policy was useful in the tested regime. They do not establish that excluded operations are unnecessary. Testing interface adequacy requires a rival operation/composition interface, another constraint-changing intervention, or an argument that projections excluded by the interface cannot improve the objective.

## Scope

Structural reach is not achieved use. Exact retained material can coexist with a lossy or missed active projection. A legal trace may never be discovered or produced reliably, and a delivered view may still fail to affect behavior. The claim here ends at exposure into active context; [storage, read-back, and contextual activation remain distinct](./knowledge-storage-does-not-imply-contextual-activation.md).

The claim does not rank interfaces. A restricted interface may trade admitted transformations for reliability, safety, inspectability, trainability, latency, or cost. An open-ended programming interface remains bounded by its primitives, sandbox, permitted compositions, model competence, and budget. The containment argument for best achievable value assumes nested legal traces at equal cost; interfaces that differ in both directions have no such ordering. The system contrasts show that these architecture coordinates vary across described systems, and the reported results come from the sources, not from checked implementations. They do not estimate the causal performance effect of changing any one coordinate, and they leave open which interface is preferable under a given set of operational constraints.
