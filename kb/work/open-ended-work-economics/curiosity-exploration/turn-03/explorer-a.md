# Explorer A: buy clarification before demanding a decisive question

## Decision and actual prior

**Select a bounded clarification of an unfamiliar direction before another deep verification comparison; keep verification as the strongest fallback.** The direction is whether accounts built from accepted changes overlook useful possibilities that never became proposals. This is not yet a claim about how often that happens. It addresses candidate origination, whereas another detailed tag chronology would mainly improve an already articulated question.

The [turn-2 integration](../turn-02/integration.md) proposed retaining an incumbent, strongest rival, discriminating distinction, and plausible evidence route when selecting inquiries. These are valuable once available. Requiring all four at admission would favor questions whose articulation someone has already paid for. The [current boundary decision](./coordination.md) correctly leaves verification eligible without making documentary accessibility the whole policy. My amendment is to distinguish investment in **making a question assessable** from investment in **answering it**. Neither warrants adopting its eventual claim.

All opportunity assessments below are documentary observations or conjectures. No external-source conclusion, executed experiment, causal demonstration, or comparative search advantage is offered. Costs beyond the bounded reading proposed here remain unknown.

## Origination: inspect what the current account leaves outside

A practical generation pass should start with a small, purpose-relevant body of work, not an invitation to imagine unlimited futures. Three prompts expose different omissions:

- Where does the same explanation cover cases whose consequential conditions differ? This produces reachable contrasts without inventing failure.
- Where did work succeed, but the contribution responsible is unresolved? This produces attribution questions, including ones presently blocked by missing encounters.
- Which relevant possibilities are excluded by the way the cases were collected? This produces unfamiliar directions without requiring an established anomaly.

The third prompt is not evidence that excluded possibilities matter. Its warrant for a small investment is the conjunction of a concrete selection boundary and a connection to the commissioned purpose. Here, a system learning to generate methodological improvements needs more than accounts of accepted improvements. It may need to understand what failed to become a candidate. That connection is specific enough to investigate, but not sufficient to promise useful discoveries.

Generation should leave genuinely different candidates visible rather than turning all three into “improve records.” Missing records sometimes block a causal question; a missed conceptual distinction can instead be investigated with existing arguments. Nor must every generation pass yield a new question. Finding that the incumbent already handles the proposed difference is a useful refusal to manufacture novelty.

## Three concrete opportunities

The [tag trace](../../../../theory/evidence/tag-readme-trace-observed-causal-connection.md) reports a changed check catching a block-YAML member that a search recipe missed. It also explicitly says the change relocated verification, not the two jobs of introduction and enumeration. These are documentary statements, not a new execution of the validator.

The candidate question is whether the improvement concerned enforcing an obligation, expanding detectable cases, or both. Its actual prior already distinguishes these matters. Consequently, merely restating “verification moved into code” would not improve the explanation. The best remaining opportunity is to locate which part of the improvement depends on checking coverage rather than enforcement alone. A stricter check over the same incomplete member set could enforce the wrong completeness judgment consistently.

The strongest rival is that the existing trace already explains everything material. Earlier requirements, the two checking methods, and the recorded missed member could discriminate an additional condition from a restatement. This opportunity has accessible named evidence and limited authority demands. Its learning value is clearer attribution of methodological improvement. Faster maintenance is a possible task benefit, not established here. Its disadvantage is diminishing explanatory return after two turns of closely related documentary work.

### 2. Successful revision: important, presently blocked

This question matters to the commissioned purpose even if it offers no immediate service saving. Yet accepted artifact differences cannot reconstruct an unavailable interaction. A further search requires a newly identified bounded recovery route, not another eloquent account of why the transcript would help. No such route is established here. Park retrospective attribution for missing evidence, not low importance. A later encounter could address a related question but would not repair this episode's missing history.

### 3. Unfamiliar direction: possibilities filtered out before proposal

The [candidate-generation survey](../../../../reference/where-change-candidates-come-from-in-commonplace.md) describes several channels: drafting shaped by contracts, connection reports, mechanical checks, and agent initiative. It explicitly presents illustrations rather than a catalogue. It also distinguishes an ephemeral report from the later act that incorporates its candidates. This is a documentary opening, not proof of current coverage or a measured loss rate.

The tentative question is: **does looking primarily at accepted changes hide differences between not noticing an opportunity, noticing without formulating a candidate, and formulating without selecting it?** These omissions would require different explanations. More evaluation would not help a possibility never noticed; better noticing would not settle why a developed candidate was rejected. This is initially unfamiliar within this run, not a claim of novelty in the KB or literature.

No complete causal rivals or test are needed to admit clarification. The initial counterpressure is already substantial: the survey may recognize all these distinctions, and failed possibilities may be too indeterminate to reconstruct. The project might have adequate accounts elsewhere. We must first discover whether the proposed distinction changes an actual judgment, rather than announcing a new architecture to preserve every rejected idea.

## A bounded clarification investment

For the third opportunity, recommend one documentary pass capped at three substantive artifacts and one result page. Start with the survey already named, follow one existing candidate-formulation example, and inspect one already-retained record of a parked or rejected direction. The first trial's compression branch is an available negative case: the brief records that it was parked without a retained comparative rationale. Do not infer its original motive.

The investment is for deciding whether further inquiry is warranted. Its sufficient output is one concrete distinction that the initial formulation missed, or a precise reason the question cannot currently be assessed. It need not settle causal explanations. The three-artifact limit bounds scope, not measured runtime or an optimal budget. If locating the third artifact requires open-ended search, return the gap rather than silently expanding the pass.

Continuation would require more than a polished promise. For example, discovering that a retained rejected candidate can be compared with its actual selection reason would establish an evidence route. Finding only successful end states would instead locate a sampling limitation, not establish systematically lost opportunities. Finding the distinction fully treated already would close this direction or redirect it to a narrower unresolved question. A proposal to collect future interactions, launch source work, or alter retention requires separate authority.

This pass is recommended, not performed as an experiment or commissioned here. Its admission evidence is deliberately weaker than its continuation evidence. The initial anchor prevents arbitrary novelty chasing; the requirement to change assessability prevents indefinite renewal on the same broad promise. Failure to clarify may reflect this learner's limitations or poor access, not an intrinsically unlearnable subject.

## Selection, rivals, and false negatives

I prefer clarification now because the active question is selection across unknowns. Verification has the clearest route to an answer but risks substituting another accessible case for that question. Revision attribution has a consequential target but no newly available route. The unfamiliar direction has a concrete documentary anchor, modest reading demands, and a chance to reveal whether our candidate pool is distorted before comparison begins. These are qualitative reasons, not estimated returns.

The strongest allocation alternative remains verification. If only one further substantive artifact could be read, or a delivery decision required a verification judgment now, that narrower opportunity could reasonably win. Neither condition is established. Operator attention should be requested for purpose or authority changes, not spent approving every interpretive choice. LLM reading effort is bounded here; no classical-compute investment is needed.

The strongest general rival is ordinary explanation-seeking inquiry that already permits preliminary reconnaissance. It can implement this entire account without compression language. This turn clarifies a usable selection judgment; it does not demonstrate an additional curiosity mechanism or superiority over that rival.

False negatives remain serious. Documentary anchors privilege recorded work; three-artifact limits may reject a direction whose vocabulary takes longer to learn; parking missing-history questions can indefinitely suppress important causal inquiry. Preserve these as limitations, not excuses to remove bounds. Reopening should follow a new accessible record, a more specific question, or a changed resource allowance—not repetition of the same future-value claim.
