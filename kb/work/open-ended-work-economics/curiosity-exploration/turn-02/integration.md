## A practical mechanism worth carrying forward

The candidate mechanism is **bounded comparison of questions generated from unresolved explanatory contrasts**. It need not begin with an error. A successful change can supply a contrast: something now works, is detected, or is accepted differently, while the contribution responsible remains unclear.

To originate questions, inspect a small set of successful changes or recurring outcomes against the explanations already assigned to them. Ask which difference those explanations leave unresolved. Candidate forms include: what changed besides the outcome; which distinction became checkable; why one case needs a condition another does not; and whether an apparent common mechanism preserves those differences. These are prompts for generating candidates, not requirements to manufacture an anomaly.

To select among candidates, retain the actual incumbent, its strongest rival, a distinction that would change the judgment, and a plausible evidence route. Compare prospective consequence for understanding, evidence accessibility, effort, and authority qualitatively. Prefer a question whose resolution could alter a reusable explanation over a merely unfamiliar topic when the evidence route and costs are otherwise comparable. A blocked high-value question can remain live without consuming the next allocation. Where these considerations conflict, record the choice and uncertainty rather than inventing a total score.

To compare progress, keep the same initial cases and actual incumbent visible. Ask whether the result changes warranted attribution, rules out an implication, supplies a missing condition, or merely restates an existing qualification. Also check whether it loses a distinction the incumbent preserved. New cases may challenge the comparison, but must be identified as additions rather than silently replacing inconvenient old cases. A longer explanation can improve understanding; fewer words or files establish nothing by themselves.

Finally, retain what the selected question actually yielded and why alternatives were parked. That could inform later selection, but this run has not demonstrated that such feedback improves it. The mechanism is practical because its inputs are inspectable arguments and records, not an unspecified prose compressor. Its vulnerability is equally explicit: fluent researchers can overstate both expected consequence and evidence reachability.
