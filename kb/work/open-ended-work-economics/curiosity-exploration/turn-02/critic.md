## Correction, revision, and discovery

Three different achievements must not be collapsed:

1. **Correcting support:** the tag episode does not warrant its assigned role in the recovery note. This changes which evidence supports an existing explanation. It is a real documentary finding, even if no library citation is edited.
2. **Revising an explanation:** specifying the actor with a feasible alternative could improve an overbroad account. Here the parent/script distinction appears to instantiate the incumbent's interpreter, not add a newly established condition. A new condition would need consequences not already supplied by the scoped account.
3. **Discovering a cause:** attributing recovery or revision to a particular resource requires more than coexistence or accepted output. The current records do not separate the effects of retained knowledge, operator selection, available tools, and alternative routes sufficiently to establish a new causal finding.

B's before/after table is explicitly an analytical foil. The [actual revision account](../../../../theory/evidence/commonplace-revision-used-theory-guided-computational-search.md) already separates computational search, human selection, artifact retention, and missing causal controls. Replacing the foil with its more careful column is therefore not new learning by the KB. B's historical retrieval may improve this worker's access to evidence; it does not by itself improve the explanation already retained.

Documentary success justifies a narrow learning claim when the comparison changes a warranted judgment on the same material. This turn can say that recovery support has been reassessed and that the distinction between acceptance and achieved purpose matters. It cannot infer a better search policy, durable future uptake, increased causal understanding, or improved task performance from producing a coherent critique. Those require different evidence. Learning value and task benefit remain separate.

## Acquisition and qualitative warrant

The explorers largely treat acquisition gaps honestly. Missing transcripts do not identify whether information was never recorded, discarded, retained elsewhere, or merely unfound. Git can recover committed wording, not uncommitted proposals or the worker's recognition of an obstruction. A future comparable encounter would answer a new empirical question; it would not fill the historical gap retrospectively.

B's proposed pre-correction passage-to-proposal trace would strengthen attribution to retained material. It would not settle necessity: task context, prior model knowledge, and redundant inputs could explain the same proposal. The operator-guided generic-synthesis rival therefore survives even with a good mediation trace. Conversely, demanding a controlled intervention before accepting any documentary involvement would discard weaker but legitimate evidence.

Qualitative constraints remain valuable without event predictions. “Composite success does not establish autonomous selection” denies an entailment. A successful human-inclusive case and an autonomous learner are logically distinguishable; no forecast of the next failure is required. An implication check can demonstrate that insufficiency without discovering which causal account is true. Likewise, loss of a recovery example can weaken support without falsifying the mechanism.

## A practical next-question mechanism

To advance question selection rather than storage design, use a bounded choice among unresolved explanations. For each candidate, state the actual incumbent, one rival, and one reachable distinction whose resolution could change the account. Select the question with the strongest current prospect of changing a consequential explanation at tolerable effort. This is a qualitative research judgment, not a numerical compression score or an immediate-service gate. If reachability is unknown, a bounded evidence-location question can precede substantive attribution.

On present evidence, B's exact historical-origin question should be parked: the missing conversation is already identified, and no new recovery route is supplied. Prefer a different positive regularity whose distinguishing evidence is available. A concrete next candidate is: **did moving completeness verification into validation change what was established, or merely enforce an already explicit requirement?** The tag chronology supplies contract changes, test claims, and a later detected omission against which to compare those explanations. The question concerns the relation between checking and knowledge, not another claim that readers recovered. Existing explanations may already suffice; that is an acceptable outcome.

This recommendation serves the larger purpose by exercising positive selection from successful change rather than endless attribution repair. It leaves whole-KB search quality and causal effects on agents untouched. The strongest steering rival can select exactly this question. Comparative credit for compression would require different prioritization, discovery yield, or effort under comparable opportunities; none is available.
