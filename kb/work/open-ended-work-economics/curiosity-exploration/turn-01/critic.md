## Shared structure: useful question, dangerous answer

B's three-way distinction should survive integration: encountered blockage with substitution; inadequacy that does not bind the task; and weak measured influence of supplied content. These can coexist. They concern different units of evidence: an operational path, exposure to a deficiency, and sensitivity to an intervention. A shared heading must not flatten those units into “support failed but the system recovered.”

The proposed shared question—what contribution did the named support make?—is useful because it prevents success from certifying an unobserved dependency. But a heading that admits every successful system adds little explanatory content. To earn more, the comparison must identify which account changes and which inference becomes unavailable. Here that is concrete: continued operation plus concern about completeness cannot establish noticed obstruction, substitution, or a recurring workaround cost.

This correction may improve understanding even if no task becomes cheaper. Its value is removal of unsupported causal support. Conversely, it would not measure a workaround tax, prove that interpretation caused resilience, or establish a general condensation failure. B's memory case is best retained as a boundary contrast, not recruited as corroboration of the Commonplace mechanism. I have not independently re-grounded that empirical branch; it is not needed for this verdict.
