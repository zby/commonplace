# Turn 1 integration: distinguish service from the learning investment

## Outcome

**Keep the bootstrap purpose, but do not count useful delivery as proof that the system has learned to automate methodologies. Nor should near-term service become an unannounced veto on exploratory research.** This turn clarifies what different progress claims would require. It does not choose their relative priority or adopt an objective function.

The [commission](../README.md) asks for a system useful across kinds of knowledge work that learns to automate methodologies through development and use. [Explorer A](./explorer-a.md) emphasizes useful service and its full operator burden; [Explorer B](./explorer-b.md) emphasizes later capability and the breaks between experience and improvement. The [critic](./critic.md) correctly objects that their service-first openings could narrow the research purpose without approval. Their shared cautions do not establish which strategy should win.

The practical revision supported here is to distinguish **the useful result delivered, the extra investment made to learn, and the later benefit actually observed**. These can occur together, but none establishes the others. This is a recommendation for interpreting the plan and reporting this trial, not a new record schema, mandatory experiment, or library change.

## What should increase remains a purpose-relative question

The existing [instrument reference](../../../../reference/commonplace-as-an-instrument.md) names more useful and better-warranted knowledge work per unit of human judgment, not automation. That supplies an orientation, not a usable accounting unit or a resolution of every investment conflict. The present commission also authorizes exploration whose immediate applicability is uncertain.

Keep four claims separate:

| Claim | What would support it | What it would not yet establish |
|---|---|---|
| Better present service | A worthwhile task served better under its actual acceptance conditions, including setup, review, repair and recovery burden | That the technical system learned anything reusable |
| Useful learning within a task family | Experience caused a reusable change that improved later work, with the claimed learner and human contributions identified | Broad transfer or reduced future method-development effort |
| Better adaptation | Under a relevant changed condition, the system recognizes where a method fits, revises or declines it appropriately, and reaches a better result | That this benefit repays developing and maintaining the method |
| Better method development | A later development episode benefits from earlier experience relative to a relevant alternative | Indefinite compounding improvement or breadth across knowledge work |

This is a distinction among claims, not a scorecard requiring every task to improve all four. A valuable one-off answer need not produce reusable methodology. An informative failed investigation can justify research expenditure without improving the immediate deliverable. A reduction in rules or attempted automation can improve work.

The critic's correction to B is important: **a reminder can constitute a learned system improvement even if the underlying model already possessed the competence.** The [retention account](../../../../theory/factory-learning-is-experience-responsive-retention-that-improves.md) locates learning at the declared system boundary and permits prompts, retrieval and checks as changes. The issue is whether experience caused a reusable beneficial change, not whether a model learned something absent from its weights. Likewise, useful local learning does not require a changed-purpose test. Such a test addresses a stronger adaptation claim.

“Human judgment” also hides different contributions. An operator can supply purpose and authority, specialist evidence, or repair of deficient execution. Reducing the last two may support an automation claim; silently removing the first changes who decides what the system is for. More operator interaction can also be worthwhile when the requested product is understanding. That does not make operator education an independent objective of every task.

## Where present usefulness and future automation genuinely conflict

The explorers supply mechanisms and hypothetical choices, not measurements of their prevalence:

- **Rescue versus diagnosis.** Human repair may save today's result while concealing what the technical system could not do. Delaying repair might expose a useful failure but impose unacceptable cost or risk. Record the decisive contribution when it matters to the claim; do not require nonintervention for cleaner research.
- **Demand versus informative cases.** Important one-off work may offer little reuse. Familiar repetitive work may offer cheap reuse but conceal a narrow method's limits. A boundary case can be worth investigating without being the best current service task. Task choice therefore affects both value and what can be learned.
- **Concise execution versus revisable evidence.** A short instruction can save effort now while omitting conditions needed for later revision. Keeping complete traces creates retrieval and review burdens. Neither “retain everything” nor “retain only the conclusion” follows from this turn.
- **Unattended operation versus feedback.** Removing review can remove the only observation of subtle defects. Apparent savings can then reflect unobserved errors rather than better performance. Whether replacement feedback is feasible remains unknown.
- **Specialization versus future change.** A checked local method may be valuable even if adapting it is expensive. Checks can also make adaptation safer. The tradeoff depends on what the check protects; there is no evidence here that standardization generally prevents learning.

These conflicts do not justify a blanket service-first or research-first rule. The allocation decision needs the purpose of the particular work and the uncertainty the extra investment would address. Calling work “research” preserves its legitimate role but does not erase its opportunity cost.

## What this evidence changes—and what it does not

### Integration decisions within the commissioned purpose

1. **Do not carry either explorer's opening forward as the complete objective.** Preserve both useful service and the commissioned investigation of automation. Their relative priority is unresolved.
2. **Credit only the claim supported, without withholding narrower credit.** Local benefit can justify local use; it need not establish broad transfer. Improved execution does not establish improved method development. Human-inclusive success does not establish learning by the technical subsystem alone.
3. **Keep a strong simple rival live.** Task-first assistance with current tools, project facts, ordinary records and selective corrections is not an artificially forgetful baseline. A single local check after a concrete failure is another serious alternative to a general extraction process. This changes the comparison we should contemplate, not which system we have selected.
4. **Choose the decision before proposing a comparison.** Reusing an existing method, investing in producing methods, and pursuing the broad bootstrap are different decisions. Varying access to an existing artifact can test its added value; it cannot alone settle whether creating it was the best investment. A null against ordinary records may reject special packaging rather than experience itself.
5. **Retain non-adoption as a useful outcome.** The turn need not manufacture a new controller or extraction queue. No tier-based maximand is adopted; the parent already excluded that default.

These are dispositions of this integration, not implemented procedures or changes to standing policy.

### Recommendations not adopted

Changed-condition probes, matched comparisons, additional episode capture and a second method-development episode remain possible methods. None is commissioned by this integration. Their expense must fit the decision they would inform. In particular, broad transfer testing is not a universal admission requirement for cheap local repairs.

The proposed distinction between delivery and learning investment does not authorize a general ledger, extraction pipeline or commitment store. Operational-record design remains with the adjacent work named in the [parent framing](../../README.md).

### Purpose choices reserved for the operator

Restricting automation research to already identifiable service returns, abandoning the bootstrap in favor of task-first assistance, or maximizing unattended execution would materially change or narrow the purpose. So would making operator understanding a universal product outcome. Prioritizing kinds of knowledge work and deciding which human decisions should remain human also require operator direction where they determine acceptance. This turn makes none of those choices.

## Discoveries, restatements and surviving objections

**The strongest incremental finding is a decision gap, not a new theory:** the commission permits exploratory investment, but does not settle how its future continuation should compete with useful service when no specific service return can yet be named. The critic makes that gap explicit. It cannot be resolved by combining the explorers' proposed dimensions.

Two further useful corrections emerged in this turn: B's knowledge-reminder exclusion was too strong for system-level learning, and the two proposed comparisons address different decisions. They prevent an unnecessarily demanding test and an overbroad interpretation of a narrow test.

Most other conclusions are applications of existing arguments. The distinction between usefulness and automation is already explicit in the instrument reference. The learner boundary and separation of learning from broad reach are already explicit in the retention account. Counts of instructions, autonomous steps or task names are therefore not newly refuted metrics here. Explorer agreement is not independent confirmation.

The strongest unresolved objection is **selection under inadequate feedback**. Neither exploration explains how the system will recognize which methods deserve investment when good outcomes and applicability failures are themselves hard to judge. Critic consensus and deterministic validation cannot supply that missing evidence. A general model with cheap reconstruction and selective repairs may deliver most of the value without a methodology-accumulation system. Conversely, that rival may repeatedly pay for the same unretained correction. This turn does not adjudicate between them.

The explorers' local episodes illustrate narrower claims: a check can catch an omission, and an investigation can finish without adoption. Their external evidence is retained and qualified in the input reports, with additional source scrutiny reported by the critic. This integration does not independently re-audit those external sources or use them to assert a performance advantage. The conceptual dispositions above do not depend on treating those studies as evidence for Commonplace's broad bootstrap.

## What this turn did not demonstrate

No learning experiment, reuse comparison or production intervention was run. This turn did not demonstrate:

- improved service, lower operator burden, or better automated methodology selection;
- transfer across kinds of knowledge work or more productive method development;
- that retained explanations outperform fresh reconstruction or a concise correction;
- that the proposed research investment pays back under actual future demand;
- superiority of two isolated explorers plus criticism and integration over simpler conversation or delegation;
- that an economic framing adds discriminating guidance beyond the already-held search-control account.

It also did not disprove any of these possibilities. This is conceptual clarification with bounded evidence inspection, not a negative performance result. Validation of the reports is not validation of their conclusions.

## Operator question before turn 2

**When a worthwhile task could be finished directly, but extra work might reveal a reusable method without an identifiable later consumer, should the plan permit a bounded exploratory investment—and what qualitative limit would make that investment acceptable?**

This asks about one practical conflict rather than an exchange rate among abstract objectives. The existing five-turn exploration is already authorized; the question concerns the policy we are investigating, not retroactive permission to conduct turn 1. No response is presumed.

**Provisional next question, responsive to that answer:** What observation would make exploratory drift worth continuing, redirecting or stopping before it becomes endless preparation?

- If the operator permits exploratory investment without a named consumer, investigate what informative progress could justify its continuation without pretending it has delivered service value.
- If the operator requires a plausible service return, investigate drift from a named real problem: what surprising result would justify revising the method while preserving that problem's purpose?
- If the operator changes the purpose, the coordinator should resolve the consequential scope change before another assignment.

These are conditional directions for turn 2, not a launched turn, protocol or adopted stopping rule.

Parked: operator understanding as an explicit product; learning when to bypass a method; method lifetime under changing models; task-order effects; retaining rejected candidates; and replacement evaluators that might reproduce human blind spots. The input reports also park external questions whose ingests lacked supporting Quotes. No new grounding or source mutation was attempted here.

The principal evidence blocker is absence of a relevant comparison and observed later use—not a tooling failure. The output is consequently partial as an answer to which investment strategy works best, but complete as a bounded integration of this turn.
