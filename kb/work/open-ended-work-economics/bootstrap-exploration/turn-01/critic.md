# Turn 1 criticism: separate useful safeguards from a new objective

## Judgment

The explorers supply useful safeguards against overstating progress, but little evidence for choosing a bootstrap strategy over ordinary task-first assistance. Their agreement is not independent confirmation: both reason from the same commission and overlapping Commonplace arguments. The strongest proposed change is to separate delivered work from the learning investment made alongside it. The most consequential unresolved choice is whether future automation is only a means to useful service or also a research purpose worth funding when near-term service would choose otherwise.

This is bounded criticism of [Explorer A](./explorer-a.md) and [Explorer B](./explorer-b.md), not a proposed replacement commission. No new experiment was run. Recommendations below are for integration and operator decision, not authority to implement them.

## 1. The opening recommendations risk deciding the purpose by interpretation

Both explorers lead with present useful, warranted work and subordinate automation to that outcome. That fits [Commonplace as an instrument](../../../../reference/commonplace-as-an-instrument.md), whose objective explicitly is not automation. But the [new commission](../README.md) deliberately adds learning to automate methodologies and exploration beyond the economic framing. A faithful reading must preserve that research purpose as well as the older objective.

This is not merely a wording dispute. Suppose task-first assistance wins on all currently valuable tasks, while a bootstrap experiment offers uncertain evidence about a substantially wider future capability. Present service cannot decide whether to fund that experiment without a horizon and an operator preference about research. Conversely, declaring an investment research does not make its opportunity cost disappear.

**Decision implication:** return this choice to the operator rather than adopting either explorer's opening as a complete objective. Does the operator want automation research only where a plausible service return can already be named, or also a bounded exploratory allowance whose point is to discover such returns? Neither requires a numerical exchange rate. A decision about one real conflict would clarify more than another list of dimensions.

A's proposed value of operator understanding also needs this boundary. Helping someone learn and completing work for them can be different products. Treating understanding as an independent outcome everywhere would change the purpose, not merely improve burden accounting.

## 2. Different claims need different tests; do not make broad transfer the admission price for learning

B separates execution, later service, adaptation and improved method development. That is useful. But its “No new information” failure says that if the model already knew how to do the work and a method merely reminded it, acquired capability should not be inferred. This is too strong without specifying the learner.

A reminder selected from experience can improve the deployed system even when the model's weights contain the relevant competence already. The local [retention account](../../../../theory/factory-learning-is-experience-responsive-retention-that-improves.md) makes the learner boundary explicit and permits prompts, retrieval and tests as changes. It also expressly separates learning from broad reach and compounding improvement. The relevant question is whether experience caused a reusable change that improved later work, not whether the underlying model lacked the knowledge beforehand.

Likewise, a later use on the same task family can establish a narrow benefit. A changed-purpose challenge is a stronger test of adaptation, not a prerequisite for all claims of useful learning. Demanding it universally would favor expensive research demonstrations over cheap, valuable repairs.

**Decision implication:** keep a ladder of claims, not a single universal test. A local improvement may justify local use; transfer needs variation; reduced method-development effort needs another development episode and a relevant comparison. Do not credit the stronger claim from the weaker evidence, but do not withhold the weaker credit either.

## 3. The selection mechanism remains the main missing link

Both explorers explain why storing instructions is insufficient. Neither establishes how this system will select valuable new methods when feedback is incomplete. B's suggestions work best where applicability failures and warranted outcomes can already be recognized. Those conditions are exactly what can be missing in broad knowledge work.

The existing [bootstrap compatibility argument](../../../../theory/a-bootstrap-fits-the-bitter-lesson-only-if-learning-outgrows-it.md) already identifies selection as the practical limitation and proposes growing a selection environment through use. It also already names self-confirming use, human-supplied evaluation and rising maintenance as failure conditions. Repeating these limits does not answer how the proposed approach overcomes them.

The strongest tension is between preserving human judgment as valuable service and treating recurrent human judgment as machinery that should eventually be automated. Some judgment supplies the goal or authority; some supplies empirical expertise; some repairs deficient execution. Those roles cannot be collapsed into an intervention count. Removing the first can be a scope violation, while failing to reduce the latter two can defeat a technical learning claim.

**Decision implication:** before selecting a test, name which decision the system should learn to make and what the operator will continue to supply. If acceptable outcomes cannot be distinguished within the allowed effort, retain that as a limitation of the proposed test. Do not replace missing selection evidence with critic agreement or validator success.

## 4. The external evidence is relevant but does not decide the investment

I checked the complete retained Quotes sections for B's three empirical sources, using the grounding-alignment gate. Their qualified use is generally defensible. Their implications remain narrower than a broad bootstrap endorsement:

- [Memento-Skills](../../../../sources/memento-skills-let-agents-design-agents.ingest.md) reports better held-out results for the full system than an ablation disabling failure attribution, rewriting and discovery together. This supports investigating that bundle; it does not isolate retained explanations or establish an advantage over the explorers' strong fresh-solver rival. The retained passage also describes skills containing helper scripts and prompts. Treating the result as evidence specifically for methodological prose would therefore be unwarranted. The quoted design includes a trained router: “frozen LLM” must not become “no learned components outside files.”
- [Faithfulness interventions](../../../../sources/llm-agents-are-not-always-faithful-self-evolvers.ingest.md) define faithfulness through behavioral response to perturbed input and report weaker dependence on condensed experience. Behavioral dependence and beneficial dependence are different questions. Sensitivity to corrupted advice is not itself useful capability; ignoring an invalid lesson can be correct. The evidence motivates causal checking, not a default preference for raw traces.
- [The phased optimizer study](../../../../sources/agent-optimizers-compound-terminal-bench.ingest.md) distinguishes static results, transfer and re-optimization under rollout budgets. B correctly declines to infer that prior retention makes later development cheaper. Rollouts alone also do not settle total operator and maintenance burden for this commission.

A's [pilot-cost case](../../../use-and-outcome-records/synthesis-pilot-cost-case.md) genuinely supports stopping without adoption and revising an over-costly delivery route. It does not demonstrate superiority of smaller experiments: the larger alternative was never run. A already states that limit. No objection here defeats its narrow use.

## 5. The proposed rival is strong, but the comparisons answer different questions

A proposes comparing task-first service with explicit learning investment. B proposes varying access to retained methodology and evidence. These are not interchangeable.

A's comparison concerns the whole investment policy, including the work chosen and the cost of creating the method. B's comparison can test whether an already-created artifact helps a later worker. It cannot alone determine whether creating that artifact was the best investment. Giving both arms ordinary episode records is sensible for a realistic rival, but those records may contain enough procedural knowledge to reconstruct the method. A null then means the special packaging added no benefit under those conditions, not that experience was useless.

A still simpler rival deserves retention: add one local check or concise correction after a concrete failure, without an automatic methodology-extraction process. This may capture most of the benefit of the examples while avoiding a general selection and maintenance system. It does not refute the wider research purpose; it challenges the extra machinery proposed to serve it.

**Decision implication:** choose the decision before the comparison. “Use this retained method again?”, “invest in producing such methods?”, and “pursue a broad bootstrap?” require progressively wider evidence. Do not charge a narrow comparison with settling all three. Any comparison is still uncommissioned.

## 6. Actual novelty and recommended disposition

The separation of useful work from automation is already explicit in the instrument reference. Behavioral dependence, learner boundaries and stronger learning claims are already separated in the retention account. The need to outgrow bespoke knowledge, challenge inherited evaluation and compare against direct alternatives is already in the bootstrap argument. These are useful applications, not new discoveries established by two explorers.

The turn's useful incremental contribution is to expose choices for this particular plan:

- **Within the plan:** distinguish current delivery from the extra learning investment; identify the learner boundary; accept narrowly useful changes without claiming broad transfer; preserve a task-first rival; allow a negative result without compensating recommendations.
- **Still proposals, not adopted procedure:** changed-condition probes, matched comparisons and extra evidence capture. Their cost must be warranted by the decision they serve. The [parent framing](../../README.md) leaves operational-record design and general stores to adjacent work.
- **Return to operator:** whether exploratory automation research has value beyond presently identifiable service returns; which kinds of knowledge work matter; whether operator understanding is a product outcome; and which human decisions are intended to remain human. Changing those choices can change the purpose rather than its implementation.

No evidence here warrants abandoning the bootstrap purpose. Equally, the turn has not shown that automatic methodology accumulation is the best route to it. Integration should retain that live rivalry rather than translate shared cautions into consensus for a larger framework.
