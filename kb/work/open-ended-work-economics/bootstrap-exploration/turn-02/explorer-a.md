# Turn 2, explorer A: let a case change the question, not authorize its own continuation

## Finding and boundary

**Exploration can produce something worthwhile before it identifies a later consumer: a concrete case can show that we were asking the wrong question. But discovering a better question and financing its investigation are separate decisions.** My proposed boundary is an episode that leaves an inspectable change in the problem account, followed by a separate continuation choice. This is a design conjecture, not a demonstrated search policy.

The purpose remains useful knowledge work and learning to automate methodologies. No change to doctrine, implementation, or the objective is proposed as adopted. The [turn-1 integration](../turn-01/integration.md) asks whether bounded research without an identifiable consumer is permitted and what would justify continuation. Permission to complete these five turns does not answer that general allocation question. This turn can explore such research under its existing commission; it cannot establish a standing entitlement to it.

The strongest useful distinction is **absence of a named consumer versus absence of a research consequence**. An investigation can change what we think is possible, expose an invalid partition, or reveal that our test cannot detect the thing we care about without promising a deliverable to a named user. Conversely, naming a plausible future user does not make a speculative investigation informative.

## 1. A surprising case can invalidate the question's categories

ADR 042 records a shift from asking where every artifact belongs in a closed three-way classification to asking which local writing obligations a collection needs. Its reported trigger was a stance-neutral evidence-mapping case. Instead of forcing that case into the existing partition, the decision removed the exhaustiveness requirement. This is a historical decision record, not my verification of the five casebook notes it reports.

The transferable mechanism is not “always add a fourth category.” A case makes the costs of an assumption visible; comparison can then move the assumption from mandatory to optional. That creates possibilities outside the first framing without requiring a general replacement architecture. The ADR also explicitly declined to name a narrative/log category merely because examples existed. That restraint matters as much as the expansion: **one successful challenge did not license filling every adjacent conceptual gap.** Some profile machinery in the ADR was subsequently superseded, which further cautions against treating the episode as proof of a final design.

For this commission, a hypothetical equivalent would be finding a useful method whose benefit is better refusal or question clarification rather than more autonomous execution. The initial question “which steps should be automated?” might then become “which judgments should the method help expose, preserve or perform?” This preserves the commissioned purpose if it improves useful knowledge work and clarifies automation's limits. Making human understanding the universal product, or giving up methodology automation altogether, would instead require an operator decision.

**Possible changed decision:** select a case that strains the current description of automation before decomposing more tasks into executable steps. The case should be allowed to overturn that decomposition, not merely fill its boxes. This is a proposed choice for later work, not an experiment run here.

## 2. A preselected target can hide productive drift in the evaluation itself

The curiosity experiment report scored runs against one preselected finding while separately listing other findings. Its own account includes runs that missed the target but investigated a different concern. That supports a narrow observation about the report's design: target attainment and production of other potentially worthwhile findings were not the same measured outcome.

I am not independently endorsing those findings about Decapod, nor the report's stronger claims that a prompt was “reliable.” Two trials per framing cannot establish that generality. The later curiosity-workshop framing explicitly acknowledges the small sample and the difference between prompted retrospective inquiry and online origination.

The consequence for productive drift is nevertheless concrete. If continuation depends only on approaching the starting target, exploration gets penalized precisely when it changes the question. But retroactively declaring every different result valuable creates the opposite failure: evaluation follows the output and can no longer reject it.

A proposed repair is to retain both accounts, briefly:

- What was the starting question, and was it answered?
- What unexpected case or distinction appeared, and what evidence makes it more than an interesting phrase?
- Does it challenge a premise of the original question, offer a separate opportunity, or merely distract?
- What claim remains unsupported even after the discovery?

These are interpretation questions, not a new compulsory report schema. A missed target remains a missed target. An incidental result can receive separate, narrower credit after scrutiny. Neither erases the other's cost.

**Possible changed decision:** later comparisons should allow adjudication of off-target findings, without replacing the original acceptance condition after seeing the output. A controller that finds a genuinely different problem may deserve research credit while still failing its commissioned service task.

## 3. Cases without later consumers need not pretend to have one

A named consumer is one way to establish significance, but not the only way available within this research commission. Three conjectural examples clarify the difference:

1. **A positive regularity:** unrelated methods repeatedly improve after removing instructions. There is no immediate repair request. A bounded comparison might distinguish redundant constraints from an evaluator that merely rewards shorter answers. Its research consequence concerns what the bootstrap should retain; it need not yet identify a production user.
2. **A newly available capability:** a tool makes a previously inaccessible intermediate judgment inspectable. Exploration could ask which old uncertainty can now be checked. The tool's existence alone does not warrant a new platform or a search through every old task.
3. **A boundary counterexample:** a method succeeds on tasks with explicit checks but fails where the question itself must be formed. The research consequence is a limit on what “methodology automation” currently covers, even if no later customer has requested that task class.

These are invented cases for reasoning, not observed results. The curiosity inventory offers corresponding possibilities but explicitly reports weak or absent prospective evidence. Its defect-centered evidence cannot establish that opportunity-seeking exploration behaves the same way.

A strong objection is that requiring an identifiable consequence still biases inquiry toward what the current frame can express. I agree at the entry point. Initial exploratory permission should allow examining an unfamiliar case before its consequence is clear. The consequence becomes a candidate basis for **renewal**, not a universal prerequisite for noticing, recording, or taking the first authorized look. Otherwise the method would demand the discovery before allowing the exploration.

This does not answer how much initial freedom is affordable. The existing five-turn commission supplies a boundary for this trial; it supplies no transferable budget for future work.

## 4. Bound the commitment, not the permissible surprise

My proposed episode boundary separates three acts that can otherwise blur together:

- **Encounter:** inspect something that may not yet have an articulated use. The authorized episode limits expenditure; novelty itself is not evidence of value.
- **Develop:** turn a promising case into an inspectable question or competing explanation. A useful endpoint can be a counterexample, a narrower scope, an identified measurement limitation, or a reason to stop. It need not be a reusable procedure.
- **Renew or park:** compare further work with the live alternatives under the original purpose. A new question does not automatically authorize the next episode.

This is a conjectured control arrangement. It has not been shown superior to ordinary conversation or a simpler one-agent investigation. Its main logical advantage is that changing the question no longer has to mean extending the budget.

The [productive-deferral argument](../../../../theory/productive-deferral-requires-option-evidence-and-convergence.md) supplies a nearby but imperfect analogy: a deferred consequential choice needs a route back to decision. Exploration may not begin with an identified option or decision, so I would not impose that note's entire test on it. The narrower borrowed idea is that a future return condition must lead to a response, not just another discussion. Here the response can be closure with an unanswered question. It need not converge on adoption or on the original question.

Candidate evidence for renewal includes a reproduced mismatch, a newly distinguishable pair of explanations, an inspected case that defeats a load-bearing assumption, or a concrete way to test an uncertainty previously inaccessible. Each remains defeasible. Reasons against renewal include rediscovering the same uncertainty under new labels, requiring another general framework before any case can be inspected, or repeatedly promising that the next preparation stage will finally make observation possible.

A genuinely new question can still be parked because its next test is too expensive, outside authority, or weaker than another live use of effort. Conversely, a failed probe can justify continuation when it localizes the obstacle. The appropriate tradeoff remains unresolved; no numerical threshold follows from these distinctions.

## Strongest rival and a comparison that could matter

**Strongest rival:** finish useful tasks directly, preserve occasional concise observations, and let a capable model reconstruct questions when needed. Add one bounded curiosity pass only when a concrete case warrants it. This rival can also discover surprising cases and retain corrections. It does not require a general exploration controller, a growing method archive, or a staged renewal record.

The rival attacks the most vulnerable assumption here: that explicit episode boundaries improve future selection enough to pay for their reporting and review. A fluent model can fabricate apparent question changes just as easily as it can fabricate apparent progress toward a target. Extra structure may merely make preparation look disciplined.

A bounded future comparison could hold the task, accessible case material, total resources and downstream reviewer conditions fixed while varying only whether a separately bounded exploration episode is offered. Assess both the original task and independently scrutinized off-target results. Include cases with tempting but unproductive surprises; otherwise broad doubt wins by construction. Record setup, review, repair and investigation costs separately where measurable. This would compare the added episode, not prove the value of every mechanism in a bootstrap system.

No such comparison was run. If precise resource matching is infeasible, report that limitation rather than claim equal cost. A finding that the simple rival obtains the same question changes with less burden would argue against special machinery, not against curiosity itself.

## Conflicts, unknowns and parked ideas

**Conflict retained from turn 1:** present usefulness and research without a named consumer can compete. Neither the absence of a consumer nor a plausible learning story settles the allocation. The operator has not selected their relative priority.

**Conflict with a strict changed-decision test:** the search-controller note gives downstream choice change a central role. A newly formed question may be worth preserving before any later decision changes. I propose distinguishing provisional research output from demonstrated controller value, not relaxing the latter's evidential demand. How much unredeemed research output to carry remains open.

**Unknowns:** frequency of useful surprises; capture and reopening costs; whether automated evaluation can reject attractive irrelevancies; how often cases change questions without human prompting; acceptable opportunity cost; and whether generative, non-defect cases need different methods. The evidence supports conceptual distinctions and local historical observations, not a demonstrated advantage or a standing allocation policy.

**Parked:** novelty-driven archives; compression-based curiosity; deliberate cross-domain case pairing; reopening old questions after tool changes; and tests that distinguish new question formation from eloquent retrospective reconstruction. No architecture or source-ingest queue is commissioned by this list.

The next useful question for turn 3 is: **what minimum retained account of a surprising case lets a later worker challenge or apply the resulting method, rather than merely repeat its conclusion?** That follows this turn without requiring immediate applicability or assuming that every discovery should become an instruction.
