# Turn 2 criticism: bounded drift is not yet selective drift

## Disposition

**Keep the separation between revising a question and renewing its allocation. Do not claim that this solves selection or demonstrates productive exploration.** [Explorer A](./explorer-a.md) and [Explorer B](./explorer-b.md) give credible reasons to permit surprises inside a bounded episode. Neither establishes how to recognize the surprises worth pursuing, or that its proposed renewal discipline improves on ordinary bounded inquiry.

Preserve useful knowledge work and learning to automate methodologies as the purpose. The operator's instruction to finish all five turns answers scheduling, not the unresolved research-versus-service priority in [turn 1](../turn-01/integration.md). The coordinator retains scheduling, integration and recovery. These are recommendations to that integration, not new policy or permission to implement.

## Strongest objections

### 1. Returning control prevents unauthorized continuation, not endless authorized preparation

Both explorers locate control outside the discovering episode. That establishes an authority boundary. It does not establish a quality boundary. A coordinator can repeatedly authorize weak investigations on the strength of plausible accounts of what changed. B recognizes cumulative expenditure, but neither report supplies evidence that its renewal challenge distinguishes this pattern from legitimate long research.

The hard case is not a worker extending its own budget. It is a succession of compliant, finite episodes, each producing a defensible new uncertainty and no improved ability to resolve the underlying question. The same criticism applies to this trial. Five completed turns will establish bounded execution; they will not establish that the preparation was worthwhile.

**Disposition:** retain bounded episodes as governance, not as evidence of productivity. Retain the unresolved selector problem explicitly. A later comparison must permit the result that renewal prose adds burden without improving choices.

### 2. A changed question is easy to report and may be costly to undo

A's strongest proposal is an inspectable change in the problem account. B usefully asks whether that change survives a serious rival. But a model can manufacture an apparent premise reversal by overstating the original premise. A question can also become less useful after revision. Novelty and a documented causal story do not establish improvement.

For example, shifting from producing methods to recognizing applicability failures may be illuminating, but applicability was already central to turn 1's adaptation distinction. It is not necessarily discovery outside this project's framing. A report that presents this as new without checking the earlier account rewards forgetting.

**Disposition:** when a claimed surprise bears on renewal, ask whether the original plan actually depended on the disputed assumption, what inspected evidence challenges it, and what alternative interpretation survives. This is a targeted criticism, not a mandatory ledger or per-step utility test. A case may still warrant provisional attention without meeting all three conditions; that weaker status must not become demonstrated progress.

### 3. “Preparation requires a necessity claim” needs narrowing

B's distinction between a prerequisite and a competing investment is useful. It would expose a claim that a general ledger is required before a reminder can be tested. But preparation can legitimately improve measurement sensitivity, reduce expected repair, or make a comparison less misleading without being strictly necessary. The smallest feasible attempt is not automatically the most informative or economical attempt.

B includes safety and validity exceptions, but the same limitation applies to ordinary quality improvements. Calling those independent research rather than preparation does not settle their relative value.

**Disposition:** require a necessity argument only when necessity is asserted. Otherwise compare the proposed preparation with a smaller attempt on the uncertainty each can resolve and the burden each introduces. Costs here are unknown. No inference that the smaller attempt dominates is currently earned.

### 4. The proposed comparisons identify different interventions

A proposes varying whether a separately bounded exploration episode is offered. B proposes varying an explicit renewal challenge while retaining ordinary bounded exploration. The first asks whether an exploration opportunity helps; the second asks whether its allocation discipline helps. Combining them would confound opportunity, prompting and governance.

A's “same total resources” also needs care: under a fixed total allocation, exploration displaces other work. Under extra resources, a better output might reflect extra effort rather than better routing. Neither design currently resolves how to judge surprising findings independently of the wording that makes them seem consequential.

**Disposition:** carry B's simpler contrast forward if the decision is whether to add renewal machinery. Carry A's contrast only if the decision is whether to reserve exploration effort. Neither test alone answers whether to invest in creating reusable methodology or pursue the broad bootstrap. Turn 1 already warned against this mismatch between decision and comparison.

## Conflicts that should survive integration

The [controller-evaluation argument](../../../../theory/a-search-controller-is-tested-by-what-it-brings-to-stronger-evaluation.md) demands changed later decisions for credit. Both explorers resist treating a not-yet-used discovery as worthless. These positions need not conflict if one concerns demonstrated controller benefit and the other concerns prospective research promise. However, the amount of promise worth carrying remains unresolved. Merely renaming it “provisional research output” supplies no allocation rule.

Protected exploration with ordinary judgment is a strong rival, not an absence of method. It can already stop, reject attractive distractions and preserve concise observations. The proposed renewal challenge must improve on that competent baseline, not on an agent that blindly follows every interesting idea. Conversely, task-first assistance is not an authorized replacement purpose. Comparing it with an investment does not adopt it as the entire plan.

Plan revisions within scope: distinguish original-task completion from incidental findings; question alleged prerequisites; keep episode closure separate from intellectual rejection; match a future comparison to its decision. Purpose changes requiring operator adoption: confining research to identifiable service returns, replacing the bootstrap with task-only assistance, or granting ongoing autonomous research irrespective of displaced work. None follows from this turn.

## Next-turn recommendation and limits

A's proposed turn-3 question about what to retain from a surprising case is warranted only if retention is not presumed beneficial. A sharper version is: **for one concrete case, what must a later worker recover to apply, revise or decline the proposed method, and what does an ordinary concise correction fail to provide?** This preserves the executable-methodology direction while testing the accumulation premise. Failure to find such a difference should leave reconstruction or a concise correction as live choices, not trigger a general record-system build.

Unknowns remain: acceptable research opportunity cost, frequency of useful surprises, assessment reliability, later demand, and comparative cost and benefit of explicit renewal. This criticism is complete as a bounded examination and partial as an empirical policy answer. There was no tool or scope blocker; absent comparative evidence blocks performance conclusions.
