## 2. Narrow coverage is established; bad allocation is not

Both explorers correctly distinguish the questions left open by [turn 4](../turn-04/integration.md): selecting a worthwhile opportunity, deriving a revision, finding it later, and improving later method development. Supplied access to a selected correction tests none of those by itself. Turn 3's separate supersession case also remains outside the chosen comparison.

However, the commission's broad purpose did not require every turn or test to span the entire development chain. A local consumption test could identify a useful limitation before a more expensive development study. The strongest rival interpretation is therefore disciplined decomposition, not avoidance of the real question. The record shows narrowing; it does not establish which interpretation caused it or which allocation was better.

**Disposition:** say that provenance packaging under-served breadth of coverage, while comparative investment value is unknown. Keep it eligible, but do not let prior design expenditure make it the automatic next task. Conversely, proximity to the broad purpose does not automatically make a development episode the better purchase. A asks that it be considered first; that preference needs an available task, feedback and a decision it could change, not just a stronger claim label.

There is also a retrospective opportunity the explorers only partly exploit. This trial itself contains candidate experience-to-procedure changes: criticism identifies a missing boundary and later integrations preserve it. Those transitions are evidence of revision within one investigation. They do not yet establish a reusable change improving a later investigation. A later authorized use of these corrections could therefore examine the trial's own proposed procedure changes before creating another synthetic case. This is a candidate evidence route, not a mandate or a demonstrated saving.

The borrowed [retention argument](../../../../theory/factory-learning-is-experience-responsive-retention-that-improves.md) distinguishes stored text, later dependence and improvement. It helps locate this gap; its existence is not independent confirmation that the trial's retained advice improves anything. Do not require broad transfer to credit a genuinely demonstrated local improvement, and do not call within-document refinement such an improvement by default.

## 4. Procedure proposals need the same criticism as the provenance proposal

B's simpler rival—one investigator, selective fresh criticism and coordinator integration—is credible. It is not cost-free: transferring integration to the coordinator can move labor into an accounting category currently unknown. A future comparison must include that work before calling role removal a saving.

A's proposed breadth check has a plausible mechanism: exposing the untouched part of the purpose at a renewal boundary could prevent an optional local branch becoming the default plan. It could also become another mandatory report without changing selection. Keep it a short, proposed decision aid at consequential narrowing, not a required gate at every exploratory move.

B's recommendation that the next allocation buy an observation risks repeating turn 2's overstrong preparation challenge. Conceptual work may resolve an actual interpretation problem more cheaply than an ill-posed experiment; no cost comparison here excludes that possibility. The defensible recommendation is narrower: **do not fund more provenance-protocol refinement without naming the unresolved design decision it would change.** This does not require immediate service payback or outlaw further conceptual exploration.

Neither a process comparison nor a development episode should automatically launch another methodological research program. Which question merits a new allocation remains a coordinator/operator decision within authorized purpose and resources. The strongest remaining conflict is not wording: selecting a clean local contrast sacrifices breadth, while investigating development directly risks uncertain evaluation and renewed preparation. This trial does not resolve that tradeoff.

## Practical disposition for integration

Intent: retain what this trial warrants and make the next allocation explicit without silently revising the purpose. Completion of this critique is a bounded recommendation, not adoption or execution.

- **Retain as plan revisions:** distinguish delivery, learning investment and later benefit; label the provenance comparison optional and unrun; preserve the supersession alternative; record costs with their boundaries; assess supplied-method use separately from development through experience.
- **Correct the retrospective:** use the intermediate attribution above. Count preserved constraints once. Replace claims of procedure dependence with visible contribution unless a comparison supplies the missing counterfactual.
- **Consider for a later allocation:** a provenance-use comparison, an experience-to-method-change episode, or selective evaluation of this procedure. Require an actual consequential decision and feasible evidence before choosing; none is mandatory merely because it appears in a report.
- **Do not infer purpose changes:** service-first research admission, indefinite speculative allocation, abandonment of automation, or automation of the operator's purpose choices. These would need explicit authorization. The instruction to continue without pauses supplies none of it.
- **Accept partiality:** if no feasible observation or decision can be named, preserve the unresolved question rather than manufacturing a controller, evaluator or general record system. No such implementation is authorized here.
