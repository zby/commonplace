# Turn 3, explorer A: recover the operative distinction, not the whole discovery

## Finding

**In the inspected case, a later writer needs the current local writing obligations, while a later method designer may need the failed classification and its replacement's limits. Neither needs the entire discovery history by default.** An ordinary concise correction can supply the decisive distinction. What it cannot supply, if omitted, is the evidence needed to challenge the historical inference. That is a contingent omission, not a defect inherent in concise corrections.

I followed [turn 2's proposed question](../turn-02/integration.md) into its concrete example: the retirement of Commonplace's closed three-register classification in [ADR 042](../../../../reference/adr/042-register-becomes-a-default-profile-under-open-ended-text-contracts.md). I then inspected its successor, [ADR 069](../../../../reference/adr/069-collection-contract-bundles-become-one-time-prototypes.md), the current [prototype catalogue](../../../../reference/collection-prototypes.md), and the [experimental dialectical/evidential template](../../../../../src/commonplace/_data/templates/user-dialectical-evidential-COLLECTION.md). This second correction matters: the response to the first surprise itself advertised more continuing reuse than the system implemented.

This is a documentary case inspection and an analysis of currently available instruction text. It is not a new execution trial or independent verification of the original casebook work. The purpose remains useful knowledge work and learning to automate methodologies. No doctrine deviation, general store, library change, or research-priority settlement follows.

## The concrete surprise, including its inconvenient sequel

ADR 042 records an earlier exhaustive partition into theoretical, descriptive and prescriptive registers. Its reported counterpressure was a collection mapping a sourced disagreement without deciding which party was right. That collection needed topical titles, explicit attribution and maintenance based on whether the map still represented the debate—not on the author's current belief. The ADR reports five notes over eight captured sources in a sibling repository. I did not inspect those notes or sources; those quantities and the claim that the contract held are the ADR's historical report, not independently reproduced results.

The recorded change was not merely adding a fourth label. It made a collection's local writing requirements primary and treated the familiar categories as optional bundles. The same ADR refused to promote narrative/log conventions without a specified and exercised contract. Thus the proposed method was not “every unusual artifact deserves a new category.”

ADR 069 later identified a different mismatch: “adopting” shared profiles implied an ongoing relationship, but the described authoring, review and validation paths used complete local contracts. The retained useful operation was copying starting text at collection creation. The decision replaced profiles with optional, independently maintained prototypes. Its account of runtime implementation is not independently audited here. However, the catalogue, template and current [add-collection skill](../../../../instructions/cp-skill-add-collection/SKILL.md) visibly express the same clone-once semantics. The skill explicitly says to adapt the model contract and that copying ends its relationship with the origin.

The surprising sequel is therefore inspectable without claiming external performance evidence: **making the category system open did not make an ongoing shared-method relationship real.** A reusable artifact can improve authoring at creation time without remaining an authority for later writing or revision. The original lesson and the later correction must not be collapsed into “the case proved profiles work.”

There is also an epistemic distinction. Under Commonplace's [standing stance](../../../../reference/epistemic-stance.md), a writing convention is an adopted rule, not a universal empirical proposition falsified by an awkward note. The episode challenged an exhaustiveness claim and supplied reasons to change a convention. It did not scientifically prove the uniquely correct organization of knowledge.

## What experience must actually change

For this case, three possible changes have different consumers:

1. **A collection designer's choice:** stop forcing a non-adjudicative evidence map into inherited categories. State the needed purpose and local obligations instead.
2. **A writer's behavior:** keep the asserter explicit even in synthesis sentences; do not turn attributed positions into the collection's own verdict. Apply the local contract, not a remembered category label.
3. **A maintainer's update decision:** change the map when attribution or relevant positions change, not merely because the maintainer changes their mind. Do not propagate prototype edits automatically into an existing collection.

The current template contains text capable of directing all three distinctions, though direction is not observed compliance. Its review section distinguishes comparable interpretive care from equal word counts. Its maintenance section distinguishes a changed debate from a changed author belief. These clauses preserve more useful operational content than the label “dialectical.”

For the declared technical system to have learned, experience must cause a persistent change that reaches one of these later choices and improves it. This inspection establishes availability of changed instruction text and a recorded decision sequence. It does not establish that a fresh worker selects it, follows it correctly, or performs better because of it. The human contributions to the original changes are also not isolated. [Turn 1's distinction](../turn-01/integration.md) between delivery, learning investment and observed later benefit still applies.

## What a later worker must recover

Here a **text contract** means the binding local declaration of a collection's purpose and writing rules, as defined in [Collection](../../../../reference/definitions/collection.md). The minimum recoverable information depends on the later decision:

| Later decision | Information that matters in this case | What need not be loaded routinely |
|---|---|---|
| Write another map entry | Local purpose; attribution and source-span requirements; topical title rule; prohibition on unattributed verdicts; local review and maintenance rules | The argument against the original three-way taxonomy |
| Create another collection for sourced disagreement | The actual intended contribution; a suitable starting template; experimental status; placeholders requiring adaptation; independent local ownership after copying | Every note in the original casebook |
| Decline this method | Whether the commission asks for a map or a warranted judgment; whether an existing collection already covers it; whether the work is temporary | Historical reasons why a different collection once needed the method |
| Revise the method's reach | Which obligations solved which original problem; current counterexamples; what was observed versus inferred; the limits of the original trial | Unrelated dialogue and unsuccessful wording drafts |
| Reassess the original adoption evidence | The actual original notes, source spans and evaluation judgments, not just their counts | No substitute: the ADR summary is insufficient for this particular reassessment |

The last row is a genuine evidence limit. I can reconstruct the reason the ADR gives for its decision, but cannot independently test its claim that the original contract held from the materials inspected here. More explanation in this report would not repair that gap. A later reassessment would need the original materials or new cases; a worker can decline the historical performance claim while still applying the current authorized local convention.

“Decline” also has two meanings that must stay separate. A designer can decline an optional prototype because its purpose does not fit. A writer cannot silently decline a binding local contract on that ground; the writer should route the task elsewhere or propose a contract change through the authorized decision path.

## Finding and applying it: a concrete path and its weak join

The inspected path has two entry points, not one universal retrieval problem.

**Existing collection.** The artifact's path identifies its local `COLLECTION.md`. The root rules require reading it, and the writing skill consumes it. The local contract is where the operative clauses must be available; the historical episode is not an obligatory intermediate step. The current collection definition expressly prohibits inferring rules from the prototype or directory name.

**New collection.** The available add-collection skill starts from the purpose, checks whether a new collection is needed, and asks the worker to adapt a named or suitable existing contract. The prototype catalogue provides an experimental starting point for sourced disagreement. A worker following links from ADR 042 reaches the superseding ADR and catalogue. A worker starting from ordinary collection creation may not traverse that history at all.

That is the weak join: the inspected add-collection instruction does not itself explicitly route an unfamiliar disagreement-mapping task to this experimental template. It can still find a suitable model through search or a caller-supplied path. I have not demonstrated a retrieval failure, and this is not a request to add a pointer. It is a concrete reason not to infer successful reuse from the template's existence. A pointer only helps if the task is recognized under the terms it exposes.

For a writer who has reached the template or a corresponding local contract, application is checkable in a limited way: a reviewer can inspect whether each substantive claim names an asserter and supplies a locatable citation, whether synthesis adopts a verdict, and whether opposing positions receive comparable care. These semantic checks still require source access and judgment. Deterministic Markdown validation cannot establish faithful treatment of a dispute.

## What an ordinary concise correction does—and does not—lack

A strong concise rival would say:

> For a collection whose purpose is mapping sourced disagreement rather than deciding it, use explicit attribution, locatable source spans, topical titles and maintenance based on the positions represented. Read the destination's full local contract. If creating one, the experimental template is optional starting text, not an inherited authority; copying makes it independently owned. Do not generalize its one reported worked case into demonstrated cross-project benefit.

This is a hypothetical compression, not an adopted instruction. Coupled with the existing local contract and template, it retains the main branch conditions, the operation, the authority boundary and the evidential limit. It could plausibly suffice for ordinary creation and writing. No result here shows that an additional explanatory artifact would improve those tasks.

A weaker correction—“allow more than three categories”—fails to carry these distinctions. But beating that sentence would show only that the sentence omitted relevant information. It would not establish that concise correction as a strategy fails.

The history adds a specific affordance when the classification or reuse mechanism is itself challenged: it shows **which constraint was removed and which obligations were preserved**, and why clone-once reuse replaced continuing profile language. This can help prevent two wrong revisions: restoring an exhaustive partition because profiles failed, or building inheritance machinery merely to make historical wording true. These are inferred possible benefits, not observed later errors prevented.

Explanations are unnecessary for a later task when the operative rule and observable conditions already determine the permitted action adequately. A writer checking whether a synthesis sentence names an asserter need not rediscover the category argument. Explanations become candidates for retrieval when the task changes, the conditions are disputed, or the worker must decide which rule to revise. Even then, fresh reasoning from the local purpose may suffice. The existing [episode-retention argument](../../../../theory/retaining-episode-evidence-keeps-a-distilled-rule-open-to.md) supports keeping a route to re-examination, but explicitly does not establish that linked history beats rules alone. This case fits that limitation rather than independently confirming the note.

## Strongest rival and possible changed decisions

**Strongest rival:** keep complete local contracts, a concise correction with current links, ordinary ADR history, and a competent worker that reconstructs reasoning when needed. Do not add a special experience-to-method pipeline. This rival already has retention, purpose checks and source access; it is not an amnesiac baseline.

The bounded difference worth testing would be whether access to selected original evidence improves a disputed revision that the concise correction plus current artifacts cannot resolve. Routine writing is a poor test of that claim because it does not require the omitted evidence. Conversely, restricting the task to reconstructing the original case would unfairly favor history. No comparison was run, and no original-evidence packet was assembled.

Possible decisions changed by this inspection, proposed rather than adopted:

- For routine application, prioritize the complete current local rule over automatic loading of the historical explanation.
- Before claiming methodology reuse, identify its actual consumption point: here collection creation and local writing are different points with different authorities.
- Preserve supersession boundaries when retaining lessons. The current useful lesson is not the original profile design unchanged.
- If considering more retention work, first identify a live revision question that the ordinary correction and current materials cannot answer. Do not require that condition for all exploratory research; it concerns the justification for this particular extra retention investment.

These preserve the bootstrap purpose. They do not settle whether speculative method development should outrank current service, nor restrict research to named consumers. The conflict retained from turns 1–2 remains: a useful explanatory possibility can deserve investigation before demonstrating later benefit, but its possibility does not establish payback or finance its continuation.
