# Turn 4 critique: choose the contrast before buying the comparison

## Disposition

**Prefer B's corrected-baseline contrast for the marginal packaging decision, with two repairs: preserve A's successful-finalization trap, and do not call fewer sessions a demonstrated cost saving.** A's current-practice arm answers an additional worthwhile question—whether the concise correction itself helps—but that question need not be purchased to decide whether extra explanation deserves another allocation. Neither proposal demonstrates benefit, and neither is yet an executable cost-bounded commission.

The purpose remains useful knowledge work and learning to automate methodologies. No doctrine deviation or purpose change is proposed here. This is criticism and a bounded comparison proposal, not authorization to run it. Coordinator scheduling, integration and recovery remain unchanged.

## What is genuinely new relative to earlier turns?

[Turn 1](../turn-01/integration.md) already distinguished useful delivery, learning investment and later benefit; required competent rivals; and separated reusing an artifact from financing its creation. [Turn 2](../turn-02/integration.md) already rejected boundedness as proof of productivity and preserved ordinary judgment as a rival. [Turn 3](../turn-03/integration.md) already supplied the concise correction, uncertainty branches, stronger-assurance distinction and packaging question.

Consequently, the explorers' cautions about blanket refusal, unknown costs, ordinary records and broad-bootstrap inference are necessary constraints, not new discoveries. Their agreement adds no independent evidence. The incremental contribution is a concrete choice of contrasts and diagnostic situations, plus proposed continuation conditions. That is progress in specifying a possible decision, not evidence that the investment is worthwhile.

[A](./explorer-a.md) proposes three conditions and isolated decisions for each situation. [B](./explorer-b.md) proposes two conditions, bundled situations and conditional repetition. These differ on two dimensions at once: which question to answer and how much within-session dependence to accept. They should not be summarized as merely a larger and a smaller version of one experiment.

## Strongest objections

### 1. The third arm buys a different decision

A's current-practice condition can reveal whether concentrating the already-available distinctions into a concise correction helps. B cannot establish that benefit: its baseline already receives the correction. Conversely, omitting that arm does not invalidate B's estimate of the addition to the corrected baseline.

For the commissioned marginal question, B is sufficient in design: current procedure, competent correction and ordinary records are jointly the rival. Keep A's third arm optional only if deciding whether to add the correction is also a live decision. Do not claim that B tests the superiority of either correction or explanation over current practice alone.

Nor should “current practice” mean that all ordinary users actually receive an incident pointer. Both proposals deliberately improve access to that record. They test provided access, not ordinary retrieval. That is acceptable if stated, but their baseline is a controlled comparison package rather than an observed deployment condition.

### 2. B can lose the very mechanism that made this case interesting

Turn 3's surprise was not simply contradictory metadata. It was **successful finalization preserving false provenance**. A includes successful completion as misleading evidence; B's mismatch opportunity does not explicitly do so. Without that trap, a worker can pass by repeating the procedure's mismatch rule without confronting the check's limit.

Retain A's trap in the bounded proposal: supplied current execution evidence contradicts the recorded partition, while an existing result shows successful finalization. Ask what that success warrants, what useful review evidence remains, and what repair is outside authority. The answer must not erase the completed historical state or pretend a future rerun itself repairs it.

This still may be rule recall. The concise correction explicitly says consistency is not authentication. Passing this situation does not establish explanatory inference or adaptation. Renaming identifiers in the second bundle mainly tests robustness to wording, not adaptation to materially different conditions. Describe any positive result accordingly.

### 3. Fewer launches are not necessarily less investment

B's first pair contains eight decisions across two contexts; A proposes twelve isolated decisions. Bundling reduces repeated setup but permits later items to teach distinctions useful on earlier or later items, even without correctness feedback. It also makes failures within a session dependent. A avoids that cross-item effect but buys repeated context loading and still has only one observation per cell.

Neither count establishes a monetary, token or attention advantage. B correctly admits dependence, but the recommendation needs to accept the consequence: the unit compared is a response to a four-item diagnostic bundle, not four independent applications. Fix the same item order in paired conditions and retain it; do not turn four correct answers into four replications. Independent item contexts are an alternative only if separating item performance is worth the extra measured burden.

An adjacent [trial-cost record](../../../use-and-outcome-records/synthesis-pilot-cost-case.md) reports a smaller planned trial whose initial call exceeded its token ceiling and prompted a delivery revision. I treat this as an attributed local record, not a re-audited cost result or a numerical forecast here. It is a useful analogy for the risk both proposals leave open: call caps do not substitute for resource caps.

### 4. “Correct the baseline” must not make explanation unfalsifiably unnecessary

Both explorers sensibly reject a win caused by E alone receiving a necessary instruction. But a stronger version of that rule would defeat any explanation result: after observing E's successful inference, one could always copy its conclusion into C and declare the explanation unnecessary.

Distinguish **a pre-existing required rule omitted from C**, which invalidates the intended competent-rival interpretation, from **a new concise repair invented after seeing a result**, which is another untested candidate. B states this distinction more clearly. Preserve it in integration. Prefer testing that cheap candidate when appropriate; do not retrospectively score it as successful.

The converse also matters. E-over-C success does not establish that causal explanation was responsible. Repetition, salience, length and answer cues move together. The local [contrast argument](../../../../theory/an-experiment-identifies-only-the-contrast-it-actually-runs.md) supplies the reasoning for this limit; its external empirical examples are not adopted here. For a package-investment decision the bundle contrast is enough. For a mechanism claim it is not.

### 5. A favorable pilot does not itself price continuation

Both explorers identify a repeated consequential difference as a reason for another bounded investigation. That is a plausible signal, not a sufficient economic decision. A useful signal could still concern a situation made obsolete by harness changes, recur rarely, or require costly grading. Conversely, an inconclusive result could justify a limited measurement repair when the unresolved question matters enough. Neither positive results nor negative results mechanically determine allocation.

The next proposal must state the decision that further evidence could change, what narrower uncertainty remains, its resource ceiling, and the alternative displaced. A comparative effect without measured cost can justify epistemic interest; it cannot establish net savings. It also cannot oblige the operator to fund continuation. No general allocation formula follows.

## Proposed smallest informative comparison

**Purpose:** decide whether this added explanation merits one further local investigation rather than a baseline repair or stopping this packaging line. Completion would be a frozen comparison record with decisions, unresolved items and measured or unknown costs. No production reviews or state mutation belong in the comparison.

Use B's two conditions: current procedure, turn 3's correction and ordinary records versus the identical material plus a frozen short explanation. Supply identical operational facts and access. Audit the treatment for unique facts and direct answers before execution. The actual explanation and scenario packets do not yet exist as frozen inputs, so no claim of protocol readiness is warranted.

Propose an initial pair of isolated worker sessions, each handling the same four-item bundle:

1. Legitimately known selection partition, missing concrete identity: permit ordinary completion without invented provenance.
2. The same evidential limit under a stronger independent-assurance commission: withhold that assurance, not automatically all useful work.
3. Contradictory execution evidence despite successful finalization: reject authentication by transaction success and distinguish future execution from authorized historical repair.
4. Partition not legitimately knowable before selection: decline guessed selection rather than infer it from self-report.

These are stipulated decision problems, not authenticated recreations of the incident. The [current procedure](../../../../instructions/run-review-batches.md), directly reread here, supports the ordinary unknown/mismatch/selection distinctions. Its permission to record exact launch configuration must not be graded as independent proof when a stronger commission requires that proof. The [incident record](../../../../reference/harness-sub-agent-model-selection-regression.md) supplies attributed history; I did not recover traces or adopt its external API claims.

Freeze a second variant bundle before seeing results. Propose at most one additional paired run if the first reveals a consequential E advantage that survives checking facts, scoring and baseline omissions. This cap is not a measured budget or a power calculation. A future coordinator must supply actual time/token/money limits and evaluator availability before launching. If these cannot be supplied within authority, return partial rather than build testing infrastructure.

Score permitted work, unsupported claims, correct uncertainty branches and repair required—not persuasive prose. Use a condition-blind first assessment where possible and record disputed interpretations as unresolved. Keep original and repaired answers distinct. Record preparation, execution, grading, repair and maintenance estimates separately; unknown components stay unknown. Do not pool bundled answers as independent trials.

A repeated consequential advantage, without false refusal or unsupported assurance, could justify proposing a more realistic local test. It would support the added package under provided access, not discovery, execution reliability, explanation as an isolated mechanism, demand prevalence or acquisition economics. If a new concise repair looks preferable, retain it as an untested alternative rather than silently changing the second-stage contrast. If both conditions succeed, stop this diagnostic packaging line absent another specific uncertainty; that is a resource decision, not proof of zero effect.

## Investment, rivals and purpose

Useful work can expose a problem, methodology can retain a distinction that prevents its recurrence, and automation can apply it more consistently. These are plausible reinforcing mechanisms, not results observed in this trial. They compete when explanation production and grading displace useful reviews, or when maintenance preserves obsolete advice. A harness fix may remove the failure source more directly than explanatory packaging; it remains a serious alternative, not an authorized implementation task.

The strongest rival is competent coordination with existing instructions, ordinary evidence and selective corrections. It already embodies methodology investment. A win for it would not reject methodology or learning. A win for added explanation would not show that a system learned to produce the explanation, that production repays its cost, or that broad automation research dominates service work.

Plan revisions supported here are choosing the marginal contrast, retaining the check-limit trap, protecting against retrospective baseline strengthening, and treating activity caps as distinct from resource budgets. Purpose changes would be making immediate service payback mandatory, making explanation production the objective, or reserving indefinite automation research. None is adopted. The unresolved service/research priority remains explicit despite permission to finish all turns.

For turn 5, the useful next question is what this investigation actually changed relative to its starting plan and which changes required the four-role process. Do not count the now-more-specific unrun proposal as demonstrated superiority of that process. A simpler discussion may have produced the same distinctions; no comparison resolves that rival.
