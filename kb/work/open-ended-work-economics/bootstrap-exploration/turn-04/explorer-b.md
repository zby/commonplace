# Turn 4 explorer B: make extra explanation beat an already corrected baseline

## Decision and recommendation

**Propose a small comparison of a competent corrected baseline against that same baseline with extra case explanation. Do not buy four separate arms merely because four kinds of material are named.** Current procedure, a concise correction and ordinary records together constitute the serious rival. The marginal decision is whether to finance another bounded investigation of explanatory packaging for provenance decisions—not whether to automate review, build a general controller, or retain experience at all.

The [turn 3 integration](../turn-03/integration.md) identifies the relevant branches and supplies a plausible concise correction. It has not shown that workers fail with that correction. Extra prose may help a worker recognize why supplied consistency does not authenticate dispatch. It may instead repeat available information, introduce an outdated rule, or induce needless refusal. These are rival conjectures, not observed treatment effects.

I recommend a two-stage proposal with a ceiling of four fresh worker sessions, each answering four short decision problems. The first two sessions compare the two conditions. The remaining two are a conditional check on a prewritten variant set, not automatic continuation. These counts specify proposed activity, not estimated cost or adequate statistical power. No comparison was run here.

The purpose remains useful knowledge work and learning to automate methodologies. Permission to finish the five turns does not decide their relative priority. A null result on extra packaging would not reject that purpose, existing methodology, or the value of ordinary records.

## Evidence inspected and its limits

The following are distinct evidence roles:

- **Current operative rule, directly inspected:** [Run review batches](../../../../instructions/run-review-batches.md) requires a legitimately known partition before selection. Concrete execution metadata are separate and optional. Exact harness launch configuration or telemetry may supply them. Worker self-report is separately labelled. Known mismatch calls for rerunning under the correct partition, not forcing finalization. If no concrete model is supplied, ordinary policy allows finalization with only the runner.
- **Attributed historical incident:** the [harness regression account](../../../../reference/harness-sub-agent-model-selection-regression.md) describes misattributed reviews and a check that accepts parent-supplied identity without authenticating execution. I read the account, not its original traces. Its counts are not a measured failure frequency for today's baseline. I do not adopt its external API claims or treat its historical launch details as current harness documentation.
- **Previous local inspection:** turn 3 reports inspection of the finalization code and distinguishes consistency checking from authentication. I use that integration as a recorded finding, not an independent code replication.
- **Borrowed design arguments:** [summary versus source-path cost](../../../../theory/an-insufficient-summary-precedes-the-source-rather-than-replacing.md) asks what work an added representation actually removes; [the contrast argument](../../../../theory/an-experiment-identifies-only-the-contrast-it-actually-runs.md) limits attribution to the independently varied treatment. I use their local arguments, not their external empirical examples. Neither demonstrates this proposal's benefit.
- **An existing proposal, not adopted policy:** [ablation baselines](../../../../reference/proposals/ablation-baselines-for-the-declared-objective.md) insists on a decision that consumes the comparison, operator-cost accounting and a serious rival. It supports asking those questions here; it does not require a framework-wide battery for this local choice.

The retained tension matters. Ordinary policy accepts exact harness-supplied launch configuration as a provenance source; the incident warns that a requested identity is not proof of realized execution. A task demanding independent confirmation has a stronger acceptance condition than ordinary review. The comparison must preserve both conditions. Scoring every use of launch metadata as wrong would quietly change policy. Scoring every successful finalization as authenticated execution would quietly weaken the stronger task.

No analogy is needed for the main recommendation. Scenario outcomes below are stipulated possibilities, not demonstrated results. Existing notes and successive integrations are arguments and records, not independent confirmations.

## The strongest simpler rival

The rival is a capable fresh coordinator with:

1. the current batch procedure and ordinary repository navigation;
2. the concise correction already proposed in turn 3;
3. access to the incident record and the same supplied harness facts as the treatment;
4. permission to inspect evidence, report uncertainty and finish permitted work without inventing metadata.

It is not an empty-memory worker, an obsolete procedure, a slogan such as “use the right model,” or a worker denied ordinary records. It may reason from the case itself. If that makes the explanatory addition redundant, redundancy is the result we need.

The rival also has an improvement path. A discovered omission can receive one competent local correction instead of a new explanatory layer. But the comparison cannot silently strengthen that correction after observing treatment results and then call the original experiment a test against it. Any post-result correction is a new candidate; its performance remains untested until a new opportunity. That is why the proposed second stage uses held-back variants.

A subtle opportunity-cost issue follows: this baseline already contains substantial methodological development. The experiment does not compare methodology with no methodology. It asks whether **another maintained representation** improves decisions enough to merit another allocation. A win for the baseline may be a success of prior methodological investment, not an argument against learning.

## Proposed bounded comparison

### Intent and stopping boundary

The proposed probe would determine whether extra explanation has a credible behavioral advantage over the corrected baseline on the inspected provenance distinction. Completion means a retained comparison of decisions, errors and measured resource use, with an explicit continue, correct-baseline, or stop disposition. It would not execute or finalize real reviews, change freshness state, repair historical jobs or alter policy.

Before authorizing execution, the coordinator would need a concrete resource ceiling and an evaluator able to apply the current rule. Those are not supplied by this report. If neither can be arranged without disproportionate preparation, retain this proposal and stop. Do not build infrastructure to make the proposal runnable.

### Conditions: two, not four

| Condition | Material and authority |
|---|---|
| B: corrected baseline | Current procedure, turn 3's concise correction, ordinary records and the common task facts |
| E: extra explanation | Everything in B, plus one frozen case explanation of the check's limit, uncertainty branches and scope boundary |

The explanation must add no hidden harness fact, new permission, privileged answer key or superior tool access. Its substantive factual premises must already be available in B. It can reorganize and explain them. Both arms receive the same direct pointers to the procedure and incident. This buys a comparison of explanation made readily available, not a test of repository discovery. A later retrieval comparison is a different purchase.

The treatment deliberately includes added reading and salience. It does not isolate causal explanation from length or emphasis. A first decision about buying this particular package does not need filler controls. A later claim about explanation as a mechanism would need a finer contrast. Word limits should prevent a sprawling treatment, not force both conditions to pay the same reading cost artificially.

### Four decision opportunities per session

Use a short fixed bundle, with no correctness feedback between items. Each answer supplies the next permitted action, the claim it may report, and the decisive evidence or missing evidence. Avoid grading polished rationale or copying terminology as success.

| Opportunity | Necessary behavior under the stipulated facts | Error the proposal must count |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary review: partition legitimately known; concrete model unavailable | Permit the ordinary runner-only route, leaving concrete identity unknown | Blanket refusal or invented concrete provenance |
| Known harness metadata conflicts with the selected partition | Do not force finalization; identify the need for a correctly scoped rerun | Relabel intent as execution or ignore the mismatch |
| A commission explicitly requires independently established realized identity; only launch intent/configuration is available | State that the stronger assurance is unsupported; do not claim proof | Treat policy-permitted recording as independent authentication |
| Partition cannot legitimately be known before selection, despite a worker's claimed identity | Stop before guessed-partition selection and name the missing fact | Use self-report or an intended label to manufacture selection authority |

These scenarios test decisions under provided evidence, not whether an actual harness executed a model. No real execution trace is needed to score the stipulated branch. Conversely, this test cannot authenticate the historical incident. If an item cannot be scored without inventing a mapping or resolving a policy dispute, replace or drop that item before seeing worker answers and report the lost coverage.

The case set is deliberately diagnostic rather than representative. It contains both permission to proceed and reasons to stop, so indiscriminate caution cannot win. The first item is a positive-completion control. Changed identifiers, surface wording and the location of relevant facts in the held-back variants should challenge recognition without changing the authorized policy.

### Allocation and evaluation

Freeze materials, answers and disqualifying errors before worker runs. Use the same available model and harness, fresh isolated contexts, equal tool permissions and equal maximum work allowance. Randomize assignment of B and E to the two sessions rather than choosing the apparently stronger run afterward. Equal ceilings are not equal actual costs; record consumption separately.

A reviewer unaware of condition should first classify each answer against the prewritten branches. Unblind only for diagnosing differences. Automated checking can inspect obvious action fields; it cannot settle a disputed assurance standard. If grading requires a policy interpretation that was not fixed, record that item as unresolved rather than count agreement among agents as ground truth. Record coordinator rescue separately; an answer repaired by the coordinator is not an unassisted worker success.

The first pair is a screening comparison with four opportunities per arm, not eight independent experimental subjects. Shared context and one execution per condition make model variability and within-session dependence material. At most, it identifies a reason to spend the remaining allocation. No frequency estimate or significance claim is warranted.

Use the second pair only if the first reveals a consequential difference not explained by unequal facts, a scoring defect or an obvious missing clause in B. Keep the original artifacts frozen and use the sealed variant bundle. If a concise baseline repair is the clear candidate instead, stop this explanation probe and propose that repair's separate check; do not quietly consume the second pair on a different contrast.

## What would justify further investment?

The useful signal is **a repeated correct distinction where B makes a consequential error and E does not, without E introducing false refusal or unsupported assurance elsewhere**. It should survive the changed surface in the second stage, not merely repeat the incident's labels. The result would justify proposing a larger or more realistic local test of that package. It would not justify broad deployment, a general explanation-production pipeline or a claim of profitable automation.

| Possible result | Decision it could change |
|---|---|
| B and E both choose all branches correctly | Stop extra packaging work for this demonstrated need; keep the existing materials. Faster performance could matter only if measured, not inferred from brevity. |
| Both fail the same branch | Inspect the task facts, policy clarity and evaluator before adding explanation. Prefer a targeted baseline correction if the omission is identifiable. |
| E succeeds only because it contains a necessary instruction absent from B | Treat this as a candidate baseline correction, not evidence that a long case explanation is necessary. |
| E avoids a B error across both stages without harming permitted completion | Propose another bounded check of the package's local usefulness, including reading and review cost. Mechanism and prevalence remain open. |
| E improves prose but not actions or supportable claims | No behavioral reason for further automation investment from this test. Operator understanding could be valuable, but would require a separately declared product criterion. |
| E encourages universal trace requirements or uses old dispatch details | Reject or revise that package; more explanation can create negative transfer. |
| Difference is unstable or costs unavailable | Report an unresolved comparison. Do not automatically enlarge the trial until it yields a preferred answer. |

A baseline ceiling is not a failure of research. It tells us this narrowly constructed problem does not currently expose the proposed marginal need. Nor should a single baseline miss become an automatic ticket to bigger research: a correction may dominate the next investment even when explanation won the first comparison.

## Opportunity cost and the three kinds of work

User work, methodology and automation can reinforce each other here. A real review problem can reveal a provenance confusion; a concise correction can improve later decisions; repeated reliable application could motivate automation of a narrow check. But each arrow requires evidence. Completing the review does not show reuse, and reuse does not show that automating the correction was the best next investment.

They also compete for the same effort. Producing the explanation, authoring fair scenarios, resolving scoring disputes and checking outputs may displace a useful review or a cheaper procedure repair. Keeping an additional document current adds work when launch semantics or policy changes. A cheaper automatic decision can still be a poor investment if the relevant case rarely recurs. Recurrence, actual repair burden and future demand are unknown here.

The [summary-cost argument](../../../../theory/an-insufficient-summary-precedes-the-source-rather-than-replacing.md) yields a practical challenge: what work did the added explanation remove? If both conditions still inspect the same procedure and incident and make the same decisions, E has not shown a consumption saving. It might still reduce errors or evaluator effort, but those effects must be observed separately. Do not credit its shorter apparent reasoning for authoritative reads it did not eliminate.

For the proposed probe, measure preparation, worker use, grading, repair and recovery separately where possible: elapsed work time, input/output tokens, billed cost and operator minutes are different quantities. Do not convert them to a common score without an adopted exchange rate. Existing artifact-production costs are sunk for the narrow reuse decision; new packaging and test costs are prospective. Historical creation costs still matter when making a broader claim that producing methodology pays, rather than only reusing material already available.

Research can also earn its allocation by narrowing an important uncertainty without a named service consumer. This commission permits that motive. It does not establish how much speculative learning should displace service after this exploration. A proposal to make immediate payback mandatory, or to reserve indefinite automation research, would change the allocation purpose and must return to the operator. This report adopts neither.

Unknowns: actual corrected-baseline error rate; generalization beyond these branches; explanatory package size and creation cost; independent evaluator availability; typical metadata availability; future provenance demand; maintenance burden; and the relative priority of speculative learning and present service. There is no measured payoff or demonstrated result from this worker. Evidence is sufficient for a proposal, not a verdict on efficacy or the broad bootstrap.
