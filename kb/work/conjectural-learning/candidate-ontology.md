# Candidate ontology

Proposed, not adopted. [Popper](./popper-foundation.md) supplies the epistemic
process; the operational boundaries below are our proposal. The
[modern-paper comparison](./comparison.md) assesses possible realizations.

## Definition and minimal roles

**Conjectural learning is learning through tentative solutions to problems,
criticism of those solutions, and uptake of that criticism in subsequent
inquiry or action.** Uptake may change a conjecture, problem, or means of
inquiry. Rejection and discovery of new problems count without acceptance of
a successor. A conjecture can also survive unchanged while a new test result
informs further inquiry.

A **tentative theory** is a proposed solution to a problem, held open to
criticism and revision. Neither definition requires verbal articulation,
symbolic formalization, external storage, fixed weights, or reflection.

| Role | Evidence needed to identify it |
|---|---|
| Problem situation | The difficulty relative to which a proposal is a possible solution |
| Tentative solution | A candidate commitment with criticizable implications or adequacy |
| Critical relation | A reason, conflict, observation, or test bearing on that candidate under stated assumptions |
| Uptake and resulting problem situation | A consequence for subsequent commitments, questions, inquiry, or action |

These are relations, not required software modules or a serial pipeline.
Background assumptions and standards make criticism intelligible and may
become later targets. Competing conjectures and repeated criticism fit.

Uptake distinguishes learning from generating and ignoring criticism. It is
our operational condition, not Popper's experimental membership test. It
need not improve outcomes: criticism can be mistaken. Cross-episode learning
additionally requires persistence capable of influencing later work; the
carrier may be internal state, retained content, code, or another mechanism.
Artifact existence alone proves neither uptake nor causal use.

## Classical refinement and fixed models

Classical refinement's symbolic-form requirement motivates the change in
foundation. Our goal includes natural-language conjectures without prior
conversion into symbolic theories. Popper's process permits that broader
starting point; reliable LLM interpretation remains an empirical question.
Symbolic form and addressability are distinct: an addressable prose artifact
need not be a formalized theory.

[EITHER](../../sources/theory-refinement-analytical-empirical-methods.ingest.md)
and [FORTE](../../sources/automated-refinement-first-order-horn-clause-domain-theories.ingest.md)
remain useful exemplars: proof traces, localization, specialization,
generalization, and successor assessment connect discrepancies to changes
under specified representations, interpreters, evidence, and operators.
Retain *theory refinement* for such scoped operations; do not infer the
ontology of all learning from them. More revision options increase the
assessment burden rather than guarantee better learning.

In a **fixed-model realization**, specified model parameters remain fixed
for a declared interval while the larger learner changes other state.
Theories, instructions, code, tools, and working memory are possible carriers.
Declare the whole learner's boundary, including human contributions and
external services. Inspectable artifacts can help attribute later effects,
but do not establish faithful interpretation, transfer, or general sufficiency
of fixed models. Whether retained theories outperform reconstruction from
records remains open; the current experiments and causal-use checks survive.

## Reflective extension

The learner's conjecture, representation, criticism, testing, or revision
machinery can itself become a subject of conjecture and criticism. Reflective
learning requires uptake affecting subsequent inquiry; answer criticism or
action simulation need not change that machinery. A learner can satisfy the
base definition while its testing method stays fixed.

The KB's [computational reflection](../../notes/definitions/reflective-system.md)
additionally requires a causally connected self-representation. Machinery
adaptation alone does not establish that structural relation. An experiment
must identify what can change, what assessment remains independent, and
which responsibilities remain human. The modern sources do not demonstrate
that this broader extension yields cumulative gains.

## Revising tests and problems

Revision may target theories, representations, auxiliaries, tests, problems,
or learning machinery. Replacing or weakening a failed test requires grounds
to doubt its measurement or relevance. Adding stronger tests or probing new
consequences does not require the old test to be defective. A successor should
answer the earlier difficulty or explain its misidentification while remaining
open to criticism. For empirical claims, independently testable consequences
matter. Retained failures and independent outcome checks are implementation
choices for making this assessable.

Problem reformulation cannot establish compliance with an unchanged external
requirement. State what it solves, what remains unsolved, and who can authorize
a changed requirement. This authority rule is our engineering constraint.

## Boundary cases

| Case | Disposition and evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Internal conjecture changes the next decision after criticism | Eligible within-episode learning; establish the critical relation and uptake, not merely a generated explanation |
| Prose conjecture is retained, revised, and used later | Eligible persistent realization; establish causal use separately from improvement |
| Symbolic rule changes after a counterexample | Eligible restricted realization; show how it answers the discrepancy and affects use |
| Conjecture survives a new test unchanged | Eligible if the result informs further inquiry; survival does not establish truth |
| Weight update without an identified critical process | Undetermined; parameter change alone neither establishes nor excludes the required relations |
| Replacement changes representation | Eligible; show how the successor addresses the problem and earlier criticism |
| Test or auxiliary changes after failure | Eligible when that target is itself criticized; account for the original failure |
| Failed conjecture reveals a better problem | Eligible when it guides inquiry; the old task need not be solved |
| Testing or revision machinery changes | Eligible extension; establish later effects and separately assess computational reflection |
| Repeated answers ignore criticism | No demonstrated uptake, regardless of candidate count or final score |

## Choices before promotion

The recommendation is to adopt the broad process and retain the external-theory
loop as a named realization. Resolve three remaining choices:

- Does *learning* include within-episode uptake, as proposed, or require a
  declared persistence horizon?
- How do tentative solutions, including actions and procedures, relate to the
  KB's narrower theory and claim types?
- Should the machinery-learning extension share the existing reflection
  vocabulary, and which structural conditions would that import?

Use the boundary cases to expose consequential differences between answers.
The [migration map](./migration-map.md) identifies their dependent consumers.
