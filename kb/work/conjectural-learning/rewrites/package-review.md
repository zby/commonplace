## Findings

### Medium, resolved: general reflection narrowed to self-theory revision

Location: [analysis skill, step 3](./kb/instructions/analyse-agentic-system/SKILL.md),
the **Reflection** bullet. The first reviewed version required a theory whose revisions could change the
machinery. The inherited
[reflective-system definition](../../../notes/definitions/reflective-system.md#scope)
instead requires a causally connected self-representation and a process that
can use it. It explicitly distinguishes intercession and says that not every
reflective architecture permits it. Representation-mediated operation can
qualify without revising a self-theory or directly changing the machinery.

This matters because the skill classifies reflection generally, not only the
narrower reflective-theory-builder condition. The bullet could incorrectly
exclude an architecture that observes its own represented state and changes
later behavior through that representation without rewriting its machinery.
It also makes the skill stricter than the staged result type's general
two-way-connection wording.

Smallest correction: use the inherited capacity wording. Reflection requires
a causally connected self-representation of selected aspects inside the
declared boundary; changes in those aspects can update the representation,
and representation-mediated operations can affect later behavior. State that
the path need not already have been exercised. Keep self-theory revision as
the narrower builder condition. Match the type and disposition wording where
they summarize this bullet. The bounded collection-contract paragraph already
uses self-representation and does not require machinery revision.

The parent corrected the skill, result type, and disposition together. I reread
their replacement passages and the proposed collection paragraph. Their
capacity wording now preserves the aspect-relative representation-mediated
path and explicitly separates machinery modification from general reflection.
This is a consumer wording correction under the inherited definition, not a
change to the settled learning definition.

### Medium, resolved: absence of later improvement overstated

Location: [three-harness note, Scope](./kb/notes/evidence/three-2026-harnesses-retain-editable-rules-or-weights-not-rationale.md#scope).
The first reviewed version retained the statement that Commonplace had not
traced a later improvement to an earlier retained theory. Its linked
[Commonplace evidence](../../../notes/evidence/commonplace-as-a-reflective-system.md#improvement-dynamics)
explicitly says that later changes read and transform operative artifacts
retained by earlier changes. The staged evidence-ladder note also correctly
preserves the bounded improvement finding. The negative statement was
inherited from the original harness note, but remained inconsistent with the
evidence cited by this package.

Smallest correction: acknowledge that bounded cumulative pathway while
limiting the unestablished claim to comparative benefit from retained rationale
or increased productivity of later improvement work. Do not treat cumulative
improvement as evidence of compounding.

The author applied that correction to Scope and the Commonplace footer link.
I reread both replacements and the linked evidence. They now agree. No new
empirical result or external-system ranking is asserted.
