# Reading HCL as deployment-time learning

> **Status:** This is a Commonplace workshop reading of Harness Continual Learning (HCL), a controlled harness-learning method. It classifies HCL's update architecture; it does not attribute Commonplace's terminology to the authors or claim that the paper demonstrates live production adaptation. The paired [source analysis](../../sources/harness-continual-learning-adaptation-beyond-model-parameters.ingest.md) records the evidence and qualifications in more detail.

## Why Commonplace calls HCL deployment-time learning

Commonplace calls a process deployment-time learning when post-execution evidence drives a proposal, reject-capable evaluation selects it, and retained behavior-shaping state becomes operative in later executions without a model-weight update. Under this definition, an unevaluated manual patch is durable maintenance, not learning: the evidence must bear on selection, and the selected result must affect later behavior. The relevant unit is [the deployed system, not the model alone](../../notes/the-deployed-system-not-the-model-is-the-unit-of-learning.md).

HCL has that architecture. Post-execution evidence from a controlled task stream drives its optimizer; its evaluator can block adoption; and atomic commitment makes revised interface, memory, capability, and routing state operative in later executions. HCL therefore instantiates the search–evaluation–retention structure described in [A proposal-selection improvement loop requires search, evaluation, and operative retention](../../notes/a-proposal-selection-loop-requires-search-evaluation-and-retention.md). This is an architectural classification under Commonplace's definition, not a claim that HCL was tested in open-world deployment.

## What HCL establishes

HCL fixes foundation-model parameters and jointly versions four parts of the deployed harness: the task interface, experience memory, capability map, and adaptive router. Its optimizer constructs an isolated candidate. When several parts can change, search proceeds through a predefined component order while the other parts stay fixed. The final transition is still all-or-none. Joint versioning and atomic commitment do not make candidate construction a joint optimization process.

The evaluator admits a candidate only when current-task improvement, historical retention, and validity all hold. Historical loss `D_n` counts a finite sample of earlier-task checks, or anchors, that the incumbent solves and the candidate newly fails. The acceptance gate requires `D_n` to remain within the permitted loss budget `B_n`. When `B_n = 0`, every incumbent-solved anchor in that sample is protected. Unsampled behavior lies outside the count, and a previously failed anchor does not count as a new loss. Even with strict sampled-anchor protection, several reported controlled regimes still showed nonzero held-out forgetting. A zero budget therefore tightens acceptance over the observed checks; it does not enlarge the set of behaviors for which the evidence warrants a no-regression conclusion. [Warranted autonomy is bounded by oracle domain](../../notes/warranted-autonomy-is-bounded-by-oracle-domain.md) develops this evidence-domain distinction.

HCL's independent textual budget sweep also exposes an evaluation-cost surface worth measuring. Each profile receives ten proposal opportunities at each of four stages and uses 80 anchors for every previous task. If every opportunity is checked against every applicable historical anchor, that schedule yields `10 * 80 * (0 + 1 + 2 + 3) = 4,800` proposal–historical-anchor case checks per profile. This is a conditional case count, not a measured runtime cost: the paper does not report the effects of caching, batching, or short-circuiting, and it leaves efficient retention evaluation over longer streams open. The count supports comparing evaluation schedules; it does not show that selective evaluation is the right optimization or that HCL is infeasible.

## What HCL does not establish for selective evaluation

HCL's four parts are an engineering partition over mutable state. Its update-permission ablations test bundles of allowed persistent updates; they do not establish those four parts as necessary components, as the correct causal units for predicting impact, or as boundaries within which behavioral effects remain local. [An experiment identifies only the contrast it actually runs](../../notes/an-experiment-identifies-only-the-contrast-it-actually-runs.md) provides this treatment-grain limit.

This workshop therefore does not treat HCL's partition as a validated causal graph. The [provisional selective-evaluation model](./selective-evaluation-model.md) states the additional locality assumptions, and the [experiment design](./experiment-design.md) compares causal selection with other evaluation schedules. The [HCL author invitation](./for-hcl-authors.md) gives the shorter external-facing account and questions.
