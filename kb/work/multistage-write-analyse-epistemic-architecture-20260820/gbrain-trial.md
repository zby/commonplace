## 6. Bounded conclusion

- GBrain acquires source assertions into O1 facts through R1-R2 and records coarse source/session lineage. Source warrant is only partly preserved: the original assertion remains the presumptive basis, but no checked span or semantic-fidelity decision connects it to the extracted text, entity, kind, metric, or confidence. O4 atoms and O7 proposals likewise retain coarse input lineage but are indeterminate between faithful reshaping and model-added interpretation. Similarity, salience, parser, and cache gates control retention; they do not accept the claims as true.

- R11 reshapes retained O1 facts into O6 takes without producing a new synthesized claim: it selects one fact verbatim, clusters related rows, aggregates metadata, and writes chronology. The take inherits at most its selected fact's warrant. The route's age, count, cosine, and confidence criteria license operational promotion into the canonical takes channel, not epistemic acceptance of durability or truth. Hard-coded `holder=self`, averaged confidence, and concatenated sources can degrade attribution relative to the input.

- R4, R6, R9, and R21 generate ampliative O2 dream pages, O3 recurring patterns, O5-L concept narratives, and O13 enriched dossiers. Each has input-side constraints—significance, corpus count, atom count/tier, or context length—and some prompts request links/citations/support. None has a recorded post-generation evidence decision against a truth criterion for an intended scope. The candidates are nevertheless integrated directly through page writes and become available to search, graph, read, and later transformation consumers. Their retention and later use therefore do not constitute acceptance.

- R8 and R10 derive/reshape O5-D concept stubs within the narrow domain of stored concept-reference metadata, titles, counts, and tiers. R17 derives O10 scorecard arithmetic from stored weights and resolution labels. These outputs can be warranted as computations over encoded premises if the implementation is correct, but their licenses do not extend to the truth of atom-concept relations, fact claims, or take-resolution premises.

- R12 produces O7 candidate predictions/judgments and stores them pending, but R13—the claimed human accept/reject and canonical-promotion transition—is absent from inspected source. No proposal is accepted merely by queue retention. The schema can represent decisions, but no implemented consumer, criterion, channel, or force was found. This absence is scoped to the checkout and does not exclude an informal external procedure.

- R14 produces O8 grade conjectures, but the default evidence stage is absent: its placeholder repeats the take rather than retrieving outcome evidence. R15 correctly withholds canonical force in the normal cycle. R16 can grant O8 operational authority through a direct call when a non-`unresolvable` verdict meets self-reported confidence at 0.95 (or the injected ensemble is unanimous above its threshold); the intended use is canonical retrospective resolution, the accepted scope is nominally that take under supplied evidence, the consumer is R17 through database resolution fields, and the force persists until re-resolution. In the inspected default route, however, neither real evidence nor activation of that transition is wired, so the threshold cannot establish epistemic acceptance.

- R18 conjectures O11 calibration patterns and abstracts O12 bias tags from O10, then stores them even if the voice gate falls back. The voice criterion accepts conversational presentation only. R19 gives these unaccepted candidates operational authority: `think` can inject them into question framing, and brainstorming can pass tags to its judge through prompt context for that invocation. Because latest-row consumers do not require `published=true`, the stored `published=false` state does not withhold this local force. No inspected evidence shows that this influence improves calibration or decisions.

- SkillOpt's material route is direct behavior/policy adaptation, not a truth-apt memory-discovery route. R22 converts successful and failed trajectories into O15 instruction edits. R23 accepts a policy candidate only when the mean of per-task median-of-three scores is strictly greater than the current best plus 0.05. Its intended use is better performance on the declared benchmark; its license is bounded to the benchmark tasks, rule/LLM/qrels judges, target model, read-only tools, sample count, and run conditions. R24 can additionally veto a candidate that scores below baseline on a supplied disjoint held-out set. These results drive the checkpoint and either R25's human-review proposal channel or R26's canonical `SKILL.md` instruction channel. R25 has retention but no behavioral force until a human applies it; R26 changes later agent behavior until the skill is edited again. The dream-cycle wrapper always chooses R25. R27's final test score is report-only and cannot retract an already selected edit.

- Consequently, the inspected routes support bounded claims that GBrain automatically acquires, generates, reshapes, retains, and operationally consumes many memory artifacts, and that SkillOpt implements benchmark-gated policy selection. They do not support the broader causal claims that these transformations make the brain/user smarter, that retained synthesis is reliably true or claim-level cited, that consolidation supplies proper attribution and semantic deduplication, that default grading tests real outcomes, or that SkillOpt edits transfer beyond their evaluators. Those conclusions require the missing retained input/output pairs, review and resolution records, runnable traces, deployment configuration, and route-specific causal comparisons identified in output 1.
