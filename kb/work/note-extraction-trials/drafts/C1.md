# Consumer, channel, and force can stay fixed while applicability changes

In the [six-path Commonplace audit](../../../reports/retained/six-commonplace-paths-establish-broad-addressability-not-completeness.md), the explanatory-reach gate prompts a reviewer to check notes carrying the `title-as-claim` trait. Removing that restriction would expose notes without the trait to the same check. The reviewer (consumer), the review (channel), and the prompt to audit (force) would stay the same. What changes is the target: which notes qualify for the check.

The tag-README validator distinguishes the target from the condition that activates the check. An affected marked tag head is the target. After the validator was extended, invoking validation on an ordinary note carrying the tag also triggered a check of that head. Naming the validator, the validation channel, and the force of the check would not tell a reader that validating the member could trigger a check of the head.

Applicability answers two questions the triple leaves open in these cases: what does the rule act on, and when does it act? A description can distinguish the uses by stating the relevant restriction alongside the triple; the examples do not require a fourth field.

---

As the [gate and validator cases show](../../work/note-extraction-trials/drafts/C1.md), consumer, channel, and force alone do not distinguish uses that differ only in which notes qualify or which invocation triggers a check.
