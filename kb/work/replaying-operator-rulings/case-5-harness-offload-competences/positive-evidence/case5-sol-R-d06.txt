# A frozen LLM must already be competent at the learning judgments it owns

In any selection-based learning system where a frozen LLM supplies the judgments
that decide updates, the model must be able to make those judgments from the
starting instructions, tools, and evidence available to it. Selection can improve
the procedures it uses later, but the first useful update cannot depend on a
competence that only that update would supply.

A Commonplace-like loop here means one that proposes, tests, and accepts or rejects
changes to inspectable artifacts that guide later work. It is autonomous with
respect to those updates when people can supply tasks, objections, and outcomes
without having to diagnose the failures, propose remedies, or decide what to keep.

## Base competences

- **Apply retained methods.** Recognize when an instruction, procedure, or tool is
  relevant, use it, and interpret the result. An accepted update cannot improve
  later work if the model cannot act on it in the cases it was meant to change.
- **Diagnose failures in the method.** Distinguish repairing the current output
  from repairing how future outputs are produced. Form plausible explanations
  involving a retained rule, its application, or conditions outside the system.
- **Generate testable alternatives.** Turn a diagnosis into a usable change to
  the retained store. Consider alternatives with different consequences,
  including leaving the current method unchanged.
- **Seek discriminating evidence.** Choose cases and checks that could favor one
  alternative over another, use available tools to run them, and distinguish
  what the results show from what they leave unresolved.
- **Make bounded adoption judgments.** Compare candidates against the current
  method on the consequences that matter, without requiring a single score.
  Accept, reject, defer, or choose a reversible trial as the evidence warrants.
- **Assign credit after deployment.** Trace a later gain or regression to
  candidate updates rather than treating sequence as causation. Use replay or
  comparisons with an update withheld when available to judge what to keep,
  revise, or undo.

## What the model need not do

As in [scheduler–LLM separation](./scheduler-llm-separation-exploits-an-error-correction-asymmetry.md),
code can track versions, execute checks, and enforce adoption or rollback
decisions. If a check settles a comparison, the model need not judge it again.
If the loop can revise its own adoption criteria, the model also needs to tell
when a bad outcome indicts the criterion rather than just the adopted change,
and to propose and test a replacement. A fixed-criterion loop does not need
that additional competence.

Competence is measured with the starting scaffold in place, across the calls
that make a decision, not by demanding a correct answer from every call.
The list specifies prerequisites, not a guarantee of improvement. To challenge
a listed prerequisite, show repeatable improvement from the starting system
while the model lacks that competence on decisions assigned to it, and verify
that neither the harness nor a person is making those decisions in its place.
