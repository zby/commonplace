**Adopt Version 1.**

1. The term is meant to put all of Commonplace's learning under one name with no success condition. Version 2's third condition works against that: by its own account, a memory file nobody revises or a gate that admits once is outside the term, although each is deliberated, and its notes even hold up the log entry as deliberative.
2. Version 1 also follows from the operator's continuity argument for fixed measures. In Version 2, adding a single rescoring step moves a system such as case 13 versus case 14 from outside to inside, which is the kind of discontinuity that argument rejects.
3. Treating correction (*none*, *bundle*, *targeted*) and displacement as reported properties keeps distinctions that Version 2's yes-or-no condition loses. It also fixes Version 2's tensions: Version 2 ties targeted correction to an addressability that its condition 1 never requires, and it defines "change without a judgment" in terms of correction (C) rather than judgment (J).
4. The name points the same way. "Deliberative", as the background traces it, is about weighing at the moment of decision, and the "conjectural" sense now has a reported property of its own instead of an unflagged condition.
5. Before adoption, Version 1 needs these repairs:
   - The loops section still says "each meets the three conditions."
   - "makes and reverses changes" in the single-loop definition presupposes correction.
   - "The reason:" has lost its attribution to the operator.
   - The checks file must be restated for two conditions, with cases 3, 4, 7a, 9 and 13 re-placed as inside with correction *none* or displacement only.

**Keep from Version 2:** the sentence "Later evidence indicts an operating state, not one line in isolation." Version 1's *targeted* grade says the system can "identify the responsible change" without noting that this is an inference from evidence about a whole state. That sentence belongs in Version 1's account of correction, so that "targeted" is not read as given by addressability alone.
