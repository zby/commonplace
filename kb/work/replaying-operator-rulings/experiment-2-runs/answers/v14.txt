## 1. Count automatic changes made under a judged rule

**What to change.**
- **Definition:** Reword condition 2 so that each change is made either by a judgment or by a standing rule that was itself adopted by judgment and remains open to criticism. Condition 3 then applies to the change or the rule that produced it; the notes on condition 3 already say this.
- **Definition:** Delete "Storing session traces is learning but not deliberative" and the log example under "What the term does not cover."
- **Checks:** Move case 2 inside, for the entries as well as the logging rule, when the logging rule was deliberated and the system can revisit it. Leave case 11 outside unless the solver's own loop can revise its add-everything rule.

**Reason.** The draft says the term exists to put all the learning in Commonplace under one name. Yet it calls Commonplace's session-trace storage "learning but not deliberative," so it fails its stated job inside its own text. The draft already treats introducing automatic storage as a deliberated change, so judging at the level of the rule closes the gap without admitting rules fixed outside the system.

## 2. Make condition 2 a decision, not a claim of improvement

**What to change.**
- **Definition:** Reword condition 2 to say each change is made by a decision that weighs the expected effects on later work. Remove "on-balance judgment that the system will do its later work better."
- **Note:** Separate two things that currently share the phrase "on-balance judgment":
  - the **decision**: weighing reasons and acting, which may proceed while the verdict is Open;
  - the **verdict**: Improvement, meaning every reasonable measure favors the successor.

  Have the definition link to the decision.

**Reason.** The note defines "on balance" as every reasonable measure favoring the change. The draft itself says "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change and that such changes are kept as decisions. Read literally, condition 2 therefore excludes most of Commonplace's changes, including ADR 085's deletion rule. It would also merge membership in the process with the separate "learned" claim that the draft insists is distinct.

## 3. Weaken the displacement clause of condition 3 to a reported property

**What to change.**
- **Definition:** Keep in condition 3 only the minimal requirement: the system's own loop can change an admitted change again in response to its later work. Drop "Being displaced by a higher-scoring alternative is not enough" as a membership condition.
- **Definition:** Report as a property, or name as a kind, whether later evidence bears on the change itself ("evidence-revisable") or the change is only replaced by a better-scoring successor ("displacement-only").
- **Checks:** Cases 4 (repairer alone), 7a, 9 and 13 become inside and displacement-only. Update "What the ruling changed" and "C now does the excluding that J used to do" to match.

**Reason.** The operator admitted fixed measures under condition 2 so that a small change to how a system judges would not move it out of the term. Cases 13 and 14 show the displacement clause does exactly that: the Darwin and Huxley Gödel machines differ only in whether descendant results are read as evidence. Keeping the distinction as a reported property preserves the sharp test the checks rely on, while following the instruction to prefer the more general definition.
