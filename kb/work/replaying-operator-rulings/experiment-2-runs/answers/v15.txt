## 1. Make condition 2 a decision to change the store, and keep "on balance" for the verdict

**Change.** Reword condition 2 so it reads roughly: "Each change is made by a judgment, taken as a step of the loop, that making it serves the system's later work. The judgment may rest on an open comparison or on a single criterion the system applies." Keep "on balance" for the *learned* verdict only. In the note, add a short passage saying that acting on an open or single-criterion comparison is a decision, not an on-balance claim.

**Reason.**
- The note defines an on-balance judgment as one where *every* reasonable measure favours the successor, and says explicitly that "it is not enough that some reasonable measure favors the step." That contradicts the draft's claim that a fixed measure is "the special case" of such a judgment.
- The draft also says "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change, and its reversibility bullet lets a change be kept without evidence of improvement. Read strictly, condition 2 would then exclude most of Commonplace's actual changes, which defeats a definition meant to cover them all.

**Change.** State that a change made automatically by a rule counts as deliberative when the system itself deliberated the rule and can revisit it. In that case, the judgment and the criticism attach to the rule.

This requires several edits together:

**Reason.**
