## 1. Condition 2 should require a decision, not a judgment of improvement

**Change:** Reword condition 2 so that each change is made by an on-balance judgment of whether to make it, weighing its expected effect on later work. Drop "that the system will do its later work better with the change than without it."

**Reason:** The draft says "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change, and that letting a change stand is a decision. The note it relies on also says this, citing ADR 085's deletion rule and the reversibility argument. As written, condition 2 excludes exactly those decisions, which make up much of the learning in Commonplace. Requiring a decision weighed on balance keeps J's discriminating force (something could have gone against the change) and drops a demand the definition's own verdict scheme says usually can't be met.

## 2. Bring rule-governed changes inside, so the definition covers all of Commonplace's learning

**Change:** In condition 2, let a change also qualify when it is made by a standing rule that the system adopted by such a decision and can revise. Then:
- Delete "Storing session traces is learning but not deliberative."
- Delete the log example under "Change without a judgment."
- Re-place case 2 as inside when the system can revisit its logging rule, as its discriminator already half-says. Case 11 stays outside on C.

**Reason:** The stated purpose is to put *all* learning in Commonplace under one name. Yet the draft calls trace storage learning and then excludes it, contradicting that purpose. The draft already says adopting automatic storage is a deliberable, revisable change. Locating the judgment at the rule is a generalization that removes the contradiction without adding a new category.

## 3. Move the single-loop/double-loop typology out of the definition

**Change:** Cut the following from the definition:
- the single-loop/double-loop paragraph,
- the Argyris, authorization, Gödel-machine and nested-loops notes,
- the two related Misuse bullets,
- the "what Commonplace adds" exclusion.

Put this material in its own note, leave a one-line pointer, and move the checks' kind columns (including cases 15, 17 and the paired table) to accompany that note.

**Reason:** The kind never affects membership. The checks file says the double-loop ruling "does not change S, J or C," yet this material is about a third of the definition. It is also where most of the per-objection qualifications have piled up. Separating it makes the membership definition match its purpose and lets the typology be revised without destabilizing the term.
