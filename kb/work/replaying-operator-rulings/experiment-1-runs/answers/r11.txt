# Review of the draft definition of deliberative learning

## What each condition and exclusion keeps out

### Conditions

**1. Legible store**
- **Keeps out:** gradient training of weights (check 5). The sharper case is a team that chooses between model checkpoints by an on-balance judgment and rolls back bad ones. That team meets J and C at its own boundary and fails only S.
- **What would be lost:** the ability to attach a judgment to one change and to criticize what the change says. With weights inside, criticism could only say "this version is worse," never "this line is wrong."
- **Weakness:** the loss is smaller than the draft says. Check 6 admits an unexplained prompt variant that is judged only on outcomes and never criticized for its content. Between check 6 and the checkpoint team, only readability differs. The kind of judgment is the same.
- **What this means:** the draft's reason ("a judgment about one change can be made and contested only if the change can be read") does not separate these two cases. The condition earns its place only if the definition values the *possibility* of criticizing content, even when no one does it. The draft should say so.

**2. Judgment in the loop**
- **Keeps out:** Recuris, whose held-out gate sits outside what it may change; PRODIGY alone; the link repairer alone (check 4); the builder in check 7.
- **What would be lost:** the line between deliberative learning and optimizing a readable artifact against a benchmark. Specifically, the definition would lose the capability that separates check 8 from check 7: rejecting the score winner for a stated reason about future work. That is the guard against overfitting the gate.
- **The autonomous claim would also become trivial.** Any language model hill-climbing a benchmark would count as a model running the outermost loop. That would say nothing about the conjecture actually under test, which is whether a model judges well with no measure given to it.

**3. Open to criticism**
- **Keeps out:** check 3, a team whose deliberated decisions are final. Real examples are a standard ratified with no errata process, or a one-off curated release.
- **What would be lost:** the term would shrink to "deliberation whose outcome is stored." The Background says the "learning" part is exactly what this condition adds.
- **The section on when a change was learned would also stop working.** "Open," which the draft calls the usual verdict, would have no path to changing behaviour. Keeping an open-verdict change as a decision is defensible only because it can be revisited.

### Exclusions

**Learning in general**
- **Keeps out:** gradient-trained models, and Simon-style learning with no retention judgment.
- **What would be lost:** the term would mean just "learning" and would do no work.
- **Problem:** this exclusion makes the purpose sentence ("all the learning that happens in Commonplace") depend on Commonplace containing no other kind of learning. See change 2.

**Retention bound to a fixed measure**
- **Keeps out:** PRODIGY in particular, through the clause "even if it revises its estimates from use."
- **What would be lost:** an adaptive estimator would count as questioning its measure. But revising an estimate of utility is not revising what utility is taken to mean.
- **Mismatch:** condition 2 says "one measure," while this exclusion says "one formula … cannot question." Recuris's gate is made of several tests, so it passes the condition's wording. Only the gloss in the checks file catches it.

**Retention without a judgment**
- **Keeps out:** check 2's append-only log, history kept by default, and caches.
- **What would be lost:** condition 3 would become empty, because there would be no judgment to find wrong. Mere accretion would also count as learning, which is the bias the on-balance note warns about.
- **Problem:** Commonplace almost certainly keeps such stores, such as session logs, traces, evidence files and git history. Later agents read them, so they change capacity. This exclusion is what makes the purpose sentence false.

**A claim of success**
- **Keeps out:** no system. It rules out an inference, not a member.
- **If success were required instead:** check 1's team would drop out once its rule turned out bad. Since verdicts are usually Open, no system could be placed when it retains a change. Commonplace's own status would be unknown for most of its history.

## The three most important changes

**1. Change what the judgment in condition 2 is about.**
- **Change:** replace "an on-balance judgment that the change improves the system's future work" with "an on-balance judgment that keeping the change is better for the system's future work than dropping it."
- **Reason:** the linked note reserves "improves on balance" for the case where every reasonable measure agrees. Yet the draft says Open is the usual verdict and that reversibility can tip a decision to keep without being evidence of improvement. As written, every keep with an open verdict and every ADR-085-style deletion fails condition 2, and because the condition says "each change," one such retention puts Commonplace outside its own term.

**2. Fix the quantifier and make the coverage claim true.**
- **Change:** replace "each change" with "each change the system retains as a candidate for its future work," and name the store. Then restate the purpose as "all the learning Commonplace retains by judgment," and say what falls outside: logs, traces and history kept by default.
- **Reason:** read literally, default-kept writes take Commonplace out entirely. Read as check 2 reads them, they are learning outside the term. Either way, "all the learning that happens in Commonplace" is false, and the checks file already shows KSI's placement depending on this choice.
- **Alternative and its cost:** you could instead move the judgment up to the *retention rule* ("keep all logs" as a judged policy). That would flip check 2 and leave "retention without a judgment" almost empty.

**3. Align the deterministic-check note with check 4 and the fixed-measure exclusion.**
- **Change:** amend "So a change kept because a validator passed meets the condition" to add "…when the named system has judged that the validator suffices for that class of change and can revise or override it." Also change "not bound to one measure fixed beforehand" to "not bound to a rule, of one test or many, that the loop cannot revise or override."
- **Reason:** as written, the note admits the link repairer alone, which check 4 and the exclusion keep out, and "one measure" admits Recuris's gate. The qualifier also says how Commonplace's inner validator loops count, which the example of training inside a research team otherwise leaves in doubt.

## Smaller points

- **PRODIGY and Minton:** the Background says "taken together with Minton, it was deliberative learning." That contradicts the training-inside-research rule and the checks' instruction not to credit designers' judgments. It should say that Minton's research was deliberative learning and PRODIGY's retention of rules was not.
- **Wrong condition cited:** the Background says keeping and revising the outcome are "the first and third conditions." But keeping, meaning retention, belongs to condition 2. Condition 1 is legibility.
