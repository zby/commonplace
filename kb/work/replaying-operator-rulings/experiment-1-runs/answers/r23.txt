## 1. Quantify condition 2 over what the system retains as learning, not over every change

**Change:** Replace "Each change is kept or dropped by an on-balance judgment" with wording such as "What the system retains as learning in the named store is selected by an on-balance judgment." Then say that automatic writes, such as logs or attempt records, are inputs to the loop and not changes the condition tests.

**Reason:** As written, one unjudged write anywhere in the store moves a system outside the term. Adding an attempt log to KSI, or any routine automatic write to Commonplace, would therefore exclude it, and the checks file already flags this. The draft exists to cover all the learning in Commonplace, so a quantifier that the project's own logging would break is the most serious discontinuity.

## 2. Restate condition 2 as "bound to no criterion the loop cannot question," not "not bound to one measure fixed beforehand"

**Change:** Make the discriminator whether the retaining loop can question and override its criterion, which is how the checks file states J. Rewrite the validator note to match: a change kept because a validator passed meets the condition only where the same loop owns that validator and can override it. Cases 4 and 4-with-team show both sides.

**Reason:** The current wording jumps at an arbitrary point. A gate of one fixed test fails, but a gate of two fixed tests seems to pass, which contradicts the Recuris placement ("several tests in a fixed rule are still a fixed rule"). The wording also conflicts with the draft's own claim that validator-gated retention meets the condition, since a validator is a measure fixed beforehand.

## 3. Make the judgment in condition 2 about keeping the change, not about the change having improved the system

**Change:** Replace "a judgment … that the change improves the system's future work" with "a judgment that keeping the change is better for the system's future work than dropping it." That judgment may rest on an open verdict and on reversibility.

**Reason:** The draft says that "open" is the usual verdict, that keeping a change is a decision rather than a claim of learning, and that reversibility can favour keeping a change without being evidence of improvement. Condition 2 as worded therefore pushes honest practice either to overclaim or to fall outside the term. As written, a team's membership would flip on how it phrases the same retention decision ("this improves" versus "this is worth trying"), which is not the difference the term exists to mark.
