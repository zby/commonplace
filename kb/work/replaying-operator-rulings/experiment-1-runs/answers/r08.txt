## 1. Reword the measure clause in condition 2

**Change:** Replace "It is not bound to one measure fixed beforehand" with "It is not bound to a measure that the system cannot question or override."

**Reason:** As written, a change kept because a fixed validator passed fails condition 2. Yet the note on deterministic checks says such a change meets it, and Commonplace keeps many changes this way. The reworded clause is the test that the notes, case 4 and the Recuris placement already apply: can anyone inside the named boundary decide otherwise than the measure? It resolves the contradiction without adding a condition, and no case changes placement.

## 2. Change "each change" to the changes the claim is about

**Change:** Make the conditions apply to the changes that are put forward as learning, not to every write to the store. For example: "A change is learned deliberatively when…", with the "Retention without a judgment" bullet scoped the same way.

**Reason:** Commonplace also writes logs, generated indexes and other records that no judgment selects. Under "each change", those writes would take the whole system outside the term, which defeats its stated purpose. The checks already show the quantifier silently flipping KSI's placement, and the draft's own verdict section treats the claim as being about one change.

## 3. Make the judgment in condition 2 a judgment to keep, not a judgment that the change improves

**Change:** Replace "an on-balance judgment that the change improves the system's future work" with "an on-balance judgment of whether keeping the change serves the system's future work."

**Reason:** The draft says a system may keep a change whose verdict is open, as a decision, and that reversibility can tip that decision. Neither is a judgment that the change improves future work. Commonplace's routine retentions, such as the routing line and ADR 085's deletion rule, would otherwise fail condition 2 or be misreported as claims of improvement. That is the misuse the draft itself warns against.
