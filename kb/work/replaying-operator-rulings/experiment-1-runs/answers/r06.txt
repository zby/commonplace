## Changed text

**Condition 2**, replacing the current item:

> 2. **Judgment in the loop.** Each change is kept or dropped by an [on-balance judgment](../../theory/on-balance-judgments-can-guide-improvement-without-a-global-measure.md) that the change improves the system's future work. The judgment is a step of the learning loop. It may use measures and may serve an objective fixed beforehand. But the loop can reject a change that a measure favors, or keep one it disfavors, for a stated reason about future work.

**New first bullet under "Notes on the conditions → Judgment in the loop":**

> - **A fixed objective is not a fixed measure.** A loop can reason at length toward a fixed objective and still meet this condition. It fails if it must keep whatever one measure favors, however much it reasons first. Whether the loop may overrule a measure depends on what the loop is permitted to do, not on how well it judges. How well it judges is a matter of capability and is not part of membership.

## Reason

The reviewer is right that the old sentence, "not bound to one measure fixed beforehand", defined the condition by an absence. It also contradicted the draft's own note that a loop retaining on a validator pass can qualify. The proposed wording would move the boundary rather than clarify it. Case 7, the Recuris placement and the lone link repairer each revise yesterday's retention, so they have "judgments open to revision". Yet the checks exclude all three because no step inside the loop may overrule the score that admitted the change. Moving that to "capability" would erase the distinction the term exists to draw, from PRODIGY-style and fixed-loss learning. It would also fold condition 2 into condition 3. The rewrite keeps the reviewer's split: it allows deliberation under a fixed objective and leaves the quality of judgment to capability. It tests only the structural power to overrule a measure, which matches J as the checks file already states it.
