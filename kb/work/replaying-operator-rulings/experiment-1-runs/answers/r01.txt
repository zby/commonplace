# Review: draft definition of deliberative learning

## What each condition keeps out

**1. Legible store**

- **Keeps out:** learning that the human judges inside Commonplace keep only in their heads. Example: an operator who, over many rounds, gets better at spotting misrouting but never writes down why. A second example: a team that keeps or rolls back whole fine-tuned checkpoints by on-balance review. That team passes conditions 2 and 3 at the level of whole checkpoints, but fails condition 1.
- **Loss if inside:** Neither the per-change "learned" verdict nor the autonomous claim would have anything to work on. Both need a change that someone else, a language model in particular, can read and point at.
- **Problem with the stated rationale:** The checkpoint case shows that "legibility makes the second condition possible" is too strong. The draft itself has people judging model versions on balance. What legibility actually supplies is criticism aimed at the *content* of a change, not the ability to judge at all.
- **Problem with the brief:** The operator's tacit learning happens inside the system's own boundary, and this condition leaves it out.

**2. Judgment in the loop**

- **Keeps out:** three systems.
  - Recuris, whose held-out gate sits outside the surface it may patch.
  - PRODIGY on its own.
  - The link repairer on its own.
- **Loss if inside:**
  - The term would merge with metric-gated self-improvement.
  - The autonomous conjecture would have nothing to test, because any hill-climber would already qualify. The conjecture is that a model can "judge on balance with no measure given".
  - Condition 3 would lose its main target: a retention judgment whose basis can be found wrong.

**3. Open to criticism**

- **Keeps out:** case 3. Also an agent that appends lessons it judged useful to an append-only notebook that no later step ever revisits.
- **Loss if inside:**
  - The term would cover one-shot deliberation. The background says that what "learning" adds to deliberation is keeping the outcome and revising it from later evidence.
  - The verdict scheme would lose its point. "Open" is the usual verdict, and keeping a change on an open verdict is defensible only because a later round can act on the evidence that arrives.
  - Accretion, which the on-balance note warns about, would count as learning.

**Scoping rule (the named system, checked on the outermost loop it runs itself)**

- **Keeps out:** a training run credited with the team's deployment judgment (case 5), and PRODIGY credited with Minton's revisions.
- **Loss if inside:** Every automated loop would qualify by borrowing its designers' judgment. The autonomous claim, that no person is needed inside the boundary, could then not be stated, because the boundary could always be drawn to include a person.

## What each exclusion keeps out

**Learning in general**

- **Keeps out:** gradient training. But conditions 1 and 2 already exclude it, so no system is kept out by this item alone. It states the genus rather than drawing a boundary.
- **Loss if inside:** "Deliberative learning" would become a synonym for learning and lose its contrast.

**Retention bound to a fixed measure**

- **Keeps out:** PRODIGY on its own, whose utility estimates were revised from use. This restates condition 2.
- **Loss if inside:** the same loss as for condition 2.
- **What it adds:** the clause "even if it revises its estimates from use". It stops revising the parameters of a formula from being mistaken for revising the formula.

**Retention without a judgment**

- **Keeps out:** case 2, the service that appends every interaction to a searchable log.
- **Loss if inside:** "Kept" would no longer imply a decision. Condition 3 would have no judgment to find wrong, and there would be no decision to report separately from learning.
- **Conflict with the brief:** Commonplace almost certainly keeps stores like this, such as session logs, git history and generated indexes. These change what later agents can do. Under the current text, that learning falls outside the term.

**A claim of success**

- **Keeps out:** no system. It keeps systems *in*, for example a Commonplace whose kept changes all turn out to be "made worse".
- **Loss if it became a condition:**
  - Membership could not be decided when a change is retained, since "Open" is the usual verdict.
  - The term would collapse into "learned".
  - The first listed misuse, citing the name as evidence of improvement, would be built into the definition.

## Three changes, most important first

**1. Reword condition 2 so the judgment is a decision to keep, not a claim of improvement.**

- **Change:** "Each change is kept or dropped by an on-balance judgment that keeping it is the better course for the system's future work." Add that this judgment may rest on an open verdict, on reversibility, or on keeping a change in order to test it.
- **Reason:** As written, the judgment must be *that the change improves* future work. Yet the draft says "Open" is the usual verdict, that keeping a change is not having learned it, and that reversibility can favour keeping a change without evidence that it helps. Read strictly, the condition excludes most of Commonplace's retentions, including changes kept as trials, which is the opposite of what the brief needs.

**2. Fix the quantifier "each change", and make the boundary and the brief agree.**

- **Change, part 1:** State that "each change" ranges over *retention decisions* about the named store. Writes made by default, such as logs, history and generated indexes, count through the judged decision that keeps the mechanism producing them, for example a policy to keep and search session logs.
- **Change, part 2:** Then do one of two things:
  - restate the purpose as "the way Commonplace learns" and list what stays outside, such as the human judges' tacit learning and vendor model upgrades; or
  - accept those as gaps against the brief.
- **Checks file:** This change flips case 2 wherever a judged, criticizable logging policy exists, so record the explanation there.
- **Reason:** The checks file already shows that KSI's placement depends on this quantifier. The same question decides whether Commonplace's own logs and indexes are inside. The purpose line promises "all the learning in Commonplace", while condition 1 and the "retention without a judgment" exclusion keep some of it out, so either the boundary or the promise has to move, and the draft should say which.

**3. Tie the degenerate-judgment note to the named system, and replace its example.**

- **Change:** A change kept because a validator passed meets condition 2 *only if the named system's own loop can question whether the check suffices*. Otherwise it is retention bound to a fixed measure. Drop "whether a link resolves" as the one-measure example; case 4 in the checks file notes that a passing check does not show the link reaches the intended page.
- **Reason:** Without "within the named system", the note readmits every fixed-gate system, because someone outside can always question the gate. Recuris and the repairer on its own would then pass, contradicting both the checks and the second exclusion. The example also undercuts the note: if even link resolution is not a one-measure question, the degenerate case is rarer than the draft implies.
