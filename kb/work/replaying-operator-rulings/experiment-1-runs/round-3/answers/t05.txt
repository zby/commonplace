# Review of the draft definition of deliberative learning

## Ten candidate changes

**1. Rewrite condition 2 as "not bound to any rule the named system cannot itself question," replacing "not bound to one measure fixed beforehand."**
**Adopt.** As written, a gate made of several fixed tests escapes the letter of "one measure," although the checks exclude Recuris on exactly that ground ("several tests in a fixed rule are still a fixed rule").

**2. In the note on deterministic checks, say that a validator pass meets condition 2 only when the named system can question whether the validator is enough.**
**Adopt.** The note now says a change kept because a validator passed "meets the condition," but case 4 places the repairer alone outside on J. The unconditional sentence contradicts the checks file. This qualifier is the note-level form of change 1.

**3. Specify what "each change" ranges over: the changes a system keeps for its future work. Let the judgment concern either a single change or a retention rule the system applies to a class of changes, such as "keep every ingest."**
**Adopt.** With "each" unqualified, any write kept by default, such as KSI's attempt log or Commonplace's routine records, disqualifies the whole system. That defeats the stated purpose of covering all of Commonplace's learning. A rule-level judgment the system can revise covers those writes. Case 2 stays outside, because no judgment inside its boundary selects or questions the append-everything policy.

**4. Define the term as a learning *process* that meets the three conditions, and state that it is a kind of process, not a kind of achieved learning.**
**Adopt.** The draft says deliberative learning has no success condition, yet "What the term does not cover" calls it "one way of learning among several" in Simon's sense, which is a lasting change in capacity. Those two claims cannot both hold unless the term names a process.

**5. Build the declared area of work, the alternative and the time horizon into the definition of "learned."**
**Adopt.** The on-balance note says that "three choices fix the comparison" before any verdict means anything. The draft mentions only the declared area of work, and only later, among the points on using the verdicts.

**6. Rewrite the PRODIGY bullet so that the named system is Minton's research program with PRODIGY, not "it."**
**Adopt.** As written, the bullet credits PRODIGY with judgments its designer made elsewhere, which the checks file forbids. It also blurs the "outermost loop the system runs itself" rule.

**7. Make the store list in condition 1 illustrative ("such as prose, structured text and code").**
**Adopt.** The list is now closed, which conflicts with "any kind of change counts" and would exclude legible stores like diagrams or tables. (Separately, Background says "the four sources are ingests," but it cites four ingests plus a Merriam-Webster entry.)

**8. Add a success condition, so that membership requires at least one change to have been learned.**
**Reject.** The checks stipulate that no case assumes improved capacity. Membership would also become undecidable at the time a system acts, because "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change.

**9. Require that every retained change can be undone.**
**Reject.** Commonplace deletes things irreversibly, and case 3 sets condition 3 on correcting the judgment, not on restoring the file. Reversibility already enters properly as a consideration inside the condition-2 judgment.

**10. Require content-directed criticism (a stated explanation of why a change works), or require that the judge is a language model.**
**Reject.** Either requirement would flip a stable case. Case 6 places an unexplained prompt variant inside. Case 1 and Commonplace's bootstrap count human judgment, and the autonomy question is meant to be the conjecture under test, not part of the definition.

## The three I would make

1. **Changes 1 and 2 together (condition 2).** Proposed wording: *"Each retained change is kept or dropped by an on-balance judgment that it improves the system's future work. The judgment is a step of the loop, and it is not bound to any rule that the named system cannot itself question."* Then edit the deterministic-check note to match. This closes the multi-test loophole and removes the direct contradiction with case 4.

2. **Change 3 (scope of "each change," with rule-level judgments).** Without it, the definition either fails its own purpose for Commonplace or silently shifts KSI's placement depending on which store is named.

3. **Change 4 (a learning process, not learning).** It removes a logical inconsistency at the head of the definition, at the cost of one word.

I re-ran the stipulated cases and surveyed systems against these three changes, and no placement flips:

- Case 4: the repairer alone stays outside; the team plus the repairer stays inside.
- Cases 7 and 8: case 7 stays outside and case 8 inside.
- Case 2: stays outside.
- Surveyed systems: Recuris stays outside, and KSI's distilled guidance stays inside.
