# Review of the draft definition of "deliberative learning"

## The five pairs

**1. Link repairer, alone and with the team.** Yes, the term should mark this difference. "The link resolves" is not the only reasonable measure of whether a repair helps, because a patch can resolve to the wrong note. So what separates the two systems is whether anyone inside them can question that measure. The draft marks this inconsistently. Its "degenerate on-balance judgment" note says a validator pass meets condition 2, which would put the repairer alone inside. And condition 2's "each change" still excludes the patches even when the team is added.

**2. Program with a fixed utility formula, alone and with its researcher.** Yes, for the same reason as pair 1, but the draft marks it at the wrong level. In both systems the rules are kept or dropped by the formula. By the draft's own model-training example, only the researcher's revisions to the formula would count as deliberative. Yet the background bullet on PRODIGY (the precedent this pair describes) calls the combined system deliberative learning outright. The definition has to say whether learning inside a fixed-measure loop is covered.

**3. Prompt search, with and without a step that can reject the score winner.** Yes. This is the central case of condition 2. But "can reject" should be tested by whether that step actually does the retaining. A veto that never fires is the benchmark plus a rubber stamp. Also, the second system as described does not show condition 3: nothing says later rounds can revisit its choices.

**4. Log service, appending everything or judging what to keep.** Yes. Keeping everything by default is learning without a judgment. But marking this difference conflicts with the stated purpose. If Commonplace keeps anything by default, such as ingests or logs, the term no longer puts "all the learning" in Commonplace under one name.

**5. Team that never revisits decisions, and team that can revise after failures.** Yes. Revising from later evidence is what "learning" adds to deliberation. But "never revisits" describes what the team does, while condition 3 says later rounds "can" criticize, which describes what it is able to do. The draft must say which one it tests. A team that is able to revise but has not yet met a failure should stay inside.

## The three most important changes

**1. Make condition 2 about a decision to keep, not a judgment of improvement.** As written, condition 2 requires "an on-balance judgment that the change improves the system's future work." The draft's own section on verdicts says "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change, and that keeping a change is a decision, not a demonstrated gain. The reversibility bullet says the same: a change can be kept because it is easy to undo, "not evidence that the instruction has improved later work." Under the current wording, honest systems either fail condition 2 or must overclaim.

Proposed wording: *"Each change is kept or dropped by a decision made on balance, for stated reasons about the system's future work. The decision is a step of the loop and is not bound to one measure fixed beforehand."*

**2. Replace the "outermost loop" sentence and the degenerate-case note with an explicit rule for loops inside loops.** The draft currently contains three statements that conflict:
- condition 2 says "each change" must be judged;
- the conditions are "checked on the outermost loop";
- the excluded cases include "retention bound to a fixed measure," while the notes say a validator pass meets the condition.

These cannot all hold, and pairs 1 and 2 fall into the gap.

Proposed rule: *"A loop that keeps changes by a fixed measure is part of the system's deliberative learning when (a) its store is legible, and (b) the measure itself is a change kept by a deliberative loop in the same named system, open to criticism there."*

This rule handles every case the draft cares about:
- Commonplace's validator-gated changes are inside, which serves the stated purpose.
- The repairer with its team, and PRODIGY with Minton, are inside. The PRODIGY bullet then reads correctly.
- Gradient training still fails, on the legible-store condition. This keeps the model-training example consistent.
- Each system without its outer loop is outside.

The rule also lets you delete the claim that one validator measure is "the only reasonable one." That claim confuses the question the validator answers ("does the link resolve?") with the question retention answers ("does this change help future work?").

**3. Make condition 3 a structural test, and drop "the same loop."** Under change 2, criticism of a measure comes from an outer loop, so "later rounds of the same loop" is too narrow.

Proposed wording: *"There is a path by which later evidence, from any loop in the named system, can show a decision or the measure it relied on to be wrong, and the system acts on that finding."*

Add a note on how to show it: name the mechanism. A record of past revisions is not required, which keeps the able-but-not-yet-tested team in pair 5 inside. A nominal ability that nothing feeds is not enough, which handles the rubber-stamp veto in pair 3.

**Runner-up: reconcile the purpose sentence with the exclusions.** Either check that Commonplace keeps nothing by default, or change the purpose to "the learning Commonplace is designed to do" (pair 4).
