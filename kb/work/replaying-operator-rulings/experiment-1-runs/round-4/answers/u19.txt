# Three proposed changes to the deliberative learning draft

## 1. Condition 2: require a decision to keep, not a judgment that the change improves

**Change:** Reword condition 2 so that each change is kept or dropped by an on-balance judgment *about whether to keep it for the system's future work*. Drop the phrase "that the change improves the system's future work". The checks already state J this way: "decides what to keep for future work". Add a sentence to the note saying the same thing. The note should say that its decision procedure, which keeps a change when a plausible answer wouldn't reverse the choice, differs from its verdict of **Improvement**.

**Reason:** The note's "improvement" requires every reasonable measure to favour the successor. The draft itself says "Open" is the usual verdict for a recent change and that keeping it is "a decision", not a judgment of improvement. Read literally, condition 2 therefore excludes the ordinary case: case 1, a routing rule kept after weighing mixed effects, would fall outside. It also clashes with the reversibility bullet, which says a reason can favour keeping a change without being evidence that the change improved later work.

## 2. Rewrite "A deterministic check is a degenerate on-balance judgment"

**Change:** Replace the claim that "a change kept because a validator passed meets the condition". The new text should say that validator-gated retention meets J only when the named system also runs the loop that judges whether the validator suffices and revises it. Without that loop, the retention is the excluded case "bound to a fixed measure". Also change the example. Case 4 itself notes that "the link resolves" is not the only reasonable measure, because it says nothing about whether the link reaches the intended page.

**Reason:** As written, the bullet contradicts the draft's own exclusion of "retention bound to a fixed measure", the PRODIGY paragraph, and case 4's placement of the repairer alone as outside on J. Readers could apply it to admit any validator- or benchmark-gated loop, which is the boundary the checks work hardest to hold (Recuris). Nothing in the note supports calling a fixed measure a "degenerate" on-balance judgment.

## 3. Fix the scope claim and the quantifier on "each change"

**Change:**
- **Unit of change:** State whether "each change" covers every write to the named store or only the changes the loop retains as candidate learning. Logs, attempt records and automatic writes are not candidate learning in the second sense.
- **Inner loops:** Say how the outermost-loop rule treats inner fixed-measure loops inside Commonplace.
- **Coverage claim:** Either narrow "put all the learning that happens in Commonplace under one name" to what the conditions actually admit, or show that they cover it.
- **Checks:** Add a Commonplace case, such as an automatically appended session log or a validator-kept repair, with its expected placement.

**Reason:** The checks already flag this. The same KSI protocol flips from inside to outside depending on the quantifier. The draft says the training loop inside a research team is *not* deliberative learning even though the outer loop is. By that logic, Commonplace's own inner loops and automatic writes would fall outside, and the stated purpose of covering all of Commonplace's learning would fail.
