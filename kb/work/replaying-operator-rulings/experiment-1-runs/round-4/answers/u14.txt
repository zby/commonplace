# Three proposed changes to the deliberative learning draft

## 1. Replace "each change is kept by an on-balance judgment" with "retention stays answerable to on-balance judgment inside the system"

**What to change**

- **Merge conditions 2 and 3 into one.** Suggested wording: *"Whatever first admitted a change (a judgment, a fixed check, or default), the named system's outermost loop can judge on balance whether keeping it serves future work. That includes judging whether the admitting rule is enough. The loop can find that judgment wrong and act on it."*
- **Make the admission mode a reported property.** Report whether a change was judged, gated or kept by default, and treat it as a property, not a condition.
- **Remove three passages from the draft:**
  - the "Retention without a judgment" exclusion;
  - the "deterministic check is a degenerate judgment" bullet;
  - the phrase "later rounds of the *same* loop".
- **Update the checks:**
  - Restate S/J/C to match the new wording.
  - Settle the "which writes count" question as *all writes to the named store*.
  - In case 4, place team plus repairer inside for the patches as well as the repair rules.
  - Re-run KSI on its attempt log.

**Reason.** Commonplace keeps many changes by default or because a validator passed. The draft as written excludes those, so it fails its stated job of covering all learning in Commonplace. The degenerate-check bullet tries to patch this, but it contradicts case 4 (repairer alone is outside on J) and the Recuris placement. The new wording still excludes weights (S) and gates that nothing inside the boundary can question (Recuris, PRODIGY alone), so it gains generality without losing the contrast the term needs.

## 2. Make the judgment about *keeping* the change, not about the change *improving* future work

**What to change**

- In condition 2 (or the merged condition), replace "a judgment that the change improves the system's future work" with "a judgment that keeping the change is, on balance, the better decision for future work."
- Add a pointer to the note's ADR 085 example: a decision committed to without claiming improvement.

**Reason.** The draft says a change may rightly be kept while its verdict is open, and that reversibility can favour keeping a change without being evidence that it improved anything. Under the current wording, those well-made decisions would fail membership. The note also separates "a decision" from "a demonstrated improvement", and condition 2 should follow that distinction rather than blur it.

## 3. Rename the "learned" verdict to "improvement on balance" and take the verdict list from the note

**What to change**

- In "When a change was learned", call the strict verdict **improvement on balance**, using the note's term. Do not call it "learned".
- Replace the draft's restated verdict list with a reference to the note's list, so the two cannot drift. They already differ: "Made worse" in the draft versus "Loss" in the note.
- Update the misuse line and the checks' "Which comparison supports 'learned'?" bullet to match.

**Reason.** The draft defines learning in Simon's sense as any lasting change in capacity, and calls the process deliberative *learning* with no success condition. Saying that most changes made by deliberative learning were "not learned" therefore contradicts the draft's own vocabulary. That includes the note's "better on some, tied on the rest" case, which can justify a step. Borrowing the note's term keeps a single definition of the comparison and removes the collision.
