# When systems learn theories about themselves

> **Draft.** This article is circulating for comments; its claims, structure, and central thesis may still change. Counterexamples, disputed readings of the sources, and boundary cases are welcome through the repository's issue tracker.

Some systems represent and modify themselves without holding any explanation of why the modifications work. Some improve without any explicit theory at all. Some learn through theories without representing themselves. These are three distinct properties, and this article treats them as independent: a learning system can be *theory-mediated*, *reflective*, or *self-improving* in any combination. At the intersection of all three, a system develops theories of its own operation, uses them to choose changes to itself, and revises both the theories and the system in light of what the changes do. The article states the three properties and their pairwise combinations, the minimum architecture the three-way case needs, what counts as evidence that the loop is closed and that its improvements compound rather than merely accumulate, where three recent self-improving harnesses sit, and what this project — Commonplace, a knowledge base operated by agents and maintainers — can and cannot show about itself. It takes as given that retained natural-language theory can be operative inside a computational system once an interpreter with application judgment consumes it. Two companion articles supply that premise: [one](./what-bound-naurs-theory-to-programmers.md) argues from Naur's account of programming that his argument for the human binding runs through a premise about machines that no longer holds, leaving an interpreter-plus-text composite a candidate holder that his own tests must vet, and [the other](./theory-building-inside-the-system.md) records which functions of theory building the computational part of one system now supplies and where that stopped; a reader who doubts the premise should start with the first.

## Three properties, taken separately

**Theory-mediated.** A learning pathway is [theory-mediated](../notes/theory-mediated-learning-may-improve-sample-efficiency-under-shifts.md) when it represents a candidate theory as an intermediate object and changes behavior by adopting, applying, rejecting, or revising it. The contrast is the direct route from a failure to a patch. What makes the intermediate object a theory rather than a rule is not that it can be edited — a rule with an applicability clause can be narrowed by editing the clause — but that consequences are derived from it and the next failure is read against it. When a premise of a theory is revised, every case that was derived from that premise changes with it; when a rule is narrowed, only that rule changes, and the other rules that share its unstated reason stay as they were. That is the gain, and it is also the risk: a broad theory that is wrong is wrong exactly as widely as it would have been useful, and a theory that fits every failure so far can still be the wrong one, diverging from a rival only under a shift not yet tried. So the pathway needs an evaluator that tests a theory's consequences beyond the cases that produced it, not only its fit to them. Nothing in the property says what the theory is about. A theory of a market, a build system, or a customer mediates learning just as well as a theory of the learner.

**Reflective.** A system is [reflective](../notes/definitions/reflective-system.md), relative to selected aspects, when an internal process uses a causally connected representation of those aspects of the same system in its operation. The causal connection runs both ways: changes in the represented aspects update the representation, and operations mediated through it change later behavior. Computational reflection is an established idea — 3-Lisp, metaobject protocols, reflective middleware all built the two-way connection decades ago — and it is an architectural property. A reflective system need not improve, and need not hold any theory of why it works.

**Self-improving.** A system is [self-improving](../notes/definitions/self-improving-system.md) when it makes operative, evidence-responsive changes to its own behavior-determining organization, read against a declared boundary, time horizon, and improvement objective. The term describes the objective of a process, not the result of each update: a system that installs a bad rule in pursuit of improvement is still self-improving in this sense, and the section on evidence returns to that case. The property says nothing about how a change is chosen. Gradient descent, random trial, local patching, and derivation from a theory all qualify.

## The pairwise combinations

Because the properties are independent, the pairs are informative. The first has many instances; the other two are possible arrangements, and the third depends on a boundary choice.

| Present | Absent | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Reflective + self-improving | theory-mediated | A harness that rewrites its own prompts or skills from a benchmark score. It inspects and modifies itself by local diagnosis and patching, and what it retains is a rule set and a score; nothing retained explains why a change helped or where it will stop helping. Most reported self-improving harnesses are here. |
| Theory-mediated + self-improving | reflective | An agent whose retained theory of a market generates the scenarios its predictor is retrained on. The theory is revisable and the retraining changes the agent's organization against its objective, but the theory describes the market, and no representation of the agent takes part. |
| Reflective + theory-mediated | self-improving | A system that develops and revises an explanatory model of its own operation — a diagnostic self-theory maintained for operators — without using it to select changes. This cell is the thinnest: a revised self-model that does drive changes becomes self-improving the moment an objective is declared, so what keeps a system here is the absence of a declared objective, not an architectural difference. |

W. Ross Ashby's Homeostat ([source analysis](../sources/ashby-design-for-a-brain-1960.ingest.md)) is self-improving with neither of the other two, on my reading: "if the trial is unsuccessful, change the way of behaving; when and only when it is successful, retain the way of behaving," and the replacement values "have no special relation either to the previous values or to the problem in hand — they are just the values that next follow in Fisher and Yates' table." The retained setting is operative, but nothing in it informs its successor. That is adaptation with nothing to revise.

The three-way case is where a theory of the system is an operative cause of self-change, and the evidence that self-change produces revises the theory. Call it reflective theory-mediated self-improvement. The rest of the article is about what it needs and how one would know it is there.

## What an explicit theory adds to reflection

Chris Argyris's distinction between single-loop and double-loop learning ([source analysis](../sources/argyris-organizational-learning-and-mis-1977.ingest.md)) locates the addition. Single-loop learning identifies and corrects errors "so the job gets done and the action remains within stated policy guidelines" — the thermostat following its program. The trouble arises "when the underlying objectives and policies must be questioned," and the case of "the thermostat questioning its order" is "what is meant by double-loop learning." Argyris describes double-loop learning as questioning the governing objectives; that it goes on to change them is my extension.

The engineering content of that reading is about decomposition. A system's current decomposition — the components it names, the failures it can represent, the revisions it can propose — determines what it can notice and change, and [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../notes/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). A loop can optimize every named component and still lack any way to represent that a responsibility is missing or that the components were divided badly. A retained natural-language theory of the system's own operation is the surface on which that kind of finding can be stated, because [it can state a commitment before any admitted formal language contains it](../notes/unformalized-improvements-need-a-pre-formal-stage-in-the-loop.md). Opaque retained learning can improve a system too, and can even compound; what an explicit theory adds is [addressability](../notes/reflection-buys-addressability.md) — the ability to name, criticize, rescope, or retire one commitment rather than the whole.

Argyris also names the failure mode specific to the reflective case: organizations "seem to have the capacity to learn primarily those lessons that are self-sealing because they maintain the status quo." A system judging a theory about itself is contradicted by the world whenever the loop tests the theory's consequences against something it did not produce — the next failure, a standard fixed before the judgment, a reader outside the loop. Where the loop instead judges the theory against a standard reconstructed from the theory itself, nothing contradicts it. The Commonplace section reports a case of the second kind.

## The minimum architecture

Reflective theory-mediated self-improvement holds under [three conditions](../notes/theory-mediated-self-improvement-needs-interpretation-and-retention.md). *Membership*: the theory participates in the self-representation; transferring domain knowledge, however well, is not reflection. *Interpretation*: something inside the boundary can say what the theory claims, derive its consequences, and judge whether they hold — addressability is a handle, not a judgment. *Retention with separable parts*: the theory persists as an object whose content, assumptions, and applicability conditions are separately accessible, so that a failure can rescope it rather than only delete it. Separability is a condition on form, and most retained prose meets it; the condition that does the work in the harness comparison below is on content — the retained object must include the rationale from which the rules were derived, so that a failure can be read against it. A capable model with no retained artifact re-derives its theory every session and cannot accumulate; a retained artifact with no interpreter is a rule set with nothing to interpret it. In current systems the language model and the retained text supply the two halves by division of labor, and the arrangement is specific to that substrate as it currently exists, not to reflective systems in general.

Two components have been scarce, and their scarcity is part of why reflective systems did not become self-improving systems decades ago. Computational reflection built the causal connection — a self-representation whose modification changes behavior — with no evaluator and no declared objective. Systems that did bind self-change to a value function, such as Lenat's Eurisko in the late 1970s and Schmidhuber's success-story algorithm in the 1990s, had an evaluator over a bounded domain and lacked a proposer able to generate plausible arbitrary changes to their own organization. The [Gödel machine](../notes/goedel-machines-are-a-proof-governed-case-of-self-modification.md) made proof the acceptance test; its evaluator was sound and almost empty, since few real improvements are provable. Declaring some objective is cheap; declaring the one the loop should be judged against is the frontier the direction section describes. Benchmarks give a cheap evaluator over a narrow domain. Language-model judgment supplies both a broad proposer and a broad, unreliable judge. The machinery Commonplace runs — external criteria, review by a model call that did not produce the change, pass/fail gates, human adoption where the model's judgment runs out — exists to turn an unreliable-but-broad evaluator into a usable one.

Whether the interpreter-plus-text composite holds a theory of the system at all is a further question, and the [Naur article](./what-bound-naurs-theory-to-programmers.md) supplies three tests for it from Naur's transfer cases: program-specific acquisition, provision of the decision premises the interpreter cannot regenerate, and reliability across occasions. The tests are harder in the reflective case than in the domain case, because the premises the interpreter must be supplied are the ones the system's own history did not record.

## What counts as evidence

Suppose an agent maintains a deployment policy. A deployment fails on a Tuesday because a credential expired. The agent mistakes the date for the cause and installs "never deploy on Tuesdays," and later runs obey. The agent changed its own policy in pursuit of improvement and installed a bad rule; by the definition above it is self-improving, and the example shows why the definition describes an objective rather than a result.

A correct policy that reduced failures would be a retained gain. If the system instead installed a causal-evidence check and later used that check to diagnose another failure and install a better policy, the second improvement would depend on the first. That is local evidence of *compounding*: an improvement's benefit helped produce a later improvement. A system can retain many useful rules while each new rule stays just as hard to find, evaluate, and install; then task performance improves but the process that produces the next improvement does not, and [improvements accumulate without compounding](../notes/improvements-can-accumulate-without-compounding.md). A change's acceptance metric shows whether it met its target, not whether it made the next revision more productive, so [compounding is tested in a later episode](../notes/compounding-is-tested-in-later-improvement-not-by-the-accepting-metric.md): did the retained benefit make a subsequent revision cheaper, broader, more reliable, or less human-dependent, and can the dependence be traced?

Three tests apply to any named improvement path:

| Test | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Occurrence** | What system boundary, time horizon, and objective are in scope? Did evidence change the system's own organization? Did later behavior use that change? |
| **Revision surface** | Which behavior-shaping artifacts could this path revise? Which did it revise, which stayed fixed, and could the path still be used afterward? |
| **Compounding** | Did a later improvement measurably depend on the retained benefit? How were the episodes connected — and since a cited dependence can be confabulated, was it shown by ablation or assigned conditions rather than by the trace alone? Has this happened repeatedly? |

The Tuesday rule passes the occurrence test and fails the compounding test. A candidate cannot set its own judging standard: each episode needs an [objective](../notes/self-improvement-is-relative-to-a-declared-objective.md) and comparison rule fixed before evaluation, and changing the standard is another revision that must be authorized separately.

Two limits frame the space. The Homeostat, above, adapts with nothing to revise. At the other limit, a Gödel machine can rewrite even the machinery that chooses later rewrites, but only after proving the switch beats continuing the search; its revision surface is broad and formal, and it excludes every beneficial change it cannot prove. Retained natural-language theory sits between these: revisable, informative to its successor, and judged by an evaluator that is fallible.

The reported systems whose paths revise readable artifacts — harness optimizers that rewrite their own prompts and tool handling, online refiners that repair an embodied agent's skills, learners that accumulate behavioral rules for a coding agent, and archive-based evolution of agent code — have been [inventoried elsewhere](../notes/evidence/six-reported-self-improvement-paths-expose-bounded-redesign-surfaces.md) against these tests. Most establish occurrence and a bounded revision surface, with the evaluator, the permitted edit surface, and the selection machinery supplied by the researchers and left fixed. The closest later-episode test among them is [HyperAgents](../sources/hyperagents.ingest.md): evolved meta-agents, with their agent-modifying components frozen, generated stronger agents for an unseen task than the initial meta-agent did. Because each meta-agent bundles prompts, insight files, and code, the result does not isolate which retained change did the work, and continued evolution from transferred rather than fresh meta-agents showed no significant advantage. It shows that retained changes improved one later episode, not that the feedback recurred.

Three systems reported in August 2026 make the property grid concrete, and [the full reading is an evidence note](../notes/evidence/three-2026-harnesses-retain-rules-or-weights-not-a-revisable-theory.md). Prime Agent keeps prompt notes, skills, and subagent specifications on disk with versioning and rollback, and lets the model edit them; it is reflective and self-improving, has no gate that can refuse an edit before installation, and retains nothing that says why an edit helped — its own long run preserved a specification exploit as a reusable skill. Recuris adds what Prime Agent lacks: a fixed meta-agent localizes a failure to one of four memory components, patches only that component, and a fixed admission gate accepts the patch only if it repairs the source failure without breaking tasks the memory already solves. That transferred to unseen tasks and to a second model. But its localization is, in the paper's words, a repair decision rather than a claim of causal identification, and its memory only grows — across eight accepted patches, fifty-one skills added, two revised, none deprecated. The package can be appended to, individual skills can be edited, and the gate protects it from regression; but no rationale from which the skills were derived is retained, so revising one skill propagates to none of the others that share its reason. That is accumulation with a good gate. Apodex 1.1 revises weights offline and keeps its coordination state run-scoped, so it is not a deployment-time loop at all; it marks the parametric end, where retention is operative without being addressable. None of the three establishes recurrent compounding, and the two artifact-based loops lack the same thing: a retained object from which the patches are derived and against which the next failure is read.

## Commonplace as a partial realization

Commonplace is this project's attempt to build the three-way arrangement: a knowledge base of natural-language theory about its own operation, routed to the model call that needs it, with procedures derived from it, parts of it codified into validators, and human maintainers inside the [declared boundary](../reference/commonplace-declared-frame.md). Because maintainers are inside the boundary, its revision surface is not directly comparable with the reported systems, whose research teams sit outside the system boundary. What it claims is narrower than autonomy and more than editability: retained procedures make human–agent redesign an explicit operating path whose installed changes stay available for later use and revision. The [companion article on human-inclusive revision](./what-makes-human-inclusive-self-revision-non-trivial.md) develops the boundary, the [audit of six operating paths](../notes/evidence/six-commonplace-paths-establish-broad-addressability-not-completeness.md), and the adoption machinery.

Two things can be shown. The audit exposed a flaw in the system's own model of [behavioral authority](../notes/definitions/behavioral-authority.md) — its account of which retained artifacts shape which operations, and with what force — which could not express when different authority paths apply. That model is now a [revision target](../reference/proposals/revise-behavioral-authority-decomposition.md), and the exposure is a decomposition-level finding of the kind the section on explicit theory said a retained self-theory should make statable. Separately, a [topic-index case](../notes/evidence/tag-readme-trace-observed-causal-connection.md) shows one codification end to end: indexes that claimed to list every note on a topic had grown past the point where anyone could check the claim, a retained note already explained the cost, the convention was criticized as prose and then codified as a frontmatter mark a validator checks, and later work reused the validator.

What cannot be shown is a traced case in which a retained theory-layer improvement helped produce a later improvement — the compounding test. And the one closely watched episode ran the other way. When the project's automated improvement pass was applied to the note behind the Naur article, it criticized correctly twice and repaired wrongly twice, retreating each time to the nearest claim no gate could touch even though the note's purpose was stated in its inputs; one sentence from the maintainer fixed it each time. The [Naur article](./what-bound-naurs-theory-to-programmers.md) reports that episode in full, because it is a test of that article's claim on its own machinery. Here it is the evidence that the interpretation condition and the self-sealing failure mode are not hypothetical: the pass measured its repair against a brief reconstructed from the text whose claim it had just defeated — a standard set after the judgment rather than before it, which the evidence section's own rule forbids — and found the contribution strengthened. In both shown cases, the interpretation work at the decisive step was done by the maintainer inside the boundary, not by the model. The [theory-building article](./theory-building-inside-the-system.md) records this as an allocation of functions rather than a verdict on possession: application, criticism, derivation, and proposal have moved to the computational side in the traced episodes, while coherent modification, admission, and the choice of demand have not — and whether they can is the endpoint question, not something one profile settles.

## The remaining frontier: direction

The proposer/judge account leaves out a further function. Given a named target, language models propose well; given a criterion, they judge tolerably. What they do not yet do well is the step above: taking a broad, fuzzy objective and deciding what to work on — noticing which strain matters, forming the sub-objective, allocating attention. In the topic-index case the agent drafted and the validator checked, but the maintainer noticed the strain, initiated the fix, and accepted the result. Two of the three human contributions were direction.

Three mechanisms plausibly combine to keep this human. Decomposing a fuzzy objective requires a causal theory of what produces value in this system — which interventions compound and which are cosmetic. The retained self-theory in Commonplace is a theory of how the system operates, not a theory of what produces value against its objective; the second has not been written, and without it proposals drift toward the demonstrable — tidying, added structure, surface legibility. The operator notices which cost recurs because the operator pays it, while the project does not retain cost observations in a form an agent reads when choosing what to work on; that is a contingent gap the architecture could close, not a limit of it. And [a proximate target is checked for achievement, not for warrant](../notes/a-proximate-target-is-checked-for-achievement-not-for-warrant.md): choosing a sub-goal is a bet on the objective that no check in the loop evaluates.

There is a trade here. Self-direction is cheap exactly where the objective is narrow. The [Darwin Gödel Machine](../sources/darwin-godel-machine-open-ended-evolution-self-improving-agents.ingest.md) chooses its own next move because its objective is a benchmark score, and the price is that it can move only in directions the score can see. Commonplace keeps its objective broad, and the price is that direction stays with the maintainer. Giving the agents a measurable proxy would buy self-direction and invite optimizing the proxy in the same move.

## What remains to test

The [objective-level ablation](../reference/proposals/ablation-baselines-for-the-declared-objective.md) would compare the full framework with variants that remove or replace parts of it, testing whether Commonplace earns its total lifecycle cost. The [article on the bitter lesson](./the-bitter-lesson-does-not-require-everything-to-live-in-weights.md) supplies the text-versus-weights context for that comparison.

The later-episode experiment would compare Commonplace with frozen-artifact and simpler-memory variants and trace how a later revision depends on an earlier benefit, directly or through freed and reinvested capacity. Tasks should require revising the problem decomposition, not only local edits; otherwise matched rules can mimic theory-mediated reinterpretation. Assigning maintainers to conditions before any episode, or using outside operators, would separate the artifacts' effect from what an experienced maintainer remembers.

Both experiments should measure revision effort, outcome quality, transfer, and full lifecycle cost, including human judgment, training, and maintenance.
