# When systems learn theories about themselves

> **Draft.** This article is circulating for comments; its claims, structure, and central thesis may still change. Counterexamples, disputed readings of the sources, and boundary cases are welcome through the repository's issue tracker.

A learning system can be *theory-mediated*, *reflective*, or *self-improving* in any combination; the three are independent properties. At the intersection of all three, a system develops theories of its own operation, uses them to choose changes to itself, and revises both the theories and the system in light of what the changes do. This article states the properties and their combinations, the minimum architecture the three-way case needs, and what would count as evidence that the loop is closed and that its improvements compound rather than merely accumulate. Its diagnosis of the reported systems, this project included, is that what they retain is rules plus a gate, never the rationale the rules were derived from — which is why occurrence is easy to show and compounding has not been shown by anyone. The article takes as given that retained natural-language theory can be operative inside a computational system once an interpreter with application judgment consumes it; a [companion article](./what-bound-naurs-theory-to-programmers.md) argues that from Naur, and a reader who doubts the premise should start there.

## Three properties, taken separately

**Theory-mediated.** A learning pathway is [theory-mediated](../notes/theory-mediated-learning-may-improve-sample-efficiency-under-shifts.md) when it represents a candidate theory as an intermediate object and changes behavior by adopting, applying, rejecting, or revising it. The contrast is the direct route from a failure to a patch. What makes the intermediate object a theory rather than a rule is not that it can be edited — a rule with an applicability clause can be narrowed by editing the clause — but that consequences are derived from it and the next failure is read against it. When a premise of a theory is revised, every case derived from that premise changes with it; when a rule is narrowed, only that rule changes, and the other rules that share its unstated reason stay as they were. The gain carries the risk: a theory that fits every failure so far can still be the wrong one, diverging from a rival only under a shift not yet tried, so the pathway needs an evaluator that tests a theory's consequences beyond the cases that produced it. Nothing in the property says what the theory is about; a theory of a market or a build system mediates learning just as well as a theory of the learner.

**Reflective.** A system is [reflective](../notes/definitions/reflective-system.md), relative to selected aspects, when an internal process uses a causally connected representation of those aspects of the same system in its operation. The causal connection runs both ways: changes in the represented aspects update the representation, and operations mediated through it change later behavior. Computational reflection is an established idea — 3-Lisp, metaobject protocols, reflective middleware — and an architectural property. A reflective system need not improve, and need not hold any theory of why it works.

**Self-improving.** A system is [self-improving](../notes/definitions/self-improving-system.md) when it makes operative, evidence-responsive changes to its own behavior-determining organization, read against a declared boundary, time horizon, and improvement objective. The term describes the objective of a process, not the result of each update: a system that installs a bad rule in pursuit of improvement is still self-improving in this sense. The property says nothing about how a change is chosen; gradient descent, random trial, local patching, and derivation from a theory all qualify.

## The pairwise combinations

| Present | Absent | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Reflective + self-improving | theory-mediated | A harness that rewrites its own prompts or skills from a benchmark score. It inspects and modifies itself by local diagnosis and patching, and what it retains is a rule set and a score; nothing retained explains why a change helped or where it will stop helping. Most reported self-improving harnesses are here. |
| Theory-mediated + self-improving | reflective | An agent whose retained theory of a market generates the scenarios its predictor is retrained on. The theory is revisable and the retraining changes the agent's organization against its objective, but the theory describes the market, and no representation of the agent takes part. |
| Reflective + theory-mediated | self-improving | A system that develops and revises an explanatory model of its own operation — a diagnostic self-theory maintained for operators — without using it to select changes. This cell is the thinnest: a revised self-model that does drive changes becomes self-improving the moment an objective is declared, so what keeps a system here is the absence of a declared objective, not an architectural difference. |

W. Ross Ashby's Homeostat ([source analysis](../sources/ashby-design-for-a-brain-1960.ingest.md)) is self-improving with neither of the other two, on my reading: "if the trial is unsuccessful, change the way of behaving; when and only when it is successful, retain the way of behaving," and the replacement values "have no special relation either to the previous values or to the problem in hand — they are just the values that next follow in Fisher and Yates' table." The retained setting is operative, but nothing in it informs its successor: adaptation with nothing to revise.

The three-way case is where a theory of the system is an operative cause of self-change, and the evidence that self-change produces revises the theory. Call it reflective theory-mediated self-improvement.

## What an explicit theory adds to reflection

Chris Argyris's distinction between single-loop and double-loop learning ([source analysis](../sources/argyris-organizational-learning-and-mis-1977.ingest.md)) locates the addition. Single-loop learning identifies and corrects errors "so the job gets done and the action remains within stated policy guidelines" — the thermostat following its program. The trouble arises "when the underlying objectives and policies must be questioned," and the case of "the thermostat questioning its order" is "what is meant by double-loop learning." Argyris describes double-loop learning as questioning the governing objectives; that it goes on to change them is my extension.

That reading is about decomposition. A system's current decomposition — the components it names, the failures it can represent, the revisions it can propose — determines what it can notice and change, and [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../notes/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). A loop can optimize every named component and still lack any way to represent that a responsibility is missing or that the components were divided badly. A retained natural-language theory of the system's own operation is the surface on which that kind of finding can be stated, because [it can state a commitment before any admitted formal language contains it](../notes/unformalized-improvements-need-a-pre-formal-stage-in-the-loop.md). Opaque retained learning can improve a system too, and can even compound; what an explicit theory adds is [addressability](../notes/reflection-buys-addressability.md) — the ability to name, criticize, rescope, or retire one commitment rather than the whole.

Argyris also names the failure mode specific to the reflective case: organizations "seem to have the capacity to learn primarily those lessons that are self-sealing because they maintain the status quo." A system judging a theory about itself is contradicted by the world whenever the loop tests the theory's consequences against something it did not produce — the next failure, a standard fixed in advance, a reader outside the loop. Where the loop judges the theory against a standard reconstructed from the theory itself, nothing contradicts it, as happens in one case reported below.

## The minimum architecture

Reflective theory-mediated self-improvement holds under [three conditions](../notes/theory-mediated-self-improvement-needs-interpretation-and-retention.md). *Membership*: the theory participates in the self-representation; transferring domain knowledge, however well, is not reflection. *Interpretation*: something inside the boundary can say what the theory claims, derive its consequences, and judge whether they hold — addressability is a handle, not a judgment. *Retention with separable parts*: the theory persists as an object whose content, assumptions, and applicability conditions are separately accessible, so that a failure can rescope it rather than only delete it. Separability of form is cheap and most retained prose has it; what separates the harnesses below is a condition on content — whether the rationale the rules were derived from is retained at all. A capable model with no retained artifact re-derives its theory every session and cannot accumulate; a retained artifact with no interpreter is a rule set with nothing to interpret it. In current systems the language model and the retained text supply the two halves by division of labor, and the arrangement is specific to that substrate.

Two components have been scarce, and their scarcity is part of why reflective systems did not become self-improving systems decades ago. Computational reflection built the causal connection — a self-representation whose modification changes behavior — with no evaluator and no declared objective. Systems that did bind self-change to a value function, such as Lenat's Eurisko in the late 1970s and Schmidhuber's success-story algorithm in the 1990s, had an evaluator over a bounded domain and lacked a proposer able to generate plausible arbitrary changes to their own organization. The [Gödel machine](../notes/goedel-machines-are-a-proof-governed-case-of-self-modification.md) made proof the acceptance test; its evaluator was sound and almost empty, since few real improvements are provable. Benchmarks give a cheap evaluator over a narrow domain. Language-model judgment supplies both a broad proposer and a broad, unreliable judge, and the machinery Commonplace runs — external criteria, review by a model call that did not produce the change, pass/fail gates, human adoption where the model's judgment runs out — exists to turn an unreliable-but-broad evaluator into a usable one.

Whether the composite holds a theory of the system at all is a further question this article does not need answered: the Naur article supplies three tests and leaves the composite a candidate, and the [theory-building article](./theory-building-inside-the-system.md) replaces the yes-or-no with a record of which functions have moved to the machinery. The tests are harder in the reflective case than in the domain case, because the premises the interpreter must be supplied are the ones the system's own history did not record.

## What counts as evidence

Suppose an agent maintains a deployment policy. A deployment fails on a Tuesday because a credential expired. The agent mistakes the date for the cause and installs "never deploy on Tuesdays," and later runs obey. By the definition above the agent is self-improving; the example is why the definition describes an objective rather than a result.

A correct policy that reduced failures would be a retained gain. If the system instead installed a causal-evidence check and later used that check to diagnose another failure and install a better policy, the second improvement would depend on the first. That is local evidence of *compounding*: an improvement's benefit helped produce a later improvement. A system can retain many useful rules while each new rule stays just as hard to find, evaluate, and install; then task performance improves but the process that produces the next improvement does not, and [improvements accumulate without compounding](../notes/improvements-can-accumulate-without-compounding.md). A change's acceptance metric shows whether it met its target, not whether it made the next revision more productive, so [compounding is tested in a later episode](../notes/compounding-is-tested-in-later-improvement-not-by-the-accepting-metric.md): did the retained benefit make a subsequent revision cheaper, broader, more reliable, or less human-dependent, and can the dependence be shown?

Three tests apply to any named improvement path:

| Test | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Occurrence** | What system boundary, time horizon, and objective are in scope? Did evidence change the system's own organization? Did later behavior use that change? |
| **Revision surface** | Which behavior-shaping artifacts could this path revise? Which did it revise, which stayed fixed, and could the path still be used afterward? |
| **Compounding** | Did a later improvement measurably depend on the retained benefit? How were the episodes connected — and since a cited dependence can be confabulated, was it shown by ablation or assigned conditions rather than by the trace alone? Has this happened repeatedly? |

The Tuesday rule passes the occurrence test and fails the compounding test. A candidate cannot set its own judging standard: each episode needs an [objective](../notes/self-improvement-is-relative-to-a-declared-objective.md) and comparison rule fixed before evaluation, and changing the standard is another revision that must be authorized separately. Retained natural-language theory sits between the two limits already named — unlike the Homeostat's settings it informs its successor; unlike the Gödel machine's proofs it is judged by a fallible evaluator, which admits useful changes proof cannot certify and bad ones too.

The reported systems whose paths revise readable artifacts — harness optimizers that rewrite their own prompts and tool handling, online refiners that repair an embodied agent's skills, learners that accumulate behavioral rules for a coding agent, and archive-based evolution of agent code — have been [inventoried elsewhere](../notes/evidence/six-reported-self-improvement-paths-expose-bounded-redesign-surfaces.md) against these tests. Most establish occurrence and a bounded revision surface, with the evaluator, the permitted edit surface, and the selection machinery supplied by the researchers and left fixed. The closest later-episode test among them is [HyperAgents](../sources/hyperagents.ingest.md): evolved meta-agents, with their agent-modifying components frozen, generated stronger agents for an unseen task than the initial meta-agent did. Because each meta-agent bundles prompts, insight files, and code, the result does not isolate which retained change did the work, and continued evolution from transferred rather than fresh meta-agents showed no significant advantage. It shows that retained changes improved one later episode, not that the feedback recurred.

Three systems reported in August 2026 make the property grid concrete, and [the full reading is an evidence note](../notes/evidence/three-2026-harnesses-retain-rules-or-weights-not-a-revisable-theory.md). Prime Agent keeps prompt notes, skills, and subagent specifications on disk with versioning and rollback, and lets the model edit them; it is reflective and self-improving, has no gate that can refuse an edit before installation, and retains nothing that says why an edit helped — its own long run preserved a specification exploit as a reusable skill. Recuris adds what Prime Agent lacks: a fixed meta-agent localizes a failure to one of four memory components, patches only that component, and a fixed admission gate accepts the patch only if it repairs the source failure without breaking tasks the memory already solves. That transferred to unseen tasks and to a second model. But its localization is, in the paper's words, a repair decision rather than a claim of causal identification, and its memory only grows — across eight accepted patches, fifty-one skills added, two revised, none deprecated. Individual skills can be edited and the gate protects the package from regression; but no rationale from which the skills were derived is retained, so revising one skill propagates to none of the others that share its reason. That is accumulation with a good gate. Apodex 1.1 revises weights offline and keeps its coordination state run-scoped, so it is not a deployment-time loop at all; it marks the parametric end, where retention is operative without being addressable. None of the three establishes recurrent compounding.

## Commonplace as a partial realization

Commonplace, a knowledge base operated by agents and maintainers, is this project's attempt to build the three-way arrangement: natural-language theory about its own operation, routed to the model call that needs it, with procedures derived from it, parts of it codified into validators, and the maintainers inside the [declared boundary](../reference/commonplace-declared-frame.md). Read against that boundary it has all three properties, and cheaply — so does any maintained codebase with design documents and a change process, which is why the reported systems, whose research teams sit outside their declared boundaries, are not directly comparable with it. What is informative about Commonplace is not that it qualifies but the allocation: which functions of the loop its machinery performs and which its maintainers still do. In the traced episodes, applying a theory, criticizing a claim against it, deriving procedures, and proposing revisions are performed by model calls; modifying the theory coherently, admitting a revision, and choosing what to work on are not. The [theory-building article](./theory-building-inside-the-system.md) records that allocation, and the [article on moving revision decisions](./moving-revision-decisions-into-the-automatic-system.md) reads the [audit of six operating paths](../notes/evidence/six-commonplace-paths-establish-broad-addressability-not-completeness.md) for which revision decisions could move and identifies the two — admission and model realization — that cannot yet, because their inputs are not represented.

Two things can be shown. The audit exposed a flaw in the system's own model of [behavioral authority](../notes/definitions/behavioral-authority.md) — its account of which retained artifacts shape which operations, and with what force — which could not express when different authority paths apply. That model is now a [revision target](../reference/proposals/revise-behavioral-authority-decomposition.md), and the exposure is a decomposition-level finding of the kind a retained self-theory should make statable — stated against a named retained model, which is what addressability buys. Whether the retained theory produced the finding, rather than the auditors' judgment producing a finding that happened to be about a retained model, is not traced. Separately, a [topic-index case](../notes/evidence/tag-readme-trace-observed-causal-connection.md) shows one codification end to end: indexes that claimed to list every note on a topic had grown past the point where anyone could check the claim, a retained note already explained the cost, the convention was criticized as prose and then codified as a frontmatter mark a validator checks, and later work reused the validator.

What cannot be shown is a traced case in which a retained theory-layer improvement helped produce a later improvement — the compounding test. And the one closely watched episode ran the other way. When the project's automated improvement pass was applied to the note behind the Naur article, it criticized correctly twice and repaired wrongly twice, retreating each time to the nearest claim no gate could touch even though the note's purpose was stated in its inputs; one sentence from the maintainer fixed it each time. The [Naur article](./what-bound-naurs-theory-to-programmers.md) reports the episode in full. Here it is the evidence that the interpretation condition and the self-sealing failure mode are not hypothetical: the pass measured its repair against a brief reconstructed from the text whose claim it had just defeated — a standard set after the judgment rather than before it, which the evidence section's own rule forbids — and found the contribution strengthened. In both shown cases the interpretation work at the decisive step was done by the maintainer, not the model; whether that can change is the endpoint question, not something one profile settles.

## The remaining frontier: direction

The proposer/judge account leaves out a further function. Given a named target, language models propose well; given a criterion, they judge tolerably. What they do not yet do well is the step above: taking a broad, fuzzy objective and deciding what to work on — noticing which strain matters, forming the sub-objective, allocating attention. In the topic-index case the agent drafted and the validator checked, but the maintainer noticed the strain, initiated the fix, and accepted the result. Two of the three human contributions were direction.

Three mechanisms plausibly combine to keep this human. Decomposing a fuzzy objective requires a causal theory of what produces value in this system — which interventions compound and which are cosmetic. The retained self-theory in Commonplace is a theory of how the system operates, not of what produces value against its objective; the second has not been written, and without it proposals drift toward the demonstrable — tidying, added structure, surface legibility. The operator notices which cost recurs because the operator pays it, while the project does not retain cost observations in a form an agent reads when choosing what to work on; that gap the architecture could close. And [a proximate target is checked for achievement, not for warrant](../notes/a-proximate-target-is-checked-for-achievement-not-for-warrant.md): choosing a sub-goal is a bet on the objective that no check in the loop evaluates.

There is a trade here. Self-direction is cheap exactly where the objective is narrow. The [Darwin Gödel Machine](../sources/darwin-godel-machine-open-ended-evolution-self-improving-agents.ingest.md) chooses its own next move because its objective is a benchmark score, and the price is that it can move only in directions the score can see. Commonplace keeps its objective broad, and the price is that direction stays with the maintainer. Giving the agents a measurable proxy would buy self-direction and invite optimizing the proxy in the same move.

## What remains to test

A first measurement is cheap and should run first: re-record the theory-building allocation at a fixed function grain after a period of operation and see whether any row moved. Compounding asks whether a retained benefit helped produce a later improvement; the allocation asks whether the machinery now performs a function it did not — and a row that moved is the kind of later improvement the compounding test would then trace.

The [objective-level ablation](../reference/proposals/ablation-baselines-for-the-declared-objective.md) would compare the full framework with variants that remove or replace parts of it, testing whether Commonplace earns its total lifecycle cost; the [article on the bitter lesson](./the-bitter-lesson-does-not-require-everything-to-live-in-weights.md) supplies the text-versus-weights context. The later-episode experiment would compare Commonplace with frozen-artifact and simpler-memory variants and trace how a later revision depends on an earlier benefit, directly or through freed and reinvested capacity. Tasks should require revising the problem decomposition, not only local edits; otherwise matched rules can mimic theory-mediated reinterpretation. Assigning maintainers to conditions before any episode, or using outside operators, would separate the artifacts' effect from what an experienced maintainer remembers. Both experiments should measure revision effort, outcome quality, transfer, and full lifecycle cost, including human judgment, training, and maintenance.
