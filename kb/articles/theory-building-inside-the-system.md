# Theory building inside the system

> **Draft.** This article is circulating for comments; its claims, structure, and central thesis may still change. Counterexamples, disputed readings of the sources, and boundary cases are welcome through the repository's issue tracker.

Peter Naur argued in 1985 that the main product of programming is not the program but a theory: the programmer's capacity to map the program onto the world, justify each of its parts, and extend it coherently when a new demand arrives. He also argued that this theory cannot be reduced to the program plus written rules, and therefore lives only in programmers. This article accepts the first claim and examines the inference behind the second. Naur reached the human binding through a premise that was accurate for the programs of his time — that a machine can judge only by criteria someone has formulated — and trained models have since separated formal execution from formulated criteria. That does not make the theory writable. Writing propositions about a theory was not sufficient to transfer it in Naur's own compiler case, and nothing here says it is sufficient now. What changed is what written theory can be combined with: a system can now contain an interpreter that applies retained natural-language theory with the kind of judgment the text alone does not carry, so an interpreter-plus-text composite becomes a candidate for holding what the text cannot. Once a theory is operative inside the system it can also become learned state. A failure need not go straight to a patch; it can revise the theory, and the patch follows from the revised theory. We call that architecture *theory-mediated learning*. *Theory-mediated methodology* — procedures derived from a retained theory, with the theory kept live as the fallback when the procedures run out — is the realization this project runs. When the retained theory is about the system's own operation the learning is reflective, and when the changes it produces improve the system against a declared objective it is self-improvement. The article states the conditions under which each of these holds, positions the arrangement against recent self-improving harnesses, gives the tests that separate improvements that compound from improvements that merely accumulate, and reports one case from this project's own maintenance in which the machinery did exactly what Naur's account predicts it would fail at.

## Naur's thesis, both halves

Naur's essay *Programming as Theory Building* ([source analysis](../sources/programming-as-theory-building.ingest.md)) defines programming as matching "some significant part and aspect of an activity in the real world to the formal symbol manipulation that can be done by a program running on a computer." What the programmer builds in doing this is, in Gilbert Ryle's sense of the word, a theory — roughly, not a body of propositions but a capacity to do certain things and to explain, justify, and answer questions about them. Naur names three capabilities that mark possession of a program's theory. The holder can map between the affairs of the world and the program text — including the decision that some affair is relevant at all, which "can only be made by someone who understands the whole world." The holder can "explain why each part of the program is what it is." And the holder can incorporate a new demand by perceiving its similarity to the facilities already built, a similarity "between aspects of the world" that "cannot be reduced to any limited set of criteria or rules."

The second half of the thesis follows from the third capability. Ryle's argument is that intelligent behavior cannot consist in following rules, because following a rule is itself done well or badly, which would require rules for following rules, and so on without end. What stops the regress is a grasp of similarity, and the similarities in question "are not, and cannot be, expressed in terms of criteria, no more than the similarities of many other kinds of objects, such as human faces, tunes, or tastes of wine." From this Naur draws what he calls "a main claim": the theory of a program "could not conceivably be expressed, but is inextricably bound to human beings." A program whose theory-holders have dispersed is dead even while it runs; death "becomes visible when demands for modifications of the program cannot be intelligently answered."

Both halves deserve to be stated at full strength before either is questioned, because the evidence Naur gives is strong. In his compiler case, a successor group received "full documentation, including annotated program texts and much additional written design discussion, and also personal advice," and still proposed extensions that the original group saw at once as "patches that effectively destroyed its power and simplicity." The original group "could propose simple and effective solutions, framed entirely within the existing structure." The text and the documentation did not carry what the original group had. Naur's conclusion is that a new programmer can acquire an existing theory only by working "in close contact with the programmers who already possess the theory."

## What a theory is here

The word *theory* in this article carries two senses that need keeping apart, and Karl Popper supplies the first ([source analysis](../sources/popper-a-realist-view-of-logic-physics-and-history-1966.ingest.md)). A theory in Popper's objective sense is a product of human activity that stands outside its producer — "like tools, they are organs evolving outside our skins" — and is objective because its logical consequences exceed what any holder grasps: "nobody, neither its creator nor anybody who has tried to grasp it, can have a full understanding of all the possibilities inherent in a theory." Two things follow. Someone other than the author can derive consequences from the theory, test them, and find where it breaks, which is why writing a theory down is not pointless even when the writing is incomplete: the text is an object later work can operate on. And a theory in this sense is consumed by criticism — consuming theories "also means criticising them, changing them, and often even demolishing them, in order to replace them by better ones," in a cycle of problem, tentative theory, error-elimination, and new problem (`P1 » TT » EE » P2`). A retained theory that nothing criticizes is not being consumed.

Naur places Ryle's notion inside this frame himself — it "appears as an example of what K. Popper calls unembodied World 3 objects" (the label is Naur's citation of a later Popper work; the retained Popper text above does not use it) — and then raises an objection within the frame: a program's theory is a genuine theory, but possessing it is a capacity, not a text. So *retained theory* in this article names the text, a Popperian object that can be stored, loaded, and criticized; *holding the theory* in Naur's sense names a capacity of whoever uses it. The claim below is that an interpreter-plus-text composite may hold a theory; the text alone does not.

## Why the binding held, and what changed

Read closely, Naur's inexpressibility argument targets something precise: criteria that can be formulated. The similarity judgment is "entirely outside the reach of what can be determined by rules, since even the criteria on which to judge it cannot be formulated." Text that must decide a case by itself is formulated criteria, and the argument bounds it. The [basis note for this article](../notes/naur-equates-machine-execution-with-formulated-criteria.md) traces how the essay gets from there to "bound to human beings." The bridge is an equation. Programming is defined as matching the world to "the formal symbol manipulation that can be done by a program running on a computer," and the view Naur rejects pairs "act[ing] like machines, by following rules" with the notion "that the human mind works like a computer." A program on a computer, rule-determination, and formulable criteria occupy one pole; human judgment occupies the other. The inference runs: the criteria cannot be formulated; what a program does is execute formulated criteria; so no program can make the judgment; so the judgment is human.

The second premise is the bridge, and the essay does not argue it because it did not need to. For the programs Naur describes, making a machine judge meant writing the criteria. Machine execution and formulated criteria were one thing, and the premise was a description, not a conjecture.

They are no longer one thing. A face recognizer is formal symbol manipulation by a program running on a computer — as formal as anything Naur describes — and it performs a similarity judgment for which nobody can state the criteria. It is not a counterexample to Naur's first claim: the criteria remain as unformulable as he said. It bears on the bridge. It does not refute the bridge as a description of the programs of 1985; it shows that the two things the bridge equated have since come apart. Formal execution no longer requires formulated criteria, so a judgment can be beyond formulable criteria and still within the reach of a program. The examples Naur chose for the inexpressible — faces, tunes, tastes of wine — are the ones on which the separation happened. One might object that any learned system escapes Naur's rule pole. It does not, because the discriminator is not learning as such: a decision tree induced from examples is learned and is still an explicit rule set, inside Naur's rule-determined pole. The discriminator is whether the resulting judgment has a formulable rubric. A large language model reading a natural-language description of a design and judging whether a new demand resembles what the design already provides is on the same side of that line as the face recognizer. It is inside the machine pole, and it makes the kind of judgment Naur reserved for people.

Naur was right that the criteria cannot be formulated. What changed is that a machine no longer needs the criteria formulated in order to make the judgment.

None of this shows that any current system holds a program's theory. Separating execution from formulated criteria removes the argument that settles the question in advance; it does not answer it. Nor does it reopen the question of writing the theory down: what the compiler case shows is that propositions about a theory did not transfer possession of it, and that lesson is untouched. The open proposition is narrower — that text plus a sufficiently capable interpreter may transfer more of the capacity than text alone. What Naur's transfer cases still supply is a set of tests that any interpreter-plus-artifact composite must pass before it can claim to hold one. The composite must acquire *this* program's mapping, justification, and modification judgments, since general competence over language and the world is not the theory of this program. Some component must supply the [decision premises the interpreter cannot regenerate](../notes/design-rationale-must-preserve-unregenerable-decision-premises.md) from the implementation and general knowledge: which world affairs were ruled relevant and why, where the intended scope stops, which alternatives lost. And the capacity must be reliable across occasions, because one coherent extension may be luck.

The same shift explains why reflective systems did not become self-improving systems decades ago. Computational reflection — 3-Lisp, metaobject protocols, reflective middleware — built the causal connection: a self-representation whose modification changes behavior. Those systems had no evaluator and no declared objective to evaluate against; of the two, the evaluator was the expensive resource. Declaring an objective is cheap; what was missing was something that could judge an arbitrary proposed change against a declared objective, which is the same application judgment Naur located in the programmer. The [Gödel machine](../notes/goedel-machines-are-a-proof-governed-case-of-self-modification.md) was the first architecture to bind self-change to a value function, with proof as the acceptance test; its evaluator was sound and almost empty, since few real improvements are provable. Benchmarks give a cheap evaluator over a narrow domain. Language-model judgment gives a broad domain and unreliable verdicts. The machinery this article describes — external criteria, review by a model call that did not produce the change, pass/fail gates, human adoption where the model's judgment runs out — exists to turn an unreliable-but-broad evaluator into a usable one.

## Operative, not documentary: theory-mediated methodology

Having an interpreter inside the system is necessary but not sufficient. A retained theory — one persisted as an artifact that later work can load — can be present, readable, and inert. Chris Argyris's distinction between espoused theory and theory-in-use names the gap ([source analysis](../sources/argyris-organizational-learning-and-mis-1977.ingest.md)): "Espoused theories of action are the theories that people report are governing their actions. Theories-in-use are the theories of action that actually govern their actions." Translated to an agent system, [an action model matters only through its consumption path](../notes/an-action-model-matters-only-through-its-consumption-path.md). A theory becomes a theory-in-use when something consumes it with binding force — when it is loaded into the context of a call that decides something, routes a decision, or gates an acceptance. A note that is stored and never surfaced at the moment of need governs nothing.

Three arrangements make a natural-language theory operative in a system built around bounded model calls.

The first is routing and loading. A model call has a bounded context, and a theory that is not in it does not participate. [Context engineering](../notes/definitions/context-engineering.md) — deciding which retained artifacts reach which call, when, and at what scope — is the discipline that turns retained theory into consumed theory. In Commonplace, notes are routed individually so that an agent can load the theory relevant to a decision rather than the whole layer.

The second is derivation of procedure from theory. A theory is expensive to reason from on every occasion. Where the same reasoning recurs, its result is promoted into a procedure — a skill, a checklist, a review criterion — that executes the common case directly. The theory does not retire when this happens; it remains the live fallback for cases the procedure does not cover. This is the [two-layer execution system](../notes/theory-and-methodology-form-a-two-layer-execution-system.md): the derived procedure is the fast path, the theory is the generator that handles out-of-coverage inputs and from which the fast path grows, and recurrence of fallback reasoning is the signal for promotion. In an open domain, where total coverage cannot be shown, the theory stays operationally necessary rather than becoming an appendix. This is the arrangement the article calls theory-mediated methodology, and [skills that derive from methodology](../notes/skills-derive-from-methodology.md) are its concrete instance here. It is one realization of the more general architecture described in the next section, not the whole of it: a theory can guide diagnosis, the choice of an experiment, or the reading of a result without any procedure ever being compiled from it.

The third is [codification](../notes/definitions/codification.md) once a part of the theory has stabilized. A theory held in prose is a [prototype](../notes/unformalized-improvements-need-a-pre-formal-stage-in-the-loop.md): explicit enough to criticize and revise, with no operational machinery bound to its commitments. Once its concepts settle, the part a formal consumer needs is crossed into a schema, a validator, or code, while the rest stays reopenable. Codifying before the concepts settle freezes one projection of the theory and raises the cost of replacing it. The pre-formal stage is where a theory is cheap to reject; the point of keeping theories in natural language while they are unsettled is that rejection there costs a paragraph rather than a rebuild.

Why hold a model of the situation at all, rather than act directly? Kenneth Craik's answer is that a working model that preserves the relevant relation-structure of its target lets an organism try alternatives on the model before committing in the world, because the model is cheaper, faster, or safer to run ([source analysis](../sources/craik-hypothesis-on-the-nature-of-thought-1943.ingest.md); cited from the source, no passage is quoted here). A retained theory of why a design is the way it is lets an agent try a proposed change against the design's rationale before trying it against the codebase. That is a use of retained text that Naur's successor group, with no interpreter inside the system, could not make. It is not yet evidence that the composite holds the theory; the Commonplace section below shows a case where it did not.

## From held theory to learned state

An operative theory can be consulted and left unchanged. The step that turns it into part of the system's learned state is that the theory is what a failure revises. The direct route from a failure is a patch: the observed case is fixed, and later runs obey the fix. The theory-mediated route inserts a revision. The failure is read against the theory; the theory is corrected, narrowed, or replaced; the patch is derived from the revised theory; and the next failure is read against the same, now revised, theory. Call a learning pathway [theory-mediated](../notes/theory-mediated-learning-may-improve-sample-efficiency-under-shifts.md) when it represents a candidate theory as an intermediate object and changes behavior by adopting, applying, rejecting, or revising it.

The difference shows in what one failure can do. Suppose a coding agent has observed that several documentation-only changes needed no integration tests. It can retain the rule "documentation files are safe," or it can retain the theory "a changed file cannot affect integration behavior when no executed process consumes it." When a build tool later starts reading one documentation file as configuration, the rule can only be deleted or narrowed by enumeration. The theory supports a precise revision — the exemption applies only to files no executable tooling consumes — and every downstream case that revision touches changes at once. That is the gain, and it is also the risk: a broad theory that is wrong is wrong exactly as widely as it would have been useful. The route therefore needs an evaluator that selects theories for reach and not for fit, which is why the evaluator recurs as the scarce resource throughout this article.

This is the point at which Naur's argument, followed through, arrives at something more general than program theory. Naur describes theory-mediated learning in human programming: the programmer's theory is what a new demand is judged against and what an intelligent modification is derived from. The section on what changed argued that an interpreter inside the system can now take part in that judgment. Three properties have appeared along the way and should be kept separate, because a system can have any of them without the others:

- **Theory-mediated (T).** A theory is an intermediate, revisable object on the path from evidence to changed behavior.
- **Reflective (R).** The retained representation is of the same system whose operation it can affect — the [causally connected self-representation](../notes/definitions/reflective-system.md) of computational reflection.
- **Self-improving (S).** The evidence-responsive changes improve that system [relative to a declared objective](../notes/definitions/self-improving-system.md).

A retained theory of a market that guides a trading agent's revisions is T without R. A harness that rewrites its own prompts from a benchmark score is R and S without T: what it retains is a rule set and a score, and nothing in the retained state explains why the rewrite helped or predicts where it will stop helping. Ashby's Homeostat, discussed below, is S with neither. Naur gives T in human programming. Language models make T available inside the technical system; when the retained theory describes that system we add R; when the theory-mediated changes improve it we add S. The intersection of the three is reflective theory-mediated self-improvement, which is what the rest of the article is about, and the grid is what the recent self-improving harnesses will be read against.

## Formalization moves the open question; it does not remove it

Codification, the third arrangement above, has a stronger relative. A stabilized part of a theory can cross not only into a validator that constrains operation but into a formal model whose consequences are proved: a specification and a proof that an implementation satisfies it, or a verified property of a protocol. [Formal systems assess a theory's reach through causal and proof obligations](../notes/formal-systems-assess-explanatory-reach-through-causal-and-proof.md), and inside a supplied language they do so with a certainty natural-language criticism cannot match. What a proof establishes, though, is conditional: it shows that a property follows from stated assumptions. The uncertainty it removes from the derivation moves to the assumptions — do they describe the relevant affairs of the world? — which is Naur's first capability, the mapping between world and program, in another guise. Stronger verification therefore raises the relative weight of the boundary between the informal theory and the formal model rather than removing it.

Language models act on both sides of that boundary. They make informal theory operative, as the previous sections argued, and they make formalization and proof cheaper to produce. What they do not supply is a check that the formal assumptions match the world; that correspondence stays with the retained natural-language theory and whoever holds it. This is one instance of a general pattern, that [automation extends as far as verification does](../notes/the-boundary-of-automation-is-the-boundary-of-verification.md): derivation inside the model can be automated because it can be checked; the theory–model correspondence is where the check runs out. So the two branches from a held theory — procedure and validator on one side, formal model and proof on the other — both push the remaining judgment toward the same place, and that place is the one Naur located in the programmer.

## The reflective turn

Everything so far applies to theories about anything — a market, a build system, a customer. The reflective case adds the second property: the retained theory is about the system's own operation and participates in the causally connected self-representation through which the system changes its own behavior. Argyris's second distinction locates what that adds. Single-loop learning identifies and corrects errors "so the job gets done and the action remains within stated policy guidelines" — the thermostat following its program. The trouble arises "when the underlying objectives and policies must be questioned," and the case of "the thermostat questioning its order" is "what is meant by double-loop learning." Argyris describes double-loop learning as questioning the governing objectives; that it goes on to change them is my extension.

The engineering content of that reading is about decomposition. A system's current decomposition — the components it names, the failures it can represent, the revisions it can propose — determines what it can notice and change, and [learning inside a fixed decomposition inherits its mistakes](../notes/learning-inside-a-fixed-decomposition-inherits-its-mistakes.md). A loop can optimize every named component and still lack any way to represent that a responsibility is missing or that the components were divided badly. A retained natural-language theory of the system's own operation is the surface on which that kind of finding can be stated, because [it can state a commitment before any admitted formal language contains it](../notes/unformalized-improvements-need-a-pre-formal-stage-in-the-loop.md). Opaque retained learning can improve a system too, and can even compound; what reflection adds is [addressability](../notes/reflection-buys-addressability.md) — the ability to name, criticize, rescope, or retire one commitment rather than the whole.

Reflective self-improvement through retained theory holds under [three conditions](../notes/theory-mediated-self-improvement-needs-interpretation-and-retention.md). These are distinct from the three arrangements that make a theory operative and from Naur's three tests: the arrangements make a theory operative, the conditions make the case reflective, and Naur's tests say whether the composite holds a theory at all. *Membership*: the theory participates in the self-representation; transferring domain knowledge, however well, is not reflection. *Interpretation*: something inside the boundary can say what the theory claims, derive its consequences, and judge whether they hold — addressability is a handle, not a judgment. *Retention with separable parts*: the theory persists as an object whose content, assumptions, and applicability conditions are separately accessible, so that a failure can rescope it rather than only delete it. A capable model with no retained artifact re-derives its theory every session and cannot accumulate; a retained artifact with no interpreter is a rule set with nothing to interpret it. The language model and the retained text supply the two halves by division of labor, and the arrangement is specific to that substrate as it currently exists, not to reflective systems in general.

Naur's tests are harder here than in the domain case, because the premises the interpreter must be supplied are the ones the system's own history did not record. Argyris adds a failure mode specific to the reflective case: organizations "seem to have the capacity to learn primarily those lessons that are self-sealing because they maintain the status quo." A system judging a theory about itself has no external oracle to contradict a flattering self-theory unless one is placed inside the loop; the Commonplace section shows what happens when the loop closes without one.

## What counts as evidence

Suppose an agent maintains a deployment policy. A deployment fails on a Tuesday because a credential expired. The agent mistakes the date for the cause and installs "never deploy on Tuesdays," and later runs obey. The example shows that [*self-improving*](../notes/definitions/self-improving-system.md) describes the objective of a process, not the result of every update: the agent changes its own policy in pursuit of improvement and installs a bad rule.

A correct policy that reduced failures would be a retained gain. If the system instead installed a causal-evidence check and later used that check to diagnose another failure and install a better policy, the second improvement would depend on the first. That is local evidence of *compounding*: an improvement's benefit helped produce a later improvement. A system can retain many useful rules while each new rule stays just as hard to find, evaluate, and install; then task performance improves but the process that produces the next improvement does not, and [improvements accumulate without compounding](../notes/improvements-can-accumulate-without-compounding.md). A change's acceptance metric shows whether it met its target, not whether it made the next revision more productive, so [compounding is tested in a later episode](../notes/compounding-is-tested-in-later-improvement-not-by-the-accepting-metric.md): did the retained benefit make a subsequent revision cheaper, broader, more reliable, or less human-dependent, and can the dependence be traced?

Three tests apply to any named improvement path:

| Test | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Occurrence** | What system boundary, time horizon, and objective are in scope? Did evidence change the system's own organization? Did later behavior use that change? |
| **Revision surface** | Which behavior-shaping artifacts could this path revise? Which did it revise, which stayed fixed, and could the path still be used afterward? |
| **Compounding** | Did a later improvement measurably depend on the retained benefit? How were the episodes connected, and has this happened repeatedly? |

The Tuesday rule passes the occurrence test and fails the compounding test. A candidate cannot set its own judging standard: each episode needs an [objective](../notes/self-improvement-is-relative-to-a-declared-objective.md) and comparison rule fixed before evaluation, and changing the standard is another revision that must be authorized separately.

Two contrasts mark the limits. W. Ross Ashby's Homeostat ([source analysis](../sources/ashby-design-for-a-brain-1960.ingest.md)) adapts, on my reading, with no theory at all: "if the trial is unsuccessful, change the way of behaving; when and only when it is successful, retain the way of behaving," and the replacement values "have no special relation either to the previous values or to the problem in hand — they are just the values that next follow in Fisher and Yates' table." The retained setting is operative but nothing in it informs its successor. That is adaptation with nothing to revise. At the other limit, a Gödel machine can rewrite even the machinery that chooses later rewrites, but only after proving the switch beats continuing the search; its revision surface is broad and formal, and it excludes every beneficial change it cannot prove. Retained natural-language theory sits between these: revisable, informative to its successor, and judged by an evaluator that is fallible.

The reported systems whose paths revise readable artifacts — harness optimizers that rewrite their own prompts and tool handling, online refiners that repair an embodied agent's skills, learners that accumulate behavioral rules for a coding agent, and archive-based evolution of agent code — have been [inventoried elsewhere](../notes/evidence/six-reported-self-improvement-paths-expose-bounded-redesign-surfaces.md) against these tests. The summary is that most establish occurrence and a bounded revision surface, with the evaluator, the permitted edit surface, and the selection machinery supplied by the researchers and left fixed. The closest later-episode test among them is [HyperAgents](../sources/hyperagents.ingest.md): evolved meta-agents, with their agent-modifying components frozen, generated stronger agents for an unseen task than the initial meta-agent did. Because each meta-agent bundles prompts, insight files, and code, the result does not isolate which retained change did the work, and continued evolution from transferred rather than fresh meta-agents showed no significant advantage. It shows that retained changes improved one later episode, not that the feedback recurred. None of the reported results establishes recurrent compounding.

### Three recent harnesses on the grid

Three systems reported in August 2026 make the grid of the learned-state section concrete, because each has two of the three properties and lacks the third in an instructive way.

[Prime Agent](../sources/prime-agent-a-self-improving-rlm-harness.ingest.md) is a persistent coding-agent harness whose continual layer keeps prompt notes, memories, executable skills, and subagent specifications on disk across trajectories, with the model itself proposing edits and a runtime applying them at turn boundaries with versioning and rollback. The base system prompt is kept outside the editable state. The retained artifacts are about the agent's own behavior and division of labor, so the path is reflective, and it is run in pursuit of benchmark improvement, so it is self-improving in the sense of this article's definition. What it retains is a typed set of rules, facts, and programs; nothing in the retained state explains why an edit helped or says where it stops applying, and nothing judges an edit before it is installed — versioning and rollback make a bad update inspectable and reversible without providing a gate that can refuse it. The consequence appeared in the paper's own long run: a refinement preserved a resource-spawning exploit of the game environment as a reusable skill. That is the Tuesday rule with better bookkeeping — an installed change that later runs obey, judged by nothing but the score it improved.

[Recuris](../sources/recursive-experiential-working-memory-evolution.ingest.md) is the closest of the three to a proposal-selection loop with a real evaluator. A fixed meta-agent reads a failed trajectory, localizes the failure to one of four memory components — experiential skills, a working-memory state specification, invocation triggers, and completion checkers — patches only the implicated component, and submits the patch to a fixed admission gate that accepts it only if it repairs the source failure without breaking a held-out set of tasks the current memory already solves. Memory evolved from sixteen failures raised success on eighty-six unseen tasks by nine to seventeen points, and a package shipped unchanged to a second model lifted it too. Two of the three properties are present: the working-memory and trigger components are reflective control state, and the gate makes the improvement evidence-responsive against a declared objective. The third is the one the paper is careful not to claim. Its localization step is described as a repair decision rather than a causal identification, and the retained package behaves accordingly: across eight accepted patches it added fifty-one skills, revised two, and deprecated none, leaving near-duplicate pairs in place. The package can be appended to and, through the gate, protected from regression; it cannot be rescoped by one premise, because no premise is retained. On this article's tests it establishes occurrence and a bounded revision surface. Its compounding claim — that a second round of evolution adds to the first — sits inside the paper's own noise estimate from rerunning an unchanged memory, and one lineage gives most of the second-round gain back in a later round. That is accumulation with a good gate, and it is exactly what a rule pile without a theory should produce: each new rule is as hard to find as the last, and nothing retained makes the next diagnosis cheaper.

[Apodex 1.1](../sources/apodex-1-1-scaling-agentic-intelligence-for-complex-work.ingest.md) belongs on the other axis. Its improvement path revises model weights through an offline training program — supervised fine-tuning and a hindsight-guided reinforcement method that localizes the consequential decision points in a trajectory — run by the developers between releases. At deployment the coordination state lives in a run-scoped task board that is discarded when the run ends; nothing survives as a revisable prompt, skill, or memory. It is not a self-improvement loop in this article's sense, and it is not trying to be one. It marks the parametric end of the [representational-form](../notes/definitions/representational-form.md) axis: retention that is operative without being addressable, so that no theory in it can be named, criticized, or rescoped. Richard Sutton and Khurram Javed [argue the case for that end directly](../sources/sutton-javed-why-ai-models-stop-learning.ingest.md): context-state adaptation, in their view, cannot substitute for continual weight learning, because concept formation is weight learning — "So context can be in the state, too. It could be both, but you still need to be able to update the weights." This article does not deny that. Its claim concerns where the addressable theory lives when one is wanted, and the [companion article on the bitter lesson](./the-bitter-lesson-does-not-require-everything-to-live-in-weights.md) takes up the argument at length.

Read together, the three sharpen what the theory-mediated arrangement is for. Prime Agent has the persistence machinery and no evaluator. Recuris has the evaluator and the persistence, and its retained state is a set of rules whose reasons are not retained, so improvement accumulates without any handle for revising it as a whole. Apodex retains everything in a form that cannot be addressed at all. The missing ingredient in the two artifact-based systems is not more retention or a better gate but a retained object from which the patches are derived and against which the next failure is read — and what such an object demands of the interpreter is what the Commonplace section below reports.

## Commonplace as testbed, with its own episode as evidence

Commonplace is this project's attempt to build the arrangement described above: a knowledge base of natural-language theory, routed per call, with procedures derived from it, parts of it codified into validators, and human maintainers inside the [declared boundary](../reference/commonplace-declared-frame.md). Because maintainers are inside the boundary, its revision surface is not directly comparable with the reported systems, whose research teams sit outside the system boundary. What it claims is narrower than autonomy and more than editability: retained procedures make human–agent redesign an explicit operating path whose installed changes stay available for later use and revision.

What can be shown. An [audit of six operating paths](../notes/evidence/six-commonplace-paths-establish-broad-addressability-not-completeness.md) found many behavior-shaping artifacts that could be inspected and revised, without showing that it found them all; it also exposed a flaw in the system's own model of [behavioral authority](../notes/definitions/behavioral-authority.md) — its account of which retained artifacts shape which operations, and with what force — which could not express when different authority paths apply. That model is now a [revision target](../reference/proposals/revise-behavioral-authority-decomposition.md), and the exposure is a decomposition-level finding of the kind the reflective-turn section said retained theory should make statable. Separately, a [topic-index case](../notes/evidence/tag-readme-trace-observed-causal-connection.md) shows one codification end to end. Topic indexes that claimed to list every note on a topic had grown past the point where anyone could check the claim, and a retained note already explained the cost: an index marked complete tells a thorough reader to stop searching while members are missing. The convention was criticized as prose, then codified as a frontmatter mark that a validator checks, and later work reused the validator. What cannot be shown: a traced case in which a retained theory-layer improvement helped produce a later improvement.

What happened to the note behind this article is a smaller and more exact case, and it is evidence about one note on one day. The [basis note](../notes/naur-equates-machine-execution-with-formulated-criteria.md) went through two cycles of the project's automated improvement pass — a procedure that runs fixed gates over a note, records defeated premises, and repairs the note. Each cycle had the same shape. The author wrote a claim that overreached in a specific way. The pass found the overreach, correctly, with counterexamples that held: in the first cycle, that the note generalized from a subset of retained quotes to a source-wide claim; in the second, that it placed a trained interpreter "outside" Naur's partition when a language model is formal symbol manipulation on a computer and so inside it. Then the pass repaired by retreating to the nearest claim no gate could touch. The first cycle's claim, that Naur's human-only premise was *assumed*, became "Naur's retained passages do not establish a human-only theory bearer"; the second cycle's claim, that a trained interpreter escapes Naur's partition, became "the inexpressibility argument does not by itself bind program theory to humans." The pass recorded the contribution as preserved, then strengthened, and stopped. Both repairs were true. Neither was the claim the note was for: that the human binding rests on an identifiable premise, and what that premise is. What fixed the note each time was one sentence from the maintainer: first, that the human binding rests on a premise the pass had not looked for; second, that at the time, execution by machine meant formal interpretation, so the premise was accurate for its day and trained recognizers separated the two rather than escaping anything.

In Argyris's terms the pass does single-loop repair within its gates, and its closing self-assessment is self-sealing: it measured the repaired note against a brief reconstructed from the text whose claim it had just defeated, and found the contribution strengthened. In Naur's terms the pass is the successor group. It had the note's text, every report, the collection contracts, and the gates, and its repairs were patches — adequate to the checks, framed outside the note's theory — because what the note was for was in none of its inputs. Critique did not need the theory: a counterexample to a universal, a source-wide claim without source-wide grounding, a misplaced system, are all visible from the text and the gates, and the pass found every one. Repair did. Of Naur's three tests, the episode fails the second: the decision premise the repair needed — what the note was for — was one the interpreter could not regenerate, and nothing in its inputs supplied it. The project's response was to [stop the pass at the point where repair would change a claim](../reference/adr/080-full-passes-hand-claim-changes-back-as-a-pending-revise.md) and hand the objections back to whoever holds the theory. That is Naur's own conclusion — transfer needs contact with the theory-holder — reached by the machinery the article is about, on the note the article is about.

## The remaining frontier: direction

The proposer/judge account leaves out a further function. Given a named target, language models propose well; given a criterion, they judge tolerably. What they do not yet do well is the step above: taking a broad, fuzzy objective and deciding what to work on — noticing which strain matters, forming the sub-objective, allocating attention. In the topic-index case the agent drafted and the validator checked, but the maintainer noticed the strain, initiated the fix, and accepted the result. Two of the three human contributions were direction. This is not a retraction of the earlier claim that a model can make Naur's similarity judgment: given a demand, the model judges its similarity to what exists. What stays human is producing the demand — the same capability applied at the level of the system's own agenda rather than a single change.

Three mechanisms plausibly combine to keep this human. Decomposing a fuzzy objective requires a causal theory of what produces value in this system, which interventions compound and which are cosmetic; without one, proposals drift toward the demonstrable — tidying, added structure, surface legibility. The operator notices which cost recurs because the operator pays it, while an agent that starts each session fresh accumulates no such history. And [a proximate target is checked for achievement, not for warrant](../notes/a-proximate-target-is-checked-for-achievement-not-for-warrant.md): choosing a sub-goal is a bet on the objective that no check in the loop evaluates.

There is a trade here. Self-direction is cheap exactly where the objective is narrow. The [Darwin Gödel Machine](../sources/darwin-godel-machine-open-ended-evolution-self-improving-agents.ingest.md) chooses its own next move because its objective is a benchmark score, and the price is that it can move only in directions the score can see. Commonplace keeps its objective broad, and the price is that direction stays with the maintainer. Giving the agents a measurable proxy would buy self-direction and invite optimizing the proxy in the same move. For this project, the objective is declared but not instrumented, and most movement is directed by the operator.

## What remains to test

Two experiments and one pre-registration requirement remain.

The [objective-level ablation](../reference/proposals/ablation-baselines-for-the-declared-objective.md) would compare the full framework with variants that remove or replace parts of it, testing whether Commonplace earns its total lifecycle cost. The [companion article on the bitter lesson](./the-bitter-lesson-does-not-require-everything-to-live-in-weights.md) supplies the text-versus-weights context for that comparison.

The later-episode experiment would compare Commonplace with frozen-artifact and simpler-memory variants and trace how a later revision depends on an earlier benefit, directly or through freed and reinvested capacity. Tasks should require revising the problem decomposition, not only local edits; otherwise matched rules can mimic theory-mediated reinterpretation. Assigning maintainers to conditions before any episode, or using outside operators, would separate the artifacts' effect from what an experienced maintainer remembers.

The addition is pre-registration of Naur's tests. Before claiming that an interpreter-plus-artifact composite holds a theory of the system, state what program-specific acquisition would look like, which decision premises the retained text must supply because the interpreter cannot regenerate them, and how many occasions of coherent modification count as reliability. The episode in the Commonplace section failed the second test in a way that was visible only afterwards; pre-registering it would have made the failure a measurement.

Both experiments should measure revision effort, outcome quality, transfer, and full lifecycle cost, including human judgment, training, and maintenance.
