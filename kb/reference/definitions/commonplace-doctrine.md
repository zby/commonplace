# Commonplace doctrine

**Commonplace doctrine** is the standing instruction a worker inherits with binding force when its runtime loads it: root `AGENTS.md`, plus the collection contracts, type specs, and skill the runtime supplies to that worker. A handoff is a delta from it — purpose, deviations, and deliberately open choices — and the worker completes the rest from the doctrine rather than from guesswork.

The compound is the technical term. Bare *doctrine* is ordinary English — a standing framework a community adopts for how it normally understands and conducts work — and may abbreviate the compound where nothing else could be meant. In the retained planning reports, unqualified *doctrine* names the source-side military doctrines under comparison (MCDP 1, ADRP 6-0); write the compound there.

## What it includes

- **Adopted, not merely available.** A methodology is available for selection and binds nothing by itself; the doctrine is the selection. The same material changes role when a binding path adopts it.
- **Interpretive as well as prescriptive.** It supplies a shared way to read a task and derive means, not only a list of rules. This is what lets a compact handoff stay intelligible.
- **The retained delta over the model's repertoire.** Departures from broad professional defaults, selections among equally conventional alternatives, and binding restatements of practices the model already endorses.

## What it excludes

- **Text no worker runtime loads.** Force comes from the consumption path, not from content or declared intent; a rule in a file nobody loads is not doctrine for anyone.
- **Institutional memory.** Observations, evaluation results, and accepted decisions are evidence or commitments until adopted into a binding path.
- **Exact protocols and codified enforcement.** Grammars, digests, validators, and schemas share the control plane but carry their own verification regime; calling them doctrine hides it.
- **Task authority.** The doctrine supplies interpretation and defaults. Authority, owned mutations, task-specific constraints, and acceptance stay explicit in the handoff.

## Misuse Cases

- Omitting a handoff rule because "the doctrine covers it" without checking that the worker's runtime loads the file that carries it.
- Treating an omitted detail the doctrine does not determine as inherited. It is delegated, irrelevant, or a gap.
- Using bare *doctrine* in a passage where source-side doctrine is also in play.
