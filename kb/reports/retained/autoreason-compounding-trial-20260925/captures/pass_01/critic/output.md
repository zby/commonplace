# Editorial critique

## The indirect causal trace is difficult to parse as one paragraph

In “A later episode also needs a causal trace,” the paragraph beginning “The trace may be direct” carries the direct mechanism, the three links of the indirect mechanism, two limiting assumptions, an example of an incomplete path, and the relation to the substitution test. Those are distinct argumentative roles. Their compression makes it difficult to keep track of which condition belongs to which path, especially when the paragraph moves from “another bottleneck” to “a fixed budget” and then back to direct consumption.

## The indirect mechanism is repeatedly explained at nearly full length

The three-link sequence—capacity is freed or preserved, an allocator makes it available, and later improvement consumes it—appears in the causal-trace section, the Agent Optimizers section, and the Harness Benefit section. The Agent Optimizers passage also repeats the usable-resource and other-bottleneck qualifications. Each study has a distinct evidential role, but the repeated general mechanism obscures how much of each passage is a new application and how much restates the protocol. The middle of the note consequently feels less progressive than its argument is.

## The practical protocol is separated from its introduction by a long evidential detour

The opening identifies two measurement displacements, and the causal-trace section concludes with “The protocol therefore combines a displaced measure with an uptake or reinvestment trace.” Three substantial study discussions then intervene before “What to measure in the later episode” and the baseline comparisons. The reader must hold an incomplete operational picture through the studies before reaching the concrete measures and comparisons. The study headings identify their individual findings, but the transition into this extended comparison does little to explain its place in the unfolding protocol.

## The Harness Benefit section changes levels of explanation abruptly

This section first gives the study's measurement ladder, then explains the additional feedback edge required by compounding, and then returns to study methods with “The study supports the outcome comparison.” At that point, “the outcome comparison” has no single clear local antecedent: the preceding paragraph concerns causal contribution to later improvement, while the ensuing methods concern task benefit and judged procedural match. The final paragraph also moves through controlled pairings, the adherence judge, a missing matched condition, fixed experimental surfaces, and the absent feedback edge in quick succession. These claims belong to different levels of the argument, and their order makes their respective evidential limits harder to distinguish.
