Try this method. Imagine a total order: a ranking that would tell you, for any two versions, which is better overall. You need not know that ranking, have a score for it, or assume everyone would rank the versions alike. Use the comparisons you can defend as a partial order: some pairs can be ranked for the work you care about; others remain open. Treat that partial order as a fallible approximation of the overall ranking, not as its definition.

Choose the work and values that matter to your judgment. Establish what changed relative to the earlier state. To move from a gain in one area to a preference overall, consider how likely losses elsewhere are and how much they would matter. Distinguish an unknown that could plausibly reverse your judgment from one that would not. Give the comparison you can defend, even if it falls short of an overall verdict.
