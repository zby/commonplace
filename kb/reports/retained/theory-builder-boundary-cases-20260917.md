## Reading the assessment

The boundary includes the roles that produce, diagnose, revise, select, and
maintain the retained knowledge or machinery. An operator doing that work
belongs inside the assessed builder for that episode. Supplying demands or
judging completed outputs can be an outside role, even when the same person
also does internal work. Role separation does not establish statistical
independence or remove evaluator bias.

External assessment, evidence access, and continuing responsibility are
separate questions. A fixed objective can be externally assessed. An
internally represented utility can refer to externally supplied outcomes.
Conversely, a separate judge does not make adaptively reused evidence an
untouched test. Continuing responsibility means maintaining and revising the
relevant commitments across later work; neither an indefinite runtime nor
mere archive retention is its test. A bounded experiment can show such
responsibility within its horizon. Where a paper does not establish it, the
answer below remains unknown.

“Observed” below means reported in the inspected source, except where the row
explicitly identifies local documentation or a proposal. None of the external
implementations was executed for this assessment. External task success also
does not establish that every retained object is a theory, or that a retained
explanation caused the success.

Commonplace's retained
[reflection case](../../notes/evidence/commonplace-as-a-reflective-system.md)
reports a two-way causal path through the tag-README change, with human
design judgment and improvement bounded by the adopted criterion. That
local evidence is distinct from the proposed downstream experiment. The
lack of a run under the new protocol does not withdraw the earlier bounded
reflection claim; this assessment does not reaudit its commit trace.
