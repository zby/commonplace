# RepoCodex reconciliation

## Reconciliation

### Boundary and evidence limits

The four reports describe the repository-memory loop fixed by [the boundary](boundary.md#boundary-and-evidence), not the external coding runtime. Its [Source register](boundary.md#source-register) supplies implementation and doctrine/design evidence only. Source-wired commands, requested agent behavior, actual compliance and causal benefit remain separate conclusions. No supplied correction answers or refusal require a further disposition. This reconciliation uses the supplied members only; it makes no additional source reads.

### Record identity and containment

No duplicate identity is established, so no supersession amendment is made. The following comparisons rule out identity rather than choosing one analyst's status over another's.

- [MEM-RTE-concept-read](memory.md#mem-rte-concept-read) and [RT-RTE-context-read](runtime.md#rt-rte-context-read) share stored concepts but are different operations. The former selects an exact identity and returns complete frontmatter/body without lifecycle filtering; the latter selects by edit paths, ranks eligible bodies and supplies related titles. Runtime mentions the direct-read alternative, but that mention does not make its path-selection referent identical to the separately traced tool. Both records remain; findings about default retrieval must not be extended to direct identity access.
- [MEM-RTE-metric-readback](memory.md#mem-rte-metric-readback) is a declared part of [RT-RTE-validate-change](runtime.md#rt-rte-validate-change). It traces cross-invocation diagnostic event consumption, not the whole change-validation operation. The parent remains valid alongside that part. The metric store, [MEM-OBJ-metrics](memory.md#mem-obj-metrics), is not identical to either route or to a concept explanation.
- [EPI-OBJ-bootstrap-body](epistemic.md#epi-obj-bootstrap-body) is a provenance-specific part of [RT-OBJ-concept-body](runtime.md#rt-obj-concept-body). The copied-note subset does not replace the general explanation object. [EPI-RTE-bootstrap-import](epistemic.md#epi-rte-bootstrap-import) is the extraction/transformation part of [RT-RTE-bootstrap-drafts](runtime.md#rt-rte-bootstrap-drafts), whose gate and persistence remain additional functions. Neither valid container requires supersession.
- [EPI-RTE-claim-check](epistemic.md#epi-rte-claim-check) and [EPI-RTE-relocation-check](epistemic.md#epi-rte-relocation-check) are distinct diagnostic parts of [RT-RTE-validate-change](runtime.md#rt-rte-validate-change). The former checks stored literals at maintenance time; the latter generates/checks relocation candidates after missed anchors. Neither duplicates write-time admission in [RT-RTE-memory-write](runtime.md#rt-rte-memory-write). Relocation checking also differs from mutation in [RT-RTE-anchor-patch](runtime.md#rt-rte-anchor-patch): an offered replacement and its application have different effects and admission conditions.
- [EPI-RTE-semantic-review](epistemic.md#epi-rte-semantic-review) and [EPI-RTE-audit-gc](epistemic.md#epi-rte-audit-gc) are separately declared parts of [RT-RTE-review-audit](runtime.md#rt-rte-review-audit). Requested agent judgment is advisory; code-driven retirement persists lifecycle status. The container already distinguishes these functions and remains valid. Memory's annotations add withdrawal and reader detail without declaring duplicate routes.
- [EPI-RTE-impact-read](epistemic.md#epi-rte-impact-read) returns advisory impacted identities through MCP. It is distinct from ranked-body delivery in [RT-RTE-context-read](runtime.md#rt-rte-context-read) and from the full verdict returned by [RT-RTE-validate-change](runtime.md#rt-rte-validate-change), even though it calls validation and shares impact machinery.
- [MEM-OBJ-reverse-index](memory.md#mem-obj-reverse-index), [MEM-OBJ-context-catalog](memory.md#mem-obj-context-catalog) and [MEM-OBJ-context-log](memory.md#mem-obj-context-log) retain generated lookup metadata, navigation summaries and operation history respectively. They are not identities of the explanation body or parsed concept metadata in [RT-OBJ-concept-body](runtime.md#rt-obj-concept-body) and [RT-OBJ-concept-frontmatter](runtime.md#rt-obj-concept-frontmatter). A producer's mention of its outputs is not a declaration of those outputs as the route's referent.
- [RT-CLM-executable-memory](runtime.md#rt-clm-executable-memory) describes the operation bundle. [EPI-CLM-pin-match](epistemic.md#epi-clm-pin-match) concerns the license claimed for attachment checks; [EPI-CLM-loop-protection](epistemic.md#epi-clm-loop-protection) concerns denial and protective consequences. These are different claims, not competing declarations of one claim. Workflow declares no new records and instead annotates the supplied coding and review routes.

### Integration-issue dispositions

**Index-consumption clarification.** Memory's [integration issues](memory.md#integration-issues) flag runtime's reverse-index wording. [RT-RTE-context-read](runtime.md#rt-rte-context-read) says selection uses a merged reverse index and retains an index-completeness limit; [MEM-OBJ-reverse-index](memory.md#mem-obj-reverse-index) supplies the retained `merged_index` passage showing reconstruction from current concepts, while persisted copies feed synchronization checks. These statements do not expressly disagree about the implementation: runtime does not assert that retrieval loads the committed files. No conflict or supersession is established. The clarification prevents treating stale committed index files as sufficient evidence that context omitted a concept. It does not establish universal retrieval completeness.

**Withdrawal is consumer-specific.** Memory's issue about residual access agrees with runtime's scoped selected-body filter. [EPI-RTE-audit-gc](epistemic.md#epi-rte-audit-gc) and the memory annotation on [RT-RTE-review-audit](runtime.md#rt-rte-review-audit) retain status retirement rather than deletion. [MEM-RTE-concept-read](memory.md#mem-rte-concept-read) can still return deprecated content, and related-title delivery differs from selected-body filtering. These are different consumers, not contradictory statuses or a missing replacement record.

**Shard identity remains a limit.** Memory reports possible same-identity collisions across shards in its [integration issues](memory.md#integration-issues), concerning [RT-OBJ-concept-frontmatter](runtime.md#rt-obj-concept-frontmatter), [RT-RTE-memory-write](runtime.md#rt-rte-memory-write), [RT-RTE-context-read](runtime.md#rt-rte-context-read) and [MEM-RTE-concept-read](memory.md#mem-rte-concept-read). Its account distinguishes dictionary replacement from first-match reads/writes. Runtime's shard-relative identity description does not assert globally unique identities, so no cross-report contradiction is established. An unambiguous cross-shard consumer guarantee is prevented; an actual collision is not evidenced.

**Initial provenance and activation remain unresolved facts, not report disagreements.** Memory's provenance annotations and [EPI-RTE-bootstrap-import](epistemic.md#epi-rte-bootstrap-import) agree that copied text and a file-touch commit pointer do not authenticate the original explanation. The host limitations in [RT-CMP-coding-agent](runtime.md#rt-cmp-coding-agent) and [RT-BAP-memory-consumption](runtime.md#rt-bap-memory-consumption) likewise permit delivery without established changed behavior. These uncertainties do not negate known persistence, deterministic transformations or later read opportunities.

### Convergence and workflow connections

The reports converge on multiple admission mechanisms rather than an all-writes gate. Memory's write-side account and epistemic's authority ledger preserve runtime's anchorless admission, direct patching and pre-rejection predecessor deprecation in [RT-RTE-memory-write](runtime.md#rt-rte-memory-write) and [RT-RTE-anchor-patch](runtime.md#rt-rte-anchor-patch). Workflow uses those same limits when assigning corrective retry and restoration to the foreground agent; it does not turn requested retry into an observed recovery.

Memory's [MEM-CLM-context-selection](memory.md#mem-clm-context-selection) adds that ranking orders full eligible bodies without a hard token cutoff. Runtime and workflow describe bounded delivery and caller inspection, not compaction or eviction of host turns. These findings are compatible: selection can withhold unrelated bodies from a response without bounding all delivered tokens or the host's accumulated window.

The review accounts independently converge on requested semantic comparison without automatic required-check force: [EPI-RTE-semantic-review](epistemic.md#epi-rte-semantic-review), [RT-RTE-review-audit](runtime.md#rt-rte-review-audit) and [workflow's review annotation](workflow.md#annotations) distinguish external review-agent work from the advisory Action's deterministic output. GC has a different consumer and effect; no single semantic-acceptance status can be assigned to the combined route.

Finally, [workflow's coding-loop annotation](workflow.md#annotations) locates completion in resolving blocking results, rechecking coverage and committing code with memory/indexes. [RT-BAP-check-enforcement](runtime.md#rt-bap-check-enforcement) makes actual denial conditional on caller, posture, acknowledgment and invocation. [RT-RTE-agent-repair](runtime.md#rt-rte-agent-repair) supplies no post-child repaired-state certificate. Epistemic's distinction between operational permission and semantic acceptance is therefore compatible with workflow completion, not a disagreement over whether storage or commit checks exist. No anchored cross-report disagreement or required undeclared part was established from the supplied records; observed compliance, accepted semantic revisions and causal protection remain unestablished rather than reconciled into stronger findings.
