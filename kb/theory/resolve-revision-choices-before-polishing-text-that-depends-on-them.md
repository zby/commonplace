# Unresolved revision choices put dependent polish at risk of rework

An unresolved revision choice can make prose improvements useless. If a claim, audience, scope, or section order changes, text may need replacement or deletion. Polishing that text first adds work exposed to the change. This gives revisers a reason to address the choice before substantial dependent polish, but not necessarily to commit immediately: evidence needs and the costs of waiting can outweigh the rework avoided.

The mechanism is dependence, not document size or level. A choice matters here when its resolution could undo the usefulness of particular work. If the text survives unchanged, or its improvements transfer to the revised version, that work is not lost through this mechanism. The argument supports a bounded revision heuristic, not a universally best schedule.

## Check early without forcing commitment

Revision starts from an existing artifact, not an empty design space. Early checking first asks whether the existing claim or structure needs changing; it does not reopen every commitment. If a commitment stands, dependent polish need not wait for a hypothetical redesign. The relevant risk arises when a consequential change is under consideration.

For choices that do need reopening, early checking identifies the prose work that depends on them. It does not require settling every choice before editing. Some revision work produces evidence needed to choose.

Consider two sections whose order remains open. Extensive polish of the transition between them is exposed to rework if their order changes. A short trial transition may instead reveal that the proposed order does not support the argument. Structural organization concerns the order and relationships of sections or paragraphs; a local transition expresses a connection at one boundary. They interact, but they are not the same task.

The trial is diagnostic only if some possible result could change the decision. Merely editing a sentence does not establish an evidence route. As explained in [the account of productive deferral](./productive-deferral-requires-option-evidence-and-convergence.md), controlled postponement requires an option that remains available, possible decision-changing evidence, and a return condition tied to a response. Those conditions do not establish that waiting is worth its cost.

Nor does early attention transfer authority. A reviser may identify a choice without permission to settle it. [Who decides, when judgment occurs, and what evidence supports it are separate questions](./fix-what-the-executor-cant-determine-not-what-it-will.md). The authorized chooser may need evidence produced during revision; urgency alone supplies neither that evidence nor missing permission.

## Delay only the work affected

Independent repairs need not wait. Correcting a broken link can proceed while a claim revision remains open if the contemplated change cannot affect that repair. Independence depends on the actual changes under consideration, not on how small the repair looks.

Potential rework is also not the only cost. Waiting, probing, maintaining alternatives, and delaying delivery all consume resources. Proceeding provisionally, or committing on current evidence, can be preferable when those costs outweigh the polish likely to be discarded. The heuristic identifies one exposure to waste; it does not calculate the lowest total cost.

## Different ordering mechanisms answer different questions

[Ordering low-degree-of-freedom choices first](./solve-low-degree-of-freedom-subproblems-first-to-avoid-blocking.md) protects viable options. An early flexible commitment can consume the few alternatives available to another choice. Addressing the less flexible choice first can preserve feasibility.

Dependent-polish ordering asks a different question: which open choice could make completed prose work unusable? A claim may have many possible revisions while much prose depends on it. A metadata requirement may permit only one value while putting no prose at risk. Few options and much dependent work are not interchangeable properties. Neither mechanism refutes the other, and neither supplies a universal priority over evidence needs.

The starting situation is a separate distinction. Designing a new artifact asks which choices to make; revising an existing one also asks which commitments to preserve, reopen, or replace. This does not restrict option preservation to new design or rework avoidance to revision. A new draft can accumulate work that later choices discard, while a revision can have tightly constrained choices whose few alternatives need protection. Starting situation and reason for ordering are two dimensions, not interchangeable classifications.

[Purdue's revision advice](../sources/purdue-higher-order-lower-order-concerns.ingest.md) prioritizes thesis or focus, audience and purpose, organization, and development over sentence structure and grammar. It also permits movement between these concerns and recognizes that punctuation or spelling errors may prevent readers from reaching the thesis. The recommendation and exception belong together.

That advice is compatible with, but does not establish, the dependency explanation here. Local readability may be needed to obtain useful reader feedback; treating that feedback as decision evidence is an application of this argument, not Purdue's stated finding. Broad work does not always precede small work, and local work is not always premature polish.

## Scope

This is a reasoned heuristic for agents and maintainers revising KB documents. It identifies a possible source of discarded polish, not demonstrated net savings, better writing, or improved agent effectiveness. Purdue supplies institutional writing advice, not a comparative test of this mechanism.

The argument provides no measured threshold for substantial polish, reliable method for finding every prose dependency, estimate of invalidation likelihood, or numerical comparison of rework and delay costs. These remain judgment gaps. Resolving a choice means selecting an answer within the chooser's authority, not making it immune to later revision.

Broader software or design applications remain conjectural; they are not established by the revision argument.
