# Context isolation for focus conflicts with global prioritization

Solving a hard problem in a bounded LLM context pushes an agent system toward **context isolation**, because [context efficiency binds first on per-window feasibility](./context-efficiency-is-the-central-design-concern-in-agent-systems.md). Context isolation loads deep task-relevant material and excludes unrelated work so the task stays within the model's usable attention budget. **Global prioritization** moves in the opposite direction: deciding how much effort a local branch deserves relative to other work, when to stop or defer on opportunity cost, and whether a blocker warrants interrupting scarce operator attention. Whenever the right decision changes as the operator's other work changes—and no locally calibrated scale translates that competing work into a compact threshold—a worker that sees only its own task cannot reliably make that decision. Isolating an agent for task focus therefore removes the live comparison set that relational global prioritization requires.

This claim is narrower than saying isolated agents lack judgment. Isolation does not prevent **task-internal** technical assessment: the worker can still judge what breaks inside its loaded scope, how soon that local failure manifests, and whether deferring the local change is reversible within that scope—precisely the judgments a later reader without deep task detail cannot cheaply reconstruct. Cross-boundary blast radius, external deadlines, and external reversibility still depend on excluded state.

**Claim mode: universal** under three conditions: bounded effective context, a competing workload whose comparison-sufficient live summary cannot sit beside deep task context without eroding task competence past the cost of a split, and open-ended trade-offs lacking a locally calibrated common scale (such as a numeric latency budget or dollar cost) that would let a compact injected threshold stand in for competing tasks.

## Missing context causes indiscrimination; defaults set the failure direction

Inside a task-scoped session, the loaded context is the agent's entire live world. A human specialist faces a milder form of local focus, but still carries an ambient cross-project model of deadlines, bottlenecks, and the cost of interrupting colleagues. A stateless, isolated agent retains generic base-rate severity priors from pretraining, but has no live, project-specific comparison set outside what its window holds.

Missing competing-work context alone explains why the worker cannot know its issue's relative priority; it does not by itself explain why the worker acts as if that priority is high. An equally isolated worker under a silent-continuation default can suppress a real emergency and keep going. The *direction* of the failure comes from a separate mechanism when relative priority is unknown—most visibly the workflow's or policy's default action, reinforced by model-level thoroughness and salience biases. When those defaults favor interrupting, fixing every noticed defect, or guarding every conjectured edge case, the missing comparison set surfaces in three familiar ways:

- **Uncalibrated escalation.** Parallel isolated workers each hit local blockers without seeing one another's work or the operator's queue. In a practitioner account that motivated this analysis, Nostalgebraist quotes a public Anthropic write-up about internal coding-agent use of Claude comparing multi-agent supervision to babysitting: "I need my juice box opened and it's an emergency that my juice box is opened. And that is voiced with the same level of concern as the one who has sliced their finger and is bleeding." ([Nostalgebraist](../sources/nostalgebraist-coding-agent-mistakes-benchmark-gaps.ingest.md), verbatim) and "When you are babysitting, every child brings to you concerns with an equal demand for your attention and the various [Claudes] also do that." ([Nostalgebraist](../sources/nostalgebraist-coding-agent-mistakes-benchmark-gaps.ingest.md), verbatim). That example also reflects weak absolute-stakes calibration, and the account does not isolate whether worker behavior or a workflow that turns every stop into an immediate ping made interruption the default; either way, removing competing work leaves the isolated worker without live relative urgency.
- **Earnest low-value tangents.** Inside a local frame, fixing a minor inconsistency or chasing a side question looks concrete and justified when competing higher-value work is absent from the window. In the same practitioner account, Nostalgebraist describes coding agents as "liable to go off on silly low-value tangents (which they treat with the utmost seriousness)" ([Nostalgebraist](../sources/nostalgebraist-coding-agent-mistakes-benchmark-gaps.ingest.md), verbatim) and observes that "Everything is flattened out; everything is assigned equal importance; every interaction looks and feels the same" ([Nostalgebraist](../sources/nostalgebraist-coding-agent-mistakes-benchmark-gaps.ingest.md), verbatim).
- **Local over-specification.** Inside one module or diff, guarding a hypothetical edge case looks prudent when system-wide complexity budgets and accepted product tolerances are absent, compounding the oracle asymmetry—tests reward a present guard but rarely penalize an unneeded one—analyzed in [a guard against a conjectured requirement removes the experiment that would test it](./a-guard-against-a-conjectured-requirement-removes-the-experiment-that.md).

Adding standing priority instructions to always-loaded context ("focus on high-leverage work; do not escalate trivia") does not resolve this conflict. Because [intent controls a local choice only when it distinguishes its live alternatives](./intent-controls-choices-by-distinguishing-live-alternatives.md), a general priority rule cannot order a local branch against competing work the worker cannot see. Explicit static rules can eliminate *previously named* non-priorities and encode known tolerances; they cannot supply live relative scale for novel trade-offs across a changing workload. Nor can an external monitor always solve the problem by injecting a compact live cost signal (such as a scalar priority bar or dynamic threshold) into the isolated worker. Injected cutoffs work when the local task and the global signal share a locally calibrated scale—for instance, a numeric latency budget, token cap, or metered dollar cost that the worker can evaluate directly. In open-ended work without such a scale, a compressed cost signal is not comparable in local scope: the isolated worker cannot calibrate its local issue against an abstract bar, whereas a global view lets the agent compare concrete tasks side by side. Injecting a compact marginal-anchor card naming the few concrete competing tasks at the current priority cutoff can bring concrete comparison into local scope without a numeric scale, but a wide-shallow global pass still has to choose and refresh those cutoff tasks—and a static card lags when sibling workers shift priorities concurrently.

## Splitting narrow-deep execution from wide-shallow triage

Because deep problem solving requires a **narrow-deep** context and relational global prioritization requires a **wide-shallow** comparison set, the two functions cannot share one bounded call once even a comparison-sufficient summary of competing work cannot sit beside deep task context without eroding task competence past the cost of a split—and no locally calibrated threshold or stable cutoff card suffices. Separating them turns the conflict into an architectural interface between separate bounded calls, consistent with [bounded-context orchestration](./bounded-context-orchestration-model.md):

1. **Narrow-deep execution (local judgment under injected boundaries).** The task worker receives scope, explicit tolerances, and local stop conditions from outside, and is not asked to judge global urgency. When it encounters an out-of-scope defect, tangent, or decision boundary, it records **scale-bearing local facts** on a shared surface or in a structured stop record. Those are the task-internal judgments its deep context uniquely supports: what breaks inside its loaded scope, how soon that local failure manifests, whether waiting is reversible locally, whether the main path is blocked, and which local branch or assumption would change.
2. **Wide-shallow triage (global comparison without execution detail).** A separate pass—an operator, coordinator, or dedicated triage call (preceded by filtering or indexing when a portfolio is too large for one pass)—reads compressed summaries of competing items side-by-side against current bottlenecks without loading deep task context for any single item. It decides which items earn execution effort, which wait on a named wake-up event, and which warrant operator attention now.

Crucially, this split unbundles two decisions that naive agent workflows collapse into one, while keeping a third workflow-memory policy orthogonal:

- **"This run should stop"** — a local execution judgment the isolated worker can make when a hard blocker or pre-injected stop rule triggers (whereas stopping on diminishing returns or global opportunity cost belongs to triage).
- **"The operator should act now"** — a global allocation judgment that depends on competing work.
- **"This finding or answer should survive"** — an orthogonal workflow-memory policy ensuring later runs do not re-ask the same question, independent of whether triage routes the item to immediate action.

A workflow that bundles local stopping with immediate operator action manufactures urgency even when the worker's local stop is sound. Unbundling them lets an unattended worker stop cleanly on a hard blocker and preserve its finding without demanding immediate operator intervention—addressing stop-time escalation when interactive oversight is removed ([unattended work needs the functions that interaction supplied](./unattended-work-needs-the-functions-that-interaction-supplied.md)), while mid-run tangents that never hit a local stop still rely on bounded budgets or external interruption.

Three mechanisms in Commonplace—the agent-operated knowledge-base framework this note belongs to—illustrate this split at different scales (scoped as local design examples, not corroboration that the split outperforms alternatives):

- *Mid-task noticing:* [traversal improvements deferred via logging](./traversal-improvements-should-be-deferred-via-logging-to-avoid-mid.md) separates noticing a defect during reading from prioritizing and fixing it by emitting a one-line log entry for a later pass.
- *Single-artifact workflow stops:* [ADR 138](../reference/adr/138-a-stop-ends-the-run-and-naming-its-output-is-the-approval.md) (an architecture decision record) unbundles these decisions for unattended note revision. A run ends at its first stop with a structured continuation record, an operator's answer to a stop can be recorded so the next run does not re-ask, and commissioning follow-up work such as a rewrite remains a separate decision.
- *Cross-surface queueing:* leaving open items in place for a later global scan over the [unresolved frontier](../reference/definitions/unresolved-frontier.md)—work actionable now by an available actor or process—lets local tasks stay focused while a global pass compares candidates against current state without each worker carrying portfolio context.

## Residual friction at the interface

Splitting execution from triage relocates the trade-off to the boundary between views rather than eliminating it:

- **Compression hides emergencies.** Wide-shallow triage compares many candidates only by compressing each one. When an isolated worker emits an uninformative blocker ("blocked on operator input") or drops the local detail that makes an issue severe, triage must either fall back to deep context or misclassify severity by **unsafe substitution**—acting on a lossy summary as if it were the source. That failure follows directly because [an insufficient summary precedes the source rather than replacing it](./an-insufficient-summary-precedes-the-source-rather-than-replacing.md).
- **Salience itself is partly global.** Because cross-boundary blast radius depends on excluded state, an isolated worker cannot always tell which locally benign detail matters to an outside caller—for instance, changing serialization order that passes every local check while invalidating an external consumer's hash. Logging every benign local detail to guard against that gap breaks triage compression.
- **Within-episode micro-prioritization remains local.** Before returning to a global layer, a worker still makes many local branch choices (which hypothesis to test next, when to stop digging). Those choices remain bounded by whatever compressed proxy of global priority—intent, tolerances, depth limits, or budgets—fits cheaply inside the local window and can be calibrated locally without eroding focus.

## Scope

- On a small workload whose comparison-sufficient live state fits inside one usable window—or a separable workload whose opportunity cost reduces to a locally calibrated metric such as a dollar cap or latency budget—the conflict disappears or can be handled locally. When a stable cutoff frontier can be captured in a small marginal-anchor card, a global pass is still required to choose and maintain the card, though routine branch comparisons can stay inside the worker.
- The claim applies to *relational* prioritization—relative scale, opportunity cost, and escalation triage. Purely intrinsic local checks (validation failures, hard invariants, explicit stop rules) need no global comparison set and operate reliably inside an isolated worker.
- Separating execution from triage supplies a comparison surface for allocation; it does not choose or revise objectives, which remain outside allocation ([open-ended knowledge work shares one epistemic layer across roles](./open-ended-knowledge-work-shares-one-epistemic-layer-across-roles.md)).
- The practitioner account and Commonplace mechanisms cited above explain the claim's origin and illustrate one architectural response; neither supplies a controlled comparison measuring execution quality, triage error, or saved operator attention.

## Open Questions

- What minimal set of scale-bearing local facts lets a wide-shallow triage pass separate high-stakes exceptions from low-stakes ones without re-reading deep execution context?
- How much compressed global state (active bottlenecks, tolerances, budgets) can be injected into a focused worker's handoff—and what makes such signals locally calibratable—before context load degrades task solving?
- Does selecting from compressed frontier summaries systematically skew global triage toward easily specified, legible tasks—as [weakly discriminated qualities tend to be underselected](./weakly-discriminated-qualities-tend-to-be-underselected.md) predicts—and if so, what summary fields counter that skew without re-introducing unbounded assessment cost?
