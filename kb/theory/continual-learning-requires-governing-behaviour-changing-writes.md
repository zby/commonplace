# Storing past incidents does not ensure their lessons guide later reviews

“Check past incidents before publication” can remain the review rule even as new incidents accumulate. The instruction tells the reviewer where to look. It does not tell the reviewer which earlier failure bears on the page at hand.

Suppose a wiki reviewer discovers that two citations offered as independent support for a figure both copied it from one report. On a later page, neither citation appears. The lesson still applies if the new sources also share an origin and the claim relies on their independence. A lookup by source name would miss that connection. A reviewer following the original instruction could still recognize it.

[Retrieving the incident does not show that the reviewer used its lesson](./knowledge-storage-does-not-imply-contextual-activation.md). The test is whether the reviewer distinguishes apparent corroboration from independent support on the new page. The incident shows what the review must distinguish, not that the review rule must change.
