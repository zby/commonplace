## Purpose and scope

Map a live, sourced disagreement: who asserts what, and on what basis,
without adjudicating who is right. Retain position-bearing claims and the
evidence needed to attribute them. Do not write verdicts in the collection's
own voice.

## Quality goal

Faithfully represent the state of contestation. Ask whether each position,
qualification, and disagreement is attributed accurately and supported by a
locatable source span—not whether the collection's author believes it.

## Title and body conventions

Use topical titles naming a sub-question or a position-holder's stance,
never an unattributed conclusion. Keep the asserter grammatically explicit
in every substantive claim, including synthesis sentences.

Every substantive proposition needs:

1. an attributed asserter: the person, institution, or source that holds it;
2. a source-span citation: a file link, a prose locator, and one grounding
   marker—`verbatim`, `paraphrase layer`, or `second-hand`.

A nearby citation does not supply attribution for a sentence that makes its
own evaluation.

## Review

- The title states no verdict in the collection's voice.
- Every substantive claim names its asserter.
- Every citation carries a prose locator and grounding marker.
- Competing positions receive comparable interpretive care—not equal word
  count, but no side padded with weaker material to make it look stronger.

## Maintenance

Revisit an artifact when attribution no longer matches what a party asserts
or when a newly sourced position changes the map of disagreement. A change in
the maintainer's own view, or a judgment that one side lost, does not by
itself make the artifact stale.
